the method behind the number

How this actually works

Three real pieces, no black box in between: how the surrogate learned, what the numerical solve actually checks, and why agreement matters as much as speed.

1 The surrogate: a small MLP, trained on real physics-labeled data

The surrogate is a plain MLP — five inputs (fin count, fin height, fin thickness, power, convective coefficient), three hidden layers of 48 units with tanh activations, one output (PhysicsSurrogate.build_mlp, trained with Adam). Its 6,000 training samples are designs run through the closed-form fin-array model (pinneapple_physics.closed_form.fin_array_conduction). Training is deterministic (fixed seed), so the same weights come back every time; only the wall-clock timing measured against them varies run to run.

T_junction ≈ MLP(fin_count, fin_height, fin_thickness, power, h_conv)

That's the whole trick: a surrogate is only as honest as what it was trained to reproduce. Here, that's a real, textbook thermal model — not a black box learning an unknown target.

2 The cross-check: an independent numerical solve

The surrogate never sees the numerical solve — it only learns the closed-form model. solve_fin_cell discretises one unit cell (base slab and one fin) with cell-centred finite volumes and solves the steady conduction equation with the heat flux in from below, a film coefficient on the fin and the exposed base, and symmetry at the cell sides. The 3-D cell has nothing depending on the fin depth, so its 2-D cross-section is the same problem. Each design is also solved on two finer grids; the grid convergence index of the temperature rise is reported with the results, and the heat balance closes to round-off.

3 The agreement metric: why speed alone isn't the story

A fast wrong answer is worse than a slow right one. So every design on this page reports a surrogate-vs-reference difference, computed on temperature rise above ambient (not raw °C, since a fixed 25°C ambient would otherwise understate disagreement on smaller rises). The 10% band is the decision bound of this study, fixed before the sweep, not tuned to it.

Built on PINNeAPPle. The closed form is pinneapple_physics.closed_form.fin_array_conduction, the reference is pinneapple_simulation.numerical_solvers.fin_cell_fv.solve_fin_cell, and the study (designs, surrogate, timings, agreement, grid check) is pinneapple_design.thermal.fin_array_speed; this page only calls them.
