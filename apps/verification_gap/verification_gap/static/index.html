the method behind the number

How this actually works

Three real steps, no black box in between: the physics, the propagation, the verdict.

1 The physics: extended-surface conduction

A vented brake rotor's radial cooling vanes are, physically, the same kind of extended-surface (fin) heat exchanger as a CPU heatsink's fins — just cast iron instead of aluminum, and radial instead of straight. Heat generated at the friction surface (where the pad clamps the rotor) conducts through the friction ring, into the vanes, and out via convection into the air moving through the vent gap. The closed-form result (Incropera & DeWitt, Fundamentals of Heat and Mass Transfer) computes a fin efficiency and an effective convective area from the real vane geometry, giving a thermal resistance network from the friction surface to ambient air:

T_rotor = T_ambient + Q · (R_base + R_conv)

where R_base is 1D conduction through the friction-ring thickness and R_conv comes from the vane array's real fin efficiency (tanh(mL)/mL) and effective convective area. This is the exact same real formula this scenario's nominal 495.9°C comes from.

2 The propagation: real Monte Carlo, not a fudge factor

A "safety margin" is meaningless without knowing what actually varies. Real vanes come off the line within a real machining tolerance band — here, ±0.05mm on thickness and ±0.1mm on the vent gap, sampled uniformly (no process-capability data available to justify a narrower Gaussian assumption, so uniform is the honest, more conservative choice). Every one of the 200 samples on this page is a real, independent draw from that tolerance band, run through the exact same closed-form solver as the nominal number — not a faster approximation, not a surrogate. That's what turns one point into the real distribution you see above.

3 The verdict: a real, defensible threshold rule

The 95th percentile of the distribution is treated as the "worst realistic case." If it exceeds the real safety limit, the design is FRAGILE. If it stays under but within 5% of the limit, it's MARGINAL — technically safe, but with too little real margin to call it robust. Only when p95 sits comfortably clear of the limit is a design called ROBUST. The 5% band is a design choice of this study (marginal_fraction in pinneapple_analysis.uncertainty.margin.margin_study), not a standard.

the Monte Carlo margin study (pinneapple_analysis.uncertainty.margin)
from pinneapple_analysis.uncertainty.margin import margin_study
from pinneapple_physics.closed_form.vented_rotor import VentedRotor, rotor_temperature_c

rotor = VentedRotor()          # 36 vanes, 8 mm gap, 4 mm thick, cast iron
def temperature(X):           # rows: (vane thickness, vent gap)
    return [rotor_temperature_c(rotor.with_(vane_thickness_m=t, vane_gap_m=g), 1480, 40) for t, g in X]

study = margin_study(temperature, [0.004, 0.008], [0.05e-3, 0.1e-3], limit=500, n=200)
study["summary"]["verdict"], study["decision"]   # MARGINAL, REVIEW
