- `pinneapple_analysis.inverse_problems.structural` identifies a cantilever's parameters from displacements measured at a few gauge stations (issue #177, item S28): `timoshenko_deflection` and `timoshenko_model` are the forward model (tip load with the shear term, which at `L/h = 10` carries 0.8 % of the tip deflection), `load_measurements` reads the displacements a real CalculiX C3D20R run produced (stored in `tests/data/calculix_cantilever_displacements.json`, regenerated with `scripts/structural_identification.py --write`), and `identify_cantilever` recovers what the experiment does not know — `unknown="modulus"` with the load on a load cell, `"load"` with the material certified, or `"both"` — through `recover` with the profile interval and the identifiability analysis. The observation sigma carries the **measured** 3.3 % model-form gap between the 3-D solid and the beam formula next to the micrometre of gauge noise, so a model that cannot represent shear and local load deformation widens its interval instead of displacing it silently. Measured on the stored data (E = 70 GPa, P = 800 N at truth): the modulus comes back at 71.2 GPa with the truth inside `[68.9, 73.7]` GPa, the load at 786 N with the truth inside `[760, 813]` N, and both runs agree on the `P/E` ratio the data actually fix to 1e-3. With both unknowns the fit runs and the report names the dead direction — "moving P down and E down together leaves the predictions unchanged: the data fix P^-1·E, not P and E separately" — with a condition number of 6.6e33 and an open interval per parameter, because doubling E and doubling P leaves every measured number identical. 13 tests in `tests/pinneapple_analysis/test_structural_identification.py`, plus `scripts/structural_identification.py` as the study that regenerates the data and prints the three cases.
