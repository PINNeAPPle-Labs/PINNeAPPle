- `pp.tensor(data, name=..., units=..., domain=..., coords=..., components=...)` — a `torch.Tensor` that keeps what a plain tensor forgets (issue #186, item X2): the name, the unit (parsed by `pinneapple_data.physical_units`, so `"m/s"`, `"Pa"`, `"W/(m·K)"` all work), the domain, the coordinate and component names, the time and free metadata, all readable back through `.name`, `.units`, `.unit`, `.domain`, `.coords`, `.components` and `.semantics`. Every torch operation keeps working and returns the same class carrying the labels of the first labelled operand, so `u * 2` and `u.sum()` do not lose the unit; **autograd is untouched** — a labelled leaf with `requires_grad=True` gets its `.grad` filled by `backward()`, and results keep their `grad_fn`. It **refuses what it cannot answer**: additive and comparative operations between two labelled tensors of different dimension raise `UnitError` (`cannot apply 'add' to 'm/s' (velocity) and 'Pa' (pressure): the dimensions differ`), and so does a *product* of two dimensioned tensors, where a unit algebra would be needed — the message names the escape hatch (`without_labels()`, then `pp.tensor(..., units=...)`). `.convert("km/h")` moves inside a dimension and refuses anything else. Documented as what it is: labels survive arithmetic, not a trip through `.numpy()`.
