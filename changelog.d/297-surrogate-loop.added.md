- `pp.optimize.loop(space, surrogate, solver, budget=N)` runs the surrogate in the loop with a verification budget (#297): search on the surrogate, send the most promising *and* most uncertain designs to the solver inside a fixed number of calls, fold the answers back into the training data, refit, and stop when the **verified** optimum stops moving. The acquisition is constrained expected improvement (`"ei"`, default) or a feasibility-weighted lower confidence bound (`"lcb"`), read from whatever spread the surrogate reports — `GaussianProcessSurrogate` (the GP of the adaptive sampler, #209, per output) or `EnsembleSurrogate` (a bootstrap MLP ensemble) — so one code path serves both. The batch is picked by score with a minimum-spacing filter (measured: overriding the score with a pure maximin pick, as `adapt` does for its own plateauing signals, cost the loop 10 % of drag). Every round reports each re-run design's predicted and verified objective, the surrogate's error trend and the reason a design was rejected, and `certify=True` lets the fidelity router's own rule (#161, `route_cases`) accept a design without a solver call when its interval is narrower than the tolerance and contains no limit — the reported optimum is always verified. Measured on the 2-D airfoil of the aero study, against the closed-form optimum of `airfoil_reference()`: 0.03 % from the optimum after 12-16 solver calls to stabilise, while a Sobol' design of the same budget lands 1.35 % above (`benchmark_loop`).
