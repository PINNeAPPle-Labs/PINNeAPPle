- `pinneapple_physics.weather.cyclones` (#366): a tropical cyclone end to end on public data. `load_best_track` / `parse_hurdat2` read the NHC HURDAT2 best track (US Government work, downloaded once into a cache outside the repository, with its URL, SHA-256 and citation carried into every figure and JSON); `vortex_centre` / `track_storm` follow the sea-level-pressure minimum through any `(n, lat, lon)` field -- an ERA5 analysis, a published IFS HRES or Pangu-Weather forecast, a model of this library -- with a parabola through the three cells along each axis to put the centre below the grid, and an explicit *not found* when the grid is too coarse to hold the core, which is counted rather than hidden; `verify_storm` / `skill_by_lead` / `storm_horizons` quote the track error in km, the intensity error in hPa and in knots through the named Atkinson-Holliday (1977) wind-pressure relation, and the lead at which the forecast stops being usable on three stated measures; `track_globe` writes the Earth-2 style globe (a single frame or a GIF of the storm crossing it) and `track_skill_figure` the two skill panels with the share of forecasts that still held a vortex. `examples/weather_forecasting/hurricane_ian.py` runs Hurricane Ian 2022 against the best track, ERA5 through the ARCO bucket and the WeatherBench2 archives, and writes `summary.json` with the protocol, the licence of each source and every number it measured.
