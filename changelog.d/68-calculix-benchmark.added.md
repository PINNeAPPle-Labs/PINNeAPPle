- **Added:** a structural case in the benchmark, with CalculiX (`ccx`) as the reference solver (issue #68): `calculix_beam` (a cantilever box beam, load spread over the tip face) and `calculix_bracket` (an L bracket, base clamped, side load on the upright). Each is a Latin-hypercube sweep of the geometry and the load -- the same protocol as the OpenFOAM campaigns -- and every sample is one `ccx` run; what it keeps is the nodal displacement, the von Mises field and the two integrals an analyst reads first (the free face's displacement and the bending stress of the mid section). The meshes are structured C3D8I hexahedra whose topology does not change with the design, so a surrogate maps the four design numbers to the fields directly, and the bracket is two blocks merged on the interface plane (no gmsh, no CAD kernel: only `ccx`). The reference solver is verified against the closed form on every prepare -- Timoshenko's tip deflection and M c / I in the mid section, the clamped reactions balancing the load, and a second mesh -- and `beam theory` sits in the tables as a no-data row, because a surrogate that cannot beat M c / I has no reason to be trained. Data checks include the reaction balance of every run and the single topology; the validation stack runs the CalculiX preflight and the FEA mesh-quality report on a complete deck of the reference design. Methods: `mlp_calculix` (design -> the two integrals) and `pod_mlp_calculix` (design -> the fields through a POD basis, the integrals read off the predicted field). Measured on the reference designs: beam deflection 0.327 mm against Timoshenko's 0.331 mm (1.1 %), bending stress 3.60 MPa against M c / I 3.60 MPa, 0.15 s per design; bracket 41.66 MPa against 39.06 MPa (the upright is a deep member, b/t = 6: the refined meshes converge to +3.6 %).
