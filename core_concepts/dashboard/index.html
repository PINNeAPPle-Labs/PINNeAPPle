    Animation-centred dashboards¶

    pinneapple_tools.visualization.dashboard turns any time-dependent simulation into one view: - the animation in the middle; - charts around it, synchronised to its time cursor; - KPI tiles across the top; - a parameter panel and a colour bar.

    The same dashboard exports as: - an interactive HTML page (one self-contained file, no server and no internet; scrubbing a chart moves the animation); - a static PNG; - a GIF/MP4 of the dashboard playing.

    It uses the dark style of the studio renders (aircraft, Formula car).

    Interactive page: rover-dashboard.html (≈ 6.3 MB, opens offline). Static: rover-dashboard.png. Video: rover-dashboard.mp4.

    from pinneapple_tools.visualization.dashboard import Dashboard, Chart, Series, Marker, Tile, Param, ColorBar

db = Dashboard("Damped pendulum", t, frames,                 # frames: RGB arrays or image paths, one per t [s]
    charts=[Chart("Angle", "angle [rad]", "time [s]", [Series("theta", theta)], slot="left"),
            Chart("Phase portrait", "rate [rad/s]", "angle [rad]", [Series("orbit", rate, x=theta)],
                  [Marker("now", theta, rate)], kind="phase", slot="right"),
            Chart("Energy", "E [J/kg]", "time [s]", [Series("E", E)], slot="bottom")],
    tiles=[Tile("Energy", E, "J/kg")], params=[Param("Length", 1.0, "m")],
    colorbar=ColorBar("Speed [m/s]", "inferno", 0, 3))
db.to_html("pendulum.html")                                  # interactive, self-contained
db.to_png("pendulum.png", k=40)                              # the dashboard at frame 40
db.to_video(gif="pendulum.gif", mp4="pendulum.mp4", fps=12)  # GIF downscaled with gif_size / gif_every

    Parts¶

    Part What it is
    Dashboard(title, t, frames, ...) the frames of the animation and their times; subtitle, footer, colorbar
    Chart(title, ylabel, xlabel, series, markers, kind, slot) slot: "left", "right" or "bottom". Units go in the labels
    kind="series" one value per frame (x defaults to the times; any per-frame axis such as distance works). A cursor line follows the animation, and clicking or dragging the chart moves it
    kind="phase" fixed curves (Series(..., x=...)) plus Markers that move with the frames, e.g. a testbed curve and the operating point of each wheel
    Tile(label, value, unit, fmt) a KPI, either a scalar or one value per frame (the tile follows the cursor)
    Param(label, value, unit) a line of the parameter panel
    ColorBar(title, cmap, lo, hi) the colour scale of the field shown in the animation
    Dashboard.scene3d optional live three.js view (below)

    Colour follows the entity: a label gets the same colour on every chart, taken in a fixed order from the categorical palette's dark-surface steps. The four-slot set was checked for colour-vision deficiency and contrast against the panel colour. A missing value (NaN) leaves a gap in a line.

    The interactive page¶

    to_html(path) writes one file.

    Animation. It is embedded as VP9 WebM plus H.264 MP4 sources with a keyframe every 4 frames, so seeking is exact and every browser engine finds a format it plays. Without ffmpeg it falls back to JPEG frames.

    Charts: - drawn on canvases at the screen's pixel density; - hovering shows a crosshair and the values; - clicking or dragging a time chart moves the animation to that instant; - play/pause, a speed selector, a time slider and the arrow keys step through the frames; - the tiles follow the cursor.

    Reuse of the studio viewer. The colour bar and the number formatting come from the studio viewer's studio-core.js, inlined, so they match the viewer and the renders.

    Live 3D. With scene3d set, a Live 3D tab draws the vehicle parts moving with the frames over the terrain. It uses the studio's vendored three.js, inlined through an import map, and a studio.lighting preset (black lunar sky, low Sun). The view orbits freely and follows the scrubbed time. html.scene3d_payload(bodies, poses, terrain, lighting) builds the data for any rigid-body animation.

    Size depends on the frames. The rover page, with 150 frames at 1024 px plus the 3D data, is 6.1 MB.

    Static PNG and videos¶

    to_png and to_video draw the same layout with matplotlib (1920 × 1080 by default). The figure is drawn once; each frame only changes the image, the cursors, the moving markers and the tiles. The MP4 keeps every frame. The GIF is downscaled (gif_size) and can skip frames (gif_every) at the same playback speed; its 256-colour palette shifts the series colours a little, so the MP4 and the HTML page are the reference versions.

    The rover dashboard¶

    pinneapple_simulation.vehicle.dashboard builds the dashboard of any vehicle run:

    import pinneapple as pp
from pinneapple_simulation.vehicle import dashboard as vdash
frames, ks, clim = vdash.render_frames(run, terrain, n=150,      # planetary renders, 3-D ruts
                                       field="normal_stress", field_alpha=0.45, field_glow=0.05,
                                       field_saturation=0.6, clim_percentiles=(5, 95))
db = vdash.vehicle_dashboard(run, frames, ks, model=wong_reece, surrogate=pp.zoo.load("rover_contact_grc1_viper"),
                             clim=clim, field_label="Peak normal stress in the ruts [kPa]", field_saturation=0.6,
                             scene3d=True, terrain=terrain)

    The animation shows: - a hard, low Sun (lunar_low_sun, 12°) under a black sky, with long shadows; - regolith with small craters and rocks; - wheels that roll with the joint angles and leave ruts deformed into the terrain mesh: a floor at the computed sinkage, walls at the angle of repose, berms of displaced soil and grouser marks spaced by the pitch and the slip, all shaded and shadowed by the low Sun; - the ruts coloured by the peak normal stress they carried [kPa], with its colour bar. The map is muted and semi-transparent (field_alpha=0.45, field_glow=0.05, field_saturation=0.6): it tints the regolith's albedo before the photometry, so the rut walls, the grouser marks and the Sun's shading and shadows dominate; its range spans the 5-95 % percentiles of the stress the ruts carry (≈ 14-21 kPa), so the variation along the ruts shows; - the ground running out to the real lunar horizon, so no mesh edge shows in any frame (checked for the frames by studio.planetary.border_visibility).

    Around it: - left: drawbar pull, driving torque and sinkage versus slip. The grey line is the single-wheel testbed curve at the static wheel load; the dots are the four wheels' live operating points; - right: speed against the command; slip per wheel; the PINN against the run's Wong-Reece model for the front-left wheel (same inputs; gaps where a wheel leaves the PINN's training box). The PINN is the pre-trained model of the model zoo, pp.zoo.load("rover_contact_grc1_viper"); a zoo model is wrapped in terramechanics.surrogate.ZooContactSurrogate; - bottom: sinkage and normal load per wheel along the traverse; the PINN's error for every wheel; - tiles: distance [m], mean slip, maximum sinkage [mm], drive energy [J] and the PINN's RMS error [N], all up to the cursor; - parameters: the vehicle, the wheel, the soil (with units), gravity, the command, the time step, the PINN model, its held-out error (from its model card) and its error along this run.

    examples/studio/07_lunar_rover_dashboard.py regenerates every file: - docs/assets/studio/rover-dashboard.{html,png,gif,mp4}: the MP4 at 1920 × 1080; the GIF at 1280 × 720, laid out natively at that size (to_video(gif_font_scale=1.0)) so the chart text stays readable, with one shared octree palette and frame differencing to stay under 6 MB (dashboard.static.write_gif); - rover-frame.png, one rendered frame.

    The run behind it is the crater crossing of the lunar rover page: NASA VIPER (450 kg) on GRC-1 at lunar gravity, a 0.2 m/s command, 45 s, 2 ms steps. The rover covers 8.63 m with a mean slip of 0.018 and a maximum sinkage of 36.6 mm (the rear-left wheel digging in while it pushes against the rock), uses 794 J (92 J/m), and reaches 10.5° of pitch and 3.8° of roll; the front-left wheel leaves the soil for a moment near 1.3 m (its sinkage and load drop to zero).

    The zoo PINN along the run. rover_contact_grc1_viper v1.1.0 is trained on the physics of this run (GRC-1 with slip-sinkage, n1 = 0.8; the dashboard checks it with ZooContactSurrogate.check_physics and shows "PINN physics vs run: same"). Its model card gives a held-out drawbar-pull RMSE of 0.22 N; along this run, at the states the wheels met, its drawbar-pull RMSE over all wheels is 0.33 N, with no error above 1 N. v1.0.0, trained before slip-sinkage was added (n1 = 0), erred by 11.3 N RMS on the same run and by up to ≈ 190 N near the rock; it stays loadable as a superseded release (see the model zoo guide).
