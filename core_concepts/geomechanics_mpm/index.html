    Geomechanics with the material point method¶

    pinneapple_simulation.particle_dynamics.geo_mpm simulates granular flows, landslides, dam breaks and soft bodies with the material point method (MPM), in physical units, in 2-D or 3-D. It is written in PyTorch from the published algorithms: - MLS-MPM with APIC transfer, Hu et al. (2018); - Drucker–Prager return mapping in Hencky strain, Klár et al. (2016).

    Material Model
    Sand(friction_deg, E, nu, rho) Cohesionless Drucker–Prager without dilation. In 2-D the default is plane strain: the out-of-plane elastic stretch is tracked, so the total out-of-plane strain stays zero, as in a laboratory flume, and the cone is matched to Mohr–Coulomb in plane strain. In 3-D it is matched in triaxial compression
    Fluid(K, rho, gamma) Weakly compressible fluid: pressure from J (Tait-like), no shear memory
    Elastic(E, nu, rho) Fixed-corotated hyperelastic solid

    Boundaries. The walls are the faces of the domain: "slip", "sticky", or "friction" with a Coulomb coefficient.

    Bodies. Each body is seeded with ppc particles per cell along each axis. A run can hold several bodies of different materials.

    Output. Frames are written in GeoTaichi's layout (<out>/particles/MPMParticle<NNNNNN>.npz, with position, velocity, volume, stress and body).

    from pinneapple_simulation.particle_dynamics.geo_mpm import GeoMPM, Sand, box, runout
sim = GeoMPM(domain=(1.4, 0.3), dx=0.01, floor="sticky", walls="slip")
sim.add_body(box((0, 0), (0.2, 0.2)), Sand(friction_deg=30.0))
frames = sim.run(t_end=1.0, save_every=0.05, out="runs/collapse")
runout(frames[-1]["position"])

    GeoTaichi¶

    GeoTaichi (Zhejiang University, GPL-3.0) is a much larger Taichi framework. It covers: - MPM, including two-phase porous media and incompressible fluids; - DEM and level-set DEM, affine bodies; - FEM and IGA; - MPM–DEM, FEM–MPM and IGA–MPM couplings, with incremental potential contact.

    pinneapple_simulation.external_solvers.geotaichi uses it as an external program. PINNeAPPle never imports or ships its code: - run_geotaichi(script, workdir) runs one of its scripts in a separate process, with GEOTAICHI_HOME pointing at a checkout. - read_geotaichi(path) reads the MPM or DEM particle frames. Pickled entries are skipped unless allow_pickle=True, which should be used only for files you trust. - frames_to_samples(frames) turns them into PhysicalSamples, the format the rest of the library trains on.

    read_geotaichi also reads geo_mpm frames, so a GeoTaichi run and a PINNeAPPle run of the same problem can be compared directly. The GeoTaichi features not yet in geo_mpm are listed in issue #471: μ(I) rheology, porous media, DEM coupling, GPU, and a learned simulator on MPM data.

    Example: granular column collapse¶

    examples/geomechanics/01_granular_column_collapse.py collapses sand columns with φ = 30°, width L0 = 0.2 m and aspect ratios a = H0/L0 of 0.5 to 3. Each column stands against a back wall, on a rough floor, with 1 cm cells. The runout is compared with the fits Lube et al. (2005) measured in two-dimensional collapses: (L∞ − L0)/L0 = 1.2 a for a < 1.8, and 1.9 a^(2/3) for a > 2.8.

    a MPM (L∞ − L0)/L0 Lube et al. (2005) MPM / experiment Final height / L0
    0.5 0.44 0.60 0.73 0.47 (H0 = 0.5)
    1 1.32 1.20 1.10 0.77
    2 3.71 2.4–3.0 (transition) 1.2–1.5 1.03
    3 6.20 3.95 1.57 1.07

    Agreement. The deposits come to rest (final speeds of a few mm/s). For a ≤ 1 the runout is within about 27 % of the experiments, and the short column keeps its top: final height close to H0 for a = 0.5, as observed.

    Limit. Tall columns run out too far, and the 2/3 power regime is not reproduced. This is the known limit of rate-independent plasticity, which has no rate- or inertia-dependent friction. The μ(I) rheology that captures it is the first item of issue #471.

    Choosing the yield cone. The cone matching matters as much as φ. Measured on the same columns (a = 0.5, 1 and 2): - with pure 2-D strains (no plane-strain constraint) and the triaxial cone, the runout was 4–10 % longer than with the default; - with plane strain but the triaxial-compression cone, it was 36–48 % shorter.

    Tests: tests/test_geo_mpm.py checks: - free fall of an elastic block at g, mass conserved; - a hydrostatic pressure gradient of ρg within 5 % (fit over 0.1–0.4 s, while the weakly compressible column oscillates); - a sand column that spreads within bounds and comes to rest; - the frame reader on GeoTaichi-style files, pickled entries included.
