## Summary

A lattice assembled from unit cells of different topology, chosen so that the global response — Poisson's ratio, stiffness anisotropy, buckling load — hits a target. The core of the work is a homogenisation step that turns a unit cell into an effective stiffness tensor, and a selection over the library under a connectivity constraint.

## Question

Can a cell-by-cell selection, scored on homogenised properties, reproduce a target Poisson's ratio of -0.30 and survive a full-lattice re-solve of a 3x3x3 patch within a stated tolerance?

## Why run it

A metamaterial claim lives or dies on whether the homogenised number and the assembled structure agree. This experiment produces that comparison, plus a buckling check against Euler and plate theory, and an assembly-level visual that reads without explanation.

## Physical model

Linear elasticity at the cell scale. The homogenised stiffness comes from the asymptotic expansion, solved as six unit macroscopic strain cases with periodic boundary conditions and averaged over the cell volume.

Metrics of interest: `nu_eff = -S^H_12 / S^H_11`, relative density `rho_bar`, anisotropy `E_1/E_2`, buckling load `P_cr`.

Validity: small strain, linear material, `l_cell / L <= 0.2`. The model cannot show strut yielding, local strut buckling, strut contact or manufacturing defects.

## Geometry and inputs

Cells from `geometry.gen.sdf_shapes`: re-entrant auxetic, chiral, body-centred cubic and simple cubic, parametrised by strut thickness and cell size, unioned with `sdf_union` and meshed with `mesh_from_sdf_3d`. Base material `E = 3.5 GPa`, `nu = 0.35`; relative density swept 0.05 to 0.30. Assembly: 3x3x3 cells on a 60 mm cube with a face-connectivity constraint.

## Solver and engine

Cell and patch FE: `numerical_solvers.solid_fem` (`HexMesh`, `box_mesh`, `SolidFEM.fix/load_face`, `von_mises`) for the structured cases, and `external_solvers.calculix` (`study.FEModel`, `study.Static`, `study.Buckle`, `study.solve`) for the body-fitted mesh and the buckling eigenvalue. `elasticity3d_fdm` is the cheap check.

## What is missing (the work)

1. A homogenisation module: periodic BCs on a hex or tet cell mesh, six unit strain cases, effective stiffness tensor, volume averaging.
2. Parametrised cell families (auxetic re-entrant, chiral, compression-twist) with a library schema and a property table.
3. A connectivity checker between adjacent cells, and a selection optimiser that respects it.
4. A patch-level effective-property solve with periodic BCs, compared against homogenisation.
5. A lattice buckling path: linear eigenvalue buckling of the patch in CalculiX against Euler and plate theory.

## Experiment protocol

1. Homogenise four cell families at five relative densities: 20 cells.
2. Verify against analytic limits: a solid block returns the base tensor, a simple cubic frame the stretching-dominated scaling.
3. Solve a 3x3x3 uniform patch at three mesh levels, GCI on `E_eff` and `nu_eff`.
4. Select cells for the target, solve the assembly, buckle the best three.

Budget 100-400 CPU-hours. Each case logs cell type, relative density, mesh size, `C^H`, `E_eff`, `nu_eff` and the patch result.

## Baselines, references and verification

Baselines: uniform simple cubic lattice at equal density; uniform re-entrant lattice at equal density; solid block of the same mass. Verification: recover the base tensor from a solid cell, recover the affine limit at low density, compare homogenised `E_eff` with the full patch, and compare buckling with `P_E = pi^2 E I / (k L)^2` and the plate formula in the solid limit.

## Metrics

Effective stiffness tensor `C^H_ijkl` (Pa). `E_eff` (MPa), `nu_eff` (-), `rho_bar` (-), anisotropy ratio `E_1/E_2` (-). Homogenisation error `|E_eff^H - E_eff^patch| / E_eff^patch` (%). Buckling load `P_cr` (N) and its ratio to `P_E`. Connectivity, the number of disconnected solid components in the assembly.

## Deliverables

The cell library as STL plus a CSV of effective properties; the selected assembly STL; patch re-solve results; figures of the deformed lattice, the `E_eff`-versus-`rho_bar` scaling law and the buckling mode; a short video of the assembly under load.

## Acceptance criteria

- A solid cell returns the base tensor to 1e-6 relative.
- Homogenised `E_eff` agrees with the patch within 5 % at `l_cell/L = 0.1`.
- The assembly reaches `nu_eff` within 0.03 of target, or the report states the closest value reachable.
- Every exported assembly is a single connected solid component.
- The 2x refined patch changes `P_cr` by less than 10 %.

## Realizability and evidence class

Fills `mathematical_consistency`, `numerical_stability`, `physical_plausibility`. `experimental_validation` is NOT ASSESSED: no printed lattice is tested. `technological_feasibility` is CONDITIONAL: minimum strut thickness and print defects are not modelled. `uncertainty_sensitivity` is partially assessed through the density sweep.

## Risks and failure modes

1. Homogenisation used outside its scale separation; mitigation: report the error against `l_cell/L` and state the limit.
2. Thin struts mesh badly; mitigation: a mesh-quality gate and a minimum elements-per-strut count.
3. Linear buckling over-predicts capacity; mitigation: report the eigenvalue as an upper bound.
4. Cells that cannot be joined; mitigation: the connectivity checker as a hard constraint.

## Out of scope

- Nonlinear post-buckling and collapse.
- Additive-manufacturing process simulation.
- Fatigue and creep.
- Multiphysics (thermal, acoustic) effective properties.
