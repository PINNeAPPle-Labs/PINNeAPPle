## Summary

Rayleigh–Bénard convection in a 3-D box: heat a plate, cool the top, let the pattern emerge, then change the cavity geometry and the heating distribution and measure again. The quantitative outputs are the critical Rayleigh number for onset and the Nusselt–Rayleigh exponent, both known independently.

## Question

Does a 3-D buoyant solver built on the library's existing case machinery recover the theoretical onset Rayleigh number and the accepted Nusselt–Rayleigh exponent within the stated tolerances?

## Why run it

Buoyant convection is the cheapest genuinely three-dimensional, genuinely transient flow with an exact benchmark at both ends — a stability threshold and a scaling law. A solver that passes both is trustworthy for the plumes that appear in other renders. The visual is a volumetric temperature isosurface: the field, not decoration.

## Physical model

Boussinesq approximation: incompressible continuity and momentum with buoyancy linear in temperature, plus an advection-diffusion energy equation.

Governing groups: `Ra = g beta dT H^3 / (nu kappa)`, `Pr = nu / kappa`, response `Nu = q H / (k dT)`. Regime: `Ra` 10^3 to 10^8, `Pr = 0.71` and 7. Valid while `beta dT << 1`; above that, non-Oberbeck-Boussinesq effects matter and this model cannot show them. No radiation, phase change or wall thermal resistance.

## Geometry and inputs

A rectangular box with aspect ratio 1:1:1 and 2:1:1, no-slip, isothermal plates, adiabatic or conducting sidewalls. A second uses a sinusoidal bottom temperature. Fluid properties `nu`, `kappa`, `beta` and `g` are set explicitly.

## Solver and engine

`external_solvers.openfoam` through `OpenFOAMCaseTemplate` and `run_openfoam_case`, with a buoyant Boussinesq case. That template does not exist today; it is the primary missing piece. In-process: `ns3d_spectral` (periodic, no buoyancy) and `lbm` (D3Q19, Smagorinsky), both needing a buoyancy term. `thermal_channel_2d` serves only the sanity check.

## What is missing (the work)

1. A buoyant Boussinesq 3-D case path: an OpenFOAM template with the gravity vector, `p_rgh` and Boussinesq thermophysics, or a buoyancy term in `ns3d_spectral`/`lbm` with a stability argument.
2. A Nusselt number from the wall-gradient average over the plates, cross-checked against the power balance.
3. An onset driver: `Ra` sweep with a small perturbation, growth detection, fitted critical `Ra`.
4. A cavity parameterisation (aspect ratio, sidewall condition, bottom temperature) that regenerates the case.
5. A volume-rendering path through `twin3d` or `blender`.

## Experiment protocol

1. Sanity: reproduce `Nu_Dh = 5.385` with `thermal_channel_2d` in its forced regime.
2. Onset: `Ra` 1500 to 2000 in 50 steps at `Pr = 0.71`, fitted growth. Twelve runs.
3. Scaling: `Ra` = 10^4 to 10^8 at `Pr = 0.71` and 7, aspect ratio 1 and 2, at three mesh levels.
4. Perturbation: sinusoidal bottom temperature at `Ra = 10^7`.
5. Render the plume at `Ra = 10^7`.

Budget 500-2000 CPU-hours. Each case logs `Ra`, `Pr`, mesh, `Nu` from both estimates, `Nu` history, boundary-layer thickness.

## Baselines, references and verification

Baselines: pure conduction (`Nu = 1`); the `Ra = 10^4` case against published DNS; the forced channel against Shah and London. Verification: `Ra_c = 1707.76` from linear stability; the Grossmann–Lohse `Nu(Ra, Pr)` prediction; a local exponent fitted over one decade; three-grid GCI.

## Metrics

`Nu` (-), from the wall gradient and from the power balance. `Ra` (-), `Pr` (-). Local exponent `gamma = d ln Nu / d ln Ra` (-). `Ra_c` (-) with fitted uncertainty. Thermal boundary-layer thickness (m). GCI on `Nu` (%). Plume count at `T = T_mean`.

## Deliverables

The `Nu`-versus-`Ra` table with both estimates and the GCI; the onset growth plot with fitted `Ra_c`; velocity and temperature fields at three times for `Ra = 10^7`; a 20-40 s volumetric video; case folders.

## Acceptance criteria

- The fitted `Ra_c` is within 2 % of 1707.76.
- `Nu` at `Ra = 10^7`, `Pr = 0.71` within 10 % of the Grossmann–Lohse prediction.
- Wall-gradient and power-balance `Nu` agree within 3 %.
- The 2x refined mesh changes `Nu` by less than 5 %.
- The subcritical `Ra = 1500` case decays to conduction.

## Realizability and evidence class

Fills `mathematical_consistency`, `numerical_stability`, `physical_plausibility`. `experimental_validation` is NOT ASSESSED: the classical experiments are cited as benchmarks, not reproduced. `technological_feasibility` does not apply. `uncertainty_sensitivity` is assessed through the closure and grid bands.

## Risks and failure modes

1. The Boussinesq case stalls at high `Ra`; mitigation: report CFL and actual step, cap `Ra` at what is affordable.
2. `Nu` depends on wall-normal resolution in a boundary layer of thickness `H/(2 Nu)`; mitigation: report cells-in-boundary-layer and refine before any scaling claim.
3. Onset detection confused by round-off growth; mitigation: run the decay case as control and report both rates.
4. LES closure dominates at `Ra = 10^8`; mitigation: label those points closure-dependent.

## Out of scope

- Compressible and non-Oberbeck-Boussinesq convection.
- Rotating, magnetic or porous convection.
- Boiling and phase change.
