## Summary

A soft continuum arm with a stiffness distribution and cable actuation that must pass through an opening narrower than its resting diameter, then exert a grasp force. The work is a large-rotation rod model, a non-penetrating contact, and an optimisation over the stiffness profile.

## Question

Can a stiffness profile be found such that the arm, driven only by admissible cable tensions, passes a gap of 0.6 times its resting diameter without exceeding 10 % strain and afterwards exerts a stated grasp force?

## Why run it

A soft arm that squeezes is easy to draw and hard to demonstrate, because large-rotation rod mechanics and contact must hold at once. The penetration and energy residuals are the scientific content. The engineering value is a stiffness distribution rather than a shape.

## Physical model

A geometrically exact (Cosserat) rod: position `r(s)` and director frame `Q(s)`, strain measures `Gamma = Q^T r' - e_3` and `K = Q^T Q'`, linear constitutive law with `EA`, `GA`, `EI`, `GJ`, and the standard balance laws.

Groups: slenderness `L/D`, material strain `eps = kappa D/2`, actuation ratio `T_c/(E A)`, gap ratio `g/D_0`. Valid at small local strain with finite global rotation: the arm may bend past 90 degrees, no material point beyond the linear range. Absent: hyperelasticity, viscoelasticity, friction, dynamic snap-through.

## Geometry and inputs

A rod of 0.5 m length, 30 mm resting diameter, piecewise stiffness over 10 segments with `E` from 0.5 to 10 MPa. Cable routing on one or two off-axis paths. Obstacle: a rigid plate with a gap of width `g` from 0.4 to 1.0 `D_0`. Grasp target: a 40 mm cylinder of 200 g.

## Solver and engine

Primary: a new Cosserat rod solver. The closest existing piece is `numerical_solvers.nonlinear_beam_fem` (`NonlinearBeamNewmarkFEM`, `solve_nonlinear_beam_transient`), a 1-D beam with moderate rotation that cannot represent finite twist and shear past 90 degrees; using it is an unjustified approximation and this issue says so. Contact: `particle_dynamics.mpm` or `sph` on the soft side, or a penalty constraint in the rod solver.

## What is missing (the work)

1. A geometrically exact rod element with a director frame, finite-rotation update, and Newton iteration with a consistent tangent.
2. Cable actuation as a follower load along a routed path, with the length constraint.
3. Contact between rod and obstacle without penetration: penalty with reported depth, or augmented Lagrange.
4. A feasibility test: given a gap and a tension limit, does a configuration sequence exist that passes it.
5. A workspace sampler and a grasp-force evaluation at a defined object.
6. An optimisation over the stiffness profile with contact in the loop.

## Experiment protocol

1. Verify against Euler's elastica for a cantilever under a tip moment, and the linear beam at small load.
2. Energy balance on a free oscillation, drift below 2 % over 10 periods.
3. Contact: rod pressed onto a rigid cylinder, penetration versus penalty stiffness.
4. Gap sweep `g/D_0` 0.4 to 1.0 in seven steps, three routings, no optimisation.
5. Optimise the stiffness profile, 3 seeds, 60 evaluations; then grasp.

Budget 200-600 CPU-hours. Each case logs the profile, tensions, peak strain, penetration, energy residual, workspace volume and grasp force.

## Baselines, references and verification

A rigid articulated arm of the same mass cannot pass the gap; a uniform-stiffness soft arm of the same mean stiffness is the fair comparison; a straight rod pushed through without actuation is the null case. Verification: Euler's elastica closed form, the linear-beam limit, the energy residual, rod mesh convergence, and the analytic contact force for a rod on a cylinder.

## Metrics

Workspace volume (m^3, sampled). Maximum traversable gap ratio `g/D_0` (-). Grasp force (N). Peak material strain `eps_max` (-). Actuation work (J). Cable tension (N). Penetration depth (mm) and energy residual (%).

## Deliverables

The stiffness profile table; rod configurations through the gap as a time series; the workspace point cloud; figures of strain along the arm and of penetration versus penalty stiffness; a 20-30 s video of passage and grasp.

## Acceptance criteria

- Penetration stays below 1 % of the maximum inscribed sphere radius of the gap.
- Energy residual stays below 2 % over the quasi-static sequence.
- The optimised arm passes `g/D_0 = 0.6` and holds a 200 g object with a stated grasp force.
- `eps_max` stays below 0.10, and the report shows where the maximum occurs.
- The rod solution converges under refinement to within 2 % in `eps_max`.

## Realizability and evidence class

Fills `mathematical_consistency`, `numerical_stability`, `physical_plausibility`. `experimental_validation` is NOT ASSESSED: no physical arm is built. `technological_feasibility` is CONDITIONAL: cable friction, elastomer fatigue and tolerance are not modelled. `uncertainty_sensitivity` is partially assessed through the penalty and stiffness sweeps.

## Risks and failure modes

1. The beam used outside its rotation range gives a plausible but wrong configuration; mitigation: the Cosserat element, beam reported only as a comparison.
2. Penalty contact permits visible penetration; mitigation: the penetration-versus-penalty curve and a stated operating value.
3. The optimum works at one gap width only; mitigation: report feasibility across the sweep.
4. Quasi-static analysis hides a dynamic snap; mitigation: one transient case, peak strain compared.

## Out of scope

- Hyperelastic or viscoelastic material models.
- Friction and wear at the contact.
- Closed-loop control and sensor modelling.
- Fabrication of the arm.
