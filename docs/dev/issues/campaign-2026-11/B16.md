## Summary

A building envelope of actuated panels: each panel has one controllable angle, and the array is optimised so that the flow around the building does what the occupants need — less mean and peak load on the structure, or more ventilation at a target location. The physics is ordinary external aerodynamics at building Reynolds numbers; the experiment's content is the coupling between a geometry that changes and a flow solution that must be recomputed for every configuration, which is exactly the cost that usually stops this kind of study.

## Question

For a stated building, wind direction and objective, how much of the achievable improvement comes from the panel angles being a free field, and how much of that survives when the panels must be manufactured, actuated and maintained?

## Why run it

- It is a direct test of whether the parametric-geometry-to-OpenFOAM pipeline is fast enough for design-space work rather than for single cases.
- Facade actuation is a plausible engineering product, so a quantified gain — including the cases where a static angle is as good — is useful outside the demonstration.
- It produces an honest cost figure: the number of CFD solutions per useful configuration, and the surrogate error that replaces them.

## Physical model

Incompressible RANS around a bluff body with a turbulence model stated explicitly (k-omega SST at the wall, with a wall-function or resolved y+ policy fixed in advance). Bulk parameters: Reynolds number based on building height and reference wind speed (10^6–10^7 class), turbulence intensity and profile from an exposure category, and the dimensionless force coefficients C_p on the surfaces, C_F (mean) and C_F' (RMS) integrated over the envelope.

Deliberately out of scope: aeroelastic response of the panels under load (the panels are treated as rigid at a prescribed angle), thermal buoyancy, and the interior flow except through a single ventilation opening whose mass flow is reported.

## Geometry and inputs

A parametric building block plus a panel array: panel count, pitch, size and per-panel angle. The CAD parameters live in the FreeCAD template the pipeline already uses; the panel field is a vector of angles at fixed locations, so the geometry is deterministic from the parameter vector. Wind directions are sampled on a stated set (for example 8 or 12 sectors), and the objective is evaluated per direction and then weighted.

Inputs: the building geometry template, the exposure profile, and the target region for ventilation (a volume in front of the openings).

## Solver and engine

`pinneapple_simulation.pipelines.freecad_openfoam` (`PipelineSpec`, `run_pipeline`, `Sweep(spec, doe(bounds, n), folder).run(parallel=)`, `fit_surrogate(rows, inputs, outputs)`) drives the case generation and the sweep, with `external_solvers.openfoam` (`simpleFoam` or `pimpleFoam`) as the solver. The optimisation loop can run through `pinneapple_autolab` (`AutonomousLab`, `Mission`, `compare_models`, `choose_next`) so the next-case selection is decided by what is unknown rather than by a fixed grid. A cheap model — panels represented as a porous jump or a distributed roughness rather than resolved geometry — is the surrogate candidate, and it must be validated against the resolved case before it is trusted.

## What already exists in PINNeAPPle (verified)

- `pinneapple_simulation.pipelines.freecad_openfoam`: parametric CAD → snappyHexMesh → OpenFOAM → sections and references, sweeps with one process per case and resume, and a surrogate with held-out error.
- `pinneapple_simulation.external_solvers.openfoam` and `pinneapple_tools.automation` (`Workflow.load`, `Runner`, `interpret`) for the FreeCAD/OpenFOAM/ParaView side.
- `pinneapple_autolab` for the mission loop, surrogate comparison, triage and next-simulation selection; `dashboard.build_dashboard` for the replay of the campaign.
- `pinneapple_design.geometry.gen` and `pinneapple_design.geometry.mesh` for parametric geometry and meshing helpers.
- `pinneapple_check` (forces balance, boundary conditions, residuals) for the per-case verification, and `pinneapple_analysis.verification.convergence` for the mesh ladder.

## What is missing (the work)

1. **A morphing-geometry loop**: regenerating the panel geometry and remeshing per configuration inside the pipeline, with a cache keyed on the angle vector so an unchanged configuration is not recomputed twice.
2. **A porous-panel reduced model** and its validation: the cheap representation of the array, calibrated on a subset of resolved cases, with its error reported as a function of angle and wind direction.
3. **A ventilation metric** computed from the solution (mass flow through the designated opening, or mean age of air in the target region), which the pipeline does not compute today.
4. **A panel energy model**: actuator torque and power per panel against wind load, so the optimisation cannot buy performance with arbitrary actuation.
5. **A geometric feasibility filter**: minimum gap, overlapping panels and panel-to-structure clearance checked before the case is meshed.

## Experiment protocol

1. Build the baseline cases: sealed building, flat facade, for every wind direction, on a coarse and a medium mesh; run the mesh-convergence ladder on two directions and record GCI.
2. Define the design of experiments over the angle field: a Sobol or Latin-hypercube sample of the reduced parameterisation (for example a small number of panel groups rather than per-panel freedom), 200–400 cases on the coarse mesh.
3. Fit the surrogate, validate on the held-out set, and report the error per wind direction.
4. Optimise on the surrogate for the objective (mean force reduction, or ventilation increase, with a panel-energy constraint), then re-solve the top candidates on the medium mesh.
5. Report the improvement against both baselines — flat facade and the best single static angle — and the number of resolved cases that were needed.
6. Compute budget: order 10^3 CPU-hours for the resolved cases, plus the surrogate fit; each case minutes to hours depending on mesh.

## Baselines, references and verification

- Flat facade (no actuation) and the best single static angle found by the same search budget. Both must be reported; a gain over the flat facade alone is not evidence for actuation.
- Mesh convergence by GCI on two directions, with the force coefficient as the observable.
- Physics checks: global force balance, mass conservation through the domain, and a wall-function y+ audit per case through `pinneapple_check`.
- Where possible, a cross-check of the flat-facade pressure distribution against published bluff-body data — and if no suitable published case is available for this geometry, the issue says so rather than citing an unrelated building.

## Metrics

- C_F (mean force coefficient), dimensionless, integrated over the envelope, per wind direction.
- C_F' (RMS of the fluctuating force), dimensionless, from the transient run on the shortlisted configurations.
- Ventilation: mass flow through the designated opening, kg/s, or mean age of air in the target volume, s.
- Panel actuation energy, J per configuration change and W held, per panel.
- Surrogate error: relative error of C_F and of the ventilation metric on the held-out cases.
- Improvement ratio: (objective at optimum) / (objective at the better baseline), dimensionless.

## Deliverables

- The parametric template and the case generator, plus the cache of resolved cases with their hashes.
- The surrogate and its held-out error report.
- The optimum panel field per wind direction, with the resolved solution for the shortlist.
- A visualisation: surface pressure and streamlines for the baseline and the optimum, from the campaign video pipeline.
- The dashboard replay of how the cases were chosen.

## Acceptance criteria

- Mesh convergence: the shortlisted configurations change C_F by less than 5 % on the refined mesh, with the observed order reported.
- The flat-facade case reproduces published bluff-body force coefficients within a stated band, or the discrepancy is reported with its cause.
- The optimum beats the best static angle by a margin larger than the surrogate's held-out error at that configuration; if it does not, the report says the actuation does not pay.
- Every accepted configuration is geometrically feasible by the stated filter and within the panel-energy budget.
- No result is reported from the surrogate alone.

## Realizability and evidence class

`PLAUSIBILITY_CLASS`: `established` for the aerodynamics; `extrapolated` for the actuated envelope as a product. Axes filled: `mathematical_consistency` (residuals, force balance), `numerical_stability` (GCI ladder), `physical_plausibility` (force coefficients against published data), `uncertainty_sensitivity` (surrogate error and the sensitivity of the optimum to the angle discretisation). `experimental_validation` stays not assessed without wind-tunnel data; `technological_feasibility` stays not assessed without a mechanism design, although the panel-energy model narrows the gap.

## Risks and failure modes

1. **The surrogate is optimised, not the building.** Mitigation: held-out error reported per direction, and the shortlist always re-solved.
2. **Remeshing cost explodes** as panel count grows. Mitigation: group panels into a small number of zones, and report the resolution of the actuation field as a design choice.
3. **Mesh-induced noise swamps the panel effect.** Mitigation: fixed meshing policy and identical topology per configuration where possible, plus a repeatability run on one configuration with two meshes.
4. **The static baseline is weakened** by a poor search. Mitigation: give the static baseline at least the same number of resolved evaluations.
5. **Overclaiming ventilation** from a RANS field that does not resolve the indoor flow. Mitigation: ventilation is reported as a mass flow through an opening, not as an air-quality claim.

## Out of scope

- Indoor comfort, thermal simulation and pollutant transport.
- Panel structure, fatigue and the actuator hardware design.
- A wind-tunnel or field validation campaign.
