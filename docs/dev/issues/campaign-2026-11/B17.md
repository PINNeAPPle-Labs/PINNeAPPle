## Summary

A re-entry capsule with deployable aerodynamic surfaces steers its own descent. The experiment optimises the control schedule — when to deflect which surface, by how much — against three competing limits: landing dispersion, deceleration on the payload, and peak heat flux. The trajectory that comes out is not authored: it is what the optimiser produces when it has to trade those three against each other, and the report shows the trade, including the configurations where no schedule satisfies all limits at once.

## Question

For a stated capsule and entry interface, can a scheduled deflection of movable surfaces reduce landing dispersion by a useful amount without exceeding a deceleration or heat-flux limit, and where is the point at which the three limits cannot be met simultaneously?

## Why run it

- It exercises the piece of the library that is hardest to fake: a coupled 6-DOF trajectory whose aerodynamic coefficients come from a compressible flow solution rather than from a fitted table.
- The feasibility boundary — the set of entry conditions for which a controllable entry is possible at all — is a real engineering answer, and it is more valuable than a single successful trajectory.
- It forces the model's validity limits into the open (continuum breakdown, no ablation, no chemistry), which is the point of running speculative-looking demonstrations under an evidence protocol.

## Physical model

**Flight mechanics.** Six-degree-of-freedom rigid-body dynamics in a rotating-atmosphere frame: bank angle and angle of attack as the controlled states, lift and drag from the aerodynamic model, gravity and centrifugal terms. The bank-reversal family of schedules is the parameterisation, because it is what real entry vehicles use and it keeps the control space small enough to optimise.

**Aerodynamics.** Compressible flow at the entry Mach range, solved through `external_solvers.openfoam` (a compressible solver with a stated thermodynamics) on a reduced geometry for a set of (Mach, angle of attack, surface deflection) points, then fitted into a coefficient model. The capsule is blunt, so the hypersonic coefficients are dominated by the windward pressure distribution rather than by viscous effects — which is what makes a coarse CFD campaign defensible here, provided the fidelity claim is stated.

**Heating.** A stagnation-point correlation (cold-wall, minus the ablation term) as the estimate, not a coupled thermal solution. This is a deliberate, stated limitation.

**Limits.** Peak deceleration on the payload, peak heat flux, and a control-surface temperature limit; plus an angle-of-attack corridor for stability.

**Deliberately out of scope:** rarefied flow at high altitude (the continuum assumption fails as the Knudsen number rises; the issue must state the altitude band where the coefficients are not to be trusted), gas chemistry and radiation, ablation and recession, and structural flexibility.

## Geometry and inputs

A parametric blunt-body capsule (forebody radius, cone or spherical-cap afterbody, shoulder radius) plus two to four movable surfaces whose hinge position, span and chord are fixed and whose deflection is the control variable. The geometry generator is `pinneapple_design.geometry.gen` (`sdf_shapes`, `primitives`, `domains3d`) with the mesh pipeline already used for external flow cases.

Inputs: the entry interface (velocity and flight-path angle at the atmospheric interface) as a design choice with a stated range, standard atmosphere tables, the mass and inertia properties, and the payload acceleration limit.

## Solver and engine

- Trajectory: the rigid-body integrator of `pinneapple_simulation.flight.rigid_body` and the control utilities of `pinneapple_simulation.flight`, wrapped in a propagation loop that carries the surface-deflection schedule.
- Aerodynamics: `external_solvers.openfoam` with `numerical_solvers.compressible_fv` as the in-process reference for a one-dimensional or axisymmetric check of the coefficient behaviour.
- Optimisation: the schedule parameters (bank reversals, deflection timings and magnitudes) optimised with the machinery in `pinneapple_design.design_optimizer` (NSGA-II) or `pinneapple_autolab` when the campaign must choose its own cases; `fit_surrogate` for the coefficient table.
- Recording: `pinneapple_lab.experiment` with `log_metric`, `check`, `add_file`.

## What is missing (the work)

1. **The coupled propagation loop**: trajectory → atmosphere → aerodynamic coefficient lookup → forces → integration, with the control schedule as an input vector, written as one reusable simulator object compatible with `pinneapple_autolab.Simulator`.
2. **The aerodynamic coefficient campaign**: a DOE over (Mach, alpha, deflection) with a compressible case template, the fit, and the explicit validity table of the fitted range.
3. **A heating estimate**: the stagnation-point correlation with its assumptions in code, and the Knudsen-number guard that flags where the continuum coefficients stop being meaningful.
4. **The three-limit feasibility analysis**: a sweep over entry interfaces that outputs the region where a schedule exists, which is the issue's headline result.
5. **A landing-dispersion estimate**: the trajectory propagated over a stated uncertainty set (atmospheric density, mass, lift-to-drag ratio), producing a dispersion ellipse rather than a single landing point.

## Experiment protocol

1. Mesh the reduced geometry and run the convergence ladder on one representative (Mach, alpha, deflection) at two refinement levels; record GCI on the integrated force coefficients.
2. Run the coefficient campaign (of order 50–150 cases) and fit the model; report the fit residual and the extrapolation guard.
3. Validate the trajectory integrator against a published entry trajectory or a closed-form ballistic case (constant drag coefficient, exponential atmosphere), reporting the disagreement.
4. Optimise the bank/deflection schedule for each entry interface on a grid, with the three constraints; record the Pareto set of dispersion against peak deceleration and peak heat flux.
5. Propagate the optimum over the uncertainty set to obtain the dispersion ellipse, and repeat for the uncontrollable (fixed-trim) baseline.
6. Compute budget: order 10^2 CPU-hours for the coefficient campaign, minutes per trajectory.

## Baselines, references and verification

- Fixed-trim capsule (no movable surfaces) under the same entry interfaces and uncertainty set — the only baseline that makes the actuation result meaningful.
- Ballistic trajectory with a constant drag coefficient in an exponential atmosphere as the integrator's analytic check.
- Force coefficient convergence by GCI, and the coefficient fit against the resolved cases at three points held out of the fit.
- Energy check on the trajectory: the total mechanical energy decrease must match the integrated drag work within the stated tolerance.

## Metrics

- Landing dispersion: 3-sigma downrange and crossrange error, km, from the uncertainty propagation.
- Peak deceleration on the payload, g, and the integrated heat load, J/m².
- Peak stagnation-point heat flux, W/cm².
- Control effort: total deflection travel, deg, and the number of reversals.
- Feasibility: fraction of entry interfaces in the sampled set for which a schedule meeting all limits exists.
- Coefficient fit error: relative error on C_L and C_D at the held-out points.

## Deliverables

- The coefficient table, fit and validity envelope, with the held-out residuals.
- The feasibility map over entry interfaces, as a figure and as data.
- The optimised schedules and the Pareto front, plus the fixed-trim counterpart.
- The trajectory and surface-state animation for one representative case, through the campaign video pipeline.
- A written statement of the model's validity band and of what the numbers mean outside it.

## Acceptance criteria

- The integrator reproduces the analytic ballistic case within the stated tolerance, with the observed order of convergence reported.
- Force coefficients change by less than 5 % on the refined mesh at the representative condition.
- The optimum reduces 3-sigma landing dispersion against the fixed-trim baseline by a margin larger than the uncertainty of the propagation itself; if it does not, the report says so.
- The Knudsen-number guard flags every condition in the campaign where continuum aerodynamics is not defensible, and no result is reported inside the flagged band.
- The heat estimate carries its correlation's assumptions in the report, and no ablation or chemistry claim is made.

## Realizability and evidence class

`PLAUSIBILITY_CLASS`: `established` for entry mechanics and controllable entry (bank reversal is flown practice); `extrapolated` for the specific movable-surface concept. Axes: `mathematical_consistency` (energy check on the trajectory, coefficient residuals), `numerical_stability` (GCI, fit residuals), `physical_plausibility` (ballistic limit, published entry behaviour), `uncertainty_sensitivity` (dispersion over the uncertainty set). `experimental_validation` is not assessed — no flight or wind-tunnel data; `technological_feasibility` is not assessed for the actuation mechanism, whose mass and torque are only parameterised.

## Risks and failure modes

1. **The coefficient model is extrapolated** beyond the campaign box. Mitigation: an explicit box in (Mach, alpha, deflection), a guard that refuses out-of-box queries, and the trajectory reported only inside it.
2. **Continuum CFD in a rarefied regime** produces confident nonsense. Mitigation: the Knudsen guard, stated altitude band, and no result inside the flagged band.
3. **The heating correlation is misread as a thermal analysis.** Mitigation: the correlation and its limits are named in every table that reports heat.
4. **Dispersion is under-sampled** and the ellipse is optimistic. Mitigation: report the number of samples and a convergence plot of the ellipse with sample count.
5. **A dramatic zig-zag trajectory that violates a limit** gets presented as the result. Mitigation: the acceptance criteria reject any schedule that breaks a limit, regardless of how it looks.

## Out of scope

- Ablation, thermal protection material selection and recession.
- Guidance, navigation and control hardware, sensors, and actuator dynamics.
- A six-DOF structural or aeroelastic model of the capsule.
