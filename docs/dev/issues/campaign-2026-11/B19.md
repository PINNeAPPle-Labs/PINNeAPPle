## Summary

A pulsed jet forms vortex rings and a body reacts to them. The experiment measures impulse per unit energy for a family of nozzle shapes and pulse frequencies, and — this is the part that is usually skipped — verifies the thrust by integrating the momentum flux through a control surface, not by applying a formula to an animation. The report places every design on a bound: the mean thrust cannot exceed what the nozzle delivers in momentum flux, and the interesting result is how close an efficient ring can get.

## Question

For a fixed energy budget, which nozzle geometry and pulse schedule produce the most impulse on the body, and what fraction of the momentum-flux bound does the best configuration achieve?

## Why run it

- It is a case where the picture is spectacular and the correct physics is a bookkeeping exercise, which makes it the best illustration of what the campaign means by "the visual is the evidence".
- The bound gives a result that survives the experiment: any claimed propulsor can be placed against it, including ones that are not simulated here.
- Ring–body interaction is a real research topic (starting vortices, added mass, formation number), so a careful parameter study has scientific content beyond the demonstration.

## Physical model

**Governing equations.** Incompressible Navier–Stokes for the fluid, with the body either held fixed (force measurement) or free (6-DOF response). The dimensionless groups are the stroke ratio L/D (jet length over diameter, which controls the ring's formation and whether it separates from the nozzle), the Reynolds number based on nozzle diameter and jet velocity, the pulse duty cycle, and the Strouhal number of the pulse train.

**Impulse accounting.** Thrust is the rate of change of fluid momentum crossing a control surface enclosing the device plus the pressure term on that surface — computed from the solver's fields, not from a model. Ring circulation and total vorticity are reported from the same fields.

**The bound.** For a jet delivering mass flow at velocity, the momentum flux sets an upper bound on the mean thrust at a given energy; the report computes it for each case and shows the achieved fraction.

**Deliberately out of scope:** compressibility, cavitation, free-surface effects, and the structural design of the nozzle. The body is a rigid bluff shape.

## Geometry and inputs

Nozzle families: straight, converging, and a lobed or notched exit that promotes ring formation; the parameters are exit diameter, contraction ratio and lobe count. The body is a plate or a sphere on a support whose force is integrated. Geometry from `pinneapple_design.geometry.gen` (`primitives`, `sdf_shapes`) and meshed through the existing external-flow path.

Inputs: the stroke schedule (pulse duration and interval), the jet velocity profile at the nozzle inlet as a boundary condition, and the fluid properties.

## Solver and engine

- Primary: transient incompressible flow with a deforming or moving boundary. `external_solvers.openfoam` (`pimpleFoam`) is the production path; `pinneapple_simulation.numerical_solvers.sph`/`isph`/`dfsph` and `lbm`/`lbm_tunnel` provide an independent, in-process cross-check on one configuration.
- Body: `pinneapple_simulation.flight.rigid_body` or `vehicle.sim` for the free-body case.
- Force and impulse: computed from the solver fields on a control surface, post-processed through `pinneapple_analysis` (vorticity, circulation, momentum flux).
- Recording: `pinneapple_lab.experiment` with metrics and checks.

## What is missing (the work)

1. **A control-surface momentum accounting module**: given the velocity and pressure fields on a closed surface over time, return the impulse history and its integral, with the discretisation error estimated. This is the core deliverable of the issue.
2. **A vortex-ring identifier**: circulation, core radius and trajectory of the ring extracted from the fields, so the rings can be reported as numbers rather than inferred from pictures.
3. **A moving-boundary or prescribed-motion case template** for the pulsed jet, and the two-way coupling for the free-body case.
4. **The bound calculator**: momentum flux and power for each schedule, with the achieved fraction.
5. **A conservation check**: total momentum of fluid plus body over the pulse must close within the stated tolerance; a failure here invalidates the thrust number, so it must be a hard check, not a diagnostic.

## Experiment protocol

1. Grid and time-step convergence on one configuration (straight nozzle, L/D = 4): at least three levels, with the impulse and the peak force as the observables.
2. Cross-solver check: the same configuration with an SPH and an LBM run, comparing impulse within a stated tolerance. This is the independent verification; disagreement is reported, not averaged.
3. Parameter sweep: nozzle family × L/D (2–8) × duty cycle × Reynolds number, of order 40–80 cases, each run long enough for the ring to clear the control surface.
4. Free-body case: one configuration with the body released, comparing the predicted response against the thrust history measured from the fixed-body run — the same physics must give the same answer.
5. Bound analysis: for every case, compute the momentum-flux bound and the achieved fraction; identify where the ring formation degrades the result.
6. Compute budget: order 10^2–10^3 CPU-hours depending on resolution; the SPH and LBM cross-checks are the expensive part.

## Baselines, references and verification

- A steady jet with the same mass flow and energy: the pulsed jet must beat it, or the pulsing is decoration.
- The momentum-flux bound: an inequality that must hold for every case; a violation means a bug.
- Formation-number literature: the L/D at which the ring stops gaining circulation is a known result, and the extractor must reproduce it.
- Momentum conservation of the fluid-plus-body system per pulse.

## Metrics

- Impulse per pulse, N·s, from the control-surface integral.
- Mean thrust, N, and thrust per unit energy, N/kW, dimensionless against the steady-jet baseline.
- Achieved fraction of the momentum-flux bound, dimensionless.
- Ring circulation, m²/s, core radius, m, and the formation number L/D at separation.
- Peak force on the body, N, and the free-body velocity increment, m/s.
- Conservation residual, relative, per pulse.

## Deliverables

## Acceptance criteria

- Momentum conservation closes within the stated tolerance for every accepted case; cases that fail are excluded and counted.
- The three solvers agree on impulse within a stated band for the cross-check configuration, or the disagreement is quantified and attributed.
- The formation-number behaviour reproduced by the extractor matches the literature value within a stated tolerance.
- No case reports a thrust above the momentum-flux bound; if one does, the accounting is investigated before anything else is reported.
- The pulsed jet's advantage over the steady jet is reported with its uncertainty, and the cases where it loses are shown.

## Realizability and evidence class

`PLAUSIBILITY_CLASS`: `established` — vortex-ring propulsion is a studied phenomenon and thrust from pulsed jets is measured in the literature. Axes: `mathematical_consistency` (momentum conservation residual), `numerical_stability` (three-level convergence, cross-solver comparison), `physical_plausibility` (the momentum-flux bound, formation number, steady-jet baseline), `uncertainty_sensitivity` (parameter sweep with reported spread). `experimental_validation` is not assessed without a bench measurement; the issue states that the bound is a physics result, not a validation.

## Risks and failure modes

1. **The control surface cuts the ring** and the impulse integral misses part of the momentum. Mitigation: place the surface from the field solution, check that the ring is inside at the end of the pulse, and report the exit flux history.
2. **Mesh resolution at the nozzle exit** dominates the result. Mitigation: exit-region refinement study as part of the convergence ladder.
3. **SPH and LBM disagree for reasons unrelated to physics** (boundary conditions, compressibility in LBM). Mitigation: document each solver's setup and compare the same dimensionless case; report the disagreement rather than picking the convenient one.
4. **A visually impressive ring that produces negligible impulse.** Mitigation: the impulse-per-energy metric is the headline, not the picture.
5. **Free-body coupling instability.** Mitigation: prescribed motion first, added-mass-aware coupling second, and the free-body result reported only after the fixed-body force history validates.

## Out of scope

- Nozzle structural design, valves, and the actuator that produces the pulse.
- Cavitation, aeration and free-surface operation.
- Scaling to a full vehicle; the study is on the propulsor unit.
