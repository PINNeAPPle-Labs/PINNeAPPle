## Summary

AERIS optimises a morphing airframe as a function of flight condition rather than once: sweep, span, section camber and winglet articulation become separate design vectors, one per point in an operating envelope. Aerodynamics comes from a vortex-lattice solve regenerated as the geometry moves; the trimmed state comes from the 6-DOF side. The claim: a per-condition schedule beats the best single fixed shape on a Pareto front of cruise L/D, trimmed C_L max and actuator mass, with the quasi-steady assumption enforced.

## Question

Does a per-condition morphing schedule give a Pareto improvement over the best single fixed geometry once the mass, power and bandwidth of the actuation are charged to the design?

## Why run it

It settles whether the multi-point advantage survives the mechanism penalty, which a single-point optimisation cannot answer, and it produces the loop later flying demonstrations need.

## Physical model

Potential flow: the lifting surfaces reduce to horseshoe vortex lattices with zero normal velocity at the collocation points,

sum_j A_ij Gamma_j = -U . n_i(geometry)

regenerated as the geometry moves; where viscosity matters the case drops to steady incompressible RANS.

Groups: Re = U cbar / nu; Mach, because the lattice is invalid above about M = 0.3; and the morphing reduced frequency k_m = omega_morph cbar / U, below about 0.05 for quasi-steady flow. Actuation is charged as a mass fraction f_act = m_act / m_takeoff and a power P_act.

Left out: separated flow, transonic effects, aeroelastic feedback, skin flexibility, real actuator dynamics. A gain here describes a schedule, not a flying aircraft.

## Geometry and inputs

A parametric airframe: root and tip chord, sweep, span, camber and thickness, and an articulated winglet. Every morphing variable sits in a bounded envelope so the optimiser cannot invent a span the baseline structure could not carry. Cases are generated from the geometry; nothing is downloaded.

## Solver and engine

Aerodynamics: `pinneapple_design.aero.vlm` (`build_lattice`, `solve`) and `aircraft3d` (`Aircraft3D`, `airframe_from`, `baseline_x`). Multi-point optimisation: `aero.optimize3d` (`Engine3D`, `summarize3d`). Trim and off-nominal conditions: `pinneapple_simulation.flight` (`trim`, `stability_derivatives`, `turbulence_encounter`, `simulate_takeoff`). Reference RANS: `aero.case3d` (`write_case3d`, `run_case3d`) or `numerical_solvers.external_flow.ExternalFlow`.

## What is missing (the work)

1. A morphing geometry model: an airframe whose planform and camber move continuously, lattice and mesh re-fitted per evaluation; `airframe_from` takes one vector today.
2. A per-condition design vector and a multi-point objective summed over conditions.
3. An actuator model (mass, peak power, rate limit, lag) with a constraint keeping k_m quasi-steady.
4. Three-objective Pareto machinery (L/D, trimmed C_L max, actuator mass) over the existing scan in `optimize3d`.
5. A trim-feasibility filter: an untrimmable geometry is discarded, not softly penalised.

## Experiment protocol

Conditions: takeoff climb, loiter, cruise, high-altitude cruise, gust encounter. Design of experiments: 400 Latin-hypercube fixed-geometry samples for the surrogate, then 30 generations of 40 individuals per condition. Every Pareto candidate is trimmed and flown through `turbulence_encounter` at three gust intensities. Convergence: hypervolume gain below 0.5 % over five generations. Budget: 10^3 CPU-hours of lattice solves plus 10^2 of RANS.

## Baselines, references and verification

Baselines: the best single fixed geometry over the envelope, the baseline airframe at its design point, and the optimiser without the actuator penalty. Verification: the winner's L/D is re-checked with RANS at two mesh levels with GCI from `mesh_independence_study`; the lattice lift slope against panel results and thin-airfoil theory; trim against a closed-form level-flight balance.

## Metrics

- Lambda = (L/D)_morph / (L/D)_fixed per condition, from lattice and trim.
- C_L,max,trimmed inside envelope and trim limits.
- f_act = m_act / m_takeoff, from the actuator model.
- P_act,avg and P_act,peak (W) over a flown schedule.
- Hypervolume of the front; GCI of L/D on verification cases (percent).

## Deliverables

The schedule table (condition to shape), a Pareto front, L/D against condition with both schedules overlaid, and a rendered flight sequence of the geometry changing between two conditions. All evaluated designs in CSV plus a JSON manifest.

## Acceptance criteria

- At loiter the morphing schedule beats the best fixed geometry in L/D by at least 8 %, net of actuator mass, or the negative result is reported.
- At cruise the two schedules are within 2 %.
- The winner's L/D changes by less than 5 % on a 2x refined mesh.
- k_m stays below 0.05 in every delivered schedule.

## Realizability and evidence class

Fills `mathematical_consistency`, `numerical_stability`, `physical_plausibility`; `uncertainty_sensitivity` partially. `experimental_validation` and `technological_feasibility` stay not assessed. `PLAUSIBILITY_CLASS`: `extrapolated`.

## Risks and failure modes

1. Potential flow cannot represent separated states. Mitigation: constrain the envelope; RANS-check boundaries.
2. The optimiser exploits lattice behaviour at extreme sweep. Mitigation: hard bounds, plus a sanity check on each geometry.
3. A generous actuator model lets morphing win by assumption. Mitigation: three penalty levels, plotted.
4. Multi-point weights decide the answer. Mitigation: report the full Pareto set, never a weighted sum alone.

## Out of scope

- Building or flying a mechanism; structural sizing of the morphing skin.
- Transonic, supersonic or separated aerodynamics.
- Aeroelastic tailoring and flutter.
