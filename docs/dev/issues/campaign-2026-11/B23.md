## Summary

AEROMORPH selects an aerodynamic configuration per track segment: low drag on the straight, downforce in the corner, cooling throughout. Lap time is the objective, computed with a point-mass vehicle model fed by an aero map. The deliverable is the delta against the best single fixed configuration — the delta is the result, not the shape.

## Question

On a stated lap, does a per-segment configuration policy beat the best single fixed configuration by more than the documented force noise of the aerodynamic solver?

## Why run it

It converts an engineering argument into a lap-time delta with a noise floor attached, and forces an actuator bandwidth and energy model into the loop so a morphing car cannot win for free.

## Physical model

Lap: m dV/dt = F_traction - 0.5 rho V^2 A C_D(c) - F_roll, with corner speed from a friction ellipse, V^2 = mu(F_z)(g + F_z,aero/m) R, mu falling with load. Configuration c sets C_D, C_L and balance. Each transition costs a time tau_act and an energy E_act, permitted only where tau_act < segment duration — the quasi-steady assumption, made explicit.

Groups: C_L, C_D, C_L/C_D, tyre utilization, tau_act/T_segment, power ratio.

Valid for quasi-steady aerodynamics and tyres. It leaves out transition hysteresis, tyre relaxation length, suspension dynamics and thermal effects.

## Geometry and inputs

The existing `FormulaCar` parameterisation through `RaceCarAero`: front_wing_aoa 0–5 deg, front_flap_aoa 12–30, rear_wing_aoa 0–7.5, rear_flap_aoa 16–40, diffuser_angle 7–18 deg, ride_height 0.025–0.060 m. The track is a segment table (length, radius, gradient) for one circuit, committed as data.

## Solver and engine

`pinneapple_autolab.domains.racecar.RaceCarAero` — OpenFOAM `simpleFoam` with `snappyHexMesh`, k-omega SST, half model, moving road, fixed at 70 m/s. The campaign runs through `AutonomousLab` with `Mission`, `Limit`, `Budget`, recording through `dashboard.build_dashboard`.

## What already exists in PINNeAPPle (verified)

`pinneapple_autolab.domains.racecar`: `RaceCarAero`, `VARIABLES`, `BASELINE`, `CONDITIONS`, `MESHES`, `force_convergence`, `solver_log_summary`, `make_car`, `racecar_domain`, `flagship_mission`, `render_run`, `flow_scene`, `build_dashboard`; `pinneapple_autolab.AutonomousLab`, `Mission`, `Limit`, `Budget`, `compare_models`, `assess_candidates`, `choose_next`; `pipelines.freecad_openfoam.Sweep`, `doe`, `fit_surrogate`; `verification.convergence.mesh_independence_study`; `pinneapple_lab.experiment`.

## What is missing (the work)

1. A lap-time integrator over a segment table with a load-sensitive tyre model; nothing in the library integrates a lap.
2. A per-segment configuration policy, with the transition budget enforced inside it.
3. An actuator model — bandwidth, transition time, energy per transition, and a mass penalty feeding back into the point mass.
4. An aero map at more than one speed. `RaceCarAero` fixes 70 m/s; a lap needs C_D and C_L at cornering speeds, so the conditions must become parametric or the map resampled with a stated correction.
5. A cooling term: inlet area as a variable, with the radiator requirement and its drag.

## Experiment protocol

Aero map: 24 Latin-hypercube points at 70 m/s on the campaign mesh, plus 12-point subsets at 40 and 55 m/s — 48 CFD runs; three selected configurations re-solved on the fine mesh. Lap model: four segment discretizations x three tyre models, seeds 0–2. Policy: forces averaged over iterations 400–800 as the module documents, and the documented ±2–3 % iteration spread is the noise floor quoted beside every delta. Budget 250–500 CPU-hours.

## Baselines, references and verification

Best single fixed configuration for the same lap with the same budget; the module's `BASELINE`; a corner-optimised fixed configuration. Verification: selected configurations re-solved on the fine mesh must shift drag and downforce within the module's own tolerances (3 %, 5 %, 5 % balance).

## Metrics

Lap time [s], delta versus fixed [s], C_L, C_D, C_L/C_D, transitions per lap, actuator energy [kJ/lap], segment time trace, CFD iteration spread [%].

## Deliverables

48-run aero map (JSON plus npz with Cp surfaces), lap trace, figures of segment time and configuration schedule, lab dashboard, and a render of the two extreme configurations.

## Acceptance criteria

- Interpolation reproduces held-out CFD points within the iteration spread.
- Re-solving the selected configurations on the fine mesh changes drag and downforce by less than 3 % and 5 %.
- The fixed-configuration baseline gets the same run budget as the policy.
- A delta inside the noise floor is reported as not a result.
- Actuator energy and mass are charged in every evaluation.

## Realizability and evidence class

PLAUSIBILITY_CLASS today: `extrapolated`: drag reduction systems and active flaps are established hardware; a configuration changing continuously within a lap is not.

Filled: `mathematical_consistency`, `numerical_stability`, `physical_plausibility`. NOT ASSESSED: `experimental_validation` (no car, no track), `technological_feasibility` (actuator hardware, legality).

To move up one class, ranked: an actuator with bandwidth to finish a transition inside a corner entry; a mass and power penalty small against the aero gain; a regulation permitting per-segment configuration; tunnel or track data on the same configurations.

## Risks and failure modes

- Transition forces are nothing like the steady map — the map stays steady-state and the metric says so.
- Corner speed is dominated by the tyre model — three models and a reported sensitivity.
- The optimiser exploits a configuration outside the validated map — a validity domain check.
- A delta smaller than the solver noise gets a pre-registered noise floor and an explicit not-a-result verdict.

## Out of scope

- Tyre thermal behaviour and degradation.
- Suspension kinematics and ride dynamics.
- Legality claims.
