## Summary

HYDROFLIGHT is a hull that leaves the water and continues on foils. The experiment builds the resistance curve, the take-off speed and the incidence schedule in a cheap lifting-line model, then checks one configuration against free-surface CFD. The claim is bounded by the disagreement between the two, which is reported, not hidden.

## Question

At what speed does the foil system carry the full displacement, and how far does the cheap model disagree with free-surface CFD at that speed?

## Why run it

It puts a number on the transition: the speed at which resistance stops rising, and the incidence schedule that gets there. It also establishes how far a cheap foil model can be trusted before CFD is needed.

## Physical model

Hull: R_T = 0.5 rho U^2 S C_T, with C_F = 0.075/(log10 Re - 2)^2 and a pressure-resistance term from a stated method, so C_T = (1+k) C_F + C_R. Foils: lifting-line lift with a submergence correction, C_L = C_L,inf k(h/c), capped by cavitation at sigma_c = (p_atm - p_v + rho g h)/(0.5 rho U^2). Take-off is foil lift reaching the displacement weight; heave and pitch follow from two equilibrium equations.

Groups: Fr = U/sqrt(g L); Fn_nabla = U/sqrt(g nabla^(1/3)); Re; sigma_c; h/c.

Valid for potential lifting flow with an empirical free-surface correction, quasi-steady. It leaves out spray, ventilation, breaking and flutter, and cannot resolve the instant a foil pierces the surface.

## Geometry and inputs

Hull from `domains3d` and `sdf_shapes`; foils from `gen.airfoil.naca_parametric` and a hydrofoil section set. Variables: foil span, chord, incidence schedule, anhedral, fore/aft position. Displacement 1–4 t, two arrangements: surface-piercing, and fully submerged on a strut.

## Solver and engine

Foils: `pinneapple_design.aero.panel.panel_solve` for sections, `aero.vlm.solve` for 3-D lifting. Motion: `pinneapple_simulation.vehicle.sim.VehicleSimulator`. The CFD check is `numerical_solvers.free_surface.FreeSurfaceFlow`, which writes an interFoam case with stated liquid and gas phases. `shallow_water_fv` is depth-averaged and hydrostatic, and is not sufficient here.

## What already exists in PINNeAPPle (verified)

`aero.panel.panel_solve`, `PanelResult`; `aero.vlm.solve`, `VLMSolution`, `build_lattice`; `aero.case3d.write_case3d`, `run_case3d`; `numerical_solvers.free_surface.FreeSurfaceFlow`, `sample_lines`, `read_profiles`, `time_average`; `numerical_solvers.shallow_water_fv` (insufficient); `vehicle.sim.VehicleSimulator`, `VehicleRun`; `gen.airfoil.naca_parametric`; `pipelines.freecad_openfoam.Sweep`, `doe`, `fit_surrogate`; `verification.convergence.mesh_independence_study`; `pinneapple_lab.experiment`.

## What is missing (the work)

1. A submergence and free-surface correction to foil lift and drag as a function of h/c and Fr, with its validity range stated.
2. A wetted-area and pressure-resistance model for both regimes, and the blend between them as the hull lifts.
3. A transition state in the motion model: nothing in `VehicleSimulator` represents a water surface.
4. A multiphase CFD case with six-degree-of-freedom motion and a stated convergence policy.
5. A lateral-stability metric for a foil-borne turn, which neither foil theory nor the vehicle stack provides.

## Experiment protocol

Matrix: Fr in eight values 0.2–0.9 x five incidence schedules x two arrangements = 80 cheap points; one CFD check at take-off, two mesh levels at ratio sqrt(2), with take-off speed required to change by less than 10 % and resistance by less than 15 %. Budget 200–400 CPU-hours, CFD-dominant.

## Baselines, references and verification

The bare hull, which fixes the resistance the foils must beat; a fixed-incidence foil with no schedule; a published resistance correlation for the hull form. Verification: the CFD case must reproduce the bare-hull resistance at the same Fr within its mesh uncertainty.

## Metrics

C_T versus Fr [-]. Take-off speed [m/s] and its Froude number. Foil L/D [-]. Minimum submergence before ventilation [m]. Heave and pitch residuals. Model-to-CFD disagreement [%].

## Deliverables

80 cheap-model records, two CFD cases, the resistance curve with the transition marked, the incidence schedule, a render at the take-off attitude, the manifest. The model-to-CFD table is the headline artefact.

## Acceptance criteria

- The CFD case reproduces the bare-hull resistance within its GCI, or it is discarded and re-meshed.
- GCI_fine below 5 % on the CFD resistance.
- Take-off speed is reported with the CFD disagreement in percent.
- A disagreement above 15 % on resistance is reported as a failure of v1, not smoothed away.
- A cavitation number below the stated inception margin is flagged for every schedule point.

## Realizability and evidence class

PLAUSIBILITY_CLASS today: `established`. Hydrofoil craft are built and raced; the open questions are efficiency and control at the transition, not existence.

Filled: `mathematical_consistency`, `numerical_stability`, `physical_plausibility`. NOT ASSESSED: `experimental_validation` (no tank), `technological_feasibility`. `uncertainty_sensitivity` is partly fillable with `margin_study` on the resistance coefficients.

To move up one class, ranked: a measured resistance curve for the same hull form; a ventilation and cavitation test on the chosen section; a foil-depth control law validated in a seaway.

## Risks and failure modes

- The pressure-resistance term dominates and is untested for this hull form — state its source and range, and plot the curve with and without it.
- The CFD disagreement is mesh, not physics — the GCI and bare-hull check are the gate.
- The transition is smeared and the take-off speed is a blend artefact — report the speed band, not one number.
- Ventilation is unmodelled, so the result is an upper bound on performance.

## Out of scope

- Foil structural design and fatigue.
- Active ride-height or incidence hardware.
- Seakeeping, added resistance in waves, ocean-going claims.
