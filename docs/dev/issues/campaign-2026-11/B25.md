## Summary

INVERTED WORLD is a six-wheeled vehicle that drives across a floor, up a wall, around a curved corner and onto a ceiling, with an explicit adhesion mechanism whose force budget is modelled rather than assumed. The experiment returns the adhesion-to-weight ratio at which each transition stops, and which wheel loses contact first.

## Question

For a stated adhesion mechanism and force density, what is the tightest corner radius the vehicle traverses before the adhesion margin goes negative, and which wheel lifts first?

## Why run it

It turns adhesion into an auditable budget instead of an assumption, and it locates the failure before any hardware exists. The same budget applies to every surface-to-surface transition the campaign attempts, including the legged and modular structures, which all need to hold themselves on something.

## Physical model

Quasi-static equilibrium per wheel against the local surface normal n. On a ceiling the adhesion must carry m g plus a stated reserve; on a corner of radius R it must also carry the change of direction over the wrap, which grows as R shrinks. Adhesion is represented by a limit surface, (F_normal/F_normal,max)^p + (F_shear/F_shear,max)^p = 1, with F_shear,max = mu_eff F_normal, so normal and shear demand compete rather than add.

Ground contact, on the floor only, follows Bekker–Wong: p = (k_c/b + k_phi) z^n, tau = (c + p tan phi)(1 - exp(-j/K)). For a magnetic mechanism the substrate permeability and thickness are design inputs, not assumptions; for suction the ambient pressure is the hard ceiling on achievable force density.

Groups: adhesion number A = F_a/(m g); curvature ratio R/L; slip ratio s; transition Froude number.

Valid for quasi-static load transfer on a rigid chassis. It leaves out the dynamics of the transition at speed, seal leakage transients, magnetic saturation and dust on an adhesive pad.

## Geometry and inputs

Path: floor, vertical wall, three corner radii {0.2, 0.4, 0.8} m, ceiling. Vehicle: six-wheel skid steer from `vehicle.build.build_vehicle` with a `VehicleSpec`; the terramechanics defaults give wheel radius 0.125 m and a 40 kg mass. Mechanisms: magnetic on a ferromagnetic substrate, suction across a sealed skirt, and a dry adhesive with stated normal and shear strength per unit area.

## Solver and engine

`numerical_solvers.bekker_wong.BekkerWongSolver` and `bekker_wong_forces_torch`, with `particle_dynamics.terramechanics.SoilParams` and `WheelParams`, for wheel–surface reaction. `vehicle.sim.VehicleSimulator` with `vehicle.build.build_vehicle` for the trajectory; `multibody.tree.MultibodyTree` for load transfer; `external_solvers.mujoco` and `newton_physics` as a contact cross-check.

## What is missing (the work)

1. An adhesion model with a limit surface per mechanism, in a new adhesion module.
2. A non-horizontal surface representation. `VehicleSimulator` builds ground from a height field and its normal, which cannot express a ceiling, an overhang or a corner wrap.
3. Per-wheel load distribution on non-horizontal surfaces, including the wrap over a corner where two wheels touch different faces.
4. A route planner that respects the margin rather than the geometry alone.
5. A power model: suction pumping power, magnet coil power, and the mass of the mechanism, so the budget closes.

## Experiment protocol

Matrix: four topologies x three mechanisms x three speeds = 36 runs, with the corner radius swept inside the topology cases at three values. Policy: force-balance residual below 1 % at each logged step; the margin reported at every wheel and every surface change. Budget 50–120 CPU-hours. Logged: wheel normal and shear force, slip, contact flag, margin, transit time, mechanism power, commit.

## Baselines, references and verification

The same vehicle with gravity reversed on a flat floor, which isolates the adhesion contribution from the driving dynamics; the same vehicle with no adhesion, which must fail on the ceiling. Static verification: the analytic ceiling threshold F_a = m g must be recovered within 2 % by the simulated force balance before any corner is attempted.

## Metrics

Maximum traversable curvature [1/m]. Adhesion margin M = F_a/F_required - 1 [-]. Wheel slip at the corner [%]. Transit time [s]. Power per newton held [W/N]. Failure mode and the wheel that lifts first.

## Deliverables

36 run records (JSON plus npz with force traces), figures of margin versus curvature per mechanism and of the first-lift wheel, a render of the corner transition, and the manifest.

## Acceptance criteria

- The simulated ceiling threshold matches F_a = m g within 2 %.
- Force-balance residual below 1 % at every logged step.
- For each mechanism the report states the curvature at which M reaches zero, or that no tested curvature does.
- A margin that goes negative without the vehicle losing contact is reported as a contact-model defect, not a result.
- Power per newton held is reported for all three mechanisms on the same axes.

## Realizability and evidence class

PLAUSIBILITY_CLASS today: `extrapolated`. Magnetic and suction wall-climbing robots are built; a six-wheeled vehicle that carries a useful payload on an arbitrary ceiling with a dry adhesive is not.

Filled: `mathematical_consistency`, `numerical_stability`, `physical_plausibility`. NOT ASSESSED: `experimental_validation` (no vehicle), `technological_feasibility` (adhesive life, seal wear, substrate dependence). `uncertainty_sensitivity` is partly fillable with `margin_study` on the adhesion force density.

To move up one class, ranked: a dry adhesive with measured normal and shear strength under shear-dominated loading; a seal that holds against the leakage the model assumes; a mechanism mass fraction small against the payload; a substrate guaranteed on the surfaces of interest.

## Risks and failure modes

- The height-field ground representation is stretched to cover a corner and the normal field is wrong — the corner geometry must be built explicitly, not as terrain.
- Adhesion is modelled as a force with no failure dynamics, so the vehicle never actually detaches — report the margin, and treat loss of contact as a post-hoc check, not a simulation outcome.
- Substrate properties are assumed rather than stated — every mechanism case names its substrate and the property used.
- The margin is quoted at the chassis rather than at the worst wheel — always report the per-wheel minimum.

## Out of scope

- Adhesive material development, wear and contamination.
- Human-scale vehicles or any payload-safety claim.
- Outdoor surfaces, weather, or transition onto a moving surface.
