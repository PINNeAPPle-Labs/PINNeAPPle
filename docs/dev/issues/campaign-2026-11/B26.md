## Summary

A floating wave-energy converter whose geometry or stiffness changes with the sea state: the device reconfigures so that its resonant response tracks the incoming spectrum, and the experiment optimises that schedule for captured energy over a year of sea states while respecting mooring and structural loads. The honest core is the coefficient source — radiation and diffraction data must come from somewhere defensible — so this issue is as much about declaring that provenance as about the optimisation.

## Question

Over a year of sea states at a stated site, how much more energy does an adaptive converter capture than the best fixed-geometry device of the same displaced volume, and what does the adaptation cost in structural and mooring loads?

## Why run it

- Wave energy is a field where the gap between simulated promise and measured output is notorious; a protocol that reports capture width ratio per sea state, with loads beside it, is more useful than a single annual number.
- The library has the wave and structure pieces but no radiation/diffraction source, so the issue forces an explicit decision about an external tool or a published coefficient set — exactly the kind of boundary this campaign is meant to document.
- It is one of the few speculative-looking subjects here with a real commercial market and real failure modes (survivability, mooring), which makes the limits concrete.

## Physical model

**Hydrodynamics.** Linear potential theory for a floating body in regular waves: the equation of motion in heave (or heave plus pitch for a two-body device) uses added mass, radiation damping and excitation force per unit wave frequency, with the power take-off as a linear damper plus a reactive term. Irregular seas are handled by spectral superposition over a stated spectrum (JONSWAP or Pierson–Moskowitz with a peak period and significant wave height).

**Capture width ratio.** Absorbed power over the wave energy flux per metre of wave crest, per sea state; this is the dimensionless metric that lets devices be compared.

**Structure and mooring.** Quasi-static mooring restoring and peak line tension, plus a coarse structural check of the primary member under the peak load; fatigue is excluded and stated as excluded.

**Deliberately out of scope:** viscous drag and flow separation at the body (a known substantial correction that linear theory omits, and whose omission is quantified if published drag coefficients allow), wave breaking, slamming, and nonlinear mooring dynamics.

## Geometry and inputs

Two families: a heaving point-absorber (a buoy with a variable waterplane area, changed by ballasting or by a geometry that spreads) and a hinged two-body device whose relative rotation is the power take-off. Design variables are the primary dimensions, the draft/ballast state, and the power-take-off damping and stiffness per sea state.

Inputs: the site scatter diagram (significant wave height versus peak period with occurrence frequencies), water depth, and the coefficient set (added mass, radiation damping, excitation) as a function of frequency for each geometry state — external, published, or computed by an external boundary-element tool, recorded with its source.

## Solver and engine

- Device dynamics and power: a frequency-domain model implemented in `pinneapple_simulation.numerical_solvers` style (a small linear system solver over frequency), integrated with the spectral machinery already present.
- Free-surface and wave propagation: `pinneapple_simulation.numerical_solvers.free_surface` and `shallow_water_fv` for a qualitative check of the near field at one sea state — explicitly not a substitute for radiation/diffraction coefficients.
- Structure: `planes_stress`/`solid_fem` and `beam_fe` for the primary member under the design load.
- Optimisation: `pinneapple_design.design_optimizer` (NSGA-II) or a direct search over the discrete state schedule; `pinneapple_simulation.pipelines.freecad_openfoam.fit_surrogate` for a surrogate when the coefficient evaluation is expensive.
- Recording: `pinneapple_lab.experiment`.

## What already exists in PINNeAPPle (verified)

- `pinneapple_simulation.numerical_solvers.beam_fe`, `plane_stress_fem`, `solid_fem` for the structure; `nonlinear_beam_fem` if the hinge needs it.

- `pinneapple_analysis.uncertainty` (`propagate_tolerances`, `margin_study`) for the annual-energy and load margins.
- `pinneapple_data` for storing the scatter diagram and the coefficient tables with provenance.
- `pp.dashboard`, `pinneapple_twin3d`, `pinneapple_blender` for the sea-state animation.

## What is missing (the work)

1. **A radiation/diffraction coefficient source**: either an adapter to an external boundary-element tool, or an imported published coefficient set with its licence and validity envelope recorded. Without this the issue cannot start, and saying so is part of the deliverable.
2. **A frequency-domain device model** assembling added mass, damping, excitation and the power take-off into the response, plus the spectral integration for capture width.
3. **A reconfiguration model**: the physical mechanism by which geometry or stiffness changes, with its time scale, its energy cost and its failure modes (a device that needs to reconfigure must survive the state in which it reconfigures).
4. **A mooring and extreme-load model**: quasi-static restoring and peak tension, plus a survivability case at the site's extreme sea state.
5. **The schedule optimisation** over the scatter diagram with a hysteresis rule to avoid chasing every fluctuation.

## Experiment protocol

1. Establish the coefficient provenance and its validity envelope; run the frequency-domain model for a fixed geometry and compare the predicted response amplitude operators against the coefficient source's own published check case.
2. Validate the spectral integration on a single-frequency limit: a regular wave must reproduce the same absorbed power as the frequency-domain evaluation at that frequency.
3. Optimise the fixed device for annual energy at the site; that is the baseline.
4. Optimise the adaptive device (geometry state plus power-take-off tuning per sea state, with the reconfiguration cost and hysteresis).
5. Propagate the scatter-diagram uncertainty and the coefficient uncertainty through the annual energy and the peak mooring load.
6. Compute budget: minutes per annual evaluation once the coefficient table exists; the deterministic wave run for the near-field check is the expensive item, of order 10^1–10^2 CPU-hours.

## Baselines, references and verification

- Best fixed-geometry device of the same displaced volume at the site: the only baseline that makes adaptation meaningful.
- A published device with a published power matrix, used to validate the frequency-domain chain before any optimisation; if no suitable published case is available, the issue says so and the model's validation is limited to the regular-wave limit and the coefficient source's check case.
- Regular-wave limit: spectral integration must reduce to the single-frequency result.
- Energy conservation in the linear model: absorbed power must not exceed the available flux through the device's capture width, and the bound is checked per sea state.
- Structural check: peak member stress and mooring tension against limits with a stated safety factor.

## Metrics

- Capture width ratio per sea state, dimensionless, from absorbed power divided by wave energy flux times device width.
- Annual energy production, MWh/year, over the scatter diagram.
- Peak mooring tension, kN, and peak member stress, MPa, with utilisation ratios.
- Reconfiguration cost: energy per transition, kWh, and transitions per year.
- Adaptation gain: annual energy of the adaptive device over the fixed baseline, with its uncertainty.
- Model-validity indicator: the fraction of sea states where the linear-theory assumptions hold by the stated criteria.

## Deliverables

- The coefficient source, its licence and validity envelope, recorded as data with provenance.
- The frequency-domain device model with its regular-wave and published-case validation.
- The annual-energy results for baseline and adaptive device, with the scatter diagram annotated.
- The load and survivability cases.
- A sea-state animation of the adaptive device, from the campaign video pipeline, with the schedule shown.

## Acceptance criteria

- The spectral integration reproduces the regular-wave result within the stated tolerance.
- The model reproduces a published device's power matrix within a stated band, or the gap is reported and explained; no annual number is presented without this validation being stated as absent.
- The adaptive gain exceeds the uncertainty propagated from the scatter diagram and the coefficients, or the report concludes that adaptation does not pay.
- Peak mooring tension and member stress stay within limits with the stated safety factor, including the survivability sea state.
- Absorbed power never exceeds the available flux bound for any sea state.

## Realizability and evidence class

`PLAUSIBILITY_CLASS`: `established` for wave-energy conversion and for adaptive tuning (both are engineered today); `extrapolated` for a geometry-changing device at this scale. Axes: `mathematical_consistency` (regular-wave limit, energy bound), `numerical_stability` (spectral integration convergence), `physical_plausibility` (published power matrix, flux bound), `uncertainty_sensitivity` (scatter and coefficient uncertainty to annual energy). `experimental_validation` stays not assessed without tank or sea data; `technological_feasibility` is partly addressed by the reconfiguration cost model but no mechanism is designed.

## Risks and failure modes

1. **Linear theory without viscous drag overstates capture.** Mitigation: state the omission, apply a published drag correction where one exists, and report the sensitivity of the annual energy to the drag coefficient.
2. **A fictional or mis-licensed coefficient set.** Mitigation: provenance recorded with licence and validity envelope; no results without it.
3. **The device captures a lot in the model and would not survive the sea.** Mitigation: survivability and mooring cases are acceptance criteria, not appendices.
4. **Adaptation gains come from an infinitely fast mechanism.** Mitigation: the reconfiguration time scale and energy are modelled and deducted.
5. **Annual energy reported as a single number** hiding the sea states where the device loses. Mitigation: the per-sea-state table is a deliverable.

## Out of scope

- Power take-off hardware (hydraulics, generators, control electronics).
- Fatigue life, corrosion, biofouling and installation.
- A CFD-resolved device in irregular waves.
