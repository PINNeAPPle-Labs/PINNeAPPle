## Summary

XENOFORM runs one 3-D density-based topology optimisation with two physics in one objective: structural compliance and Stokes dissipation of a fluid in the same design domain, coupled by a Brinkman penalisation over a shared density field. A single distribution must carry a transverse load and pass a fluid without throttling it. The claim: the field beats a uniform lattice and a hand-designed truss at equal solid volume and survives a body-fitted re-solve. It describes a benchmark, not a manufacturable part.

## Question

Does a single shared density field give both lower compliance and lower fluid dissipation than a uniform lattice of the same solid volume when the optimum is re-solved on a body-fitted mesh?

## Why run it

It tests whether two physics can be optimised together without one absorbing the other's budget, produces the 3-D extension the topology package lacks, and forces the extract-and-remesh path to exist.

## Physical model

Solid: -div(sigma(u)) = f with SIMP interpolation E_e = E_min + x_e^p (E0 - E_min), p = 3.

Fluid: mu lap(u) - alpha(x) u - grad(p) = 0, div(u) = 0, with alpha(x) = alpha_min + (alpha_max - alpha_min)(1-x)^q, q = 0.1, and alpha_max = 2.5 mu / h_min^2 following Borrvall–Petersson.

Objective: J = w_s C/C_ref + w_f Phi/Phi_ref subject to sum(x_e)/N <= Vf.

Groups: solid volume fraction Vf; the penalisation ratio alpha_max h_min^2 / mu, held near 2.5; and the fluid Reynolds number, which must stay in the Stokes range for dissipation to be the right flow cost.

Deliberately left out: fluid–structure interaction. The pressure does not load the solid; the physics share only the density variable and the volume constraint.

## Geometry and inputs

A box domain with fixed inlet and outlet patches on opposing faces and a wall-mounted solid region carrying two load cases, tip and mid-span. No external dataset: geometry is the design variable; loads and inlet profiles are constants.

## Solver and engine

Driver and 2-D problems: `pinneapple_design.topology`; the 3-D solver is the substance of this issue. Re-verification: `external_solvers.openfoam`, staged through `numerical_solvers.external_flow.snappy_dict`, and `external_solvers.calculix` (`study.solve`) for the structure; GCI from `pinneapple_design.fitmesh.report.gci`.

## What is missing (the work)

1. A 3-D grid and element matrices: `Hex8Grid` and `elasticity_ke_3d` alongside the existing `Q4Grid` and `elasticity_ke`.
2. A 3-D Stokes/Brinkman problem class with an iterative saddle-point solver; the 2-D code solves directly and will not scale.
3. A shared-volume mixed objective in the driver, which today optimises one problem with one constraint.
4. The extract-and-remesh step: density isosurface to STL, then `snappyHexMesh` for the fluid and a CalculiX mesh for the solid.
5. Minimum-length-scale and greyness control adequate for 3-D, so the extracted geometry is not lacework.

## Experiment protocol

Resolution chain: 48x24x24, 64x32x32 and 96x48x48, the finest being 221k hexahedra. Design of experiments: w_s in {0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1} x Vf in {0.15, 0.25, 0.35} = 15 medium runs, plus 5 finest-resolution runs at w_s = 0.5. Convergence: relative objective change below 1e-4 over ten iterations, or 300 iterations. Budget: 10^4 CPU-hours.

## Baselines, references and verification

At equal solid volume: a simple-cubic strut lattice, a hand-designed truss, the pure-solid limit (w_f = 0) and the pure-fluid limit (w_s = 0). The extracted optimum is re-solved body-fitted in `simpleFoam` and CalculiX at two mesh levels. The 2-D class is checked against the published double-pipe benchmark and `finite_difference_check`.

## Metrics

- C: compliance (J), normalised by the pure-solid limit.
- Phi: Stokes dissipation (W), normalised by the pure-fluid limit.
- G = (1/N) sum 4 x_e (1-x_e): greyness, dimensionless; 0 is black-and-white.
- Vf_achieved = sum(x_e)/N against the constraint.
- GCI of C and Phi on the refinement chain (percent).

## Deliverables

The STL of each optimum, pressure and velocity fields on the extracted geometry, objective histories, the per-iteration density sequence for the video, and a case-matrix manifest.

## Acceptance criteria

- At w_s = 0.5, Vf = 0.25 the optimum beats the strut lattice in both C and Phi by at least 15 %.
- Re-solved body-fitted at 2x refinement, C and Phi change by less than 10 %.
- Greyness G stays below 0.1 at the finest resolution.
- The solid is one connected component carrying both load cases.

## Realizability and evidence class

Fills `mathematical_consistency`, `numerical_stability`, `physical_plausibility`; `experimental_validation` and `technological_feasibility` stay not assessed. `PLAUSIBILITY_CLASS`: `established` for the method, `hypothetical` for any real part.

## Risks and failure modes

1. Grey material absorbing the objective. Mitigation: Heaviside continuation and the greyness test.
2. One physics dominates. Mitigation: the weight sweep is reported as a front.
3. Remeshing loses thin members. Mitigation: compare volume before and after.
4. Disconnected solid fragments. Mitigation: connectivity check with rejection.
5. Results read as FSI. Mitigation: the exclusion is repeated in the report abstract.

## Out of scope

- Fluid–structure interaction, transient flow, turbulence.
- Manufacturing constraints beyond a minimum length scale.
- Thermal or electrical physics in the same field.
