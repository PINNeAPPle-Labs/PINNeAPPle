## Summary

LIVING MATTER reconfigures a lattice of actuated cells, each switching between discrete stiffness or conductivity states, to meet a task: global stiffness, a target compliance pattern, or a heat path. The study measures two different optima and the distance between them: the best configuration over the whole state space, and the best one reachable from the as-built state under a switching budget. The claim is that the distance is a property of connectivity and switching cost, not of the optimiser. It says nothing about a material that does this.

## Question

For a lattice of N switchable cells under a stated switching budget, what fraction of the best achievable stiffness is reachable from the as-built state, at what switching cost and holding power?

## Why run it

It separates the best configuration from the best reachable one, the only distinction that matters for a structure expected to reconfigure in service.

## Physical model

Linear elasticity per configuration: K(s) u = f, with K(s) = sum_e K_e(s_e) and s_e from a discrete state set {soft, nominal, stiff}. The objective is either compliance C(s) = f^T u(s) or a shape error ||u(s) - u_target||, over the feasible set of the cell-connectivity graph.

Switching: a budget S of flips, an actuation time t_act = ceil(S / n_ch) tau over n_ch channels, and a holding power P_hold summed over cells not in their nominal state.

Groups: stiffness contrast kappa = k_max / k_min; actuated fraction n_act / N; lattice slenderness; holding-to-mechanical power ratio P_hold / (f . v).

Left out: no material changes stiffness. The switching is a parameter change in the finite-element assembly, not a phase change; cycling, fatigue, thermal expansion and manufacturing tolerance are absent. A living lattice here is a schedule of boundary-value problems.

## Geometry and inputs

Cantilever lattices of 3 x 3 x 6, 3 x 3 x 12 and 4 x 4 x 12 cells from the brick part library, three states per cell. Loads come from the brick load helpers; the target displacement field for the shape task is produced by solving one declared configuration first and is frozen before any search.

## Solver and engine

Assembly statics: `pinneapple_design.bricks.statics` (`analyse_assembly`, `from_assembly`, `Load`, `capacity`). Continuum reference: `bricks.fea.solve`. Independent verification: `numerical_solvers.solid_fem` (`SolidFEM`). Continuous upper bound: `pinneapple_design.topology` (`optimize`, `Compliance2D`).

## What is missing (the work)

1. A cell-level state model: one state per cell, with a connectivity graph deciding when a switch invalidates a neighbour's load path.
2. A reachability computation: best-reachable configuration under a switch budget, exhaustive where small enough, constrained search elsewhere.
3. A switching and holding cost model: budget, channels, transition time, holding power.
4. Fast reanalysis: incremental assembly or a surrogate with a stated error bound.

## Experiment protocol

3 lattices x 3 contrasts (kappa = 2, 10, 100) x 2 tasks = 18 cases, seeds 0 to 4. The smallest lattice is searched exhaustively over a two-state restriction; the larger two use simulated annealing with greedy repair at 5,000 solves per case. Budget: order 10^2 CPU-hours.

## Baselines, references and verification

Baselines: the uniform nominal lattice; random configurations at matched switch count; greedy local search; the continuous relaxation as an upper bound. Verification: every delivered configuration is re-solved with `SolidFEM` and must agree within 2 %; the exhaustive result is ground truth for the heuristics.

## Metrics

- C / C0: compliance relative to the uniform nominal lattice.
- Reachability gap: (C_best - C_reachable) / C_best, percent.
- S: switches from the as-built state; t_act (s); P_hold (W).
- Task error ||u - u_target|| / ||u_target|| for the shape task.

## Deliverables

A configuration atlas per lattice (state maps for the best and the best-reachable), the switching-cost against stiffness Pareto, the compliance table with residuals, and a short render of the lattice reconfiguring.

## Acceptance criteria

- At kappa = 2 the reachable configuration comes within 10 % of the best; the gap at kappa = 100 is reported whatever its size.
- Every delivered configuration agrees with the independent solver within 2 %.
- The exhaustive ground truth is reproduced by the heuristic search, or the failure is reported.
- Switching and holding costs appear in every result.

## Realizability and evidence class

Fills `mathematical_consistency`, `numerical_stability`, `physical_plausibility`; `experimental_validation` and `technological_feasibility` stay not assessed. `PLAUSIBILITY_CLASS`: `established` for the finite-element statement, `hypothetical` for a switchable-stiffness material.

## Risks and failure modes

1. Combinatorial explosion. Mitigation: exhaustive ground truth on the smallest case.
2. A surrogate wrong where it matters. Mitigation: independent re-solve of every delivered configuration.
3. Connectivity makes the problem trivial. Mitigation: report feasible-set size against the full state count.
4. The result holds only for a material nobody has. Mitigation: the contrast sweep is the deliverable.
5. A reachability gap read as an optimiser failure. Mitigation: report it against the exhaustive reference.

## Out of scope

- Active materials, shape-memory alloys, cycling fatigue.
- Manufacture or tolerance of a real cell.
- Heat-path tasks beyond linear conduction.
