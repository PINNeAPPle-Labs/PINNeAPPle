## Summary

FLOW GOD closes a loop on a confined laminar flow using wall-normal blowing and suction, asking whether a policy learned from a reduced model beats a hand-tuned schedule at the same actuation energy. Mixing, heat transfer and drag are targeted in a planar channel; a learned policy, optimised steady forcing and a tuned sinusoid are compared on one solver. The claim: the learned policy buys the objective at lower actuation energy and survives an unseen disturbance. It is silent on turbulence.

## Question

At a stated Reynolds number and budget, does a learned policy reach the objective at lower actuation energy than the best open-loop schedule and stay stable under an unseen disturbance?

## Why run it

Actuation energy is the honest figure of merit in flow control, and the field is full of schedules that work on one case. Comparing a learned policy with optimised steady forcing on one solver is the only way to know whether the phrase means anything at all.

## Physical model

Incompressible Navier–Stokes with a passive scalar:

du/dt + (u . grad)u = -grad p + nu lap u + f_act, div u = 0

dc/dt + u . grad c = D lap c

Groups: Re = U h / nu; Pe = U h / D; blowing ratio m = v_w / U; Strouhal St = f h / U; control energy coefficient C_mu = u_rms^2 / U^2.

Left out: turbulence. A 2-D channel is not a turbulence model, so nothing here extends to turbulent drag reduction. Compressibility, secondary instabilities and wall compliance are absent.

## Geometry and inputs

A planar channel of aspect ratio 8:1 with two actuation strips on the lower wall, a split scalar inlet for mixing and a heated section. Actuation is v_w(x, t), budgeted by the integral of v_w^2 over wall and time; the inflow is declared.

## Solver and engine

In-process: `numerical_solvers` (`StableFluids2D`, `LBMSolver`, `tvd_advection`). Cross-check: `internal_flow.InternalFlow`, an OpenFOAM pipe case with a passive scalar. Heat: `thermal_channel_2d.ThermalChannel2D`. Reduced models: `neural.architectures.rom` (`DMDc`, `KoopmanAutoencoder`) and `accelerated_foam.warm_start` (`PODWarmStart`, `ProjectionWarmStart`).

## What is missing (the work)

1. A channel case with actuation boundary conditions callable every control step; `StableFluids2D` has none and `InternalFlow` is a pipe route.
2. Scalar transport on the same velocity field in a channel; the existing scalar path is pipe-only.
3. A policy class and a training loop that optimises the closed-loop objective, not prediction error.
4. A reduced-order model fast enough for the loop, with its observation operator and error bound.
5. Control-cost accounting and the protocol against optimised steady forcing.

## Experiment protocol

3 objectives x 3 Reynolds numbers (100, 300, 1000) x 3 budgets = 27 cases, each with 3 policies and 3 seeds. Grid 256 x 64, CFL below 0.5, verified at 2x. Each run spans 200 convective time units, with a shear pulse at t = 150 absent from training. Budget: 10^3 CPU-hours.

## Baselines, references and verification

Baselines: no control; the best steady blowing from an open-loop search; a sinusoid at the dominant instability frequency; a reduced-model LQR if buildable. Verification: Poiseuille profile and Orr–Sommerfeld growth rate, a 1-D advection–diffusion solution, held-out snapshots for the ROM.

## Metrics

- J: CoV of outlet concentration, Nu / Nu0, or Cf / Cf0.
- E_act = integral of 0.5 rho v_w^2 over wall and time (J).
- eta = objective improvement per unit actuation energy, the ranking metric.
- C_mu = u_rms^2 / U^2; recovery time to within 5 % of pre-disturbance J.

## Deliverables

Time histories of J, E_act and C_mu, a Pareto of J against E_act, a wall-velocity map, and the policy code with its seed.

## Acceptance criteria

- At Re = 300 the learned policy beats the best steady forcing in eta by at least 15 %.
- J improves by at least 10 % over no control for mixing and heat.
- After the disturbance, J returns within 5 % of its prior value in five convective times.
- Re-run on the full solver, the policy degrades by no more than the ROM error bound.
- A 2x refined grid changes J by less than 5 %.

## Realizability and evidence class

Fills `mathematical_consistency`, `numerical_stability`, `physical_plausibility`; the other axes stay not assessed. `PLAUSIBILITY_CLASS`: `established` for laminar shear flows, `extrapolated` above them.

## Risks and failure modes

1. The policy optimises the ROM, not the flow. Mitigation: full-solver re-run is part of acceptance.
2. The 2-D channel cannot show the instability suppressed. Mitigation: report base-flow stability per Re.
3. Inconsistent energy accounting. Mitigation: one function for all three.
4. A weakened baseline. Mitigation: the sinusoid's frequency and amplitude are optimised too.

## Out of scope

- Turbulent flow and engineering-Reynolds drag reduction.
- 3-D actuation arrays and actuator dynamics.
- Blowing hardware, valves, compressors.
