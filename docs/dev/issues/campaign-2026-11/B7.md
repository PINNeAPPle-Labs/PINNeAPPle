## Summary

VORTEX TREE is a three-dimensional internal manifold: one inlet, eight outlets, fixed split, minimum total pressure loss under a uniformity constraint on the outlet fluxes. The geometry comes from Brinkman-penalized Stokes topology optimization, is remeshed body-fitted and re-solved with incompressible RANS. The claim: a 3-D optimum beats a bifurcating manifold of equal solid volume at the Reynolds number of the re-solve, by a stated margin.

## Question

At fixed split and Reynolds number, how much total pressure loss can a 3-D optimised manifold remove against a bifurcating manifold of equal solid volume, and does the gain survive inertia?

## Why run it

Manifolds are ordinary hardware: cooling plates, fuel rails, spargers. A 3-D method with a working mesh round trip serves all of them, and the value is the 2-D to 3-D step plus the measured gap between the creeping-flow model and the separated flow it designs.

## Physical model

Brinkman-penalized Stokes: `-mu lap(u) + alpha(rho) u + grad p = 0`, `div u = 0`, objective `J = integral[mu |grad u|^2 + alpha(rho)|u|^2] + sum_k p_k Q_k`. Groups: branch Reynolds number `Re = rho U D/mu`, penalization ratio `q`, Darcy number `Da`.

The optimiser sees Stokes flow only. Above `Re` of a few hundred the loss is set by separation at the branch mouths, which the model cannot show; the re-solve carries the claim. Left out: turbulence, unsteadiness, compressibility, roughness, manufacturing limits.

## Geometry and inputs

Design box 3 x 2 x 0.6 inlet diameters; inlet on one face, eight outlets on a 4 x 2 grid opposite at `Q_total/8`; one density per hexahedral element on 64^3, 128^3 and 160^3 grids. A four-outlet variant tests dependence on the split. Inputs: flow rate, density, viscosity, solid volume fraction, filter radius.

## Solver and engine

Optimiser loop in `pinneapple_design.topology`, with the 3-D Stokes problem reimplemented. STL extraction, `snappyHexMesh` via `pinneapple_simulation.pipelines.freecad_openfoam`, then `simpleFoam` via `pinneapple_simulation.external_solvers.openfoam`. Without OpenFOAM the fallback is `pinneapple_simulation.numerical_solvers.fvm`.

## What is missing (the work)

1. A 3-D Stokes/Brinkman problem class on hexahedral elements in `pinneapple_design/topology`; existing problems are 2-D.
2. A solver reaching 10^6 degrees of freedom (pressure correction or Schur complement, multigrid preconditioning).
3. Outlet-flux uniformity as a constraint; only volume fraction exists today.
4. Density-to-geometry extraction: marching cubes, watertightness repair, decimation to a snappy-compatible STL.
5. A manifold case template with a per-outlet fixed flow rate and stated `y+`.
6. Objective history and per-iteration geometry export for the video pipeline.

## Experiment protocol

Grid study at 64^3, 128^3, 160^3 on the eight-outlet case; then `{4, 8}` outlets x three volume fractions x two filter radii = 12 runs, each from three initial fields. Re-solve the best three at `Re = 100` and `Re = 2000` on three meshes: 18 CFD cases. Convergence: density change below 1e-3, KKT residual below 1e-2. Budget 400–800 CPU-hours. Logged: objective, uniformity, pressure drop.

## Baselines, references and verification

Baselines: a bifurcating manifold (one to two to four) at equal solid volume; the empty box as the loss floor; the 2-D optimum extruded. Verification: branch minor loss against published resistance coefficients within 10 %; GCI from `mesh_independence_study`; outlet-plane momentum balance closing within 1 %.

## Metrics

Loss coefficient `K = (p_t,in - mean p_t,out)/(0.5 rho U^2)` [–]. Non-uniformity `max(Q_k)/mean(Q_k)` [–]. Solid volume fraction [–]. Minimum branch diameter [m]. GCI on `K` [%]. Brinkman-to-RANS deviation [%].

## Deliverables

STL per optimum, VTK fields of the re-solved cases, objective history CSV, per-iteration geometry sequence for the render, manifest with commit hash, report on the 2-D to 3-D gain and the model-to-CFD deviation.

## Acceptance criteria

- GCI on `K` below 5 % at the fine pair for every re-solved design.
- `K` of the optimum below the bifurcating baseline at `Re = 2000`, or the report says it is not.
- Outlet fluxes within 5 % of the target split.
- Model-to-CFD deviation reported per design; above 20 % it is a model limitation, not a result.
- The STL meshes without manual repair, or the repair steps are documented.

## Realizability and evidence class

Filled: `mathematical_consistency`, `numerical_stability`, `physical_plausibility`. NOT ASSESSED: `experimental_validation` (no printed manifold), `technological_feasibility` (powder removal, build orientation, roughness). `PLAUSIBILITY_CLASS`: `extrapolated` — 2-D density-based fluid topology optimization is established, 3-D is published.

## Risks and failure modes

- The 3-D solve is too slow and resolution quietly drops, changing the topology. Mitigation: fix resolution in advance.
- Grey material at the outlets makes the geometry ambiguous. Mitigation: threshold, then check the extracted geometry keeps the predicted split.
- The optimiser wins because the baseline is badly designed. Mitigation: size it from published branch-loss coefficients.
- A non-watertight STL gives a silently wrong mesh. Mitigation: watertightness and mesh-quality gates.

## Out of scope

- Additive-manufacturing constraints: overhang, powder removal, self-support.
- Pulsatile or multiphase inlets; fouling.
- A manufactured prototype.
