## Summary

THERMAL ORGANISM is a heat sink grown by topology optimization inside a fixed footprint and volume: one density field decides where metal and where coolant go, minimizing thermal resistance under a pressure-drop budget. The optimiser uses a single-domain Brinkman model; the claim rests on extracting a manufacturable fin set and re-solving it as a two-region conjugate case.

## Question

At fixed footprint, solid volume and pumping power, does a topology-optimised sink reach a lower junction-to-inlet thermal resistance than a straight-fin array sized by fin correlations, and by how much after a body-fitted conjugate re-solve?

## Why run it

Heat sinks are common thermal hardware and the existing path is correlation-based. A verified optimise-extract-remesh-measure loop serves cold plates; the content is the measured gap between the Brinkman optimum and the resolved conjugate case.

## Physical model

Single-domain model: `-div[mu(grad u + grad u^T)] + alpha(rho) u + grad p = 0`, `div u = 0`, `rho_f c_p u . grad T = div[k(rho) grad T] + q'''` in the die. Objective: thermal compliance `integral[k(rho)|grad T|^2]` with a pressure-drop penalty, subject to solid volume and `Delta p <= Delta p_budget`.

Groups: channel Reynolds number, Prandtl number, Darcy number, dimensionless pumping power, fin parameter `m H = H sqrt(2h/(k_s t))`. Validity: forced convection, steady, constant properties, laminar unless the re-solve says otherwise. Left out: radiation, which dominates at low air speed; contact resistance; buoyancy; header maldistribution.

## Geometry and inputs

Footprint 40 x 40 mm, base 4 mm, design block 20 mm tall, die 10 x 10 mm at uniform heat flux, slots on opposite faces, density field on 120^3. Inputs: heat load `{50, 100, 200}` W, air at 2 and 5 m/s or water at 0.5 and 1 m/s, conductivity, volume fraction, filter radius.

## Solver and engine

Optimiser in `pinneapple_design.topology` with the 3-D conjugate problem added. Re-solve: a two-region conjugate case via `pinneapple_simulation.pipelines.freecad_openfoam`, run with `pinneapple_simulation.external_solvers.openfoam`. Baseline from `pinneapple_design.thermal.heatsink` and `fin_array_speed`.

## What is missing (the work)

1. A 3-D conjugate problem class in `pinneapple_design/topology`: flow plus energy on one density field.
2. Pressure drop as a constraint, not only solid volume.
3. A density-to-fin mapping yielding a manufacturable geometry: threshold, minimum feature, preserved die contact, stated process.
4. A conjugate case template with solid and fluid regions, a die heat-flux patch and interfaces.
5. Geometry reduction: a raw isosurface makes a mesh too large for CHT.
6. A radiation and contact-resistance sensitivity.

## Experiment protocol

Mesh study first; then heat load x velocity x volume fraction x filter radius = 16 optimisations; then extract, remesh and re-solve the best two at two mesh levels: 8 CHT cases. The fin array is sized by `heatsink.sizer.size` per condition and re-solved in the same case. Convergence: objective change below 0.5 % over ten iterations; budget 200–500 CPU-hours.

## Baselines, references and verification

Baselines: the straight-fin array at equal solid volume and pumping power; the bare plate as the conduction floor; the extruded 2-D `ThermalCompliance2D` optimum. Verification: the CHT fin-array case must reproduce `engine.evaluate_design` and `fin_array_speed.reference_solve` within 10 % on `R_th` before any optimum is measured; GCI from `mesh_independence_study`.

## Metrics

Thermal resistance `R_th = (T_max - T_in)/Q` [K/W]. Pressure drop [Pa]. Pumping power `P_p = Q_vol Delta p` [W]. Coefficient of performance `Q/P_p` [–]. Minimum feature [mm]. GCI on `R_th` [%].

## Deliverables

STL and STEP, conjugate fields, constraint history CSV, density-slice sequence for the render, manifest, comparison table against the fin array.

## Acceptance criteria

- The CHT fin-array case matches the correlation within 10 % on `R_th` and 20 % on `Delta p`; outside that the case is suspected, not the correlation.
- GCI on `R_th` below 5 % between the two mesh levels.
- The optimum's `R_th` is below the fin array's at equal pumping power and volume, or the report says it is not.
- The geometry meets the stated minimum feature size, or the violating feature is named.
- Brinkman-to-CHT deviation reported per design.

## Realizability and evidence class

Filled: `mathematical_consistency`, `numerical_stability`, `physical_plausibility`. NOT ASSESSED: `experimental_validation` (nothing is built), `technological_feasibility` (process, tool access). `PLAUSIBILITY_CLASS`: `extrapolated` — conjugate topology optimization of heat sinks is published; manufacturing and radiation are not modelled.

## Risks and failure modes

- The optimiser fakes a heat path through the artificial conductivity of grey material. Mitigation: report the density histogram, re-solve sharpened fields.
- At 2 m/s of air, radiation and natural convection rival the modelled convection. Mitigation: state the omitted flux with a magnitude estimate.
- The fin set is unmanufacturable or unmashable. Mitigation: minimum-feature enforcement and a meshing gate.
- Pressure drop taken across different sections. Mitigation: one reference plane everywhere.

## Out of scope

- Two-phase cooling, heat pipes, jet impingement.
- Reliability, fouling, dust loading.
- Any claim about a measured part.
