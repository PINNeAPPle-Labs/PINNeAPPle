## Summary

AERODYNAMIC EVOLUTION evolves a parametric three-dimensional body — a simplified passenger car and an axisymmetric bluff body — to cut drag at fixed frontal area and volume. Geometry comes from signed distance fields, evaluation from RANS with a stated turbulence model, and the search is surrogate-assisted so CFD budget goes where the ranking is uncertain. The claim: the final body's drag coefficient beats the initial body, a spheroid of equal volume and a random-search control of the same budget, verified on a refined mesh.

## Question

At fixed frontal area and volume, how much drag does a surrogate-assisted evolutionary search remove over an equal-budget random search, and does the ranking the surrogate produces survive a fine-mesh re-evaluation?

## Why run it

Shape optimisation is the clearest case where a surrogate can quietly become the result instead of a tool. Publishing the ranking error next to the drag reduction is what makes the number usable. The generation sequence also gives the render project a physically honest animation: every frame is a computed pressure field on a computed shape.

## Physical model

Steady incompressible RANS, `div u = 0`, `u . grad u = -grad p/rho + div[(nu + nu_t) grad u]`, closed with k-omega SST; drag `C_D = D/(0.5 rho U^2 A)` with `A` the frontal area, split into pressure and friction parts. Groups: Reynolds number on body length, blockage ratio, ground clearance over length, and the slenderness the volume constraint fixes.

Validity: attached and mildly separated flow at moderate Reynolds number. The model leaves out large-scale unsteadiness, wheel rotation and underbody detail, and it predicts separation only as well as the turbulence model does. Panel and VLM methods cannot show separation, so they are used for initial sizing only and never for the reported ranking.

## Geometry and inputs

SDF parameterisation with twelve variables: length, width, height, roof curvature, plan taper, boat-tail angle, diffuser angle, front and rear radii, greenhouse inset, ride height. Volume and frontal area are enforced by a projection step, so every evaluated shape satisfies both. Marching cubes to STL, then one fixed external-flow case: 30 body lengths long, blockage below 2 %, wake refinement box, prism layers at a stated `y+`.

## Solver and engine

OpenFOAM `simpleFoam` with k-omega SST, cases written by `pinneapple_design.aero.case3d.write_case3d` and run through `pinneapple_simulation.external_solvers.openfoam`. Surrogate and search from `pinneapple_design.aero.surrogate`, `train`, `optimize`, `verify`. Fallback without OpenFOAM: `pinneapple_simulation.numerical_solvers.external_flow`, labelled as coarse.

## What is missing (the work)

1. A shape-parameterisation module mapping the twelve variables to a watertight SDF, enforcing volume and frontal area exactly and reporting the enforced values.
2. A ground-vehicle external-flow case template with domain-size and blockage policy built in.
3. A surrogate loop with an explicit acquisition rule and a held-out split, recording every CFD evaluation with its input vector.
4. Force-coefficient convergence control: iteration count and moving-average criteria.
5. A mesh-sensitivity driver refining wake and surface together, reporting GCI on `C_D`.
6. A generation export format for the render: geometry, surface pressure, objective history in one record.

## Experiment protocol

Latin hypercube of 240 coarse cases, then four surrogate-guided rounds of 40 cases: 400 evaluations per body class, two classes. Top ten of the final generation re-evaluated on a fine mesh at two refinement levels. Coarse cases at 1–2 million cells, 1500 iterations, coefficients averaged over the last 200. Budget 2000–4000 CPU-hours. Logged: input vector, `C_D`, `C_L`, residuals, `y+`, cell count, surrogate prediction.

## Baselines, references and verification

Baselines: the initial body; a spheroid of equal volume and frontal area; a random-search control using the same 400 evaluations without surrogate guidance. Verification: `C_D` of a fixed reference body against published wind-tunnel data for the same class at matched blockage and Reynolds number; GCI on `C_D` from `mesh_independence_study`; surface pressure and wall shear integrating to the reported total within 1 %.

## Metrics

Drag coefficient `C_D` [–] and drag area `C_D A` [m²]. Lift `C_L` [–], reported so a downforce-for-drag trade is visible. Volume and frontal-area residuals [%]. Surrogate RMSE and Spearman rank correlation against fine-mesh `C_D` [–]. GCI on `C_D` [%]. CFD evaluations consumed [–].

## Deliverables

STL of each generation's best shape, surface pressure and wall-shear fields, objective and constraint history CSV, the surrogate validation table, a generation sequence for the render, manifest with commit and case hashes.

## Acceptance criteria

- Volume and frontal area of every evaluated shape within 1 % of target.
- GCI on `C_D` below 5 % at the fine pair.
- Spearman rank correlation above 0.7 between surrogate and fine-mesh `C_D` on the top ten, or the misses are reported with the shapes that inverted.
- The final `C_D` is below the initial body and below the random control, or the report says it is not.
- The reported drag reduction is the fine-mesh number, never the surrogate's.

## Realizability and evidence class

Filled: `mathematical_consistency`, `numerical_stability`, `physical_plausibility`. NOT ASSESSED: `experimental_validation` (no wind tunnel), `technological_feasibility` (manufacturability, underbody, wheels, ground motion). `PLAUSIBILITY_CLASS`: `extrapolated` — RANS-based vehicle shape optimisation is established in industry; the published result here is the ranking error, not a new class of shape.

## Risks and failure modes

- The optimiser exploits surrogate error and returns a shape that is good only in the model. Mitigation: hold out 10 % of cases and re-evaluate the top ten on the fine mesh.
- `C_D` is read before the solution is stationary. Mitigation: fixed averaging window and a reported moving-average trace.
- Domain size or blockage varies between cases and biases the comparison. Mitigation: one template, one domain, verified per shape.
- The turbulence model sets the separation and the search follows it. Mitigation: one alternative model on the final shape, reported as a spread.

## Out of scope

- Wheel rotation, moving ground, underbody and cooling flows.
- Transient aerodynamics and crosswind.
- Any claim about a measured body.
