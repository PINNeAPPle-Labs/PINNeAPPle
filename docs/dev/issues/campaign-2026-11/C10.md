## Summary

A member that assumes one configuration when hot and another when cold, executing a mechanical task with no motor: a clamp that grips, a lock that releases. The experiment designs the layer stack and the thermal profile for a target stroke and force, and validates against the classical bimetal curvature formula before any shape-memory claim is made — so the physics is anchored in a closed form rather than in a constitutive model with free parameters.

## Question

For a target stroke and blocking force, what layer geometry and thermal excursion minimise the required temperature change, and does the resulting design stay within the material's elastic limits over repeated cycles?

## Why run it

- It is a passive actuator: no power to hold a position, no controller, no failure mode in electronics — a real engineering option for valves, vents and latches.
- The bimetal case has a closed-form solution, so the model is verifiable end to end.
- The design question (stroke versus force versus temperature) is genuinely three-way, which makes it a case where optimisation produces a trade rather than a single answer.

## Physical model

**Thermoelasticity.** Two or more bonded layers with different thermal expansion coefficients; curvature from the classical formula `κ = 6 Δα ΔT (1+m)² / (3(1+m)² + (1+mn)(m² + 1/(mn)))` with `m` the thickness ratio and `n` the modulus ratio, plus the axial force that appears when the member is constrained.

**Shape memory (second stage).** For a shape-memory alloy, a transformation-strain constitutive law with the transformation temperatures and the hysteresis band; this stage is added only after the bimetal case validates.

**Thermal.** A lumped or one-dimensional conduction model of the member in its environment (`thermal_channel_2d`, `energy_balance` for the boundary exchange), giving the time response.

**Deliberately out of scope:** fatigue of the transformation, oxidation and creep, and coupled convection in a full 3-D environment.

## Geometry and inputs

Layer materials and thicknesses, member length and cross-section, the cold and hot temperatures, and the mechanical boundary conditions (free, pinned, or constrained for blocking force). Targets: a stroke of a stated magnitude at a stated blocking force for each of three applications (vent flap, latch, clamp).

## What is missing (the work)

1. **A layered thermoelastic element** with the transformation-strain extension, since no existing element carries eigenstrain from a thermal mismatch across layers.
2. **The bimetal closed-form module** and its comparison harness — the validation reference.
3. **A stroke-and-blocking-force evaluator**: free deflection and the force at zero displacement, computed from the same model.
4. **A thermal response model** tying the environment to the member's temperature versus time, so the actuation time is a result rather than an assumption.
5. **A cycle-limit check**: strain range against the material's allowable, for the stated number of cycles.

## Experiment protocol

1. Validate the layered element against the bimetal formula across a range of thickness and modulus ratios; report the relative error surface.
2. For each application target, optimise the layer geometry and material for minimum temperature excursion subject to stroke and blocking-force requirements.
3. Compute the actuation time from the thermal model for a stated environment and report it per design.
4. Check the strain range over a cycle against the material limit; report the designs that fail.
5. Compute budget: minutes per design evaluation; hundreds of evaluations per target — order 10^1 CPU-hours.

## Baselines, references and verification

- Bimetal closed form as the primary reference.
- A hand-selected commercial bimetal stack (published properties) as the practical baseline.
- Energy balance across the cycle: heat supplied must equal the work plus the losses in the stated model.
- Strain-limit check per material.

## Metrics

- Free stroke, mm, and blocking force, N, per design.
- Required temperature excursion, K, and actuation time, s.
- Strain range against the allowable, dimensionless utilisation.
- Work output per unit heat input, dimensionless efficiency.
- Model error against the bimetal closed form, per cent.

## Deliverables

- The layered thermoelastic element and the closed-form comparison.
- Optimised designs for the three applications, with their trade curves (stroke versus force versus temperature).
- The thermal response curves and actuation times.
- An animation of one member cycling, from the campaign video pipeline.

## Acceptance criteria

- The layered element reproduces the bimetal formula within the stated tolerance across the parameter range reported.
- Each optimised design meets its stroke and blocking-force target, and its temperature excursion stays within the stated limit.
- Strain range is below the material allowable for the stated cycle count, or the design is reported as failing.
- Actuation time is reported from the thermal model, not assumed.
- The energy balance closes within the stated tolerance.

## Realizability and evidence class

`PLAUSIBILITY_CLASS`: `established` — bimetals and shape-memory actuators are commercial. Axes: `mathematical_consistency` (closed-form comparison, energy balance), `numerical_stability` (mesh convergence), `physical_plausibility` (strain limits, published material data), `uncertainty_sensitivity` (material property spread). `experimental_validation` stays not assessed without a bench; published actuator data could fill it and should be used where a comparable device exists. `technological_feasibility` is partly filled by the cycle and strain checks.

## Risks and failure modes

1. **A shape-memory model with free parameters that fit anything.** Mitigation: bimetal validation first; the SMA stage is reported separately and never as the validated result.
2. **Blocking force computed at a state the material cannot reach.** Mitigation: the transformation completion temperature is an input, not a result.
3. **Thermal response assumed instantaneous.** Mitigation: the conduction model supplies the actuation time and it is reported.
4. **Fatigue ignored.** Mitigation: the cycle count and strain range are reported together; the omission is named.
5. **Efficiency overstated** by ignoring the heat lost to the environment. Mitigation: energy balance is a check, not a remark.

## Out of scope

- Material selection beyond the stated shortlist, and heat treatment.
- Control electronics and sensors.
- Manufacturing tolerances and joining of the layers.
