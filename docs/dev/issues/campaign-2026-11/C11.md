## Summary

Active particles that convert energy into motion, with alignment and noise: the experiment maps the order–disorder transition, the clustering regime and the collapse regime as a function of density, noise and alignment strength, and compares the phase diagram with the known Vicsek-style results. The deliverable is the phase diagram with error bars, not a swirling particle render — the render is what comes out of it.

## Question

Where exactly are the boundaries between the ordered, clustered and disordered regimes for this active-particle model, and how do the measured critical noise and the order-parameter exponent compare with the published values for the same model?

## Why run it

- Active matter is where the library's particle machinery can produce a result with a literature reference, which makes it the cheapest credible experiment in the programmable-matter series.
- A phase diagram with uncertainties is reusable: every later claim about "self-organising matter" can be checked against it.
- It exercises the UQ and statistics machinery on a stochastic system, which is the part most demonstrations skip.

## Physical model

**Dynamics.** Particles move at constant speed with a heading updated by the average heading of neighbours within a radius plus a noise term of amplitude `η`; position updates follow from the heading. The standard update is used, with the order parameter `φ = |Σ v_i| / (N v)`.

**Control parameters.** Density `ρ`, noise `η`, alignment radius `r`, and speed; the ratio of radius to mean spacing is the quantity that governs the transition, so the sweep is stated in those terms.

**Measured quantities.** Order parameter versus noise (the transition curve), the Binder cumulant for locating the transition, the susceptibility peak, and the cluster-size distribution in the clustering regime.

**Deliberately out of scope:** hydrodynamic (continuum) descriptions, inertia, and any specific biological system; the model is the minimal Vicsek-type one.

## Geometry and inputs

A two-dimensional periodic box of stated size with `N` particles at a stated density; three sizes are run to check finite-size behaviour. Initial conditions are random positions and headings with a recorded seed.

## What is missing (the work)

1. **The active-particle model**: heading update, speed, periodic boundaries, neighbour averaging — differentiable if a surrogate is to be trained on it.
2. **The phase-diagram driver**: sweep over density and noise, with equilibration and sampling windows defined in advance and recorded.
3. **Estimators**: order parameter, Binder cumulant, susceptibility and cluster-size distribution, each with a bootstrap interval.
4. **A finite-size study**: three system sizes, so a critical exponent is estimated rather than asserted.
5. **A comparison harness against a published set of transition values** for the same model, with the disagreement reported.

## Experiment protocol

1. Validate the neighbour search and the periodic wrapping against analytic particle counts at known densities.
2. Equilibrate, then sample: define the equilibration time from the order-parameter autocorrelation and record it.
3. Sweep noise at fixed density (20–30 values), then density at fixed noise; repeat for three sizes.
4. Estimate the transition noise from the Binder-cumulant crossing and from the susceptibility peak; report both and their difference.
5. Fit the cluster-size distribution in the clustering regime and report the exponent with its interval.
6. Compute budget: minutes per run at these sizes; the sweep is thousands of runs — order 10^1–10^2 CPU-hours.

## Baselines, references and verification

- Published transition values for the same minimal model, as the comparison target; the difference is the result.
- The mean-field prediction for the order parameter as a coarse reference.
- Stability of the statistics: results must not depend on the seed beyond the reported intervals (repeated seeds reported).
- Conservation checks: particle count and periodic momentum bookkeeping.

## Metrics

- Order parameter `φ`, dimensionless, versus noise `η`, with intervals.
- Critical noise `η_c` from two independent estimators, and their difference.
- Susceptibility peak height and position.
- Cluster-size distribution exponent, dimensionless, with its interval.
- Equilibration and sampling times, in steps, recorded per run.

## Deliverables

- The model and estimators with tests.
- The phase diagrams (density–noise) for three sizes, as data and figures.
- The finite-size analysis and the estimated critical values.
- A particle render at one state on each side of the transition, from the campaign video pipeline, with the order parameter printed on the frame.

## Acceptance criteria

- The neighbour search and periodic-wrap particle counts match the analytic values.
- Every reported point carries an interval from repeated seeds or bootstrap, and the sweep is reproducible from the recorded seeds.
- `η_c` estimated by two methods agrees within the stated tolerance, or the disagreement is reported as a finding.
- The finite-size trend is measured, not assumed; no exponent is reported from a single size.
- The comparison with published values states the model variant precisely enough that the difference can be attributed.

## Realizability and evidence class

`PLAUSIBILITY_CLASS`: `established` — this is a standard model with published results. Axes: `mathematical_consistency` (particle-count checks), `numerical_stability` (seed and size convergence), `physical_plausibility` (known transition behaviour), `uncertainty_sensitivity` (intervals on every metric). `experimental_validation` is not assessed (the reference is a numerical literature value, not a measurement) and the report says so; `technological_feasibility` is not assessed.

## Risks and failure modes

1. **Equilibration guessed**, producing a transition shifted by a transient. Mitigation: equilibration time from the autocorrelation, recorded per run.
2. **One seed per point**, making the diagram noise. Mitigation: repeated seeds and bootstrap intervals as acceptance criteria.
3. **The comparison uses a different model variant** (different boundary or update order). Mitigation: state the variant exactly and cite the matching study.
4. **A striking render presented as the result.** Mitigation: the diagram is the deliverable; the render carries the measured order parameter on the frame.
5. **Density and radius conflated**, so the sweep passes through different effective regimes. Mitigation: report the radius-to-spacing ratio per point.

## Out of scope

- Continuum/hydrodynamic theories of active matter.
- Biological modelling of specific organisms.
- Three-dimensional active matter in this first campaign.
