## Summary

A coarse-grained molecular assembly closes around a payload and releases it when the interaction parameters change. The experiment computes the free-energy difference between the open and closed states, so the preference for one over the other is a number with an error bar rather than an observation that the cage "looked closed". It is deliberately coarse-grained: beads with bonded and non-bonded interactions, thermostatted, with the scope limit stated at the top.

## Question

For a stated cage geometry and interaction set, what is the free-energy difference between the open and the closed state, and how does the closing preference depend on the payload size and on the interaction strength?

## Why run it

- Free energy is the quantity that decides whether a molecular cage works, and it is the quantity a simulation can actually deliver, unlike a picture of a closing cage.
- The repository already has a differentiable Lennard-Jones integrator, so the marginal cost is the free-energy machinery — a reusable capability for any molecular or mesoscale problem.
- It is the honest boundary case of the whole programmable-matter series: what can be computed at this level of description, and what needs atomistic or quantum methods that this library does not have.

## Physical model

**Interactions.** Bonded beads through harmonic bonds and angle terms; non-bonded through Lennard-Jones with per-species parameters; the payload is a single large bead or a small rigid cluster. Implicit solvent is a design choice: either a Langevin thermostat with a friction that stands in for the solvent, or no solvent at all with the caveat stated.

**Ensemble.** NVT with Langevin dynamics for the sampling, and the free energy from a biased-sampling method — umbrella sampling with the weighted histogram analysis, or thermodynamic integration along a coupling parameter that switches the cage–payload interaction.

**Reaction coordinate.** The distance or the number of contacts between the cage arms and the payload, chosen so that the open and the closed basins are separated.

**Deliberately out of scope:** explicit water, electrostatics beyond a Debye–Hückel correction, hydrogen bonding, and any specific nucleic-acid or protein chemistry. This model cannot support a claim about a named molecular structure, and the issue says so.

## Geometry and inputs

A cage of `n` arms (6 to 12) arranged on a sphere, each arm a chain of beads with a hinge at the base; the payload radius, the arm length and the interaction strengths are the design variables. Inputs: bead mass and size, bond stiffness, temperature, thermostat friction, and the coupling path for the free-energy calculation.

## Solver and engine

- Integrator: `pinneapple_simulation.particle_dynamics.molecular_dynamics` (Lennard-Jones, velocity Verlet, differentiable in PyTorch).
- Thermostat and biasing: to be added — a Langevin thermostat and the umbrella/thermodynamic-integration driver.
- Analysis: `pinneapple_analysis` for the histograms and the free-energy reconstruction; `pinneapple_analysis.uncertainty` for the error bars.
- Recording: `pinneapple_lab.experiment`.

## What already exists in PINNeAPPle (verified)

- `pinneapple_simulation.particle_dynamics.molecular_dynamics`: Lennard-Jones pair potential, analytic forces, velocity-Verlet integration, differentiable, with the package's differentiable-physics style.
- `pinneapple_simulation.particle_dynamics.particles`, `rigid_body`, `mpm` for the other particle back-ends.
- `pinneapple_analysis.uncertainty` (resampling, intervals) and `pinneapple_analysis.verification.convergence` for the statistics.
- `pinneapple_check` for the energy and boundary checks; `pinneapple_lab` for the record.
- `pinneapple_twin3d`/`pinneapple_blender` for the cage rendering.

## What is missing (the work)

1. **A thermostat**: Langevin or Nosé–Hoover, verified against the Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution of velocities and the correct diffusion behaviour.
2. **Bonded and angular terms** in the molecular-dynamics module, since today it provides non-bonded interactions only.
3. **A free-energy driver**: umbrella windows along a chosen reaction coordinate with WHAM, or thermodynamic integration along a coupling parameter, with the overlap diagnostics that decide whether the result is trustworthy.
4. **A sampler-quality check**: the effective sample size and the autocorrelation time per window, so an unconverged window is visible.
5. **A coarse-graining disclaimer module**: a recorded statement in the result manifest of what this level of description cannot represent.

## Experiment protocol

1. Validate the thermostat: velocity distribution, temperature stability, and the diffusion coefficient of a single bead in the friction-dominated limit.
2. Validate the free-energy machinery on a system with a known answer — for example a particle in a harmonic well or the ideal-gas excess of a hard-sphere pair — before applying it to the cage.
3. For each cage design, run the umbrella windows along the reaction coordinate with stated window spacing, and reconstruct the free-energy profile; report the overlap between neighbouring windows.
4. Sweep payload radius and the cage–payload interaction strength; report the closing free energy as a function of both.
5. Compute budget: the dominant cost is sampling — order 10^2–10^3 CPU-hours for the sweep, with individual windows minutes to hours.

## Baselines, references and verification

- The harmonic-well or hard-sphere free-energy check as the machinery validation.
- A cage with no attraction to the payload (interaction set to zero) as the null case: the closed and open states must then be degenerate within the error bar.
- Overlap diagnostics between umbrella windows; a gap means the profile is not trustworthy and must be re-run.
- Energy conservation in the NVE limit (thermostat off) as an integrator check.

## Metrics

- Free-energy difference closed minus open, in `kT`, with its error bar and the method used.
- Cage–payload contact number at each basin, dimensionless.
- Payload radius and interaction strength at which the preference reverses, with intervals.
- Autocorrelation time per window and the overlap between neighbouring windows, dimensionless.
- Temperature stability across the run, K.

## Deliverables

- The thermostat, bonded terms and free-energy driver, with tests.
- The validation cases and their error reports.
- The free-energy surfaces for the cage designs, and the closing map over payload radius and interaction strength.
- A render of the open and closed states, from the campaign video pipeline, with the free-energy profile beside it.
- A statement, in the result manifest, of the level of description and what it cannot represent.

## Acceptance criteria

- The thermostat reproduces the expected velocity distribution and the diffusion behaviour within the stated tolerance.
- The free-energy machinery reproduces the known test case within a stated error, before any cage result is reported.
- Every reported free-energy difference carries an error bar and passes the window-overlap criterion; profiles failing overlap are not reported.
- The zero-interaction null case is degenerate within its error bar.
- No result is phrased as a claim about a specific molecular structure; the level-of-description statement is present in every deliverable.

## Realizability and evidence class

`PLAUSIBILITY_CLASS`: `hypothetical` for the cage as a functional device; `established` as a coarse-grained simulation exercise. Axes: `mathematical_consistency` (integrator energy conservation, known-answer test), `numerical_stability` (window overlap, autocorrelation, seed repeats), `physical_plausibility` (Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution, degeneracy of the null case), `uncertainty_sensitivity` (error bars on the free energy). `experimental_validation` stays not assessed; `technological_feasibility` stays not assessed, and the issue is explicit that atomistic or quantum methods, which this library does not provide, would be required to say anything about a real molecule.

## Risks and failure modes

1. **A free-energy profile from non-overlapping windows** looks like a result and is not. Mitigation: overlap is an acceptance criterion.
2. **Poor sampling of the closed state**, biasing the answer toward the open one. Mitigation: effective sample size per basin reported.
3. **The reaction coordinate misses the transition path.** Mitigation: at least one alternative coordinate is tried and the profiles compared.
4. **Implicit solvent assumed to be harmless.** Mitigation: the friction and its justification are stated, and the no-solvent limit is run for comparison.
5. **The result is read as a statement about a real molecule.** Mitigation: the level-of-description statement travels with the result into every figure and table.

## Out of scope

- Atomistic or ab-initio treatment; explicit water; electrostatics beyond a mean-field correction.
- Synthesis, targeting and biological delivery.
- Kinetic rates of opening and closing (the experiment computes thermodynamics only).
