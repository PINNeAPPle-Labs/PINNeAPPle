## Summary

A few thousand mesoscale grains (0.5 mm to 5 mm equivalent diameter, a design choice, not atoms) with controlled shape and Coulomb friction are driven from a loose poured state into a bridge that carries a specified load, then re-driven into an arch when the load path reverses. Actuation is base vibration or a magnetic body force on functionalized grains. The claim is narrow: for this grain population and actuator, does one open-loop schedule reach a load-bearing arrangement often enough to matter, and what defects appear when it fails.

## Question

For fixed grain shape, friction and polydispersity, does a single open-loop actuation schedule reach a bridge carrying the target load in at least 50 % of sealed seeds, and reach a distinct arch after the load path reverses?

## Why run it

Granular jamming is already used industrially — vacuum bags, variable-stiffness grippers — but the actuation is tuned on a bench by trial. A reproducible pile-to-structure model answers three questions before anyone builds a rig: whether the transition exists at all for a given shape and friction, which regions of the actuation space produce a structure versus a heap, and which defect (missing crown grains, over-coordinated clusters, porosity above percolation) each actuation parameter causes. The force network can be drawn on the same frame as the assembly, which is what makes the animation informative rather than decorative.

## Physical model

Rigid-body dynamics with a Hertz–Mindlin contact: normal spring-dashpot `F_n = (4/3) E* sqrt(R*) delta_n^(3/2) + c_n delta_n_dot`, tangential spring with history capped by `|F_t| <= mu F_n`, plus a rolling-resistance torque standing in for angularity. Actuation is a prescribed floor acceleration `A omega^2 sin(omega t)`, or a body force `chi V grad|B|^2 / (2 mu_0)` on grains with susceptibility chi.

Five dimensionless groups fix the regime: shaking intensity `Gamma = A omega^2 / g` (kept in 1 to 5, above which grains eject); inertial number `I = gamma_dot d sqrt(rho_s/p)`, crossing from quasi-static (I < 1e-3) to inertial inside the event; the Coulomb ratio mu, which decides whether a jammed state is reachable; restitution e, which sets how fast the assembly dies after the drive stops; granular Bond number `Bog = F_ext/(m g)`, which says whether the field competes with gravity at grain scale. Jamming is sought at packing fraction above 0.60 against random close packing near 0.64.

Left out: grain breakage, true polyhedral geometry, humidity and triboelectric charging beyond the imposed field. Below roughly 100 microns van der Waals cohesion dominates and this contact law is the wrong model; the grain size is floored there for that reason.

## Geometry and inputs

The apparatus is parametric: a box 0.30 x 0.10 x 0.15 m with support piers at x = 0.02 and 0.28 m, an actuable floor, and two load pads. Grains are monodisperse spheres, 8-bead rigid clumps for angularity, or a bidisperse 1:1.4 mixture that suppresses crystallization. Targets are regions, not imported meshes: a bridge as a spanning layer of prescribed thickness between the piers, an arch as a curved band with a given rise. Inputs are the contact-parameter table and the actuation schedule; no experimental dataset is required and none is used.

## Solver and engine

No discrete-element solver exists in PINNeAPPle, and that is the central fact of this issue. `pinneapple_simulation.particle_dynamics.mpm` (`MPMSimulator`) is a material-point method — a continuum on a background grid — and cannot resolve inter-grain force chains. `rigid_body` (`RigidBody`, `RigidBodySystem`) supplies rigid integration but has no contact model.

Recommended route: run the reference through the verified external bridges, `pinneapple_simulation.external_solvers.geotaichi` or `pinneapple_simulation.external_solvers.genesis`, and write the actuation and analysis in-process. Building an in-process Hertz–Mindlin DEM on `rigid_body` plus `numerical_solvers.neighbors.build_neighbor_list` is the alternative, and it is a quarter's work; the value of this issue is the actuation map and defect taxonomy, not a second contact implementation. The mechanical check is separate either way: freeze the grain positions and solve with `numerical_solvers.solid_fem.SolidFEM`.

## What is missing (the work)

1. A DEM contact model — Hertz–Mindlin normal, Coulomb-capped tangential history, rolling resistance, documented parameter table. New `pinneapple_simulation/particle_dynamics/dem.py`.
2. Grain-shape representation: rigid sphere clumps, optionally superquadric contact. `pinneapple_simulation/particle_dynamics/grain_shapes.py`.
3. A validated external-DEM wrapper: script generation, parameter provenance, and a hard check that returned frames match the requested grain count and snapshot times. `pinneapple_simulation/particle_dynamics/dem_bridge.py`.
4. Actuator models — base excitation and magnetic body force, both driven by a named schedule. `pinneapple_simulation/particle_dynamics/actuation.py`.
5. A rigidity and capacity metric for a grain assembly: coordination analysis, force-network extraction, support-reaction capacity, and a rigid-versus-flowing decision. `pinneapple_analysis/granular/capacity.py`. `pinneapple_design.bricks.statics.analyse_assembly` solves the right shape of problem (an equilibrium LP over a joint network) but on bricks with stud joints; it does not transfer unmodified.
6. An open-loop schedule optimizer over Gamma, frequency, duty cycle and field strength, plus the seed-ensemble driver.
7. Defect metrics: missing grains against the target region, over-coordinated clusters, porosity field, per-seed pass/fail.

## Experiment protocol

Matrix: N in {2 000, 8 000, 32 000}; shape in {spheres, clumps}; mu in {0.2, 0.4, 0.6}; e = 0.3 fixed; polydispersity in {monodisperse, bidisperse}; drive in {vibration, field}; Gamma in {1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 3.0} or Bog in {0.3, 1.0, 3.0}. Full factorial gives 288 cases, each with 8 sealed seeds. Per case: pour and settle, record initial packing fraction; drive for 2.0 s; settle 1.0 s; freeze, extract the contact network, solve capacity; load to failure; reverse the load path and repeat for the arch.

Time step comes from the Rayleigh wave speed of the contact stiffness with at least 20 steps per contact oscillation; a settled state whose energy drifts more than 2 % is rejected. One case is repeated at 2x and 4x grain count with geometry scaled to hold packing fraction constant, and the capacity is reported only with a GCI below 10 %. Budget: about 2 000 CPU-hours for the matrix at N = 8 000. Logged per seed: packing fraction per phase, coordination histogram, contact-force distribution, support reaction, peak load, failure mechanism, schedule hash.

## Baselines, references and verification

Contact law first: a single grain on an incline slips at `tan theta = mu` to within 2 %; two-grain collision reproduces the requested restitution; a free pour reaches a repose angle within 3 degrees. The assembly is checked against the Beverloo discharge correlation (exponent near 2.5 against orifice diameter) and a Janssen pressure saturation in a silo. Once frozen, the grain assembly and the same geometry as an effective continuum solved by `SolidFEM` must agree in capacity within a factor of two; a larger gap is reported as a result.

Baselines to beat: the same grains poured with no actuation, loaded identically; a glued uniform lattice of the same count and bounding box; a hand-placed catenary arch of the same population. The result is interesting only if the actuated assembly beats the unactuated pour clearly and approaches the hand-placed arch within a stated fraction.

## Metrics

- phi: packing fraction, grain volume over occupied convex volume, dimensionless.
- Z: mean coordination number, contacts per grain, with standard deviation.
- F_c: peak support reaction before failure, N, from the capacity solve.
- delta: maximum displacement between unloaded and loaded states at design load, mm.
- eta: fraction of the target region volume occupied by grains, dimensionless.
- Anisotropy: deviatoric part of the fabric tensor over its trace, dimensionless.
- p: porosity, one minus local packing fraction on a 5 mm grid.
- Success rate over 8 sealed seeds with a Wilson interval.

## Deliverables

`manifest.json` and `metrics.csv` (288 cases x 8 seeds, about 3 MB). Particle snapshots for 12 representative cases as compressed `.npz`, about 400 MB; other cases retained by hash. Figures: actuation maps colored by success rate with intervals, coordination histograms at the three phases, log-scale contact-force distribution with the exponential tail marked, capacity-versus-phi plot. One 20 s video of a bridge forming and re-forming as an arch through `pinneapple_twin3d.scene`, force network colored by magnitude. A report stating the success rate, the actuation region where nothing happens, and the defect taxonomy.

## Acceptance criteria

- Incline-slip and restitution checks pass before any assembly result is reported.
- Beverloo exponent recovered within 15 % of 2.5.
- Capacity reported with a `mesh_independence_study` GCI under 10 %.
- Best schedule reaches eta above 0.70 and F_c above 50 N in at least 4 of 8 seeds, and an arch with eta above 0.60 after reversal in at least 3 of 8.
- Actuated capacity exceeds the unactuated pour baseline in at least 6 of 8 seeds, with the ratio reported where it does not.
- Every case in the matrix has a recorded outcome, including those producing no structure.

## Realizability and evidence class

Filled: mathematical_consistency (contact law and capacity solve checked against closed forms and conserved quantities); numerical_stability (time step, energy drift, grain-count convergence measured); physical_plausibility (jamming, force chains and Beverloo discharge are established laboratory phenomena and the model sits inside their regime). Partially filled: uncertainty_sensitivity — the seed ensemble gives a binomial success rate, but contact parameters are held fixed, so parameter uncertainty is not covered.

NOT ASSESSED: experimental_validation (no grains were poured, no capacity measured; schedules are hypotheses) and technological_feasibility beyond a scale argument — magnetic actuation of 1 mm grains at Bond number of order one is within reach of coil hardware, but nothing here speaks to a manufacturable material, and nothing to nanomaterials.

PLAUSIBILITY_CLASS today: extrapolated. The phenomenon is established at the modeled scale; the system claim — one open-loop schedule reliably constructs and re-constructs load-bearing granular structures — is an extrapolation. Moving to established requires a bench rig with the same population reproducing the predicted success rate and capacity.

## Risks and failure modes

1. The external bridge returns frames that are plausible but not the requested case. Mitigation: provenance record plus hard checks on grain count and snapshot times before analysis.
2. The capacity LP will report a load for almost any configuration, including one that would collapse. Mitigation: report capacity only for assemblies passing a rigidity criterion and a stability eigen-check, and state both.
3. Clumps and perfect spheres may be what makes the schedule work. Mitigation: shape is an axis and success rate is reported per shape, never pooled.
4. Eight seeds at a true rate near 0.5 give a wide interval, so a win may be noise. Mitigation: pre-registered criterion, Wilson intervals, and a difference smaller than the interval treated as no difference.
5. The 100 micron floor is easy to forget when results are described. Mitigation: the size range appears in the summary, the metric table and the figure captions.

## Out of scope

- Any nanoscale or molecular claim; the contact law is invalid below roughly 100 microns.
- Grain breakage, attrition and wear.
- Closed-loop control of the actuation; only open-loop schedules are tested.
- Manufacture of grains with the assumed susceptibility or friction; those are design inputs.
