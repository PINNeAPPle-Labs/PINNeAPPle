## Summary

A truss of order 10^2 mesoscale members, each a lattice unit rather than an atom, is loaded, loses members, and is repaired by mobile units that migrate into the gap and re-form them. Comparing intact, damaged and repaired states on one load case yields recovered stiffness per unit of added mass and energy: a claim about a repair policy, not about a self-healing material.

## Question

On one load case, does re-forming a broken member recover 90 percent of intact stiffness under 10 percent added mass, and where does repair leave it weaker than the damage?

## Why run it

Repair can pull load into a neighbour near capacity, so repaired collapse load need not be monotone in repair amount; that non-monotonicity is the scientific content. `pp.futures.atlas()` lists "self-healing and adaptive structures" as `planned`, and this issue fills that gap.

## Physical model

Linear elastic truss, K u = f, with k_e = E A_e / L_e, assembled over the connectivity graph. Failure: |sigma_e| > sigma_y in tension, Euler load P_cr in compression; damage removes members under load to the collapse multiplier lambda_c. Repair restores a member with A' <= A and joint efficiency eta_j <= 1, at a mass and energy cost.

Deciding groups: slenderness L/r, indeterminacy from the Maxwell count, eta_j, repair-mass fraction mu. Valid for small strain, linear elasticity, quasi-static load; absent are fracture, fatigue, plasticity and any mechanism by which a unit re-forms a member.

## Geometry and inputs

Ground structure on a fixed node lattice: candidates are all node pairs within a radius; connectivity and repair order vary, node positions stay fixed so reachability stays meaningful. Topologies: an octet-truss block of 8x4x4 cells, a 6-cell cantilever, a 2 m span bridge, each with a clamped root and one point load at 0 and 45 degrees.

## Solver and engine

Member-level direct stiffness assembly, to be built, cross-checked by `numerical_solvers.solid_fem` on the voxelized repaired geometry and by `external_solvers.calculix` for an independent body-fitted solve. Damage-order baselines reuse `bricks.progressive_failure`.

## What already exists in PINNeAPPle (verified)

- `pinneapple_design.bricks`: `Assembly`, `analyse_assembly`, `from_assembly(asm, loads, broken=)`, `progressive_failure`, `bricks.fea.solve` — damaged-state equilibrium exists, for brick assemblies.
- `numerical_solvers.solid_fem` (`SolidFEM`, `von_mises`), `plane_stress_fem.solve_plane_stress`, `elasticity3d_fdm.solve_static_elasticity_3d`.
- `pinneapple_design.topology`: `optimize`, `Compliance2D`, `Q4Grid`; geometry for the STL check from `pinneapple_design.geometry.gen`.
- `pinneapple_lab.experiment`, `pp.futures.plausibility.assess`, `pinneapple_analysis.verification.convergence`.

## What is missing (the work)

1. Member-level truss solver with damage flags and collapse bisection — `pinneapple_simulation/structure/truss.py`.
2. Repair model — `pinneapple_simulation/structure/repair.py`: units as agents on the graph, migration time, waiting queue, rebuild order.
3. Joint-efficiency model, so a re-formed member is weaker than an original.
4. Reachability check: can a unit reach the gap while the structure is loaded.
5. Recovery metrics that price mass and energy, plus a detector for the degrading case.

## Experiment protocol

3 topologies x 3 damage levels (1, 2, 4 members) x 3 policies (targeted, greedy by strain, random at equal budget) x 2 load directions = 54 cases, seeds 0/1/2, damage chosen by utilisation rank. Two-mesh checks with `convergence.mesh_independence_study`, target GCI below 5 percent on tip compliance. Budget: order 10 CPU-hours member-level, order 10^2 with CalculiX.

## Baselines, references and verification

A determinate 3-bar truss has closed-form stiffness and collapse load, reproduced to 1e-8 before any repair run. Maxwell counting checks determinacy; energy balance checks each solve. Baselines: no repair; 15 percent over-provisioning; the continuum optimum at equal mass.

## Metrics

- k_r = K_repaired / K_intact and sigma_r = lambda_c,repaired / lambda_c,intact, dimensionless.
- Recovery per cost = (k_r - k_damaged) / (m_added + c_e E_repair), 1/kg at a stated rate c_e.
- n_over = members reaching capacity during the repair-disturbed load; t_rep = unit-steps to 0.9 k_r.

## Deliverables

Per-case JSON manifest, roll-up CSV, the recovery table, k_r and sigma_r against damage per policy, recovery per cost against over-provisioning, and a migration render from `pinneapple_twin3d.scene`.

## Acceptance criteria

- 3-bar closed form reproduced to 1e-8; GCI on repaired-state compliance below 5 percent.
- Intact, damaged and repaired solved on the same mesh, load case and solver.
- At least one case where repair lowers lambda_c below the damaged value; if none occurs in 54 cases, that null result is the finding.
- Every number reproduces from its manifest at the same seed.

## Realizability and evidence class

Fills `mathematical_consistency`, `numerical_stability`, `physical_plausibility`; leaves `experimental_validation`, `technological_feasibility` and `uncertainty_sensitivity` not assessed. `PLAUSIBILITY_CLASS` today: `extrapolated` — 10^2 members under linear elasticity with no chemistry is no evidence that a physical material heals. `established` needs a bench truss measured before and after a performed repair.

## Risks and failure modes

1. Free placement flatters repair. Mitigation: mobility constraint, migration time, energy budget.
2. The member model hides joints. Mitigation: eta_j sweep, continuum cross-check.
3. The metric is chosen green. Mitigation: cost and mass recovery both reported.
4. Damage lands on an unloaded member. Mitigation: damage by utilisation rank.
5. Model error swamps the repair effect. Mitigation: repair-without-damage control.

## Out of scope

- Any physical realization of the re-forming unit or its binding chemistry.
- Fracture, fatigue, creep, plasticity.
- Atomistic modeling; claims of a manufacturable self-healing nanomaterial.
