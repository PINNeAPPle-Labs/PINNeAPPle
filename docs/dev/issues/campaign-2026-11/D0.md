## Summary

This is the index and the protocol for a campaign of reproducible Physics-AI demonstrations: one series of experiments in which geometry is generated by an algorithm, the physics is solved by a real solver, something is optimised or altered, and the result is published with the evidence that says what it does and does not prove. The campaign's proposition is narrow: a demonstration is an experiment, not an animation, and the difference is a protocol — a sealed case, a numbered lineage, a convergence check, a baseline, and a written statement of the claim the result supports.

The protocol is not invented here. It is the one the library already runs on: `pp.futures` scores a proposal on six evidence axes and issues two independent verdicts, `pinneapple_lab` records an experiment with a manifest, a grade and a seal, and `pp.science` keeps the atlas of which areas are available, partial or planned. This issue defines how the campaign uses them, so that forty speculative renders and one forecast cannot borrow each other's credibility.

## Question

Can a series of demonstrations, spanning a sealed operational forecast and technologies that do not exist, share one evidence protocol strict enough that a reader can tell, from the issue and its artefacts alone, which claims are supported by a solver, which by a measurement, and which by neither?

## Why run it

- Without a shared protocol, the speculative renders contaminate the credible work: an audience that has seen a levitating platform and a sealed forecast side by side will price both at the level of the weaker one.
- The library already has the machinery, and using it consistently turns the campaign into a small body of methodological evidence — on where each model family helps and where it does not — instead of a portfolio of pictures.
- It gives every issue the same deliverable list, so a demonstration can be audited when the person who ran it is no longer around.

## Physical model

There is no governing equation at this level; the protocol is about what a computation may claim. It fixes four things.

**The five stages.** Every experiment states its promise, separates the physics that is known from the physics that is assumed, builds and runs the model, pushes it to the limit, and maps what is missing. These are the `STAGES` of `pinneapple_futures.schema` (`speculation`, `formalization`, `modeling`, `experimentation`, `assessment`) and the issues use those names.

**The epistemic class.** Every claim carries a `PLAUSIBILITY_CLASS` of `established`, `extrapolated`, `hypothetical` or `fictional`, and every input carries a `PROVENANCE_KIND` of `constant`, `measured`, `design_input`, `derived` or `hypothesis`. A passing simulation never upgrades the class; a hypothetical system simulated perfectly is still hypothetical physics.

**The six evidence axes.** `mathematical_consistency`, `numerical_stability`, `physical_plausibility`, `experimental_validation`, `technological_feasibility`, `uncertainty_sensitivity`. A simulation fills the first three and parts of the sixth. It cannot fill `experimental_validation` (needs a bench or published data) or `technological_feasibility` (needs a build), and the campaign rule is that those two stay not assessed in any pure-simulation issue, however convincing the render.

**The two verdicts.** Physics: `SUPPORTED`, `CONDITIONAL`, `CONTRADICTED`, `NOT ASSESSED`. Engineering: `FEASIBLE`, `CONDITIONAL`, `NOT FEASIBLE`, `NOT ASSESSED`. They are reported separately and never averaged into a single number.

## Geometry and inputs

Not applicable at the campaign level; each issue generates its own. What this issue does fix is the common input discipline: every case records the geometry generator and its seed, the parameter set, the solver and version, the input hashes, and the git commit. A case that cannot be rebuilt from that record is not a case.

## Solver and engine

The campaign uses the library as it is: `pp.solve`/`pp.compare` for the physics, the `pinneapple_simulation` solver families and the external bridges (`external_solvers.openfoam`, `.calculix`, `.fenics`, `.geotaichi`, `.genesis`, `.turbosigner` where relevant), `pinneapple_design` for generation and optimisation, `pinneapple_neural` for the learned models, `pinneapple_check` for the physics checks, `pp.futures` for the assessment, `pinneapple_lab` for the record and the seal, and the rendering pipeline defined by the companion issue for the footage.

## What is missing (the work)

1. **A campaign manifest schema**: one file per experiment declaring id, series, promise, plausibility class per claim, the axes it intends to fill, its deliverables and its issue number. Proposed: `docs/experiments/campaign.json`, validated by a small script, mirroring the shape of `pinneapple_futures.Scenario`/`Proposal`.
2. **A campaign report generator**: read the manifests plus the `pinneapple_lab` results and emit one table — experiment, class, axes filled, verdicts, artefacts present or missing — so the whole series can be inspected at a glance and a missing artefact is visible rather than forgotten.
3. **A claim-linting rule**: a check, in the spirit of `pinneapple check`, that fails when an experiment's prose claims an axis it has no evidence for, or a verdict its evidence does not support. The rule set is small: no `experimental_validation` without a bench or a cited dataset; no `technological_feasibility` without an engineering check; no `SUPPORTED` on fewer than the required passing checks.
4. **A "what would have to be true" register**: for every speculative issue, the ranked list of what would move it up one plausibility class — material, actuator, energy, control, measurement — kept in one place so the campaign accumulates a research agenda rather than scattered caveats.
5. **The campaign table**: the index of all issues in this series, by number, with series, class and status. Maintained in this issue, updated when an experiment is added or closed.

## Experiment protocol

1. When an experiment starts, its issue is created from the campaign template and its row added to the campaign table here.
2. Its manifest is added with the promise, the class of each claim and the axes it intends to fill; the claim-lint rule runs in CI from the first commit, so a claim without evidence fails early rather than at publication.
3. The case is built and recorded through `pinneapple_lab.experiment`, with the metrics and checks that fill the intended axes. A result without a `check` call contributes nothing to any axis and the report shows it.
4. The assessment is produced by `pp.futures` or by `plausibility.assess` over the recorded checks — not typed by hand.
5. The artefacts (case, video, limit map, gap analysis, evidence level) are attached to the experiment manifest; the report generator marks the experiment complete only when all five are present.
6. When an experiment contradicts its own premise, that outcome is recorded as a result with the same standing as a success, and the class is updated rather than the claim being quietly dropped.

## Baselines, references and verification

- The protocol's own reference implementation is `pp.futures` and its guide; where this campaign deviates from it, the deviation and its reason are recorded in the manifest.
- The negative control for the whole campaign is the pair already committed in `pinneapple_worldmodel.physical_world`: an honest video and `corrupt(video, kind)` variants. Any verification method used to support a campaign claim must reject the corrupted variants — that test is the campaign's calibration, and it is run before any claim is published.

## Metrics

- Coverage: fraction of experiments with all five artefacts present; must reach 1.0 for the series to be called complete.
- Evidence density: number of axes filled per experiment, and the count with `experimental_validation` or `technological_feasibility` filled by a real bench or build (expected: zero for pure simulation, and that zero is the honest headline).
- Reproducibility: fraction of experiments whose case rebuilds from its manifest on a clean machine.
- Claim integrity: number of claims rejected by the lint rule after the first publication (target: zero; a non-zero count means the rule was added too late).
- Verdict distribution: how many experiments end `CONTRADICTED` or `NOT FEASIBLE` — a campaign that only ever reports success is not measuring anything.

## Deliverables

- `docs/experiments/campaign.json` plus its schema and validator.
- The report generator and the campaign table in this issue.
- The claim-linting rule and its tests, including a deliberately overclaiming fixture that must fail.
- The "what would have to be true" register, one entry per speculative issue.
- A short methods note, `docs/guides/campaign_protocol.md`, that states the protocol in the same terms as `docs/guides/futures_lab.md` and links to it rather than restating it.

## Acceptance criteria

- Every issue in the series appears in the campaign table with its series letter, plausibility class and issue number.
- The report generator produces the coverage and evidence-density tables from the committed manifests without manual editing.
- The lint rule rejects at least the fixture claims: "a simulation proves the device can be built", "the render validates the model", "the result agrees with experiment" with no dataset cited.
- The corrupted-video negative control is rejected by every verification method used anywhere in the campaign.
- No experiment reports `SUPPORTED` on the physics verdict without at least one `analytic` or `residual` check and one `convergence` check recorded in its manifest.
- The register contains, for every speculative issue, at least three ranked prerequisites, each with the discipline it belongs to (materials, actuators, energy, control, measurement, theory).

## Realizability and evidence class

This issue is about method, so its class is `established` and its evidence axes are `mathematical_consistency` (the lint rule is a decidable statement about the text and the recorded checks), `numerical_stability` (reproducibility of a case from its manifest) and `uncertainty_sensitivity` (the effect of the check thresholds on the verdicts). It cannot and does not claim `experimental_validation` or `technological_feasibility`. The point of the issue is precisely that those two cannot be manufactured by better prose.

## Risks and failure modes

1. **The protocol becomes paperwork.** Mitigation: every artefact it requires is generated, not written by hand; if a field must be typed, it is optional or removed.
2. **The lint rule is gamed** by rewording a claim. Mitigation: the rule keys on the recorded checks, not on adjectives, and the campaign report shows the axis scores rather than the prose.
3. **The speculative series borrows credibility from the credible one.** Mitigation: the campaign table and every issue header state the class, and the report's headline metric is the count of axes filled, not the count of experiments.
4. **Experiments are abandoned mid-way** and linger in the table. Mitigation: a status field with an explicit `abandoned` state and the reason, which counts against coverage until it is either finished or closed as abandoned.
5. **The register turns into a wish list.** Mitigation: each prerequisite must be a quantity with a target value and a discipline, not a technology name.

## Out of scope

- Hardware. The campaign builds nothing physical; bench work, if it ever happens, enters through `experimental_validation` as a separate issue.
- Publication and media strategy.
- A new epistemic framework; the campaign uses `pp.futures` as it exists and records deviations.
