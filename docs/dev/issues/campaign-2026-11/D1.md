## Summary

One fixed inventory of identical modular units is re-planned, without new parts, into a two-wheeled vehicle, a quadrotor frame and a serial manipulator arm. Unit mass and connector strength decide which configuration survives, and each assembly is checked against its own structural and mass/power budget. The claim it supports is a boundary in the module-count versus connector-strength plane, not a machine that morphs on command.

## Question

For one fixed inventory with stated connector tensile, shear and bending capacity, at what module count does each target stop meeting its structural and mass/power budget, and which target fails first?

## Why run it

It turns a fictional promise into a trade-off surface with numbers on the axes, and yields three reusable artifacts: a connector model with explicit capacity and stiffness, a collision-free planner, and a mass/power budget. D24 and D28 need that connector model to be real. The informative render is the configuration that fails.

## Physical model

A unit is a rigid cuboid of mass m with a connector patch transmitting normal force up to F_c, shear up to F_s and moment up to M_c. Assembly equilibrium is rigid-body statics per unit, interface forces inside the friction cone |f_t| <= mu f_n and below capacity. The arm is a chain of rigid links on rotational springs k_theta, so tip deflection follows delta ~ sum M L / k_theta, not a beam formula. Rotor lift uses momentum theory, P_hover = T^(3/2)/sqrt(2 rho A), disc loading T/A being the deciding group. Groups: connector utilization u = f/F_c, slenderness L/d, disc loading, power margin. Valid quasi-statically, small deformation, no fatigue. Left out: transformation dynamics, connector wear, pack derating, blade-element detail, ground effect, control bandwidth.

## Geometry and inputs

Targets are parametric, not drawn: `design.geometry.gen.sdf_shapes`, `primitives` and `domains3d` give the wheel pair, the four-armed hub and the serial chain with joint spheres, on the voxel grid `pinneapple_design.bricks.Assembly` already uses. Optimized: unit placement per target. Inputs: unit mass and side, F_c, F_s, M_c, k_theta, pack specific energy in Wh/kg and continuous power in W/kg, thrust per disc area.

## Solver and engine

Structural check is `pinneapple_design.bricks.statics.analyse_assembly`, with `capacity()` for load margin and `progressive_failure()` for the first joint to go, refined for the arm with `numerical_solvers.solid_fem`. Transformation replay is `particle_dynamics.rigid_body`. Rotors have no module: `design.aero.panel` and `vlm` are fixed-wing potential flow, so the hover check is momentum theory to be built, with one `external_solvers.openfoam` confirmation case.

## What already exists in PINNeAPPle (verified)

- `pinneapple_design.bricks`: `Assembly`, `analyse_assembly`, `capacity`, `progressive_failure`, and `JointModel` in `bricks.parts` at 0.98 N per stud with an assumed band.
- `numerical_solvers.solid_fem`: `box_mesh`, `SolidFEM`, `von_mises`; `design.topology.optimize` for member layout.
- `particle_dynamics.rigid_body`; `pinneapple_lab.experiment` for the manifest.

## What is missing (the work)

1. A connector model with bending and shear capacity and stiffness outside the brick catalogue, including the interaction the LP omits when `shear_n` is unbounded: `pinneapple_design/morph/connector.py`.
2. A planner producing a collision-free move sequence with no intermediate loss of support: `pinneapple_design/morph/planner.py`.
3. A rotor model with disc loading and power, `pinneapple_simulation/flight/rotor.py`; nothing in `design.aero` covers a rotor.
4. A mass/power budget closing all three targets from one inventory: `pinneapple_design/morph/budget.py`.
5. A cross-target driver reporting the failure count and the binding constraint.

## Experiment protocol

Matrix: N in {32, 64, 128, 256, 512}, three targets, two connector classes, five placement seeds, 150 cases. Per case: plan, verify with an independent collision checker, structural LP, budget, record the governing constraint. The LP returns a clean optimum or an explicit infeasibility certificate; planner timeouts are logged apart and never counted as infeasible. Budget 10^2 to 10^3 CPU-hours. Logged: N, target, connector class, seed, plan length, LP status, utilizations, payload and thrust margins, first failing constraint.

## Baselines, references and verification

Baselines: a uniform lattice of equal mass, a welded-perfect connector as structural upper bound, a hand-placed configuration per target. Verification: the LP against closed-form cantilever and simply supported beam; the planner against exhaustive search at N <= 32; hover against the analytic momentum value; the arm solid check at 2x refinement, target under 5 percent change in peak stress.

## Metrics

N*, module count at failure, dimensionless. u_max = max f/F_c, dimensionless, from the LP. Payload margin, kg. Thrust margin P_hover/P_avail. Tip stiffness k_tip, N/m. Plan length in unit moves.

## Deliverables

A 150-case `.jsonl` dataset; three assembly renders plus one failure render as PNG and MP4; the plan file per accepted target; a report with N* and the binding constraint; the `pinneapple_lab` manifest.

## Acceptance criteria

Each target either carries its stated load at N <= 256 with u_max <= 1.0, or the report states the count where it stops and names the connector capacity that binds. Every accepted assembly has a collision-free plan verified by an independent checker. Enabling a shear cap changes N* by a stated amount. Arm tip stiffness differs by under 10 percent between chain model and solid check.

## Realizability and evidence class

PLAUSIBILITY_CLASS today: `extrapolated` for the statics and planner, which are ordinary mechanical engineering, and `hypothetical` for the compound claim that one unit population serves as road vehicle, aircraft frame and manipulator at once.

Filled axes: `mathematical_consistency` (LP and planner exact within their discretization), `numerical_stability` (conditioning across the matrix, planner completeness at small N), `physical_plausibility` (the budgets are the physics). Not assessed: `experimental_validation`, no hardware; `technological_feasibility`, since no connector exists at the assumed capacities and 0.98 N is a toy-brick figure with an assumed band; `uncertainty_sensitivity` beyond a one-at-a-time pass.

To reach `established`, ranked: a connector with measured capacity and stated uncertainty; a unit with measured specific power closing the quadrotor budget at N <= 256; a two-target transformation from one physical inventory; a drive or flight test of one target. One target at `established` is reachable; all three at once is not, and the report should say so.

## Risks and failure modes

Unbounded shear lets layers act as a truss and flatters cantilevers; mitigation is a capped and an uncapped run of the matrix. Budgets loose enough that everything closes; mitigation is published pack data plus a sensitivity sweep. A planner "no plan" caused by search budget rather than geometry; mitigation is an infeasibility certificate from a relaxation. Momentum theory overpredicting hover on a blocked frame; mitigation is one OpenFOAM case and a stated correction band. Per-target optimization hiding the conflict; mitigation is a joint scoring pass over the accepted configurations.

## Out of scope

- Control of any assembled machine.
- Connector hardware, actuation, power electronics.
- Fatigue, wear, certification, human safety.
