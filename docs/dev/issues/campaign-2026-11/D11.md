## Summary

A cracked member is repaired by an activated material that fills or bridges the crack, and the experiment measures how much of the original stiffness and strength comes back and where the new stress concentration appears. The physics is linear elastic fracture mechanics with a repair patch that has its own modulus and a finite bond strength. The result is a percentage, not a demonstration: repaired strength relative to the intact member, the mechanism that limits it, and — the case that matters — at least one geometry where the repair moves the failure somewhere else instead of removing it.

## Question

For a member with a crack of stated length and orientation, what fraction of the intact stiffness and strength is recovered by a repair patch of stated modulus and bond strength, and at what crack length does the repaired configuration fail at a location other than the original crack?

## Why run it

- Self-healing and repair are researched materials with a recurring gap between the healing chemistry and the structural result, and the structural result is exactly what a finite-element experiment can produce.
- The before-and-after stress field is a figure that carries its own argument: the repaired member's stress concentration either moved back to the original site or it moved somewhere unhelpful, and the second case is the interesting one.
- Fracture mechanics is absent from the library, so the experiment also establishes the crack-tip machinery that the rest of the series has no access to.

## Physical model

Linear elasticity with a traction-free crack surface:

    ∇·sigma = 0,  sigma = C : epsilon,  sigma·n = 0 on the crack faces

The crack-tip field is characterised by the stress intensity factors `K_I` and `K_II`, with the criterion `K_I = K_IC` for initiation and a mixed-mode criterion for the combined case. `K_I` is computed by the interaction integral or by the displacement extrapolation from the near-tip field; the two must agree, which is one of the acceptance checks. The repair is modelled as a patch region with its own modulus `E_p` and a cohesive or spring interface with a stated shear and normal strength, so the repair can fail either in the bulk of the patch or at its bond.

Dimensionless groups: `K_I / (sigma sqrt(pi a))` (the shape factor for the geometry), the modulus ratio `E_p / E`, the patch-length-to-crack-length ratio, and the bond-strength-to-bulk-strength ratio. Validity: small-scale yielding is assumed; plasticity at the crack tip is not modelled, so the results are valid for brittle or high-yield-strength members and are not valid near general yield. The model cannot show healing chemistry, void closure, or the fatigue of the patch under cycling — all three are deliberately outside it, and the report must say so before the numbers are read.

## Geometry and inputs

A plate in tension with a center crack: width 100 mm, thickness 5 mm, crack half-length `a` in {5, 10, 20, 30} mm, material with `E = 70 GPa` and `K_IC` in {20, 35} MPa sqrt(m). Two repair configurations: a bonded patch over the crack (patch thickness 1, 2, 4 mm, modulus ratio 0.5 and 1.0), and a filled crack with a lower-modulus filler. A second specimen is a beam in three-point bending, to have a geometry where the crack starts on the tensile surface and the repair is asymmetric. Load: monotonic to failure, plus one cyclic run at a stated stress ratio for the qualitative note on patch fatigue, explicitly not claimed as a fatigue life.

## What is missing (the work)

1. **A crack-tip module**: `pinneapple_simulation/numerical_solvers/fracture.py` with `stress_intensity(mesh, displacement, crack_tip, mode=)` computed by the interaction integral and by displacement extrapolation, `crack_growth_direction(K_I, K_II, criterion=)`, and a self-check that the two `K` computations agree. No crack-tip field computation exists in the library today.
2. **A cohesive or spring interface element** for the bond between patch and substrate, with a stated traction-separation law (bilinear is sufficient), so the repair can fail at the bond rather than only in the patch.
3. **A repair patch model**: geometry generation for a bonded patch or a filled crack, with the modulus and thickness as parameters, and a mesh that refines at both the crack tip and the patch edges, since the bond-edge singularity is where the answer often comes from.
4. **A repaired-strength metric**: the load at which `K_I` reaches `K_IC` anywhere in the member, including the patch-edge and interface locations, so the report can say which site fails first.
5. **A refusal rule**: a repair configuration whose patch is too short or too thin to alter the crack-tip field is reported as ineffective rather than optimised into a marginal improvement.
6. **The three-way comparison driver**: intact, cracked, repaired, with identical meshes and identical extraction radii where possible, so the differences are attributable to the patch and not to the discretisation.

## Experiment protocol

1. Verify `K_I` against the closed-form solution for a center-cracked plate in tension, `K_I = sigma sqrt(pi a) F(a/W)`, at four crack lengths and three mesh refinements, reporting the convergence of `K_I` and the agreement between the two extraction methods.
2. Verify the beam case against the standard single-edge-notch three-point-bend solution.
3. Intact baselines: stiffness and failure load for the uncracked plate and beam, with mesh refinement.
4. Cracked baseline: stiffness and failure load for each crack length, with the load path and the plastic-zone proxy (the radius where the elastic field exceeds yield) reported.
5. Repair campaign: patch thickness by modulus ratio by crack length, 4 x 2 x 2 = 16 plate configurations, plus 8 beam configurations, plus the filled-crack variants. Report stiffness recovery and strength recovery per case.
6. Search for the failure relocation: for each repaired case, record the location and mode of first `K_I` equality, and identify the cases where it is not the original crack.
7. Uncertainty: modulus ratio, bond strength and patch thickness varied over a stated range, 200 draws, reporting the distribution of recovered strength.
8. Compute budget: minutes per 2-D solve, ~10^3 solves — order 10^1 to 10^2 CPU-hours.

## Baselines, references and verification

- The closed-form `K_I` for the center-cracked plate and the standard single-edge-notch bend solution are the two analytic references; the numerical path must reproduce them within a stated tolerance before any repair result is quoted.
- Mesh convergence of `K_I` at three levels, with the extraction radius varied, since `K_I` computed by extrapolation is sensitive to it.
- Energy release rate cross-check: `G = K_I^2 / E'` computed by the interaction integral must match the same quantity from the compliance derivative of two crack lengths, `G = (1/2t) dU/da`, within a stated tolerance.
- Baselines the repair must beat: the unrepaired cracked member. And the comparison that matters: the intact member, which is the upper bound no repair reaches.
- For the failure relocation: the intact member's failure mode is the reference that the repaired member either restores or replaces.

## Metrics

- Stiffness recovery `R_k = (k_repaired - k_cracked) / (k_intact - k_cracked)`, dimensionless, where `k` is the load-line stiffness.
- Strength recovery `R_s = F_repaired / F_intact`, dimensionless, at the failure load defined by the `K_I = K_IC` criterion.
- Patch efficiency `R_s` per unit added mass, as a derived figure that penalises thick patches.
- Failure site: a label (original crack, patch edge, interface, substrate away from the crack) and the corresponding `K_I` components.
- Bond utilisation: the ratio of the interface traction to its stated strength at the failure load.
- Sensitivity of `R_s` to `E_p/E` and to the bond strength, from the Monte Carlo, as a ranked list of the two most influential parameters.

## Deliverables

- The fracture module, the interface element and the repair patch generator as importable code with the analytic verification tests.
- The stiffness and strength recovery table over the full campaign, as CSV.
- Figures: the stress field for intact, cracked and repaired states on a common colour scale, the `K_I` against crack length curves, and the failure-site map.
- The relocated-failure cases as a separate section with their load paths drawn.
- A `pinneapple_lab` manifest per configuration, with mesh, extraction radius and seed recorded.
- A report that leads with the achieved percentage and the limiting mechanism.

## Acceptance criteria

- The computed `K_I` matches the closed-form plate solution within 3 percent at the finest mesh, and converges monotonically as the mesh is refined.
- The interaction-integral `K_I` and the extrapolated `K_I` agree within 5 percent for every reported case; cases that fail are excluded and listed.
- The energy-release-rate cross-check between the interaction integral and the compliance derivative agrees within 5 percent.
- Stiffness recovery is reported for every configuration; strength recovery is reported only where a failure load exists under the stated criterion.
- At least one configuration is identified in which the repaired member fails away from the original crack, with the site and load stated, or the report states that none was found across the campaign.
- The report states that plasticity, fatigue and healing chemistry are outside the model, in the same section as the recovery percentages.

## Realizability and evidence class

`PLAUSIBILITY_CLASS`: `established` for the mechanics of a repaired cracked member; `extrapolated` for an autonomously activated repair in service, where the delivery, the activation and the repeatability are the open problems and none are modelled here. Axes this run fills: `mathematical_consistency` (two independent `K` computations, energy-release-rate cross-check, mesh convergence), `numerical_stability` (extraction-radius sensitivity, interface-element conditioning), `physical_plausibility` (values against published `K_IC` and typical adhesive strengths), `uncertainty_sensitivity` (modulus ratio, bond strength, thickness). `experimental_validation` stays not assessed: no specimen is tested. `technological_feasibility` is not assessed beyond the mass efficiency metric.

To move up one class, in order: (1) a measured recovery from a tested repaired specimen, since the bond strength and the patch modulus in service are the two parameters the answer hinges on and neither is predicted here; (2) a healing or delivery mechanism with a demonstrated fill ratio and repeatability, which is chemistry and is absent; (3) fatigue data for the repaired configuration, since most real repairs are loaded cyclically and this experiment is monotonic; (4) a demonstration that the repair can be executed without access to the crack, which is the engineering problem the whole idea exists to solve. Item 1 is decisive; items 2 and 4 decide whether the subject is a repair technique or a maintenance concept.

## Risks and failure modes

1. **The crack-tip field is under-resolved and `K_I` is a mesh artifact.** Mitigation: three-level mesh refinement with a fixed extraction protocol, the two-method agreement check, and a bar element size at the tip reported per case.
2. **The bond-edge singularity dominates the answer and is not resolved.** Mitigation: dedicated refinement at the patch edge, and a reported sensitivity of the failure load to the patch-edge element size.
3. **The repair is credited with strength that comes from added material, not from repair.** Mitigation: the comparison is against a plain reinforcement of equal added mass, and both numbers are reported.
4. **Small-scale yielding is violated and the linear-elastic criterion is used anyway.** Mitigation: the plastic-zone proxy radius is reported per case, and cases where it exceeds a stated fraction of the remaining ligament are flagged as outside the model's validity.
5. **A single unhelpful case is buried.** Mitigation: the failure-site map covers every configuration, and the relocated-failure cases are a required section, not an optional one.

## Out of scope

- Plasticity, ductile tearing and large-deformation failure.
- Fatigue crack growth and the life of the repaired member.
- The healing chemistry, the delivery mechanism and the activation method.
- Residual stress from the repair process, and thermal effects during cure.
