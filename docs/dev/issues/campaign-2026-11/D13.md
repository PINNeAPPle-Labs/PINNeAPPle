## Summary

Null geodesics are integrated through a Morris–Thorne-type traversable wormhole metric, and the ray-marching renderer produces what a distant observer would see: the throat, its Einstein-ring structure, the multiple images of a background star field, and the shadow. Separately, the metric's throat parameters are fed to an energy-condition calculation and the violation at the throat is reported as a number. The boundary of the claim is fixed at the outset: solving a metric is mathematics, and it says nothing about whether such a metric can be produced or maintained.

## Question

For a Morris–Thorne metric with stated throat radius, shape function and redshift function, what is the image a distant observer sees of a background field, and what is the magnitude of the energy-condition violation required at the throat to support that metric?

## Why run it

- Gravitational lensing by a compact object is a solved problem with a known reference, so the integrator and the renderer can be validated before anything exotic is drawn; that ordering is what makes the wormhole image credible rather than decorative.
- The energy-condition number converts a well-known caveat into a quantitative result, which is the point of putting the two halves in one issue.
- The image is a strong visual and costs almost nothing beyond the integrator, since the rendering stack is present.

## Physical model

The metric of a static, spherically symmetric traversable wormhole in Schwarzschild-like coordinates:

    ds^2 = -e^{2 Phi(r)} dt^2 + dr^2 / (1 - b(r)/r) + r^2 dOmega^2

with shape function `b(r)`, throat at `b(r_0) = r_0`, and redshift function `Phi(r)`. Null geodesics follow from the two conserved quantities of the static and spherical symmetry, `E = -g_tt dt/dlambda` and `L = r^2 dphi/dlambda`, giving an effective radial equation:

    (dr/dlambda)^2 = E^2 - L^2 e^{2 Phi} / r^2 * (1 - b/r)

which is integrated alongside `dphi/dlambda = L/r^2`. The impact parameter is `b_imp = L/E`, and the image is built by shooting rays from the observer backwards, one per pixel, and following them through the throat to the other asymptotic region.

The energy-condition content is the Einstein tensor computed from the metric: at the throat, the required radial tension has `T_{tt} + T_{rr} < 0`, i.e. a violation of the null energy condition of a magnitude that scales with `1/r_0^2` up to the shape-function slope. The experiment reports the violation normalised to a reference energy density, and the total exotic mass obtained by integrating the violation over the throat region.

Validity and omission: this is general relativity in vacuum plus an assumed stress-energy, with no quantum field theory. It cannot say whether a quantum field can furnish the required negative energy density, and it does not attempt the semiclassical calculation; the report must state that this is the whole distance between the mathematics and the object. Also omitted: rotation (the metric is static and spherically symmetric, so no frame dragging), and any accretion or emission physics in the image, which is a stated radiative-transfer assumption and not a solved one.

## Geometry and inputs

Metric family: `b(r) = r_0^2 / r` and a second family with `b(r) = r_0 (r_0/r)^n` for `n` in {0.5, 1, 2}; `Phi(r) = 0` (zero tidal force) as the baseline, and `Phi(r) = -r_0/r` as the second case. Throat radius `r_0` in {1, 3, 10} in units of the observer's distance unit. Observer at `r_obs = 100 r_0`, camera with a 60 degree field of view. Background: an analytic star field on the celestial sphere plus a coordinate grid pattern for the second image, since a grid makes the multiple-image structure legible in a way stars do not.

## Solver and engine

- Geodesic integration: the ODE integrators in `pinneapple_simulation.numerical_solvers`, specifically the implicit Gauss–Legendre family available through `irk_gauss_legendre.get_irk_tableau(q)` and the explicit RK integrators, with an adaptive step that is restricted near the throat; the step-control logic is new code.
- The metric and its derived quantities (Christoffel symbols, Einstein tensor, energy-condition evaluation) are new code in a dedicated module.
- Rendering: `pinneapple_blender.cycles` for the final image and `pinneapple_twin3d.scene` for the geometry and the interactive view; the ray-marching renderer itself is new but writes into the same image buffer as the existing pipeline.
- Assessment: `pinneapple_futures.plausibility.assess` for the verdict on the technological claim, kept strictly separate from the mathematical result.
- Recording: `pinneapple_lab.experiment`.

- `pinneapple_physics.astro.lensing` — the existing gravitational lensing module, which is the closest precedent and must be read before writing a new one.

- `pinneapple_physics.astro.validation` for the known-answer test conventions.

## What is missing (the work)

1. **A metric module**: `pinneapple_physics/astro/wormhole.py` with `Metric` classes holding the shape and redshift functions, and `einstein_tensor(metric, r)` and `energy_condition(metric, r)` returning the null-energy violation with explicit units and constants. General-relativistic metric handling does not exist in the library.
2. **A null-geodesic integrator with throat-adaptive stepping**: `trace_null_ray(metric, observer, direction, steps)` returning the exit direction and the fate of the ray (escaped to the far side, captured at the throat, deflected). The step controller must refine where `1 - b/r` approaches a small value, and the failure mode when a ray grazes the throat is what the adaptive logic exists for.
3. **A ray-marching renderer** that shoots one geodesic per pixel, maps the exit direction onto a background sphere, and writes a linear radiance image; with a super-sampling option and a per-pixel step-budget report so the image's numerical quality is auditable.
4. **The energy accounting**: `exotic_mass(metric, r_0)` integrating the violation over the throat, and the reporting convention that converts it to an equivalent mass in kilograms for a stated throat size in meters.
5. **A validity gate**: the metric parameters are rejected when the shape function violates the flare-out condition `b'(r_0) < 1` or when the redshift function produces a horizon, so the experiment cannot quietly render a black hole and call it a wormhole.

## Experiment protocol

1. Verify the integrator on the Schwarzschild case first: the photon-sphere radius and the critical impact parameter must match their analytic values within a stated tolerance, and the weak-field deflection must match `4GM/(c^2 b_imp)` for large impact parameter. Without this step no wormhole image is produced.
2. Verify against the existing `pinneapple_physics.astro.lensing` module for the Schwarzschild deflection, so two independent implementations are compared.
3. Integrate the geodesic fan for each metric configuration: 2048 by 2048 pixels is unnecessary for validation, so the validation images are 512 by 512 and the final renders are 2048 by 2048.
4. Report the Einstein-ring radii and the number of images as a function of `r_0` and the shape-function exponent; compare with the analytic ring prediction where one exists.
5. Energy-condition sweep: the violation magnitude against `r_0`, against the exponent `n` and against the redshift-function choice, as a table and a figure.
6. Convergence: the image difference between two step tolerances, and between 512 and 1024 pixels, to establish that the reported features are not integration artifacts.
7. Compute budget: seconds per pixel with adaptive stepping on one core, so a 2048-square image is order 10^2 CPU-hours; the validation images are minutes.

## Baselines, references and verification

- The Schwarzschild deflection, photon sphere and critical impact parameter are the three analytic references the integrator must satisfy first.
- The existing `lensing` module is the code-to-code cross-check for the Schwarzschild case.
- Known-answer wormhole checks: the ring structure for the zero-tidal-force metric has a stated analytic form for the primary ring, and the ray fate classification must be continuous in impact parameter except at the critical value.
- Convergence in step tolerance is a numerical-quality acceptance item, not an option: the reported image features must be stable between two tolerances.
- The energy-condition result is verified against the analytic expression for the Morris–Thorne stress-energy at the throat, which is a closed-form relation in `b`, `b'` and `Phi'`.

## Metrics

- Deflection angle `alpha`, radians, as a function of impact parameter; compared against the weak-field and strong-field references.
- Critical impact parameter `b_c`, in units of `r_0`, and the corresponding shadow radius.
- Einstein-ring angular radii, degrees, and the number of distinct images for a background point source.
- Null-energy violation at the throat `rho + p_r`, in geometrized units and in J/m³ for a throat of stated size, and its integral as an equivalent exotic mass in kg.
- Image convergence residual: the pixel difference between two step tolerances, as a fraction of the image's dynamic range.
- Ray-budget adequacy: the fraction of pixels whose step budget was exhausted, which must be reported, not hidden.

## Deliverables

- The metric module, the geodesic integrator and the ray-marching renderer as importable code, with the Schwarzschild verification tests.
- The image set: one render per metric configuration at final resolution, plus the validation images.
- The ray-fate map and the deflection curve as figures.
- The energy-condition table and figure, as CSV and PNG.
- An interactive scene in the existing viewer for at least one configuration, so the reader can move the observer.
- A `pinneapple_lab` manifest per run, with the step tolerance, the pixel count and the exhausted-step fraction.
- A report that separates three sections explicitly: the mathematics, the numerical verification, and the physical assessment.

## Acceptance criteria

- The Schwarzschild weak-field deflection matches `4GM/(c^2 b)` within 1 percent at an impact parameter of 200 gravitational radii.
- The photon-sphere radius and the critical impact parameter match their analytic values within 1 percent.
- The two independent implementations of Schwarzschild deflection agree within 1 percent.
- The image features reported as results are stable between the two step tolerances within the stated residual, and the exhausted-step fraction is below 1 percent for the reported renders.
- The energy-condition violation matches the closed-form Morris–Thorne expression within 1 percent at the throat.
- The report states, in the same section as the image, that the metric's realizability is not addressed by the computation, and the `pp.futures` verdict for the traversable-wormhole technology is `CONTRADICTED` or `NOT ASSESSED`, not `SUPPORTED`.

## Realizability and evidence class

`PLAUSIBILITY_CLASS`: `established` as a general-relativistic ray-tracing computation, which is standard numerical relativity practice; `hypothetical` for a Morris–Thorne metric as a description of an object, since the stress-energy it requires is not known to be producible; `fictional` as a transport technology. Axes this run fills: `mathematical_consistency` (conserved quantities, analytic limits, energy-condition closed form), `numerical_stability` (step-tolerance convergence, ray-budget adequacy), and partly `physical_plausibility`, in the weak sense that the required stress-energy is computed and shown to violate the null energy condition rather than being asserted. `experimental_validation` is not assessed and cannot be by this method; `technological_feasibility` is not assessed; `uncertainty_sensitivity` is limited to the parameter sweep.

To move up one class, in order: (1) a semiclassical calculation showing that a quantum field configuration can produce the required negative energy density over the required region, which is the actual open problem and is absent here; (2) a demonstration that the required configuration is stable under perturbation, since a static metric with a throat supported by negative energy is not automatically stable; (3) an energy budget showing the total exotic mass is finite and bounded for a macroscopic throat, which the integral in this experiment quantifies and does not solve; (4) an observational signature distinguishing the image produced here from that of a black hole of similar mass, which is the only route by which the subject could ever be tested rather than calculated.

## Risks and failure modes

1. **The renderer produces a plausible image from a wrong integrator.** Mitigation: the Schwarzschild verification is a blocking prerequisite, not a parallel task, and the two-implementation cross-check must pass before exotic metrics are run.
2. **Rays that graze the throat are misclassified and the image gains spurious structure.** Mitigation: the ray-fate classifier is tested for continuity in impact parameter, and the exhausted-step fraction is reported per render.
3. **The visual is read as evidence that wormholes exist.** Mitigation: the report structure enforces separation of mathematics, numerics and physical assessment, and the `pp.futures` verdict is stated alongside the image.
4. **The energy-condition number is quoted in geometrized units and means nothing to the reader.** Mitigation: it is also given as an energy density in J/m³ and as an equivalent mass in kg, with the throat size stated.
5. **The metric is silently non-traversable** — a horizon appears for some parameter choice. Mitigation: the flare-out and horizon conditions are checked before the render, and configurations that fail are excluded with the reason recorded.

## Out of scope

- Rotating (Kerr-like) wormholes and frame dragging.
- The semiclassical or quantum-field origin of the negative energy.
- Any accretion, emission or radiative-transfer model in the image.
- Any claim about travel, transport or a mission, and any engineering estimate of throat construction.
