## Summary

A rotating habitat is sized for a target apparent gravity, and the experiment measures what the rotation costs: the gravity gradient across a standing body, the Coriolis deflection of a thrown object, the hoop stress in the ring and the structural mass it implies. The principle is established and the physics is ordinary; the value of the experiment is that the trade between radius, rotation rate and structural mass is made explicit, and that the comfort limit is separated from the structural limit. The report must not claim that spin gravity is solved, because the physiology is not modelled and the mass is an estimate.

## Question

For a target apparent gravity of 1 g held over a stated body height, what radius and rotation rate bound the gravity gradient and the Coriolis deflection to acceptable values, and what structural mass does the smallest such ring require at a stated material strength?

## Why run it

- The trade-off is genuinely counter-intuitive to a first reading — small radius means cheap structure and violent Coriolis effects, large radius means comfortable and heavy — and putting both curves on one plot settles the argument.
- The Coriolis trajectory of a thrown object is the demonstration that lands with an audience: a ball thrown upward does not come back to the hand, and the miss distance is a number.
- All the pieces exist: rigid-body dynamics in a rotating frame, a finite-element stress solve, and the scene and render stack.

## Physical model

Rigid rotation at angular rate `omega` with ring radius `r`. The apparent gravity at the floor is `g_eff = omega^2 r`. Two consequences follow from the geometry rather than from any assumption.

**Gradient.** Over a body of height `h` measured radially, the fractional change is

    Delta g / g = 2h/r + (h/r)^2  ~  2h/r

so a 1.8 m person in a 100 m radius ring experiences about a 3.6 percent head-to-foot difference, and in a 10 m ring about 36 percent.

**Coriolis.** In the rotating frame the equation of motion for a free object is

    m x_ddot = -2m (omega x x_dot) - m omega x (omega x x) + F

and a body thrown vertically at speed `v` at the floor drifts sideways with an initial acceleration `2 omega v`, producing a miss distance of roughly `(2 omega v) t^2 / 2` over the flight time. The drift direction depends on the throw direction and on the hemisphere of the ring, and the two cases must be reported separately because they are not symmetric.

**Structure.** A thin ring under its own hoop stress carries `sigma = rho omega^2 r^2`, so the required specific strength scales with `omega^2 r^2 = g_eff r`. This is the whole structural argument: at fixed apparent gravity, the ring mass per unit floor area grows linearly with radius, and the achievable radius is set by the material's specific strength.

Dimensionless groups: `h/r` (the gradient parameter), `omega v / g_eff` (the Coriolis parameter for a throw), and `rho g_eff r / sigma_y` (the structural utilisation). Validity: rigid-body dynamics and linear elasticity are adequate; the model omits pressurisation loads, micrometeoroid shielding, rotation-axis bearings, attitude control and life support — all of which dominate a real design's mass and none of which are modelled. It also omits physiology entirely, so the comfort limit is an input assumption and not a computed threshold.

## Geometry and inputs

Rings of radius `r` in {25, 50, 100, 200, 500} m, each sized to 1 g apparent gravity at the floor. Ring cross-section: a torus of 6 m floor width and a stated pressure-vessel radius, plus a simple truss ring section as the structural alternative. Materials: steel at 1.5 GPa usable stress, titanium at 1.0 GPa, and a stated carbon-fibre composite at 3.0 GPa, with densities. Body height 1.8 m and mass 75 kg for the gradient metric; thrown object at speeds of 2, 5 and 10 m/s at angles of 90, 60 and 30 degrees to the floor.

## Solver and engine

- Rotating-frame dynamics: a small rigid-body integrator in the experiment module, using the general-purpose integrators from `pinneapple_simulation.numerical_solvers`; `pinneapple_simulation.multibody.arm`/`tree` and `pinneapple_simulation.particle_dynamics.rigid_body` where the thrown object or a walking body needs a multi-body treatment.
- Structure: `pinneapple_simulation.numerical_solvers.solid_fem` and `beam_fe` for the hoop stress of the ring section, with `elasticity3d_fdm` as a cross-check on one ring section.
- Orbital context: `pinneapple_physics.astro` (`orbits`, `facts`, `conditions`) for the environment the ring sits in and for the physical constants.
- Visualisation: `pinneapple_twin3d.scene` for the ring geometry and the viewer, `pinneapple_blender.cycles` for the render, `pp.dashboard` for the plots.
- Recording: `pinneapple_lab.experiment`.

## What is missing (the work)

1. **A rotating-frame throw and walk model**: `pinneapple_simulation/spacecraft/rotating_frame.py` with `throw_trajectory(omega, r, v0, angle, release_height)` integrating in the rotating frame and returning the landing offset and the flight time, plus `walk_loop(omega, r, v_walk)` returning the lateral force a walking body must supply. The Coriolis demonstration is the deliverable and nothing in the library does it.
2. **A gradient-and-comfort assessment** that converts `h/r` into a head-to-foot gravity profile and compares it against a stated comfort band, with the band's provenance recorded as an assumption, not a measurement.
3. **A ring structural model** with the hoop stress, the required cross-section for a stated material, and the mass per unit floor area, including a buckling check for the compression ring and the mass of the non-structural systems as a stated fraction.
4. **A closed trade study** producing, for each radius, the structural mass, the gradient, the Coriolis miss distance and the rotation rate, on one comparable table — the piece that turns four separate calculations into a design argument.
5. **A consistency gate**: any configuration with a rotation rate above the stated comfort limit or a structural utilisation above one is rejected rather than reported as the optimum, so the optimiser cannot buy comfort with an unbuildable ring.

## Experiment protocol

1. Verify the rotating-frame integrator against two analytic cases: a free particle released from rest in the rotating frame must follow the involute-like trajectory given by the closed-form solution, and a body at rest on the floor must experience exactly `omega^2 r` outward.
2. Verify the ring stress against the analytic thin-ring hoop stress, and the beam model against the closed-form bending solution for a curved beam segment.
3. Throw campaign: 5 radii by 3 speeds by 3 angles by 2 hemispheres, 90 trajectories; report the landing offset in meters and the flight time.
4. Walk campaign: 5 radii by 2 walking speeds, reporting the lateral force as a fraction of body weight.
5. Structural campaign: 5 radii by 3 materials, reporting cross-section, mass per floor area and utilisation.
6. Trade study: assemble the four quantities into a Pareto set over radius, and identify the radius at which each constraint binds.
7. Compute budget: seconds per trajectory, minutes per stress solve — order 10^0 to 10^1 CPU-hours.

## Baselines, references and verification

- The analytic free-particle trajectory in a rotating frame and the exact `omega^2 r` floor acceleration are the two integrator references.
- The analytic thin-ring hoop stress `sigma = rho omega^2 r^2` and the curved-beam bending solution are the structural references.
- The gradient formula `2h/r` is checked against the exact expression at the smallest and largest radii, and the difference is reported where the approximation is poor.
- Baselines for the trade: a non-rotating station at the same floor area (zero apparent gravity, so the comparison is only about mass), and a centrifuge-on-a-tether concept with a stated tether mass, which is the alternative the ring must be compared against.

## Metrics

- Apparent gravity at the floor, m/s², and at head height, with the fractional gradient `Delta g/g` dimensionless.
- Rotation rate `omega`, rad/s and rpm, against the stated comfort limit.
- Coriolis landing offset, m, and its ratio to the flight distance, dimensionless.
- Lateral force for walking, as a fraction of body weight.
- Hoop stress, Pa, utilisation `sigma / sigma_usable`, and ring mass per floor square meter, kg/m².
- The binding constraint at each radius, as a label, and the radius where it changes.

## Deliverables

- The rotating-frame module and the ring structural model as importable, tested code.
- The trade table and the Pareto figure over radius, with the comfort limit and the structural limit drawn on the same axes.
- The throw-trajectory figure: the same throw at three radii, plotted in the rotating frame, with the landing offsets annotated.
- A scene and render of one ring with a walking figure and a thrown object, from the existing viewer and renderer.
- A `pinneapple_lab` manifest per run, with the parameters and the assumption set recorded.
- A report that states the rotation rate is a design input and the comfort limit an assumption, in the same paragraph as the result.

## Acceptance criteria

- The rotating-frame integrator reproduces the analytic free-particle trajectory within 1 percent of the characteristic length over the flight time.
- The floor acceleration matches `omega^2 r` to machine precision at a body at rest.
- The ring hoop stress matches the analytic thin-ring value within 2 percent, and the finite-element model is converged at three mesh levels.
- Every reported radius has all four quantities — gradient, miss distance, rotation rate, mass per floor area — and no radius is reported with a missing entry.
- The configuration with the lowest mass per floor area that satisfies both the comfort limit and the structural utilisation is identified explicitly, and the radius where the binding constraint switches is reported.
- The report states that pressurisation, shielding, bearings and life support are excluded from the mass, and gives the stated fraction assumed for them.

## Realizability and evidence class

`PLAUSIBILITY_CLASS`: `established` for the dynamics and the structure; `extrapolated` for a habitat of the stated size, since the structural mass is an estimate with excluded systems and the comfort limit is an assumption; `hypothetical` for the claim that a population remains healthy under the resulting conditions, which is physiology and out of reach of this model. Axes this run fills: `mathematical_consistency` (analytic trajectory and analytic hoop stress), `numerical_stability` (integrator and mesh convergence), `physical_plausibility` (utilisation against real material strengths), `uncertainty_sensitivity` (material choice, excluded-mass fraction). `experimental_validation` is not assessed. `technological_feasibility` is partly addressed, in that the utilisation number says whether a ring of a given radius is buildable at all with a stated material.

To move up one class, in order: (1) a measured physiological response to the gradient and Coriolis conditions over long durations, which is the assumption this experiment cannot supply and which decides the comfort limit; (2) a structural design including pressurisation, shielding and bearings, since the excluded mass is likely to dominate; (3) a demonstrated rotating joint or bearing that operates at the required radius without leakage or wear over a service life; (4) an assembly and spin-up plan with its propellant cost. Item 1 is decisive, items 2 and 3 decide whether the number is a design or a sketch.

## Risks and failure modes

1. **The comfort limit is treated as physics.** Mitigation: it is labelled an assumption with its provenance in every table it appears in, and the sensitivity of the recommended radius to that assumption is computed.
2. **The structural mass ignores the systems that dominate a real habitat.** Mitigation: a stated excluded-mass fraction is applied and reported, and the report says plainly that the structural number is a lower bound.
3. **The Coriolis demonstration is presented as a physiological result.** Mitigation: the metrics are mechanical — offsets and forces — and the report states that no adaptation claim is made.
4. **The trade study optimises radius and reports a single number.** Mitigation: the deliverable is the Pareto set with the binding constraint labelled per point, not a chosen radius.
5. **The analytic gradient approximation is used at a small radius where it is poor.** Mitigation: the exact expression is used in all reported gradient values, and the approximation is shown only where its error is small.

## Out of scope

- Human physiology, adaptation timescales and any health claim.
- Life support, agriculture, radiation shielding and power.
- Attitude dynamics, spin-up, and the docking of a non-rotating vehicle at the hub.
- Construction, assembly sequence and in-orbit manufacturing.
