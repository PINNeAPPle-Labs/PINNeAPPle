## Summary

A rigid payload sits on a platform that carries internal moving masses and dampers, and the platform is driven by a prescribed maneuver acceleration history. The experiment asks how much of that acceleration reaches the payload, and what the platform pays in stroke and actuator force to achieve it. The claim it can support is narrow: for a stated stroke budget and force budget, peak payload acceleration and settling time improve by a measured factor over the unmitigated case, and no controller can improve the integrated acceleration dose past the bound that the conservation of momentum sets. The bound is the deliverable; the controller is the demonstration.

## Question

For a fixed maneuver profile with internal stroke bounded by `s` and actuator force bounded by `F`, what is the minimum achievable peak transmitted acceleration, and how far from that minimum does a physically realisable controller land?

## Why run it

- Passive and active isolation are established engineering, and the honest question is not whether isolation works but how much a given stroke and force budget buys on a specific maneuver. That number is a design input and is not published in a form a reader can reuse.
- The conservation argument is easy to state and easy to violate by accident: a design that reports a 60 % acceleration reduction without reporting the stroke it consumed has quietly moved the problem, not solved it. Computing the bound first and the controller second makes that failure visible.
- The visual is a two-panel render of the payload through the same maneuver with and without mitigation, on a common time axis with the stroke trace underneath. It is a good picture because it shows the cost next to the benefit.

## Physical model

A planar chain: base of mass `m_b`, payload `m_p`, internal moving mass `m_i`, joined by springs and dampers and by one actuator exerting a force `f(t)` between the internal mass and the base. The base is driven by a prescribed floor acceleration `a_g(t)` taken from a maneuver profile. In relative coordinates `x` between payload and base:

    m_p (a_b + ẍ_p) = -k_p x_p - c_p ẋ_p
    (m_b + m_i) a_b = -m_p (a_b + ẍ_p) - f - m_i (a_b + ẍ_i) - F_ext

Three constraints define the regime. The stroke limit `|x_i - x_p| ≤ s`. The actuator force and rate limits `|f| ≤ F`. And CoM invariance: with only internal forces, the acceleration of the mass centroid of the closed stack is `F_ext / M` with `M = m_b + m_p + m_i`, independent of any internal actuation. Every reduction of payload acceleration over a window of duration `T` therefore requires a relative velocity `Δv` between the payload and the internal mass, and acquiring `Δv` within the force budget costs stroke `s ≥ Δv² / (2 F/m_i)`. That inequality, integrated over the maneuver, is the bound.

Valid for rigid bodies, linear dampers, small rotations, and a single axis. The model deliberately omits structural flexibility of payload and frame, actuator bandwidth and backlash, thermal drift, and any high-frequency structure-borne path. It cannot show what a real mount does at frequencies above the first flexible mode of the payload, and it cannot show fatigue.

## Geometry and inputs

Parametric in the plan: two bodies with stated masses and inertias, one internal carriage on a linear guide with a stated stroke, four spring/damper pairs, and a payload modelled as a box with a stated first bending mode used only as a check on the rigid assumption. The design variables are the stiffness and damping schedule, the carriage mass, and the actuator force law; the stroke is a constraint, not a variable. Manoeuvre profiles come from the vehicle scenarios already in the library; the payload mass cases are 5 kg, 20 kg and 50 kg.

## Solver and engine

`pinneapple_simulation.multibody.tree` (`MultibodyTree`, `Body`, `RevoluteJoint`, `Kinematics`) for the assembly, with a prescribed-motion base joint, integrated by the Gauss–Legendre implicit Runge–Kutta tableau in `pinneapple_simulation.numerical_solvers.irk_gauss_legendre` (`get_irk_tableau`, `step_irk`). `pinneapple_simulation.particle_dynamics.rigid_body` provides the rigid-body state and quaternion algebra if the assembly is promoted to a three-dimensional mount. The optimal schedule search runs over `pinneapple_design.topology.optimize` only if the layout itself is a variable; the baseline experiment optimises force laws, not layout.

## What is missing (the work)

1. A payload-isolation model: `pinneapple_simulation/inertia_lab/model.py` with `IsolationStack(masses, springs, dampers, stroke, force_limit)` that assembles the multibody tree, applies a base acceleration history, and returns payload acceleration, relative displacement, actuator force and power as time series.
2. The dose bound as a first-class object: `pinneapple_simulation/inertia_lab/bound.py` with `dose_bound(profile, stroke, force, masses)` returning the minimum achievable peak acceleration over all admissible internal force laws, together with the stroke the optimum consumes. Without this the experiment reports a number with no scale.
3. A controller layer: `pinneapple_simulation/inertia_lab/control.py` with a state-space linearisation, an LQR/H-infinity design routine, and a saturating nonlinear simulation of the closed loop. No general control-design module exists in the library today.
4. A stiff-actuator solver path: the force law has a bandwidth of order 100 Hz, which at the required step must be handled implicitly; the coupling of `step_irk` to a discontinuous (saturating) force needs a documented treatment.
5. An energy audit: `pinneapple_simulation/inertia_lab/energy.py` comparing mechanical work done by the actuator with the change in kinetic energy of the stack, to catch a controller that appears to create energy through the saturation discontinuity.

## Experiment protocol

1. Build three manoeuvre profiles (braking from 30 km/h, a 0.5 s lateral step, a rough-road segment) and three payload masses; nine passive cases.
2. For each case, solve the passive baseline with a fixed step, then halve the step four times; accept the baseline when the peak acceleration changes by less than 1 %.
3. Compute the dose bound for `s` in {10, 25, 50, 100} mm and `F` in {0.5, 2, 10} kN — 12 bound cases per profile, 36 total.
4. Design the controller for each (profile, mass, stroke) triple with the bound available as a target; run 27 closed-loop cases plus 27 with a 3 x mass/damping corner stackup.
5. Seeds: the deterministic cases need none; the corner stackup uses `margin_study` with a fixed seed and 200 draws.
6. Budget: about 200 CPU-hours, dominated by the closed-loop sweeps.

## Baselines, references and verification

- Passive single-DOF transmissibility `T(r) = sqrt((1 + (2ζr)²) / ((1 − r²)² + (2ζr)²))` with `r = f/f_n`: the passive case must reproduce it to 1 % at three frequencies before any controller is added.
- CoM invariance: the integral of the total momentum over the maneuver must match `∫F_ext dt` to 1e-6 relative, with and without actuation. This is the check that catches a model error in the internal force sign.
- Passive isolation only reduces acceleration above `r = sqrt(2)`; the experiment must show the model reproduces the amplification below it.
- Baselines the controller must beat: no mitigation, an optimally tuned passive mount at equal mass, and a hand-tuned skyhook damper.

## Metrics

- `a_pk`: peak payload acceleration over the maneuver, m/s², from the payload node's acceleration series.
- `J`: acceleration dose, `∫|a_p| dt`, m·s⁻¹, integrated over the same window.
- `t_s`: settling time to ±5 % of the post-maneuver mean, s.
- `s_used` and `s_max`: peak relative displacement and the constraint, mm; utilisation `s_used/s_max`.
- `P_act`: peak and RMS actuator power, W, from force times relative velocity.
- `η`: ratio of achieved `J` to `dose_bound`, dimensionless. This is the number that says how good the controller is, on the scale physics allows.

## Deliverables

Time series for every case as a columnar dataset in Zarr (about 2 GB compressed), a `manifest.json` with per-case hashes, a figure of `η` against stroke for each profile, a two-panel video of payload position and stroke trace, and a re-runnable script per case.

## Acceptance criteria

- The passive case reproduces the closed-form transmissibility to 1 % at three frequencies.
- Total momentum matches `∫F_ext dt` to 1e-6 relative in every case, actuated or not.
- Re-solved at half the step, `a_pk` and `s_used` change by less than 1 %.
- No case reports `J < dose_bound` by more than 2 %; a violation is treated as a bug, not a discovery.
- The best controller achieves `J/J_passive` between the bound and 2x the bound, and the report states which constraint (stroke, force, bandwidth) binds in each case.

## Realizability and evidence class

Filled: `mathematical_consistency` (CoM invariance, transmissibility closed form, energy audit), `numerical_stability` (step refinement, stiff saturation treatment), `physical_plausibility` (stroke and force budgets taken from off-the-shelf linear guides and voice-coil actuators), `uncertainty_sensitivity` (corner stackup over mass and damping). Not assessed: `experimental_validation`, since no rig is built; `technological_feasibility` beyond the observation that a 50 mm stroke and 2 kN voice coil exist as catalogue parts.

PLAUSIBILITY_CLASS today: `established` for passive and active vibration isolation as engineering, `extrapolated` for the framing this issue sells — a payload that "feels less of a manoeuvre" — because the stroke to do that on a real manoeuvre is the open question. A simulation cannot upgrade the class; only a bench rig with an accelerometer on the payload can. What the simulation can do is move the strong claim, that inertia is cancelled, to `CONTRADICTED`, which is the expected verdict and the main result. Moving to a supported engineering statement needs a measured `J/J_passive` on a rig at a stated stroke.

## Risks and failure modes

1. The controller cheats through the saturation discontinuity and the energy audit misses it. Mitigation: audit every case, reject on residual above 1 % of actuator work.
2. The rigid assumption makes the isolation look better than it is, because the payload's first mode is where the isolation stops working. Mitigation: report the model's validity band against the payload's first mode and refuse to score cases inside it.
3. The dose bound is wrong in the direction that flatters the controller. Mitigation: verify the bound solver on a linear-quadratic case where the optimum is known analytically.
4. A single manoeuvre profile is generalised into a claim about all manoeuvres. Mitigation: three profiles with different frequency content, reported separately, never averaged.

## Out of scope

- Any claim about human comfort, injury or restraint loads; the numbers are mechanical.
- Six-degree-of-freedom mounts, gimballed payloads, and rotating machinery isolation.
- Real-time controller implementation, sensor noise, and actuator nonlinearity beyond saturation.
- Active noise cancellation and acoustic transmission, which is a different path.
