## Summary

Two separated three-dimensional rooms are meshed independently and identified along a pair of matching boundary patches, so that a flow entering one leaves the other. The field problem is ordinary finite-volume work once the identification is imposed; what the experiment produces is the price of the identification. Specifically: what happens to mass, momentum, energy and to a potential field (pressure, temperature) when a closed loop in the domain passes through the identification and comes back with a difference. The physics claim is `established` boundary-condition engineering. The portal as a transport device is `hypothetical`, and the same document must keep those two verdicts apart.

## Question

When two domain patches are identified, does the resulting field solution conserve mass, momentum, energy and the loop integral of any potential field around the identified loop, and what is the smallest modification to the identification that restores conservation?

## Why run it

- Identified boundaries are a real technique that is under-taught and frequently wrong: cyclic and mapped patches appear in turbomachinery, in periodic channel flow, in conjugate heat transfer and in coupled multi-domain runs, and a mis-specified weight produces a solution that looks convergent and is not conservative. The experiment produces a reusable verification suite for the operator.
- The loop integral question is the interesting one and it generalises: it is the same failure whether the physical subject is a periodic blade passage, an identified pair of room walls, or anything else where a loop closes through a non-physical join.
- The visual is two rooms side by side with a streak of dye in one and the same streak leaving the other, plus the residual plot that says what it cost. The contrast with the baseline duct is the deliverable.

## Physical model

Incompressible flow with a passive scalar:

    ∇·u = 0
    ∂u/∂t + (u·∇)u = -∇p/ρ + ν∇²u
    ∂T/∂t + u·∇T = α∇²T

Identification is a pair of maps `Φ: Γ_A → Γ_B` between patch face centroids plus an interpolation operator `W` from `A` faces to `B` faces. The imposed conditions are `u = W u`, `T = W T` (or `n·∇T_A = -W(n·∇T_B)` for the flux form), and a pressure relation `p_B = W p_A + Δp`. Conservation requires `W` to be column-stochastic under area weighting, `Σ_f A_f (W_nf) = A_n`; the experiment measures the defect of that identity as the mass residual across the pair.

The regime is incompressible, `Re = UL/ν` from 10² to 10⁴, `Pe = UL/α` from 10² to 10⁵. The model deliberately omits compressibility, free surfaces, radiation and any metric. It cannot say what a traversable throat would do to a field, because a fluid solver on a fixed background has no access to a Lorentzian metric; that question is not answered here and the report must say so.

The result that makes the issue worth running: if `Δp ≠ 0`, a closed streamline passing through the identification returns to its start with a pressure deficit, and the loop energy balance `∮(∇p·dl)` is non-zero with no work input. The same holds for `T` with a `ΔT` across the pair, which is a heat engine with no cold reservoir. Either the identification carries the corresponding work or heat term explicitly, or the solution is a source.

## Geometry and inputs

Two rooms generated procedurally, 4 m × 3 m × 2.5 m each, separated by 6 m, with one 0.5 m × 0.5 m patch per room. Three identification variants: (a) conforming, vertex-matched; (b) non-conforming, offset by 0.37 of a face and rotated 20°, requiring interpolation; (c) one-to-two face splits. The optimisation variable is the interpolation operator `W` itself, scored on mass residual and second-order accuracy. Inflow 1 m/s at the inlet, outlet at fixed pressure, `ν = 1e-5 m²/s`, `α = 7e-6 m²/s`, dye injected as a step at t = 0.

## Solver and engine

External: `pinneapple_simulation.external_solvers.openfoam` driving `pimpleFoam`, using OpenFOAM's own `cyclic` and `cyclicAMI` patch types for the conforming and non-conforming variants, and a `mapped` patch for the flux form. In-house alternative: `pinneapple_simulation.numerical_solvers.fvm` and `fdm` with a hand-assembled identification operator, used for the one-to-two case where the OpenFOAM setup is awkward. Both paths are run for variant (a) so the two implementations check each other.

## What is missing (the work)

1. An identified-boundary operator in our own discretisation: `pinneapple_simulation/numerical_solvers/identified_patch.py` with `build_identification(patch_a, patch_b, map=)` returning a sparse conservative interpolation matrix, and an assertion that its column sums weighted by face area equal the partner area.
2. A conservation auditor: `conservation_residuals(identification, fields)` returning mass flux defect, momentum flux defect, enthalpy flux defect, and the loop integral of each potential field around every independent loop through the identification. Loop enumeration in a non-simply-connected domain is the piece that does not exist anywhere in the library.
3. An energy-accounting bound that turns the loop residual into a statement about what the identification must supply: `required_exchange(residual, Δp, ΔT)` in the same module.
4. A one-to-two (non-conforming, non-matching-count) identification with an explicit no-overshoot interpolant.
5. A metrics layer matching the in-house and OpenFOAM setups case by case, so a difference in the answer is attributable to the operator and not to the scheme.

## Experiment protocol

1. Variant (a) conforming, both solvers, mesh refinement at 4 levels: 8 runs.
2. Variant (b) non-conforming at 20°, three interpolation operators (nearest, area-weighted linear, and the optimised one), 3 levels: 9 runs.
3. Variant (c) one-to-two split, 3 levels: 3 runs.
4. `Δp` sweep in {0, 5, 20, 100} Pa and `ΔT` in {0, 1, 10} K on variant (a): 12 runs.
5. Baseline duct case at matched pressure drop: 3 levels.
6. Steady cases to 1e-8 residual; transient cases at fixed `Co < 0.5` for 20 flow-through times. Deterministic; no seeds. Budget about 300 CPU-hours.

## Baselines, references and verification

- The two implementations (in-house `fvm` and OpenFOAM `cyclicAMI`) on variant (a) must agree to 1 % in the mass flux across the pair.
- The baseline duct between the two rooms is the reference for the portal: the comparison is pressure drop, dye arrival time and mixing at the outlet, reported as a ratio.
- Conservation: total mass in minus total mass out plus accumulation equals zero to 1e-10 of the inflow in every case. This is a tautology in a conservative FV code and a strong test of the identification.
- Second-order accuracy: the patch-face interpolation error must fall as `h²` when the face offset is held at a fixed fraction of `h`. If the operator is only first-order accurate, the whole domain degrades to first order, which is the failure the experiment is designed to detect.
- Periodic channel flow at `Re_τ = 180` (Kim, Moin & Moser) as the identified-boundary reference solution, compared on mean velocity profile.

## Metrics

- `ε_m`: mass residual across the pair, `|Σ A u·n| / (Σ A|u|)`, dimensionless.
- `ε_e`: enthalpy flux residual, same normalisation.
- `ΔL`: loop residual of `p` around each independent loop, Pa; and of `T`, K.
- `p_obs`: observed order of accuracy of the patch interpolation from a three-level refinement.
- `t_arr`: dye arrival time at the outlet, s, and its ratio to the duct baseline.
- `Δp_pair`: pressure drop across the identification, Pa, versus the duct baseline at matched mass flow.

## Deliverables

Case directories for both solvers (OpenFOAM cases are a few hundred MB each), the auditor as a runnable module, a figure of `ε_m` against refinement level for all three variants, a two-room render with dye and velocity field exported through `Scene`, a table pairing the `established` and `hypothetical` claims with their separate verdicts, and a `manifest.json`.

## Acceptance criteria

- `ε_m < 1e-10` for the conforming variant in both implementations.
- `p_obs` between 1.8 and 2.2 for the optimised non-conforming operator; below 1.5 is reported as a failure of the operator, not tuned away.
- The period-180 channel case reproduces the published mean profile within the stated plateau tolerance.
- With `Δp = 0` and `ΔT = 0`, every loop residual is below 1e-8 in the appropriate units.
- With `Δp ≠ 0`, the loop residual is reported and the report states, in watts, the work the identification would have to supply per unit mass flow.
- The duct baseline is solved at the same three levels and the ratio is reported for both the portal and the duct.

## Realizability and evidence class

Two classes in one document, deliberately. `established` for the boundary-value problem: identified and periodic patches are standard practice in finite-volume codes, the operator is textbook numerical analysis, and the experiment fills `mathematical_consistency` (conservation identities, loop residuals), `numerical_stability` (refinement and observed order), and `physical_plausibility` (comparison with the published periodic channel). `uncertainty_sensitivity` is partly filled by the interpolation-operator sweep and is otherwise not assessed. `experimental_validation` and `technological_feasibility` stay not assessed, because nothing is built.

`hypothetical` for the portal as a traversable connection. It stays at `hypothetical` and its verdict is `NOT ASSESSED`, not because the experiment failed but because a fluid solver has no term that could speak to it. Moving that second claim up one class requires four things in order: an exact solution of the field equations with a throat and the stress-energy it implies; a source with that stress-energy, or a modified theory; a stability result for the throat against semiclassical backreaction; and a measurement. This issue contributes to none of them, and the report must state so on the same page as the flow results.

## Risks and failure modes

1. The two claims blur and a successful conservative solve is read as support for the portal. Mitigation: the verdict table is a required deliverable, and the two verdicts are never combined into a summary sentence.
2. A first-order interpolation operator is hidden by a fine enough mesh. Mitigation: the observed order is computed and reported even when the answer looks right.
3. The `Δp` loop residual is dismissed as a pressure gauge freedom. Mitigation: the energy form of the residual is reported in watts per unit mass flow, which is gauge-independent.
4. OpenFOAM `cyclicAMI` and our operator disagree and the difference is absorbed rather than explained. Mitigation: variant (a) is run in both and the difference is a reported metric with a stated tolerance.

## Out of scope

- Any metric, gravitational or otherwise; the domains are flat and the model is not relativistic.
- Compressible, multiphase and reacting flow through the identification.
- Solid mechanics across the pair beyond a traction-continuity remark; a structural identified boundary is a separate issue.
- The rendering of a "portal" as a visual effect; the render shows the fields, not a light effect.
