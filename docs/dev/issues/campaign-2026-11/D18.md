## Summary

A spatial metric is deformed so that a path which is long in flat space becomes short, geodesics are integrated in the deformed geometry, and the experiment then computes what the deformation costs under the field equations: the curvature, the stress-energy that sources it, and the energy density an observer would measure. The geometry half is textbook differential geometry and the result there is a length ratio. The cost half is where the subject stops being a picture and becomes a number, and the two halves are reported separately so the distinction is concrete rather than rhetorical.

## Question

For a metric family with a tunable deformation, what path-length ratio between the deformed and the flat route is achievable, and what energy density and total energy does the corresponding stress-energy tensor require to sustain it?

## Why run it

- The length-ratio calculation is the part that fiction skips, and doing it properly produces a number for the shortcut that is comparable with the number for a normal route.
- The energy accounting converts "this would need exotic matter" from an assertion into a computed quantity with units, which is the same service D13 performs for a wormhole throat and with the same discipline.
- It reuses the geodesic integrator and the renderer built for D13, so the marginal cost of this issue is the metric family and the energy calculation.

## Physical model

A metric of the form

    ds^2 = -dt^2 + (dx - v_s f(r_s) dt)^2 + dy^2 + dz^2

in the Alcubierre form, with `f` a smooth top-hat of width `R` and `r_s` the distance from the bubble centre, or a simpler static conformal deformation

    ds^2 = -dt^2 + psi(x)^2 (dx^2 + dy^2 + dz^2)

which is the geometric version: a static conformal factor that shortens one direction's proper length at the price of lengthening it elsewhere. The second is the better teaching object because it has no motion, no horizon and no negative-energy requirement hidden in a shift vector; the first is the one the fiction invokes, so both are run, and the report keeps them apart.

**Length ratio.** For the static conformal family, the proper length of a path is `∫ psi dl`, so a channel with `psi < 1` along its axis has a shorter proper length than the flat geodesic. The geodesics are computed, not assumed, so the shortening is verified against the actual geodesic and not against a straight line.

**Cost.** The Einstein tensor of the chosen metric is computed symbolically where possible and numerically otherwise, and the required stress-energy follows from `G_{mu nu} = 8 pi G T_{mu nu}`. For the static conformal family this yields a positive energy density, which is the honest and often overlooked result: a static conformal deformation is a well-behaved spacetime with ordinary matter, and the reason it does not short-cut anything useful is that the total mass is enormous, not negative. For the Alcubierre shift-vector family the wall of the bubble requires a negative energy density whose magnitude is computed explicitly.

Dimensionless groups: the deformation depth `1 - psi_min`, the ratio of the channel width to its length, and the ratio of the required energy to the rest mass of the object that would be moved. Validity: this is general relativity with an assumed metric. It cannot say whether the required stress-energy can be produced, and for the Alcubierre family it cannot say whether the configuration is stable, which it is not known to be.

## Geometry and inputs

Two metric families. The static conformal family with `psi(x) = 1 - A exp(-(y^2 + z^2)/w^2)` inside a channel of length `L`, with `A` in {0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7} and `w` in {0.5, 1, 2} in units of `L/10`. The Alcubierre family with bubble radius `R` in {1, 5, 20} and wall thickness in {0.1, 0.3} `R`, at a stated velocity. Endpoints: two points separated by a flat-space distance `D`, with the channel aligned along the separation. The comparison path is the flat geodesic between the same two points.

## Solver and engine

- Metric and curvature: a new module computing the metric, its inverse, the Christoffel symbols and the Einstein tensor, symbolically where the metric is analytic and by finite differences otherwise, in `pinneapple_physics/astro/metric_deformation.py`. The symbolic path uses `pinneapple_simulation.numerical_solvers.symbolic_fd` conventions and `sympy` through the existing dependency.
- Geodesics: the same null and timelike geodesic integrator built for D13, with the adaptive stepping near regions of strong curvature.
- Validation of the curvature computation: `pinneapple_simulation.electromagnetics.verification`-style analytic comparisons are not applicable, so the check is the Bianchi identity `∇·G = 0` and the known Einstein tensor of the Schwarzschild metric, both computed by the same code.
- Rendering: `pinneapple_twin3d.scene` and `pinneapple_blender.cycles` for the embedded-surface and light-path visual.
- Assessment: `pp.futures` and `pinneapple_futures.plausibility.assess`.
- Recording: `pinneapple_lab.experiment`.

## What is missing (the work)

1. **A metric-deformation module**: `pinneapple_physics/astro/metric_deformation.py` with the two metric families as constructors, `christoffel(metric, coords)`, `einstein_tensor(metric, coords)`, and `stress_energy(metric, coords)` returning the required `T` with units explicit. This is the D13 metric machinery extended from a fixed family to a parameterised one.
2. **A geodesic length comparison**: `path_length(metric, p0, p1)` computing the geodesic and its proper length, plus a flat-space reference and the ratio, with the geodesic solved rather than assumed.
3. **An energy accounting**: `total_energy(stress_energy, slice)` integrating the required energy over the deformation region, reported in joules and as an equivalent mass, and compared with the rest mass of a stated payload.
4. **A divergence check**: numerical verification that `∇·G = 0` to a stated tolerance for every metric configuration, which is the only thing that makes the stress-energy trustworthy.
5. **A visualisation of the deformation**: an embedded-surface render of the conformal factor and a light-path render showing the apparent shortening for an observer outside the channel, with the caveat that the embedding is a visual aid and not the geometry.
6. **A feasibility separator**: a table that places each family in one of three columns — ordinary matter with an unaffordable energy, negative energy with an unknown source, or no deformation at all — so the reader cannot take the rendering as the result.

## Experiment protocol

1. Validate the curvature code on the Schwarzschild metric: the Einstein tensor must vanish in vacuum to numerical tolerance, and the Kretschmann scalar must match the analytic `48 G^2 M^2/(c^4 r^6)`.
2. Validate the geodesic length computation in flat space: a straight line must be the geodesic and the length must match the coordinate distance.
3. Static conformal sweep: 4 depths by 3 widths = 12 configurations; for each, the geodesic, the length ratio, the peak and total energy density, the total energy and the equivalent mass.
4. Alcubierre sweep: 3 radii by 2 wall thickness ratios by 2 velocities = 12 configurations; the same quantities plus the negative-energy wall integral.
5. Boundary of the claim: for each configuration, the ratio of the required total energy to the rest mass of a 1 kg payload, and the ratio for a 10^3 kg payload.
6. Renders: the conformal family's channel and the light paths through it; one Alcubierre bubble wall with the energy density mapped on it.
7. Compute budget: the symbolic curvature is seconds; the geometric integrations are minutes — order 10^0 to 10^1 CPU-hours.

## Baselines, references and verification

- The Schwarzschild Einstein tensor and Kretschmann scalar are the curvature code's references; a code that cannot produce zero for vacuum Schwarzschild cannot be trusted with a deformed metric.
- The flat-space geodesic and its length are the path-length reference.
- The divergence identity `∇·G = 0` is the strongest available self-check and is applied to every configuration.
- The Alcubierre negative-energy wall magnitude is checked against the published order-of-magnitude scaling for the wall thickness, which is a published analytic result and not a fit.
- Baselines: the flat route's length and zero energy cost. The deformed route must be reported against both numbers, never against the flat length alone.

## Metrics

- Length ratio `Lambda = L_deformed / L_flat`, dimensionless, from the computed geodesics.
- Effective shortcut speed `L_flat / L_deformed` in units of `c`, stated as a ratio of lengths and explicitly not as a travel velocity in the flat frame without the corresponding time calculation.
- Peak energy density, J/m³, and its sign; the fraction of the deformation volume with negative energy density.
- Total required energy, J, and equivalent mass, kg.
- Energy-to-payload ratio `E_required / (m_payload c^2)`, dimensionless, for two payload masses.
- Divergence residual `|∇·G|` normalised by the largest component of `G`, as a numerical-quality metric.

## Deliverables

- The metric-deformation module, the length comparison and the energy accounting as importable code, with the Schwarzschild and flat-space validation tests.
- The table over both families: length ratio, energy density sign and magnitude, total energy, equivalent mass, and the feasibility column.
- Figures: the length ratio against the deformation depth, and the required energy against the same, on a log scale so the magnitude is visible.
- The rendered channel and light paths, labelled as a visual aid rather than a result.
- A `pinneapple_lab` manifest per configuration.
- A report whose first paragraph gives the length ratio and the energy, in that order, and does not present the render first.

## Acceptance criteria

- The curvature code reproduces zero Einstein tensor in vacuum Schwarzschild to a stated numerical tolerance and the analytic Kretschmann scalar within 1 percent.
- The computed flat-space geodesic length matches the coordinate distance within 1e-6 relative.
- The divergence residual is below 1e-8 relative for every reported configuration; a configuration that fails is excluded and the failure recorded.
- Every length ratio is reported with its required energy and the energy's sign, in the same table, with no row missing either entry.
- The Alcubierre wall energy magnitude agrees with the published scaling to within a stated factor, or the discrepancy is reported and explained.
- The report states that the embedded-surface rendering is a visual aid and not the geometry, and that no travel-time claim follows from the length ratio alone.

## Realizability and evidence class

`PLAUSIBILITY_CLASS`: `established` as a differential-geometry and curvature computation; `hypothetical` for the static conformal deformation as an engineered structure, because the required mass is astronomical and ordinary; `fictional` for a faster-than-light fold as a technology. Axes this run fills: `mathematical_consistency` (Bianchi identity, Schwarzschild vacuum, flat-space geodesics), `numerical_stability` (symbolic against numerical curvature, integration tolerance), `physical_plausibility` (the sign and magnitude of the required energy density, computed rather than asserted), `uncertainty_sensitivity` (deformation depth and width). `experimental_validation` is not assessed and is not reachable by calculation. `technological_feasibility` is not assessed beyond the energy number.

To move up one class, in order: (1) an energy source with the required density and sign over the required volume, which for the static family means an astronomical mass and for the shift-vector family means negative energy that no known field provides; (2) a stability analysis of the deformation, since a configuration that requires exotic matter is not automatically a configuration that persists; (3) a causal analysis showing that the deformed spacetime admits a well-posed initial-value problem, which is where such metrics usually fail; (4) a laboratory-scale analogue demonstration, for example in an optical or acoustic metamaterial where the effective geometry is real, which would validate the mathematics without claiming the spacetime. Item 1 is the wall.

## Risks and failure modes

1. **The length ratio is reported as a travel speed.** Mitigation: the metric definition and the report both separate the two, and the time calculation for an outside observer is a required column or an explicit refusal.
2. **The curvature code is wrong and the energy number is meaningless.** Mitigation: the Schwarzschild vacuum and Kretschmann checks are blocking, and the divergence identity is a per-configuration gate.
3. **The visual carries the argument.** Mitigation: the rendering is labelled as an aid, and the report's first paragraph is the length ratio and the energy, not the picture.
4. **The static and Alcubierre families are conflated.** Mitigation: they are reported in separate tables with their separate verdicts, and the difference in the sign of the required energy is stated explicitly.
5. **The energy integral is taken over an arbitrary volume and the number is not comparable.** Mitigation: the integration volume is defined per configuration and reported with the number, and the peak density is reported alongside the total.

## Out of scope

- Any claim about travel, missions or vehicles.
- Quantum field theory on the deformed background, and Hawking-like radiation from a horizon.
- Experimental realisation in a metamaterial analogue, which is the route named in the evidence class and not attempted here.
- The Alcubierre metric's stability under perturbation, beyond a statement that it is not established.
