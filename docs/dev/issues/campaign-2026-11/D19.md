## Summary

The standard quantum teleportation protocol is simulated on a small register: prepare an unknown single-qubit state, distribute a Bell pair, perform a Bell-basis measurement on the input and one half of the pair, send the two classical bits, and apply the corresponding correction. The experiment reports the fidelity of the recovered state against the prepared one, as a function of the noise model, and then demonstrates the no-signalling property by showing that the receiver's reduced state is unchanged before the classical bits arrive. The scope is stated once and never relaxed: states teleport, matter does not, and there is no faster-than-light signalling.

## Question

For depolarizing and amplitude-damping channels of stated strength on each leg of the protocol, what is the teleportation fidelity as a function of the channel parameters, and what is the fidelity at which the protocol stops beating the trivial classical bound of 2/3?

## Why run it

- Quantum teleportation is a laboratory result that is routinely misread in popular accounts, and a simulation that reports the fidelity and the classical bound gives the reader a reference point that survives contact with the literature.
- The no-signalling check is the part most often omitted and is the one that makes the scope statement (no faster-than-light communication) demonstrable rather than asserted.
- The noise sweep is where the experiment can fail informatively: the channel strength at which the advantage disappears is a number, and it is the useful design target for any experiment claiming a quantum link.

## Physical model

A two-qubit system in a pure or mixed state, initially

    |psi>_1 (x) |Phi+>_23,   |Phi+> = (|00> + |11>)/sqrt(2)

followed by a Bell measurement on qubits 1 and 2, a classical outcome `(m1, m2)` in {00, 01, 10, 11}, and the correction `X^{m2} Z^{m1}` applied to qubit 3. The recovered state on qubit 3 is `|psi>` in the ideal case. Fidelity is `F = <psi| rho_3 |psi>`, and for a mixed or unknown input it is averaged over a uniform (Haar or Bloch-sphere) sample of input states, which gives the standard average fidelity.

**Channels.** Depolarizing: `rho -> (1-p) rho + p I/2` per qubit. Amplitude damping with rate `gamma`: the standard Kraus operators `K0 = diag(1, sqrt(1-gamma))`, `K1 = sqrt(gamma) [[0,1],[0,0]]`. Channels are applied at three places, separately and in combination: on the Bell-pair preparation, on the qubits during storage, and on the correction gate.

**Reference values.** The ideal fidelity is 1. The best fidelity achievable by a classical measure-and-reprepare strategy from one qubit to another is 2/3 for a uniform input distribution, and this is the comparison line. The no-signalling property is not a numerical tolerance but a structural fact of the formalism; the experiment shows the receiver's reduced density matrix is independent of the sender's measurement outcome before the classical information is applied, and reports the numerical residual.

Validity and omission: this is a non-relativistic quantum-information model. It omits any relativistic field theory, it treats no-signalling as a property of quantum mechanics without discussion of the measurement problem, and it does not model detector inefficiency beyond a stated effective channel strength. The distinction between teleporting a state and transporting matter is not a limitation of the simulation; it is a statement about what quantum mechanics allows, and the report must make it.

## Geometry and inputs

Systems: 3, 4 and 5 qubits, with the 4-qubit case being two sequential hops of the same state and the 5-qubit case being a teleportation through one intermediate repeater station. Input states: the six Bloch-sphere cardinal states plus 1000 states sampled uniformly on the Bloch sphere with a fixed seed. Noise: depolarizing `p` in {0, 0.01, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.5}, amplitude damping `gamma` in the same set, applied at each of the three stages independently and then jointly.

## Solver and engine

- Circuit construction and state-vector evolution: `pinneapple_quantum` — `pinneapple_quantum.circuits.base.QuantumCircuitConfig` for the register definition, `pinneapple_quantum.models.QuantumModel` for the parameterised circuit, and the classical state-vector backend in `pinneapple_quantum.backends.backend.ClassicalBackend` obtained through `get_backend(BackendConfig(provider="classical"))`, which requires no external quantum package. Optional PennyLane and Qiskit paths exist and can be used to cross-check.
- Sampling: `BackendConfig(shots=)` for the measurement statistics; `shots=None` for exact expectation values.
- Cross-check: a hand-written 2^n dense linear-algebra implementation in the experiment module, so the circuit result is verified against direct matrix multiplication for n up to 4.
- Recording: `pinneapple_lab.experiment`; figures through `pp.dashboard`.

The package's stated purpose is variational quantum machine learning — a wavefunction ansatz for the Schrödinger equation, hybrid models and quantum kernels. It is not a general-purpose circuit simulator, and the gap below reflects that.

## What is missing (the work)

1. **Mid-circuit measurement and classical feed-forward**. The existing circuits terminate in expectation values of Pauli observables; a Bell-basis measurement with a conditional correction on a later gate is a different execution model. This needs `pinneapple_quantum/circuits/protocols.py` with `bell_measurement(state, qubits)` returning the outcome probabilities and the post-measurement state, and `apply_correction(state, qubit, m1, m2)`.
2. **Density-matrix evolution with channels**. The classical backend is a state-vector simulator, and depolarizing and amplitude-damping channels are not unitary, so a density-matrix path is required: `density_matrix(circuit)` and `apply_channel(rho, kraus_ops, qubits)`. No channel support exists in the package today.
3. **A fidelity estimator**: `fidelity(rho, psi)` for a pure target against a mixed state, `average_fidelity(rho, n_samples, seed)` over a Haar sample, and `entanglement_fidelity` for the two-qubit state, with the estimator's bias documented for small sample counts.
4. **A no-signalling check**: `reduced_state_after_measurement(...)` returning the receiver's reduced density matrix conditioned on each of the four sender outcomes, and the trace-distance spread between them, which must be numerically zero before the classical bits are applied.
5. **A classical-bound reference**: `classical_fidelity_bound(n_qubits)` implementing the measure-and-reprepare strategy, so the 2/3 line is computed by the same code rather than hard-coded.
6. **A noise-placement harness** that applies a named channel at a named stage, so the degradation is attributable and not an aggregate.

## Experiment protocol

1. Verify the circuit path against dense matrix multiplication for n = 2 and n = 3 on the ideal protocol: the recovered state must match the input to 1e-12 in trace distance.
2. Verify the Bell-pair preparation and the Bell-measurement outcome distribution against the analytic 25 percent each.
3. Ideal sweep: the six cardinal inputs and 1000 Haar samples, reporting the average fidelity, which must be 1 to within the estimator error.
4. Noise sweep: 7 depolarizing strengths by 3 application stages, and the same for amplitude damping, then the joint cases; 28 configurations (fewer precisely), each with 200 sampled inputs.
5. Threshold search: for each channel type, the strength at which the average fidelity falls below 2/3, computed by bisection and reported with its uncertainty from the sampling.
6. Multi-hop: the 4-qubit two-hop and the 5-qubit repeater cases, with the per-hop noise scaled so the total is comparable, and the fidelity against the number of hops.
7. No-signalling: for each noisy configuration, the trace distance between the receiver's reduced states conditioned on the four sender outcomes before correction, with the residual reported.
8. Compute budget: milliseconds per trajectory for n up to 6 on the density-matrix path; the whole campaign is order 10^0 CPU-hours, dominated by the 1000-sample sweeps.

## Baselines, references and verification

- The ideal protocol's fidelity of 1 and the recovery of the state to 1e-12 are the correctness references; a simulation that cannot do this has a bug and is not a result.
- Dense matrix multiplication is the independent implementation for n up to 4.
- The classical bound of 2/3 from the measure-and-reprepare strategy, computed in the same run, is the comparison line below which the protocol has no advantage.
- The Bell-measurement outcome distribution being uniform and independent of the input state is checked explicitly, since a dependence there would be a no-signalling violation.
- Published experimental teleportation results are cited as the order-of-magnitude reference for achievable fidelity, not as a numerical target for the simulation.

## Metrics

- Average fidelity `F_avg`, dimensionless, over the sampled input states, with the sample standard error.
- Worst-case fidelity `F_min` over the Bloch sphere sample, which is the stricter quantity for a mixed state.
- Fidelity relative to the classical bound, `F_avg - 2/3`, the advantage, dimensionless.
- Threshold channel strength `p*` or `gamma*` at which the advantage vanishes, with its bisection interval.
- No-signalling residual: maximum trace distance between the four conditional reduced states before correction, numerically zero in exact arithmetic.
- Entanglement fidelity of the Bell pair after the noise model, as the intermediate quantity that the final fidelity tracks.

## Deliverables

- The protocol, channel and fidelity modules as importable code with the dense-matrix and ideal-protocol tests.
- The fidelity-versus-noise curves for both channel types and all application stages, as data and figures, with the 2/3 line drawn.
- The threshold table with the bisection intervals, as CSV.
- The no-signalling residual table, with the statement that it is zero before the classical information arrives and nonzero only after the correction.
- A `pinneapple_lab` manifest per configuration, with the seed and the channel placement recorded.
- A report whose first paragraph states the scope: states teleport, matter does not, no faster-than-light signalling.

## Acceptance criteria

- The ideal protocol recovers the input state to a trace distance below 1e-12 for every cardinal input and for every sampled input.
- The circuit path agrees with the dense-matrix implementation to within 1e-10 on the noisy cases where both are run.
- The average fidelity is 1.000 within the sample standard error for the noiseless case, and no configuration reports a fidelity above 1 beyond the estimator error.
- The classical bound is computed, not assumed, and appears in the same figure as every fidelity curve.
- The no-signalling residual before correction is below 1e-10 in trace distance for every noisy configuration, and the report does not present it as an approximate or a tolerance-limited result.
- The threshold strength is reported with its bisection interval and the number of input samples used, for both channel types.

## Realizability and evidence class

`PLAUSIBILITY_CLASS`: `established`. Quantum teleportation has been demonstrated experimentally on photonic, atomic and superconducting platforms, and the protocol's mathematics is a standard result. Axes this run fills: `mathematical_consistency` (ideal recovery, dense-matrix cross-check, uniform Bell outcomes), `numerical_stability` (density-matrix positivity and trace preservation under the channels), `physical_plausibility` (fidelity degradation against the published experimental values), `uncertainty_sensitivity` (sample count and channel placement). `experimental_validation` is not assessed by a simulation, although the subject itself is experimentally established and the report should say so rather than implying a gap. `technological_feasibility` is partly addressed by the threshold strength against realistic gate and memory error rates.

There is no class to move up for the protocol. The honest limitation is a different one: the protocol teleports a qubit, and a macroscopic object requires a description of order `10^27` bits and an assembly mechanism, which are not in quantum mechanics' teleportation theorem. The report must state this in the same section as the fidelity, so the two claims cannot be read as one.

## Risks and failure modes

1. **The simulation is read as a statement about matter transport.** Mitigation: the scope statement is in the summary, in the acceptance criteria and next to the fidelity result, and the information requirement is quantified rather than dismissed.
2. **The noise model is applied where it is convenient rather than where it is physical.** Mitigation: channels are applied at three named stages and the experiments are separated, so the reader sees the attribution; combined cases are labelled as combined.
3. **The no-signalling check shows a nonzero residual because the conditioning is done after the correction.** Mitigation: the check is defined on the state before the correction is applied, the ordering is stated in the metric definition, and a deliberately wrong ordering is run once to confirm the check detects it.
4. **The average fidelity is computed over too few samples and the threshold is noisy.** Mitigation: the sample count and standard error are reported with every fidelity, and the threshold search uses enough samples that the bisection interval is narrower than the curve's step.
5. **The classical bound is treated as a universal constant of 2/3.** Mitigation: it is computed for the actual input distribution and system size in the same run.

## Out of scope

- Any physical realisation, hardware and experimental design.
- Relativistic quantum information, field-theoretic teleportation and any signalling claim.
- Quantum error correction and repeater protocols beyond the single multi-hop case used as a degradation study.
- Teleportation of a state of more than one qubit beyond the stated 4- and 5-qubit cases.
