## Summary

Proper time is integrated along two trajectories — one that stays and one that travels fast, sits in a gravity well, or both — and the accumulated difference is reported with a numerical error analysis. The physics is special and general relativity in the weak field, where the answer is closed-form in each limit. The value of the experiment is the verification discipline: the integrator is checked against the constant-velocity, circular-orbit and weak-field closed forms, with the order of convergence reported in each limit, so the composition for a real mission profile is a number that can be trusted rather than a slogan.

## Question

For a stated round-trip mission profile, what is the accumulated proper-time difference between the travelling and the staying clock, and does an integrator that shares the trajectory machinery with the orbital propagator reproduce each closed-form limit at its expected order of convergence?

## Why run it

- This is the most rigorous issue in the series: every number has a closed-form counterpart, so the acceptance criteria can be convergence orders and not tolerances that were chosen to fit.
- It makes the relativistic corrections to a timing system auditable. The Global Positioning System already runs on this arithmetic, and having the machinery in the library with the limits checked is directly useful for any timing, navigation or clock-synchronisation case.
- The result is quantitative and unglamorous, which is the correct tone for the subject: a number in microseconds, and the statement that the effect is real, measured and small.

## Physical model

Proper time along a timelike worldline:

    Delta tau = ∫ sqrt(1 - v(t)^2/c^2 - 2 Phi(x(t))/c^2) dt  (weak field)

with `Phi` the Newtonian potential, `v` the coordinate velocity in the chosen frame, and `t` the coordinate time of a stated frame. In the exact Schwarzschild case the factor is `sqrt(1 - 2GM/(r c^2))` for a static observer and picks up the velocity term for a moving one:

    dtau/dt = sqrt( (1 - r_s/r) - v^2/(c^2 (1 - r_s/r)) )  (radial component separated)

The three limits that carry the verification:

1. **Constant velocity, flat space**: `Delta tau = T sqrt(1 - beta^2)`, the special-relativistic result, exact and closed form.
2. **Circular orbit in Schwarzschild**: the exact expression in terms of `GM`, `r` and the orbital period, giving the standard satellite result, of which the GPS correction of about 38 microseconds per day is the engineering instance.
3. **Weak field, static**: `Delta tau = T (1 + Phi/c^2)` to first order, for a clock at fixed radius, which is the Pound-Rebka and the gravitational-redshift case.

Dimensionless groups: `beta = v/c`, the potential depth `GM/(r c^2)` or `Phi/c^2`, and their product, since the leading corrections add. The interesting numerical regime is where both are of order `1e-10`, which is exactly where naive integration loses precision: the integrand differs from one in the tenth decimal place, and a double-precision integration that accumulates `2*10^5` steps can lose the signal to rounding if it is written carelessly. That is the technical content of the experiment.

Validity and omission: the model is special and general relativity in the weak field plus the exact Schwarzschild case for a spherically symmetric body. It omits frame dragging (the Lense-Thirring term), which for a low Earth orbit is about a tenth of the special-relativistic term and is therefore not negligible for a precision answer; it omits the non-sphericity of a real body; and it omits the clock's own stability, which for a real comparison is the limiting factor and is not modelled.

## Geometry and inputs

Trajectories: (a) a clock at rest on the surface of a stated body; (b) a circular low orbit at 400 km; (c) a highly eccentric orbit with a stated perigee and apogee; (d) a hyperbolic flyby; (e) a constant-velocity straight run at `beta` in {0.1, 0.5, 0.9, 0.99} in flat space. Bodies: Earth (`GM` and radius stated) and a neutron-star-like case at `r_s/r = 0.1` to exercise the strong-field code path without leaving the Schwarzschild solution. Durations: one day, one year, and 10^4 s for the flyby.

## Solver and engine

- Trajectory generation: `pinneapple_physics.astro.conjunction.propagate_two_body_j2` and `pinneapple_simulation.numerical_solvers.nbody_rebound` for the orbital cases; a constant-velocity closed form for the flat case.
- Proper-time integration: the RK and Gauss–Legendre integrators from `pinneapple_simulation.numerical_solvers`, with the integrand written in a numerically stable form that computes `1 - sqrt(...)` as a difference of small quantities only after a reformulation.
- Method comparison: `pp.compare` over the integrator families and step sizes, with the reference being the closed form.
- Analytic limits: `pp.closed_form` where an existing expression applies, and the closed forms written explicitly in the experiment module.

## What is missing (the work)

1. **A proper-time integrator that shares the trajectory machinery**: `pinneapple_physics/astro/proper_time.py` with `integrate_proper_time(trajectory, metric, frame, steps)` accepting the state history produced by the existing propagators, and returning `Delta tau` with an error estimate from two step sizes. Relativistic kinematics does not exist in the library.
2. **A numerically stable integrand**: the `1 - sqrt(1 - eps)` form must be computed from `eps` without catastrophic cancellation, using the rationalised expression; this is a small piece of code and it is the difference between a correct answer and noise at `1e-10`.
3. **A weak-field metric layer** with `Phi(x)` from the same body model the propagator uses, so the potential and the force are not inconsistent, plus the exact Schwarzschild factor for the strong-field case.
4. **A Lense-Thirring term** for the frame-dragging contribution, at least as a first-order correction evaluated along the trajectory, so the omission is quantified rather than mentioned.
5. **A closed-form library for the four limits**, each as a function, so the verification is a comparison against code and not against a hard-coded number in the test.
6. **A precision audit**: the accumulated `Delta tau` computed in double and in higher precision for one case, to establish that the double-precision result is not dominated by rounding over the stated durations.

## Experiment protocol

1. Verify each closed-form limit: constant velocity at four `beta` values, circular orbit at three radii, weak-field static at three radii, and the exact Schwarzschild circular orbit; report the relative error against the step size for four step counts and fit the observed order.
2. Verify the composition: a trajectory with an acceleration phase, a cruise and a deceleration must give the sum of the parts within a stated tolerance.
3. Mission profiles: the Earth orbit cases (b), (c) and (d) over one day and one year, with the accumulated difference in microseconds and in nanoseconds.
4. Frame-dragging correction: the Lense-Thirring term along the low orbit, reported as a fraction of the total and as an absolute number.
5. Precision audit: one case integrated in higher precision, with the double-precision error reported.
6. Method comparison through `pp.compare` over three integrator families at matched cost, reporting accuracy against cost, since that comparison is the honest way to say which integrator a user should pick.
7. Compute budget: milliseconds per trajectory for the reduced integrand, seconds for the full propagator — order 10^0 CPU-hours.

## Baselines, references and verification

- The four closed forms are the references. Every reported number has one, and the convergence order against it is an acceptance item, not a tolerance.
- The GPS correction of about 38 microseconds per day, from the special-relativistic retardation and the gravitational blueshift, is the engineering cross-check for the low Earth orbit case; the experiment must reproduce it from first principles within a stated tolerance, decomposed into its two parts.
- The Pound-Rebka and the Hafele-Keating results are the experimental anchors for the sign and magnitude of the two contributions, cited as the measured references.
- The composition check is the internal consistency test: the sum of the segments equals the whole.
- The higher-precision integration is the numerical reference for the double-precision result.

## Metrics

- Accumulated proper-time difference `Delta tau`, in nanoseconds, with its sign, per mission profile and duration.
- Special-relativistic contribution and gravitational contribution separately, in nanoseconds, so the cancellation or the addition is visible.
- Lense-Thirring contribution, in nanoseconds, and its fraction of the total.
- Observed order of convergence `p` against step size for each closed-form limit, dimensionless.
- Relative error against the closed form at the finest step, dimensionless.
- Double-precision rounding error, from the higher-precision comparison, in nanoseconds.

## Deliverables

- The proper-time module, the weak-field metric layer and the four closed forms as importable, tested code.
- The convergence study tables and figures for each limit, with the fitted order on the plot.
- The mission-profile table: total, decomposed, and with the frame-dragging correction, as CSV.
- The method-comparison result from `pp.compare`: accuracy against cost for three integrator families.
- A `pinneapple_lab` manifest per run, with the trajectory parameters, step sizes and the precision mode.
- A short report with the microsecond number in the first sentence and the convergence orders in the second.

## Acceptance criteria

- The observed convergence order for each closed-form limit is within 0.2 of the integrator's theoretical order; an order that is not reproduced indicates an implementation error and stops the result.
- The relative error against the constant-velocity closed form is below 1e-12 at the finest step for the lowest `beta` and below 1e-9 for `beta = 0.99`.
- The low Earth orbit case reproduces the 38 microseconds per day result within 2 percent, with the two contributions decomposed and each matching its own closed form.
- The composition check agrees to within 1e-3 relative between the segmented and the whole integration.
- The double-precision result is shown to be free of rounding dominance at the stated durations, by comparison with the higher-precision run.
- The frame-dragging contribution is quantified rather than mentioned, for every orbital case, and its omission is stated as a known bias with a number attached.

## Realizability and evidence class

`PLAUSIBILITY_CLASS`: `established`. Special and general relativistic time dilation is measured routinely — in the GPS, in clock comparisons on aircraft and in optical clock experiments over height differences of millimetres. Axes this run fills: `mathematical_consistency` (four closed-form limits, composition, convergence orders), `numerical_stability` (rounding audit, step-size convergence), `physical_plausibility` (the GPS number and the measured Hafele-Keating and Pound-Rebka results), `uncertainty_sensitivity` (the frame-dragging contribution and the neglected multipoles as a bias budget). `experimental_validation` is not performed by the simulation but the subject is experimentally established, and the report must credit the measurements rather than implying the effect is theoretical.

No class to move up. The limitation is scope: the model stops at the Schwarzschild weak field and the first-order frame-dragging term, and it does not include the clock's own Allan deviation, which for any real comparison is dominant beyond a day. The report must say that the simulation gives the physical difference and that a real measurement would be limited by the oscillator, not by the theory.

## Risks and failure modes

1. **Catastrophic cancellation destroys the answer.** Mitigation: the integrand is written in rationalised form, the precision audit is a blocking step, and any case where the double-precision error exceeds a stated fraction of the signal is re-run in higher precision or excluded.
2. **The potential and the force come from different body models.** Mitigation: both are taken from the same body description, and the consistency is checked by comparing the numerical gradient of `Phi` against the propagator's acceleration.
3. **Frame dragging is omitted and the result is quoted to more digits than the omission allows.** Mitigation: the term is computed, its magnitude is reported, and the number of significant digits in the summary is limited by it.
4. **The convergence order is fitted on too few points and the fit is meaningless.** Mitigation: at least four step sizes per limit, the fit residual reported, and a limit whose order does not come out is investigated rather than reported.
5. **The GPS comparison is treated as an independent check when it uses the same constants.** Mitigation: the constants are stated, the comparison is described as a consistency check at engineering precision, and the measured experimental references are distinguished from it.

## Out of scope

- The full post-Newtonian formalism beyond the first-order frame-dragging term.
- Relativistic geodesy, clock networks and time-scale realisation.
- Quantum clocks, their systematic shifts and Allan deviation.
- Any claim about time travel; the subject here is the difference between two clocks, both moving forward.
