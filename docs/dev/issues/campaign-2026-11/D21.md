## Summary

Trajectories are integrated in a spacetime whose causal structure contains closed timelike curves, and the experiment maps where self-consistent histories exist and where the constraint set is empty, as a function of the metric parameters. The local physics is well defined everywhere; what the experiment interrogates is the global consistency condition, which is the standard way this subject is treated in the literature. The output is a boundary in parameter space and a measure of how much the allowed initial data are restricted, not a machine.

## Question

For a spacetime with closed timelike curves of a stated family, what fraction of initial conditions on a stated surface admits a globally self-consistent history, and how does that fraction vanish as the metric parameters leave the region where the causal structure is benign?

## Why run it

- The consistency question is the interesting one mathematically, and it is answerable by computation, whereas the engineering question is not answerable at all. Keeping the two apart is the experiment's discipline.
- The result is a boundary in a two-parameter space, which is a clean, compact deliverable, and it can be produced with the geodesic machinery already committed for D13 and D18.
- The empty-set outcome is a legitimate and probably the most likely result, and reporting it as a boundary rather than as a failure is the point.

## Physical model

A spacetime with a causality-violating region, taken in the standard form: a metric whose `t` coordinate is periodic with period `T` in a bounded spatial region, matched to an ordinary exterior, so that worldlines with `dt/dlambda` of one sign close on themselves inside the region. The local geometry is smooth and the metric is a solution of the field equations with a stated stress-energy; the causal pathology is global rather than local.

Timelike and null geodesics are integrated from a Cauchy surface `Sigma`:

    d^2 x^mu/dlambda^2 + Gamma^mu_{alpha beta} dx^alpha/dlambda dx^beta/dlambda = 0

and the consistency condition is posed on the map that the region induces from `Sigma` to itself: a worldline entering the region at event `p` re-emerges at `q = F(p)`, and a self-consistent history requires the data at `q` to equal the data transported from `p` through the region. The standard fixed-point form of the condition, `rho_out = Tr_env[U (rho_in (x) rho_env) U^dagger]`, is the quantum version; the classical version is a fixed-point equation on the state on `Sigma`.

The dimensionless groups: the ratio of the period `T` to the light-crossing time of the region, the misalignment angle between the identified surfaces, and the width of the region relative to its length. Validity: this is general relativity plus a classical or coarse quantum consistency condition on a fixed background. It cannot say whether such a metric can be produced, whether the required stress-energy is physical, or whether the fixed-point map is the correct quantum prescription — the last is an open question in the literature and the report must not present the chosen prescription as settled.

## Geometry and inputs

Region geometry: a slab of length `L` and width `W` with the time coordinate identified across it, and a second family with the identification applied to a rotating frame, so the surfaces are identified with a twist. Parameters: `T/L` in {0.5, 1, 2, 4}, twist angle in {0, 30, 60, 90} degrees, region half-width `W/L` in {0.1, 0.3, 1.0}. Cauchy surface: a flat slice outside the region with a stated matter configuration — a set of test particles with stated positions and velocities, plus a scalar field with a stated profile.

## Solver and engine

- Geodesic and field integration: the geodesic integrator built for D13, extended to the identified-region topology, plus the RK and Gauss–Legendre integrators from `pinneapple_simulation.numerical_solvers`.
- The fixed-point solver: an iteration on the state at `Sigma` with a convergence criterion and a cycle-detection guard, which is the new numerical content.
- Curvature and the energy-condition check: the metric module from D18, so the exotic-matter requirement is reported alongside the consistency result.
- Assessment: `pp.futures` and `pinneapple_futures.plausibility.assess`.
- Recording: `pinneapple_lab.experiment`.

## What is missing (the work)

1. **An identified-time metric family**: `pinneapple_physics/astro/ctc_metric.py` with the slab and twisted-identification constructors, the identification map `F`, and the interior-to-exterior matching, with the flare and horizon conditions checked before use. This is the D18 metric machinery with a periodic time coordinate.
2. **A consistency solver**: `consistent_history(metric, sigma_state, map, ...)` returning the fixed point, the number of iterations, and the residual, plus `consistency_fraction(...)` running it over a sample of initial states and returning the fraction that converges. The iteration with cycle detection is entirely new.
3. **A measure of the restriction**: `admissible_measure(...)` reporting the volume of the admissible set on `Sigma` relative to the sampled set, and the dimension of the constraint, so "how constrained" is a number.
4. **An empty-set certificate**: a routine that, when the sampled admissible fraction is zero, reports the obstruction — the specific cycle whose consistency cannot be satisfied — rather than only the zero. This is what makes the negative result informative.
5. **A prescription-separation layer**: the classical and the quantum fixed-point prescriptions implemented separately and run on the same cases, so a difference between them is visible and not averaged.
6. **A validity gate**: the exotic-matter requirement at the region boundary computed and reported, so no consistency result appears without the price.

## Experiment protocol

1. Validate the geodesic integrator on the same background with `T -> infinity`: closed timelike curves must disappear and the ordinary geodesics must match the D13 results.
2. Validate the identification map: a worldline crossing the region at a known event must re-emerge at the analytically predicted event, within the integration tolerance.
3. Parameter sweep: 4 period ratios by 4 twist angles by 3 widths = 48 metric configurations; for each, 200 sampled initial states, 9600 consistency solves.
4. Convergence: for each configuration, the fixed-point iteration count and the residual against the tolerance, so a configuration reported as inconsistent is distinguished from one that failed to converge.
5. The empty-set search: for the configurations with zero admissible fraction, identify the obstruction cycle and report it.
6. Prescription comparison: the 12 configurations with the highest admissible fraction, run under both prescriptions.
7. Energy accounting: the exotic-matter violation at the boundary for every configuration, so the consistency map and the price are in the same table.
8. Compute budget: seconds per trajectory and per fixed-point solve — order 10^1 to 10^2 CPU-hours.

## Baselines, references and verification

- The `T -> infinity` limit is the primary reference: ordinary causal structure must be recovered exactly, and any residual closed curves in that limit indicate a bug in the identification.
- The identification map is verified analytically for the untwisted slab, where the re-emergence event is closed-form.
- The fixed-point solver is verified on a synthetic map with a known fixed point and a known non-convergent case, so the solver's behaviour is characterised before it is used on a metric.
- The classical consistency condition is checked against the vacuum case, where any state is trivially consistent, and against a deliberately contradictory case, where the admissible fraction must be zero.
- The published analyses of the Cauchy problem in spacetimes with closed timelike curves are the conceptual reference for the consistency formulation, and the report must state that the polynomial-time-machine sense of the term is a different question from the existence of the boundary.

## Metrics

- Admissible fraction `f_adm`, the fraction of sampled initial states with a converged self-consistent history, dimensionless, with a binomial confidence interval.
- Admissible measure, the volume ratio on `Sigma`, dimensionless.
- Convergence rate and mean iteration count of the fixed-point solve, with the residual at convergence.
- Obstruction cycle length, in the configurations where `f_adm = 0`.
- Exotic-matter requirement at the boundary, in geometrized units and as a normalised energy density, reported per configuration.
- Difference between the classical and quantum prescriptions, as the change in `f_adm`.

## Deliverables

- The metric family, the consistency solver and the prescription layer as importable code, with the limit and synthetic-map tests.
- The boundary map over the (period ratio, twist) plane, with the admissible fraction contoured, as data and figure.
- The obstruction catalogue for the empty configurations.
- The table combining the admissible fraction with the exotic-matter requirement, so no row reports one without the other.
- A rendering of the region with a few consistent and inconsistent worldlines drawn, labelled as an illustration of the map and not as a physical picture.
- A `pinneapple_lab` manifest per configuration, with the seed and the tolerance.
- A report whose first paragraph states that this is a mathematical exploration of a stated metric.

## Acceptance criteria

- The `T -> infinity` limit recovers the D13 geodesics to within 1e-6 relative, and no closed curve remains in that limit.
- The identification map matches its analytic value to within the integration tolerance for the untwisted slab.
- The fixed-point solver reproduces the known fixed point and the known non-convergent case in the synthetic test, with the iteration counts reported.
- Every configuration reported as having zero admissible states is accompanied by an obstruction, or by a statement that no obstruction was found within the sampled set; a bare zero fails the issue.
- The classical and quantum prescriptions are reported separately, and the report states which is an open question and which is not.
- No configuration is reported without its exotic-matter requirement in the same table.
- The report states that the metric's producibility is not addressed.

## Realizability and evidence class

`PLAUSIBILITY_CLASS`: `established` as a study of causal structure in a stated Lorentzian geometry, which is a well-defined mathematical question; `hypothetical` for the metric as a description of any real region of spacetime; `fictional` for a time machine. Axes this run fills: `mathematical_consistency` (causal limit, analytic identification map, synthetic fixed point), `numerical_stability` (fixed-point iteration convergence, step-size convergence), and partly `physical_plausibility`, in the sense that the exotic-matter requirement is computed rather than invoked. `experimental_validation` and `technological_feasibility` are not assessed. `uncertainty_sensitivity` is limited to the sampling and tolerance.

To move up one class, in order: (1) a stress-energy that supports a causality-violating region and satisfies the energy conditions somewhere else, which no known field provides and which the energy calculation in this experiment prices; (2) a resolution of the consistency prescription question, since the classical and quantum prescriptions disagree in some regimes and the choice is not settled; (3) a stability result for the identified region under perturbation, which is where such constructions usually fail; (4) an observational signature, which would move the subject from mathematics to physics. Items 1 and 2 are prior to everything else.

## Risks and failure modes

1. **A numerical non-convergence is reported as an inconsistent history.** Mitigation: the two are distinguished by the residual and the iteration count, and each is reported separately, never merged into one number.
2. **The empty set is reported as a bare zero.** Mitigation: the obstruction certificate is an acceptance requirement.
3. **The metric is silently non-smooth at the identification and the integrator's behaviour is an artifact.** Mitigation: the matching conditions are checked numerically by computing the curvature on both sides of the boundary, and the discontinuity is reported.
4. **The chosen quantum prescription is presented as the answer.** Mitigation: the classical and quantum prescriptions are run separately, and the report's language distinguishes the computed result from the open question.
5. **The consistency condition is confused with the polynomial-time-machine sense.** Mitigation: the report states what it does and does not claim, and the formulation is described as a fixed-point condition on a Cauchy surface with the classical and quantum versions named.

## Out of scope

- Any machine, device or communication claim.
- The many-worlds resolution of the paradoxes and any interpretation-of-quantum-mechanics discussion.
- Semiclassical backreaction and the chronology-protection arguments, beyond citing them as the reason the empty-set outcome is expected.
- Cosmological or observational consequences.
