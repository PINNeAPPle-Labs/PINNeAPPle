## Summary

A protective layer whose local stiffness or damping switches on impact is compared with a passive layer of the same areal mass, and the comparison is the whole experiment. The impact drives an elastic wave through a heterogeneous medium whose modulus changes with the local stress or strain rate, and the metrics are the transmitted impulse and the peak pressure at a protected interface. Equal mass is the constraint that makes the comparison honest: a thicker passive layer would win trivially, and the question is whether switching buys anything a well-chosen passive layer of the same weight cannot.

## Question

At equal areal mass and for a stated impact, does a layer with a stress- or rate-dependent modulus reduce the transmitted impulse and peak interface pressure relative to the best passive layer, and by how much?

## Why run it

- The equal-mass comparison is the crux and is routinely omitted in claims about adaptive protection; making it the primary constraint turns an appealing idea into a measurable question.
- It is a transient wave-propagation problem with a nonlinear constitutive law, which is precisely the class of problem the current solvers do not cover, so the issue also establishes the capability.
- The negative result is likely and is useful: if the best passive layer wins, that is a design conclusion a practitioner can use.

## Physical model

One-dimensional, then two-dimensional, elastodynamics in a heterogeneous medium:

    rho u_tt = sigma_x + ...,  sigma = C(eps, eps_dot) : eps

with a constitutive law that stiffens with strain rate or with pressure:

    E_eff = E_0 (1 + alpha (eps_dot / eps_dot_ref)^n)   or   E_eff = E_0 (1 + beta p / p_ref)

and a switching threshold, so the material is compliant at low rate and stiff at high rate. The impact is applied as a stated pressure pulse with a stated rise time, duration and peak, representing a blast or a hard impact, and the protected interface is a fixed boundary behind the layer where the transmitted traction history is recorded.

The metrics follow directly: transmitted impulse `I_t = ∫ sigma(t) dt` at the interface, peak pressure, and the transmitted energy. The dimensionless groups are the impedance ratio between the layer and the protected medium, the ratio of the pulse duration to the layer's wave transit time (which decides whether the layer behaves as a mass, a spring or a filter), and the switching strength `alpha` or `beta` together with the ratio of the imposed rate to the reference rate.

Validity and omission: this is continuum mechanics with a phenomenological constitutive law. It cannot show the mechanism by which a real material switches — no microstructural model, no fluid-structure interaction, no shear thickening of a suspension, no phase transformation. It also omits spall and fracture, so the layer never fails and a design that would be destroyed in reality can look good here. The report must state both omissions before the numbers, and the material switching is an assumed law, not a measured behaviour.

## Geometry and inputs

Layer configurations at fixed areal mass `m_A = 20 kg/m^2`: a homogeneous passive plate; a layered passive stack of alternating impedance (two, four and eight layers); a graded passive layer with a smooth impedance profile; and the switching layer with the same base density and the same static modulus. For each, the phase change and the static modulus are held, so the only difference is the rate dependence. Impact: peak pressure in {1, 5, 20, 50} MPa, rise time in {0.1, 0.5, 2} ms, duration equal to five rise times. Protected medium: a steel-like half-space and a compliant backing, to see how the impedance ratio changes the answer.

## What is missing (the work)

1. **A rate- or pressure-dependent constitutive update** for the time-domain solver: a state variable carried per element with the switching law, and a return-mapping step that preserves stability at the pulse's rise time. The existing elasticity solvers are linear-elastic.
2. **A transient impact driver** with a stated pulse shape, an absorbing or impedance-matched boundary on the loaded side and a recording boundary at the protected interface, plus the impulse and peak extraction.
3. **An equal-mass parameterisation**: given the areal mass, generate each configuration family — homogeneous, layered, graded, switching — with the mass constraint satisfied exactly and reported, since an unequal-mass comparison invalidates the experiment.
4. **A transmitted-impulse and peak-pressure metric layer** with the deconvolution of the incident pulse stated, so the reduction is relative to the pulse actually applied and not to a nominal value.
5. **A stability guard**: the explicit time step must satisfy the Courant condition for the stiffest modulus reached, which for a switching material is much smaller than the static one; the guard reports the step used and refuses a run that violates it.
6. **A passive-upper-bound baseline**: the best passive layer found by optimisation at the same areal mass, which is the only fair competitor. A comparison against a homogeneous passive plate alone would understate the passive option.

## Experiment protocol

1. Verify the time-domain solver against the analytic solution for a one-dimensional elastic pulse in a homogeneous bar, with the transmitted and reflected amplitudes checked at an impedance interface against the closed-form coefficients, at three mesh refinements.
2. Verify the nonlinear path in the small-switching limit: with `alpha -> 0` the result must reproduce the linear run to numerical tolerance.
3. Cross-check one configuration against the CalculiX explicit run; report the difference.
4. Equal-mass configurations: 4 families by 2 backing media = 8 base configurations, each with the mass constraint verified and reported.
5. Impact sweep: 4 peak pressures by 3 rise times by 8 base configurations = 96 runs, each recording transmitted impulse, peak pressure and energy.
6. Passive optimisation: for each impact level, optimise a graded passive layer at the same mass, 4 optimisations, which produces the upper bound the switching layer must beat.
7. Uncertainty: switching strength, reference rate and pulse rise time varied, 200 draws per configuration, reporting the distribution of the reduction.
8. Compute budget: minutes per 2-D explicit run, of order 10^2 runs — order 10^2 CPU-hours including the optimisations.

## Baselines, references and verification

- The analytic elastic pulse in a bar and the closed-form impedance-interface reflection and transmission coefficients are the solver references.
- The linear limit of the switching law is the code check: the switching path must reduce to the linear path when the switching strength vanishes.
- The CalculiX explicit run is the independent implementation check.
- The baseline hierarchy is explicit and each level is reported: a homogeneous passive plate at equal mass, the best layered passive stack, the optimised graded passive layer, and only then the switching layer. The honest question is whether the switching layer beats the last one.
- Conservation: the incident energy equals the reflected plus transmitted plus absorbed energy at every run, within numerical tolerance, which is a strong check on the boundary conditions.

## Metrics

- Transmitted impulse `I_t`, N·s/m², from the interface traction history, and the reduction relative to the incident impulse.
- Peak interface pressure, MPa, and its ratio to the peak incident pressure.
- Transmitted energy, J/m², and the energy partition.
- Areal mass, kg/m², reported for every configuration to demonstrate the constraint is met.
- Reduction relative to the optimised passive layer, percent, which is the headline number.
- Switching activation fraction: the fraction of the layer's volume that actually switches during the pulse, since a layer that switches in a small region cannot be doing much.

## Deliverables

- The constitutive update, the impact driver, the equal-mass generator and the metric layer as importable code with the verification tests.
- The 96-run table with all metrics and the mass constraint verified, as CSV.
- The passive upper bound per impact level, and the switching layer's position relative to it.
- Figures: the interface pressure history for the four families at one impact level, and the reduction against impact peak pressure.
- A two-dimensional wave-propagation animation for the best case, from the campaign video pipeline, with the switching region highlighted.
- A `pinneapple_lab` manifest per run, with the pulse parameters, the constitutive parameters and the time step.
- A report whose first paragraph gives the reduction against the optimised passive layer, or states that the passive layer wins.

## Acceptance criteria

- The solver reproduces the analytic bar-pulse solution within 2 percent at the finest mesh, and the impedance-interface coefficients within 2 percent.
- The switching path reduces to the linear path within 1e-6 relative when the switching strength is zero.
- Every configuration's areal mass is within 1 percent of the target, reported in the table.
- The energy partition closes within 1 percent for every reported run; a run that fails is excluded and listed.
- The explicit time step satisfies the Courant condition for the maximum modulus reached during the run, and this is verified rather than asserted.
- The switching layer is compared against the optimised passive layer at equal mass, and the report states which wins; a comparison against a homogeneous plate alone fails the issue.
- The calculix cross-check agrees within 10 percent on the transmitted impulse, or the discrepancy is reported and diagnosed.

## Realizability and evidence class

`PLAUSIBILITY_CLASS`: `established` as transient elastodynamics with a phenomenological law; `hypothetical` for a layer of the stated performance, because the switching law is assumed and the sensing-and-switching hardware is not modelled at all; `fictional` for a shield that stops a blast, which is not the claim here. Axes this run fills: `mathematical_consistency` (analytic pulse, impedance coefficients, energy partition, linear limit), `numerical_stability` (Courant condition, mesh convergence, cross-implementation), `physical_plausibility` (the switching strength against values real rate-dependent materials achieve, stated as a plausibility range), `uncertainty_sensitivity` (switching parameters, pulse shape). `experimental_validation` is not assessed: no material is tested, and this is not a materials qualification. `technological_feasibility` is not assessed.

To move up one class, in order: (1) a measured constitutive law for a real switching material, since the assumed law is the largest free parameter and the whole result scales with it; (2) the hardware — the sensing and the switching — with a stated response time compared with the pulse rise time, because a material that switches in a millisecond cannot help against a microsecond pulse; (3) a test at equal mass, since a laboratory comparison that does not fix the areal mass cannot support the claim; (4) a damage and spall model, since a layer that survives numerically may not survive in reality. Items 1 and 2 are decisive.

## Risks and failure modes

1. **The comparison is at unequal mass and the switching layer wins trivially.** Mitigation: the mass constraint is enforced in the generator and verified in the table, and a run that violates it is not reported.
2. **The assumed switching law is chosen to make the result.** Mitigation: the parameters are stated as a plausibility range and the sensitivity to them is a reported distribution, not a single optimised value.
3. **The time step violates the Courant condition for the stiff state and the run is unstable.** Mitigation: the guard computes the step from the maximum modulus reached and refuses the run otherwise.
4. **The layer never fails, so a fragile design looks strong.** Mitigation: spall and fracture are absent and this is stated, and no configuration is reported as a protection claim, only as an impulse reduction.
5. **The passive upper bound is not really optimal.** Mitigation: the graded optimisation is run with a stated topology budget and mesh, and its own convergence is reported so the reader knows how tight the bound is.

## Out of scope

- Materials qualification, testing, and any certification or protection-standard claim.
- The sensing and switching hardware, and its response time.
- Penetration, fragmentation, spall and any projectile that passes through the layer.
- Three-dimensional effects and oblique impact beyond the stated two-dimensional check.
