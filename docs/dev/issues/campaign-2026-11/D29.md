## Summary

A soft, pressure-actuated body deforms to reach into a confined space, grasps a delicate object and carries a load. The optimisation chooses the body geometry and the actuation pressure profile; the constraints are the reachable volume, the grasp force, the peak contact pressure on the object and the pressure the system can supply. The result includes the failure case: the object fragility limit at which no available pressure can produce a grasp, which is the honest boundary of what a soft gripper of a stated mass and pressure budget can do.

## Question

For a soft body of stated mass and a supply pressure of stated maximum, what reachable volume and grasp force are achievable while keeping the peak contact pressure on the object below a stated fragility limit, and at what fragility limit does the constraint make the grasp impossible?

## Why run it

- Soft grasping is a real and active engineering area, and the pressure-versus-fragility trade is the quantity that decides whether a soft gripper is useful for a given object rather than a demonstration.
- It exercises large-deformation mechanics with contact, which is a capability the library touches but does not carry out to this regime, so the issue builds something reusable.
- The negative result is a single curve: the minimum achievable peak pressure as a function of grasp force, and where the supply pressure runs out. That is a compact, defensible deliverable.

## Physical model

Finite-strain hyperelasticity in the body, with the actuation modelled as an internal pressure applied to a cavity, and unilateral contact with a compliant object:

    Div P + b = 0,  P = dW/dF,  W = a neo-Hookean or Ogden form
    cavity: p n on the internal surface
    contact: gap >= 0, t_n <= 0, and a friction law on the tangential component

The object is modelled as a compliant body with its own modulus and a stated fragility limit expressed as a maximum contact pressure and a maximum contact-pressure gradient, since a soft object can fail under a local pressure peak even when the total force is small. The grasp force is the resultant of the contact traction over the contact patch.

The dimensionless groups that decide the regime: the actuation pressure normalised by the body's shear modulus, `p/mu`; the object-to-gripper stiffness ratio; the ratio of the object size to the gripper's characteristic length; and the confinement ratio, the gap width over the gripper's inflated width. Validity: quasi-static, isothermal, no viscoelasticity, no poroelasticity, no membrane wrinkling model of the kind a real fabric-reinforced actuator has, and no fluid dynamics inside the cavity. The model cannot show how the pressure is delivered, whether the cavity admits the needed volume, or the actuator's hysteresis; those are stated omissions, and the cavity-volume limit is imposed as an explicit constraint rather than neglected.

## Geometry and inputs

Body geometries: a bending actuator (a segmented cavity along a beam), a radial expansion ring, and a three-finger star, each parameterised by a length, a cavity radius, a wall thickness and a segment count. Sizes: an outer diameter of 30, 60 and 100 mm, to reach into a gap of 40, 80 and 130 mm respectively. Objects: a sphere and a cylinder of 20, 40 and 60 mm diameter, with moduli from 10 kPa (very soft) to 1 MPa, and a fragility limit swept from 1 to 50 kPa peak contact pressure. Supply pressure: 0.1 to 0.4 MPa, with a cavity volume limit tied to a stated pump.

## Solver and engine

- Large-deformation solid: a finite-element path with a hyperelastic material and pressure loading, built on `pinneapple_simulation.numerical_solvers.solid_fem` where the formulation permits, and otherwise on a new formulation; `nonlinear_beam_fem` (`NonlinearBeamNewmarkFEM`, `solve_nonlinear_beam_transient`) provides the geometrically nonlinear beam path for the bending actuator, which is the cheapest usable model for that geometry.
- Meshless alternative and the cross-check: `pinneapple_simulation.particle_dynamics.mpm`, which handles large deformation and contact without remeshing, and is the natural second implementation.
- Fluid-side: `pinneapple_simulation.numerical_solvers.sph` and `isph` for the pressure delivery if the cavity dynamics are included in one configuration, and not otherwise.
- Contact and grasp analysis: `pinneapple_simulation.multibody` and `robot_sim` for the kinematic reach and the finger layout; the contact model is written in the experiment module.
- Optimisation: `pinneapple_design.design_optimizer` over geometry parameters and the pressure profile, with `pinneapple_design.surrogate_inverse` for a fast inverse with re-verification.
- Recording: `pinneapple_lab.experiment`.

## What is missing (the work)

1. **A pressure-actuated soft body model**: a hyperelastic formulation with an internal cavity, pressure loading on the deformed surface (which is the part that makes it a follower load and not a dead load), and a cavity-volume constraint. No pressure-actuated cavity model exists in the library.
2. **A soft contact model with a fragility criterion**: unilateral contact with friction, reporting the contact pressure distribution, its peak, and its gradient, so the object's failure criterion is evaluated and not approximated by an average.
3. **A reachable-volume metric**: the set of object poses the body can contact while remaining in the stated confined gap, computed by sweeping object placement and recording success — this is the quantity the "reach" claim reduces to.
4. **A supply constraint model**: the relationship between supply pressure, cavity volume, actuator volume change and the achievable force, so a design that needs more air than a stated pump can deliver is rejected rather than accepted.
5. **The fragility frontier**: the minimum achievable peak contact pressure as a function of grasp force, per geometry, and the point at which the supply limit makes the grasp impossible. This curve is the experiment's headline.
6. **A cross-implementation check**: the same configuration run with the finite-element path and with `mpm`, with the difference reported, since large-deformation contact is exactly where two implementations disagree.

## Experiment protocol

1. Verify the hyperelastic formulation against a known-answer case: a thick-walled cylinder under internal pressure, for which the closed-form solution exists for a neo-Hookean material, compared at three mesh densities.
2. Verify the contact model against a Hertzian sphere-on-plane case in the small-deformation limit.
3. Verify the cavity-volume constraint: the volume change of the cavity against the analytic value for the cylinder case.
4. Reach campaign: 3 geometries by 3 sizes by 3 gap widths, sweeping the object position over a grid, reporting the reachable volume fraction and the pressure needed.
5. Grasp campaign: for each reachable pose, the grasp force at three supply pressures, with the peak contact pressure on the object at three object moduli.
6. Fragility frontier: for each geometry, sweep the fragility limit and find the minimum force at which the grasp becomes impossible; report the curve and the endpoint.
7. Optimisation: geometry and pressure profile optimised for a stated objective — maximum grasp force at a stated fragility limit — with 5 restarts per case.
8. Cross-check: two configurations through both implementations.
9. Compute budget: minutes to hours per three-dimensional finite-element solve with contact — order 10^2 to 10^3 CPU-hours for the campaign.

## Baselines, references and verification

- The thick-walled cylinder under internal pressure is the formulation reference, and the Hertzian sphere-on-plane is the contact reference. Both are closed form and both are checked before the campaign.
- The cavity-volume constraint is checked against the analytic cylinder result.
- Two independent implementations (finite element and MPM) on the same configuration is the practical verification for a regime with no closed-form answer.
- Baselines the soft body must beat: a rigid two-finger gripper with a stated compliant pad, and a simple balloon-like gripper with a single cavity, both at the same mass and the same supply pressure. The second baseline is the honest one; a soft gripper that does not beat a balloon has no claim.
- The optimisation's inverse path uses `surrogate_inverse` with a `VerificationCard`, so a surrogate-predicted optimum is re-verified with the full solver, and the re-verification is reported.

## Metrics

- Reachable volume fraction, dimensionless, the fraction of the object-pose grid reachable in contact.
- Grasp force `F_g`, N, at a stated supply pressure.
- Peak contact pressure `p_max`, kPa, and its gradient, kPa/mm, on the object.
- Fragility margin: the ratio of the stated limit to the achieved peak pressure, dimensionless.
- Pressure and volume required, MPa and cm³, against the supply limit.
- Mass of the body and its inflation hardware, kg, since a grasp that needs an oversized pump is not a result.
- Finite-element and MPM agreement on `p_max`, as a relative difference.

## Deliverables

- The pressure-actuated body model, the contact model with the fragility criterion, and the reachable-volume driver as importable code with the closed-form tests.
- The fragility frontier curves per geometry, as data and figures, with the supply-limited endpoint marked.
- The reach maps: reachable volume shaded over the object-pose grid, per geometry and gap.
- Deformation and contact-pressure renders of one grasp per geometry, from the scene builder and the video pipeline.
- The cross-implementation comparison table.
- A `pinneapple_lab` manifest per run, with the material parameters, mesh and pressure profile.
- A report whose first paragraph gives the grasp force at the fragility limit and the object class at which the grasp fails.

## Acceptance criteria

- The thick-walled cylinder pressure solution matches the closed form within 2 percent at the finest mesh, and the mesh convergence is monotone.
- The contact model reproduces the Hertzian small-deformation pressure within 5 percent in that limit.
- The cavity volume change matches the analytic cylinder value within 2 percent.
- Every reported grasp has a peak contact pressure below the stated fragility limit, or is reported as an infeasible configuration rather than as a result.
- The finite-element and MPM paths agree on the peak contact pressure within 15 percent, or the discrepancy is reported and attributed; a mismatch that is neither reported nor explained fails the issue.
- The mass and the required supply pressure and volume are reported for every configuration, and no configuration exceeds the stated supply limit without being labelled supply-limited.
- The baseline comparison includes the single-cavity balloon at equal mass and supply, and the report states whether the optimised body beats it.

## Realizability and evidence class

`PLAUSIBILITY_CLASS`: `established` for soft-body mechanics and pressure actuation, both of which are demonstrated in the literature and in commercial grippers; `extrapolated` for a body of the stated reach and force in a confined space with a fragile object, since the wall thickness and the cavity-volume limits are the binding engineering quantities; `hypothetical` for a device that adapts to an arbitrary object without prior knowledge, which is a control and sensing problem and not modelled here. Axes this run fills: `mathematical_consistency` (cylinder and Hertzian references, cavity volume), `numerical_stability` (mesh convergence, cross-implementation agreement, contact convergence), `physical_plausibility` (pressures and forces against real soft-actuator values, contact pressures against real object fragility), `uncertainty_sensitivity` (object modulus, friction, pressure). `experimental_validation` is not assessed. `technological_feasibility` is partly addressed by the mass, supply and volume constraints.

To move up one class, in order: (1) a measured pressure-contact-pressure relationship for a fabricated body, since the friction, the reinforcement and the material's actual behaviour dominate the contact pressure and are all assumed; (2) a measured fragility limit for a real delicate object, since the criterion used here is a stated threshold and not a measured failure; (3) the actuation hardware with its own mass, response time and hysteresis, since the supply model here is static; (4) a grasp on a real object with a real object pose uncertainty, which is the sensing problem this model excludes.

## Risks and failure modes

1. **The contact pressure is an artifact of the contact discretisation.** Mitigation: the Hertzian verification, the mesh refinement on the contact patch specifically, and the cross-implementation check are all required, since large-deformation contact is where discretisation artefacts live.
2. **The cavity-volume limit is omitted and a mimic design looks feasible.** Mitigation: the supply model is a constraint and every configuration reports its required volume against the limit.
3. **The object is modelled as linearly elastic when the interesting objects are not.** Mitigation: the object is a stated linear model, the omission is stated, and the fragility criterion is a pressure threshold rather than a modelled failure.
4. **The baseline is a straw man.** Mitigation: the single-cavity balloon at equal mass and supply is a real competitor and is included, with its inflated geometry matched so the comparison is fair.
5. **The surrogate inverse proposes an optimum the full solver cannot reproduce.** Mitigation: every reported optimum has a `VerificationCard` from a full re-solve, and the discrepancy is reported.

## Out of scope

- Fabrication, material selection and the reinforcement architecture.
- Control of the grasp, sensing, and grasp-force regulation.
- Viscoelasticity, creep and the fatigue life of the body.
- Any medical or in-body application, and any biological tissue model.
