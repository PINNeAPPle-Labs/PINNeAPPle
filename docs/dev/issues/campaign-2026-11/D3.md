## Summary

Many units ferry and attach modules in orbit under a coordination policy, and the experiment scores the assembly: total propellant, elapsed time, and — the constraint that kills most real plans — whether the structure is stable at every intermediate step, not only when it is finished. It is a planning experiment wrapped around a rigid-body orbital simulation and a structural check that runs after each berthing.

## Question

For a stated station and a stated pool of modules and tugs, is there an assembly order whose intermediate states are all structurally and dynamically stable, and what does it cost in propellant compared with the best plan that ignores the intermediate-stability constraint?

## Why run it

- Intermediate instability is the failure mode that separates a plausible assembly video from an assembly plan, and it is cheap to check because the structural tools already exist.
- Multi-agent construction in orbit is industrially relevant (servicing, modular stations), so the plan format and the propellant accounting transfer.
- It gives the campaign a case where the answer may be "the greedy plan is infeasible", which is a better result than a successful animation.

## Physical model

**Dynamics.** Rigid bodies in orbit under a two-body plus J2 field, with berthing as a discrete event: relative motion, contact, and a resulting combined body whose inertia changes. Attitude control of the combined structure is a torque budget, not a controller design.

**Structure.** At each assembly state, the connected structure is analysed for the loads of the next manoeuvre: a quasi-static load from the thrust of the remaining tugs plus the gravity-gradient and centrifugal terms of the current rotation state.

**Resources.** Each tug has a propellant budget and a thrust; each manoeuvre has a cost from the rocket equation with the actual inertia at that step — so an early bad berthing that makes the structure massive and off-axis costs more later.

**Deliberately out of scope:** rendezvous sensor models, communication, thermal, and detailed contact mechanics; berthing is a discrete event with a stated capture envelope.

## Geometry and inputs

A station layout (a truss segment with module ports), a module pool, a tug pool with stated capabilities, and an initial orbit. The assembly target is defined as a set of port assignments, so a plan is a sequence of moves plus port choices.

## Solver and engine

- Orbital dynamics: `pinneapple_simulation.numerical_solvers.nbody_rebound` and `pinneapple_physics.astro` (`propagate_two_body_j2`, `conjunction`).
- Structure: `pinneapple_design.bricks.analyse_assembly` for the connected-assembly statics, with `solid_fem` for the detailed check on the shortlisted plan.
- Planning: `pinneapple_autolab` (`Mission`, `Simulator`, `choose_next`) for the search with a simulator in the loop; `pinneapple_simulation.multibody` for the kinematics of berthing.
- Recording: `pinneapple_lab.experiment`.

## What is missing (the work)

1. **A berthing event model**: relative motion to a capture envelope, contact, and the inertia update of the combined body.
2. **A structural check per assembly state**: the load case as a function of the next manoeuvre, run automatically after each addition.
3. **A plan representation and a planner** that respects the intermediate-stability constraint, with the cost function in propellant and time.
4. **A combined-attitude budget**: available torque versus the disturbance torque of the current structure, per state.
5. **A greedy baseline planner** (nearest-first, cheapest-first) to measure what the constraint costs.

## Experiment protocol

1. Validate the orbital propagation against a published two-body with J2 reference; report the error over one day.
2. Validate the structural check against a hand-calculated truss case.
3. Run the greedy planner without the intermediate-stability constraint; record the first state where it fails and why.
4. Run the constrained planner; compare propellant, time and the number of manoeuvres.
5. Report the sensitivity to the module pool (what happens if one module is unavailable).
6. Compute budget: seconds per orbital propagation, minutes per structural check; the planning search dominates — order 10^1–10^2 CPU-hours.

## Baselines, references and verification

- Greedy unconstrained plan (the "ignore the physics" baseline).
- A published station-assembly sequence for a comparable modular structure, if one is documented, as a sanity reference — and if none is available, the issue says so rather than inventing one.
- Structural check validated against a hand calculation.
- Propellant accounting verified against the rocket equation for a single manoeuvre.

## Metrics

- Total propellant, kg, and propellant per module berthed.
- Elapsed time, in orbits or days, and number of manoeuvres.
- Minimum structural margin over all intermediate states, dimensionless.
- Number of states failing the stability or attitude-budget check, for each planner.
- Cost of the constraint: propellant and time difference between the constrained plan and the best unconstrained one.

## Deliverables

- The berthing model, the per-state structural check and the planner, with tests.
- The unconstrained-versus-constrained plan comparison.
- The margin history of the accepted plan, plotted against assembly step.
- An animation of the accepted assembly, with the margin printed per step, from the campaign video pipeline.

## Acceptance criteria

- Orbital propagation matches the published reference within the stated tolerance over the stated duration.
- Every intermediate state of the accepted plan passes the structural and attitude-budget checks, verified by re-analysis and not by interpolation between states.
- The propellant accounting matches the rocket equation per manoeuvre.
- The unconstrained baseline is reported with the state at which it first fails.
- The cost of the constraint is reported as a number, not as a qualitative remark.

## Realizability and evidence class

`PLAUSIBILITY_CLASS`: `established` for orbital assembly and structural checking; `extrapolated` for this level of autonomy. Axes: `mathematical_consistency` (propagation reference, rocket equation), `numerical_stability` (integrator step convergence), `physical_plausibility` (margins, attitude budget), `uncertainty_sensitivity` (module and tug availability). `experimental_validation` is not assessed; `technological_feasibility` is not assessed, though resource accounting is its proxy.

## Risks and failure modes

1. **Berthing treated as instantaneous and free**, hiding the hard part. Mitigation: a stated capture envelope and a delta-v per berthing.
2. **Structural checks only on the final state.** Mitigation: per-state check as an acceptance criterion.
3. **The planner searched too little** and reports infeasible. Mitigation: report search effort and frame the result as "no plan found within effort".
4. **Propellant bookkeeping that ignores growing inertia.** Mitigation: inertia updated at each state and used in the rocket equation.
5. **A tidy animation mistaken for a feasible plan.** Mitigation: margin history is the deliverable, the animation rides on it.

## Out of scope

- Rendezvous and docking sensors, communications, thermal control.
- Crew operations and extravehicular activity.
- Launch and deployment of the modules.
