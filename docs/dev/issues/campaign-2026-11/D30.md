## Summary

Small robots ferry and place parts and assemble a bridge, a tower or a shelter without a central plan, following only local rules. After every placement the structure is checked for stability under its own weight and a stated live load, because an intermediate configuration that cannot stand is not a valid state of the plan. The experiment reports whether the result stands, how long it took, how much material was handled more than once, and — the interesting output — the first placement after which the uncorrected rule set would have produced a collapse.

## Question

Under a local-rule construction policy with a stated sensing range, does the assembled structure reach a stable state for each of three target forms, and at which placement step does the incremental stability check first bind?

## Why run it

- Decentralised construction is a real research programme with a real failure mode — the intermediate structure — and that failure mode is exactly what a structural check can detect and a video cannot.
- The experiment produces the placement-by-placement stability record, which is a reusable artefact: it converts a construction policy into a sequence of states each of which is either admissible or not.
- Both outcomes are informative. A policy that collapses at step 40 under its own weight is a result that bounds the approach, and the correction that fixes it is a second result.

## Physical model

Two coupled models.

**Agent model.** Each unit has a carrying capacity (mass and size of the part it can transport), a travel speed, a placement reach, a sensing range for local information (the positions and orientations of nearby placed parts and of nearby units), and a stated energy budget. The policy is a local rule mapping the sensed state to the next action — pick up a part here, place it there, or move — with a small parameter set and no global plan.

**Structure model.** A jointed assembly of placed parts, checked after every placement:

    K u = f_self + f_live,  member forces from u,  checked against capacity
    stability: the support polygon must contain the loaded centre of mass, and the
    structural stiffness matrix must be positive definite for the placed set

where the second condition is the formal statement of "cannot stand" and the first is the tipping criterion. Ground interaction, which decides whether the support is real, uses a bearing capacity and a sinkage relation from the terramechanics model.

Dimensionless groups: the part mass relative to the unit's carrying capacity, the number of units relative to the part count, the sensing range relative to the structure size, and the utilisation of the members at the most loaded placement. Validity: quasi-static checking after each placement, with no dynamics during placement; the model cannot show the transient during a placement (a part dropped rather than set) or the unit falling, and it assumes the ground is a stated uniform soil which for a real site it is not.

## Geometry and inputs

Targets: a 4 m span footbridge with 60 parts, a 3 m tower with 120 parts, and a 2 m by 2 m shelter with 90 parts. Parts: 2 kg blocks and 4 kg beams, with a stated connector. Units: 4, 8 and 16 units, each carrying 5 kg, with a 100 mm placement accuracy and a stated sensing radius of 0.3, 1.0 and 3.0 m. Ground: a stated bearing capacity and a slope of 0 and 5 degrees.

## What is missing (the work)

1. **A local-rule construction policy** with a stated sensing range and no global state: each unit's decision depends only on what it can sense, and the policy has at most four parameters. A policy that reads the target geometry globally is not a local rule and would trivialise the experiment, so the sensing restriction is part of the model.
2. **A placement-sequence planner with support constraints**: a part may be placed only where it has a path, a support, and a stable intermediate configuration — the feasibility predicate, not the plan itself, so the local policy can query it.
3. **An incremental stability analyser**: the check after every placement, returning a pass or fail with the limiting member or the tipping margin, and a support-polygon test. `bricks.analyse_assembly` solves the joint forces; the incremental version that reports a per-step verdict is the new piece.
4. **A material-handling metric**: parts carried more than once, travel distance per placed part, and wasted parts (placed and then removed), which is what "how much material was wasted" means operationally.
5. **A collapse-attribution record**: the first placement after which the uncorrected policy would produce a configuration that fails the stability check, and the correction that avoided it. This is the required output and nothing produces it today.
6. **A replay renderer** driven by the placement log, so the animation shows the structure growing and the stability state changing, rather than a sped-up robot.

## Experiment protocol

1. Verify the statics and the stability test against `bricks.analyse_assembly` on a shared brick case, and against the closed-form determinate truss for a simple frame.
2. Verify the stability test's ability to reject: a deliberately unstable configuration (a cantilever overhang beyond the tipping margin, and a mechanism with a zero eigenvalue) must be rejected; a test that accepts them is wrong.
3. Baseline: a centralised planner that knows the whole target and computes the sequence by search, which is the performance ceiling.
4. Local-policy campaign: 3 targets by 3 unit counts by 3 sensing radii = 27 configurations, each with 10 seeds, giving 270 construction runs.
5. Stability record: for each run, the per-placement verdict, the first binding step, and the correction applied if the policy is allowed to backtrack once.
6. Ground variation: the slope and bearing-capacity cases for the best configuration per target.
7. Material handling: the carry count, the travel distance and the wasted parts per run.
8. Compute budget: a statics solve per placement, of order 10^2 placements per run and 10^3 runs — order 10^1 to 10^2 CPU-hours.

## Baselines, references and verification

- The closed-form determinate truss and `bricks.analyse_assembly` are the statics references; the stability test's rejection cases are the functional references.
- The centralised planner is the ceiling: the local policy's cost in completion time, material handling and failure rate is measured against it.
- The final-structure check with `beam_fe` or `solid_fem` is the independent confirmation that the assembled form analysed by the discrete model really stands, on one configuration per target.
- Global equilibrium at every placement step, with the residual reported, is the per-step correctness condition.
- A null policy (place parts in arrival order with no support logic) is the second baseline, and it is expected to produce collapses; if it does not, the stability check is too weak.

## Metrics

- Completion: the fraction of runs that reach a stable final structure meeting the target's form tolerance, percent.
- First-binding step: the placement index at which the incremental stability check first fails for the uncorrected policy, and the fraction of runs in which it does.
- Time to completion, in simulated hours, and the ratio to the centralised planner.
- Material handling: mean carries per placed part, total travel distance, and wasted parts as a fraction of the placed count.
- Load utilisation at the most loaded placement, dimensionless, and the tipping margin at the tightest placement.
- Structural agreement: the difference between the discrete model's member forces and the finite-element check on the final configuration.

## Deliverables

- The local-rule policy, the incremental stability analyser and the handling metrics as importable code with the rejection tests.
- The per-placement stability record for every run, as data, with the first-binding step marked.
- The completion and handling tables, as CSV, with the centralised and null baselines alongside.
- A replay animation per target from the dashboard and scene builders, showing the structure growing with the stability state on screen.
- The collapse-attribution figure: the configuration at the first binding step, with the limiting member or the tipping direction shown.
- A `pinneapple_lab` manifest per run, with the seed, the policy parameters and the ground conditions.
- A report whose first paragraph answers whether the structure stood, for which target, and at which step the check first bound.

## Acceptance criteria

- The statics model agrees with the closed-form truss within 1 percent and with `bricks.analyse_assembly` within the reported model difference.
- The stability test rejects both deliberate rejection cases; a test that accepts either fails the issue.
- The joint equilibrium residual is below 1e-9 relative at every placement step of every reported run.
- Every run's outcome is one of: completed, failed at a named step with a named cause, or out of budget; the counts sum to the run total.
- The first-binding step is reported for every run in which the uncorrected policy would fail, with the correction stated.
- The local policy is evaluated at all three sensing radii, and the report shows how completion depends on the sensing range rather than reporting one radius.
- A final structure analysed as stable by the discrete model is confirmed by the finite-element check on one case per target, or the discrepancy is reported.

## Realizability and evidence class

`PLAUSIBILITY_CLASS`: `established` for the statics and for decentralised construction as a demonstrated research result on small structures; `extrapolated` for the stated spans and part counts, since the unit's carrying capacity, placement accuracy and energy budget are assumed and all three are binding; `hypothetical` for an autonomous construction system working on an unprepared site without a survey or a human, which is not modelled. Axes this run fills: `mathematical_consistency` (equilibrium, truss reference, rejection cases), `numerical_stability` (seed repeatability, solver conditioning at marginal configurations), `physical_plausibility` (carrying capacity, placement accuracy and bearing pressure against real small-robot values), `uncertainty_sensitivity` (sensing radius, ground conditions, seed). `experimental_validation` is not assessed: no robots are built. `technological_feasibility` is partly addressed by the handling and energy budgets.

To move up one class, in order: (1) physical units with a measured placement accuracy and a measured carrying capacity, since placement accuracy is what decides whether the connector closes and the whole plan is premised on it; (2) a dynamic model of the placement event, since dropping a part is not the same as setting it and the transient is where a marginal configuration fails; (3) an unprepared site with surveyed-in uncertainty, since the ground model here is uniform and known; (4) a failure-tolerant policy for a unit that breaks mid-run, since the policy assumes units only succeed.

## Risks and failure modes

1. **The stability check is too weak and invalid intermediate states pass.** Mitigation: the two deliberate rejection cases, the finite-element confirmation and the null-policy baseline together; a null policy that never collapses means the check is too weak.
2. **The local policy is quietly given global information.** Mitigation: the sensing restriction is implemented as a hard information barrier, and the policy interface only exposes the sensed set; this is verified by running the policy with a scrambled global state and confirming identical behaviour.
3. **The centralised ceiling is not actually optimal and the local penalty is understated.** Mitigation: the centralised planner is a search with a stated budget, its own gap to a relaxed bound is reported, and the local penalty is expressed against the search's result with that gap noted.
4. **The reported first-binding step is an artifact of a marginal numerical eigenvalue.** Mitigation: the stability test uses a stated margin rather than a bare positive-definiteness test, and the margin is swept to show whether the binding step moves.
5. **Material waste is defined so that it always looks small.** Mitigation: three separate handling metrics are reported, and the wasted-part count includes any part placed and subsequently removed.

## Out of scope

- Robot hardware, manipulation and the mechanical design of the units.
- Site preparation, surveying and ground improvement.
- Building codes, structural safety factors and any certification claim.
- Human interaction, supervision and the economics of construction.
