## Summary

Micron-scale magnetic carriers are released into a branching vessel and steered toward a target against the flow, with Brownian diffusion competing against the steering force. The experiment computes the targeting efficiency, the time to target and the fraction lost to the walls or to the wrong branch, as a function of particle size, flow rate and field strength. The content is where particles actually go: margination toward the wall, branch selection determined by the local streamline, and the size range over which the steering force wins. The scope is stated once: transport only, with no drug release, no biocompatibility and no claim about a living organism.

## Question

For a stated vessel tree, flow rate and magnetic dipole, what fraction of released carriers reaches the target, and what is the smallest field strength and the largest vessel size at which the targeting fraction exceeds a stated threshold?

## Why run it

- The targeting fraction in a branching vessel is the number that decides whether a carrier concept is a delivery method or a demonstration in a straight tube, and branch selection is where most of the loss happens.
- The size range over which a magnetic force beats both drag and diffusion is a design input rather than a result, and it is cheap to compute properly and expensive to discover experimentally.
- The transport problem is a reusable solver: a Lagrangian tracker with Brownian motion and wall interaction, coupled to a Stokes flow, is applicable to aerosol transport, to microfluidic sorting and to the deposition problems the library already touches.

## Physical model

Carrier motion is a Langevin equation with the dominant forces at low Reynolds number:

    m dv/dt = F_drag + F_mag + F_brownian + F_lift + F_wall

with `F_drag` from Stokes drag with the Faxen and Basset corrections neglected on the ground that they are small at the particle-to-vessel size ratio used here (which must be checked and reported), `F_mag` from the field gradient on a magnetized particle, `F_brownian` from the fluctuation-dissipation relation with the local temperature and viscosity, and `F_wall` a short-range repulsion that prevents particles from crossing the wall rather than allowing an artificial escape.

The flow is Stokes flow in a branching geometry: a parent vessel with a bifurcation into two daughters at a stated angle and with a stated flow split. Reynolds number is of order `10^-3` to `10^-1`, so the flow is computed either by a Stokes solver or by a Poiseuille/Dean approximation in each branch, and the choice matters because the near-bifurcation streamline structure is what decides branch selection.

The physics that makes the answer non-obvious: a particle much smaller than the vessel follows the flow closely and its steering force is negligible; a particle comparable to the vessel radius is subject to margination, which pushes it toward the wall in a shear field, which helps wall contact and hurts the fraction that stays in the core. The competition is captured by two dimensionless numbers, the ratio of magnetic to drag force and the ratio of the Brownian displacement over the residence time to the vessel radius.

Validity and omission: rigid spherical particles, Newtonian fluid, steady or pulsatile flow, and a magnetic force computed from the field solution. The model omits the non-Newtonian behaviour of blood, the deformability of a real carrier, the cell-particle interactions (which dominate margination in reality), and any biological interaction with the endothelium. Those four omissions are the distance between this experiment and a biomedical claim, and the report must say so explicitly rather than only in the evidence-class section.

## Geometry and inputs

Vessel tree: a parent of 500 µm diameter bifurcating into two daughters of 300 and 400 µm, with a second generation bifurcation on the larger daughter, and a third geometry with a 60 degree side branch, so branch selection has a case where the flow split is unfavourable. A smaller tree at 100 µm and 50 µm vessels is the regime in which margination matters. Particle diameters: 0.5, 1, 2, 5 and 10 µm. Flow: mean velocities giving Reynolds numbers of `10^-3`, `10^-2` and `10^-1`. Magnetic: a dipole or a coil pair providing a gradient of `10^-2` to `10 T/m` at the target, with the particle having a stated saturation magnetization.

## Solver and engine

- Flow: `pinneapple_simulation.numerical_solvers.internal_flow` and `lbm` for the branched geometry, with `lbm_tunnel` and `external_solvers.openfoam` for the higher-fidelity case; the analytical Poiseuille/Dean approximation in each branch is the cheap path used for the sweep and is compared against the computed field.
- Particle transport: `pinneapple_simulation.particle_dynamics.particles` and `molecular_dynamics` (for the stochastic integration conventions), with the Brownian term from the fluctuation-dissipation relation; the coupled particle-flow path is the work item.
- Field: `pinneapple_simulation.electromagnetics.statics` for the magnetic field and its gradient at the target, from the coil or dipole configuration.
- Verification: `pp.compare` between the approximation and the computed flow; `pinneapple_analysis.verification.convergence`; `pinneapple_analysis.uncertainty.margin.margin_study` for the parameter spread.
- Recording: `pinneapple_lab.experiment`.

## What is missing (the work)

1. **A Lagrangian particle tracker coupled to the flow solution**: `pinneapple_simulation/transport/lagrangian.py` with `track_particles(flow_field, particles, forces, dt, seed)` integrating the Langevin equation with drag, magnetic force, Brownian motion and wall repulsion, and a reporting of the particle mass-flow rate through each branch. Neither the coupled tracker nor a wall-repulsion model exists.
2. **A Brownian-motion integrator with the fluctuation-dissipation relation** at a stated temperature and local viscosity, with a convergence test against the analytic mean-square displacement `2Dt`, which is the reference and the correctness check.
3. **A margination model** at least as a stated lift force correlation for a particle in a shear field near a wall, with its source and its validity range, since without it the wall behaviour is wrong and the targeting fraction is wrong with it.
4. **A targeting metric layer**: fraction reaching the target region, mean time to target, branch selection probabilities, and the wall-accumulation fraction, computed from the particle histories.
5. **A branch-selection check**: the particle split ratio at each bifurcation compared with the flow split and with the streamline-based prediction, since a tracker that splits particles strictly by flow split is not modelling the interesting physics.
6. **A scope gate**: the four omissions are encoded as named assumptions in the run manifest, so a result cannot be quoted without them.

## Experiment protocol

1. Verify the flow solver on the parent vessel against the Poiseuille profile, and on the bifurcation against published velocity-field or particle-split data if available; report the comparison or state that none was available and that the approximation is used with its own error estimate.
2. Verify the Brownian integrator against `2Dt` at three time steps and two temperatures.
3. Verify the magnetic force against the analytic dipole-dipole and gradient force expressions.
4. Branch-selection baseline: passive particles with no field, giving the natural split, which is the reference for the pattern the field must change.
5. Steering sweep: 5 particle sizes by 3 Reynolds numbers by 4 field gradients by 3 geometries = 180 configurations, each with 10^4 particles (an ensemble, not an individual trajectory) and 3 seeds.
6. Threshold search: for each particle size, the field gradient at which the targeting fraction reaches 0.5, by bisection, and the vessel size at which it becomes unreachable with a stated maximum field.
7. Uncertainty: viscosity, particle size polydispersity and flow-rate variation, 200 draws.
8. Compute budget: seconds per flow solve, hours per ensemble — order 10^1 to 10^2 CPU-hours.

## Baselines, references and verification

- The Poiseuille and Dean profiles are the flow references; `2Dt` is the Brownian reference; the analytic dipole field and gradient force are the magnetic references. All three are closed form and all three must be reproduced before the ensemble runs.
- `pp.compare` between the analytical branch approximation and the computed flow field, with the difference reported as the approximation's error budget.
- Passive-particle branch split is the baseline for every steering result.
- Two independent flow paths (the in-house solver and OpenFOAM) on one geometry, so a targeting result is not resting on a single implementation.
- The wall repulsion is verified by checking that no particle crosses the wall over a long run, and that the particle distribution near the wall in the absence of the magnetic force matches the expected near-wall depletion or accumulation for the particle size used.

## Metrics

- Targeting fraction `eta`, the fraction of released particles that reach the target region, dimensionless, with the binomial confidence interval.
- Mean time to target, s, and its distribution, since a targeting fraction with a long tail is not a delivery metric.
- Branch selection probabilities at each bifurcation, dimensionless, against the flow split.
- Wall-accumulation fraction, dimensionless, reported separately from the lost fraction.
- Threshold field gradient `dG/dx*` at which `eta = 0.5`, T/m, with its bisection interval.
- Magnetic-to-drag force ratio and the Brownian-to-vessel displacement ratio, the two dimensionless groups, reported per configuration so the regime is legible.

## Deliverables

- The Lagrangian tracker, the Brownian integrator and the targeting metric layer as importable code with the three verification tests.
- The 180-configuration table with `eta`, time to target, branch splits and wall fraction, as CSV.
- The threshold table: the field gradient at which each particle size reaches the 0.5 targeting fraction, and the vessel size it cannot reach at maximum field.
- Figures: targeting fraction against particle size for each field strength, and against field gradient for each size, with the 0.5 line drawn.
- A particle-trajectory render through the bifurcation, coloured by fate, from the visualisation pipeline.
- A `pinneapple_lab` manifest per run, with the seeds, the particle count and the four named scope assumptions.
- A report whose first paragraph gives the targeting fraction at the stated baseline conditions and the size below which the field cannot steer at all.

## Acceptance criteria

- The flow solution reproduces the Poiseuille profile within 1 percent and the analytical branch approximation is compared with it with a reported error.
- The Brownian integrator reproduces the mean-square displacement `2Dt` within 3 percent over the sampled durations, at two temperatures.
- The magnetic force matches the analytic gradient-force expression within 2 percent at the sampled positions.
- No particle crosses the vessel wall in any reported run.
- Every targeting fraction is reported with its binomial confidence interval and with the particle count and seed count.
- The passive-particle branch split is reported alongside every steered split, and a steered split that does not differ from the passive one is reported as a null result rather than omitted.
- The report names the four scope assumptions — Newtonian fluid, rigid particles, no cell interactions, no biological interaction — in the same section as the targeting fraction, and makes no in-vivo claim.

## Realizability and evidence class

`PLAUSIBILITY_CLASS`: `established` for the transport physics and for magnetic steering at small scale, both demonstrated in vitro; `extrapolated` for a targeting fraction in a vessel tree of the stated size, because the omissions (blood rheology, cell interactions, particle deformability) are precisely the effects that dominate margination and thus the wall behaviour; `fictional` for a clinical therapeutic claim, which is not made here. Axes this run fills: `mathematical_consistency` (Poiseuille, `2Dt`, analytic force), `numerical_stability` (time-step convergence, seed repeatability, ensemble size), `physical_plausibility` (magnetic-to-drag ratios against achievable fields, residence times against realistic flows), `uncertainty_sensitivity` (viscosity, polydispersity, flow rate). `experimental_validation` is not assessed. `technological_feasibility` is partly addressed by the field-gradient threshold and the vessel-size limit.

To move up one class, in order: (1) an in-vitro experiment in a microfabricated branching channel with the same geometry and a measured targeting fraction, which would validate the transport model against the one thing it is trying to predict; (2) blood rheology and cell-particle interactions, since margination in blood is dominated by red-cell collisions and not by the lift correlation used here; (3) a steering field that can be produced at the required gradient over the required volume in a living subject, which is the hardware question; (4) a demonstrated safety and clearance profile, which is outside this experiment entirely. Item 1 is the next step and is within reach; item 2 is the modelling gap that would change the answer most.

## Risks and failure modes

1. **The margination model is a correlation with a narrow validity range used outside it.** Mitigation: its source and range are stated, and the sensitivity of `eta` to the lift coefficient is reported as a range rather than a point.
2. **The particle split is set by the flow split and the physics is trivial.** Mitigation: the branch-selection check is an acceptance item, and a split equal to the flow split is reported as a null result.
3. **The ensemble is too small and the targeting fraction is noise.** Mitigation: 10^4 particles per configuration, three seeds, and binomial or bootstrap confidence intervals reported with every fraction.
4. **The wall boundary is a numerical artifact.** Mitigation: the no-crossing check and the near-wall distribution check are both required, and a configuration that fails is excluded.
5. **The result is read as a delivery or a therapy claim.** Mitigation: the four scope assumptions are named in the same section as the number, and the report makes no in-vivo statement anywhere.

## Out of scope

- Drug loading, release kinetics and any therapeutic payload.
- Biocompatibility, toxicity, clearance and immunology.
- In-vivo behaviour, animal models and any clinical claim.
- Carrier fabrication and the particle synthesis route.
