## Summary

A structured barrier reduces the impulse transmitted to a protected region from a blast or a wave, and it is compared against a solid wall of the same mass and against no barrier at all. The physics is transient wave propagation in a heterogeneous medium with impedance mismatch and internal scattering, and the metrics are the transmitted impulse, the peak pressure and the transmitted energy. The claim is bounded at the outset: the barrier works over a stated band or pulse range, at a stated mass penalty, and it is not a universal shield.

## Question

At equal areal mass, does a structured barrier reduce the transmitted impulse and the peak pressure relative to a solid wall, over what range of pulse duration and frequency, and what is the mass penalty at which the advantage disappears?

## Why run it

- The equal-mass comparison against a solid wall is the correct control and is frequently omitted; a lightweight structure that beats nothing is not a result, and a structure that beats a heavy wall while weighing the same is.
- It is a transient wave problem in a heterogeneous medium, which is the capability the library lacks and which several issues in this series need.
- The band or pulse range over which the barrier works is a compact deliverable, and the frequency dependence is where the physics actually lives.

## Physical model

Linear elastodynamics or acoustics in a heterogeneous medium:

    rho u_tt = div(sigma),  sigma = C(x) : eps

with a piecewise or smoothly varying `C(x)`, an incoming pulse or a harmonic wave, and an absorbing boundary on the protected side so the transmitted quantity is measured and not reflected back. The barrier is a slab of thickness `H` with an internal structure: alternating layers of two impedances, a periodic lattice with a stated unit cell, or a graded impedance profile.

The transmission metrics come from the traction history at the protected interface: the transmitted impulse `I_t = ∫ sigma dt`, the peak pressure, and the transmitted energy. For the harmonic case the transmission coefficient `T(omega)` gives the band directly.

The mechanism is impedance mismatch and internal scattering: a periodic structure has band gaps whose positions are set by the unit-cell size and the impedance contrast, so a barrier is a stop-band filter and its band is narrow by construction. The dimensionless groups are the impedance contrast `Z_2/Z_1`, the ratio of the unit-cell size to the wavelength, the ratio of the barrier thickness to the pulse wavelength, and the ratio of the pulse duration to the barrier's transit time.

Validity and omission: linear elastodynamics; no plasticity, no fracture, no compaction, no spall. A real barrier under a near-field blast undergoes all four, so the model applies to the far-field or to a moderate acoustic loading and not to a close detonation, and the report must state that boundary with a number — the peak pressure above which the linear assumption fails — rather than with an adjective. It also omits the mounting and the joints, which in practice short-circuit the impedance mismatch.

## Geometry and inputs

Barrier configurations at a fixed areal mass of 50 kg/m² with a stated two-material set (steel and a polymer, and steel and a foam-like material with a stated modulus): a solid wall, a two-layer stack, a periodic stack of 8 and of 16 unit cells, a graded profile, and a lattice with a stated void fraction. Thickness follows from the mass constraint and is reported. Loading: an acoustic pulse with duration in {0.1, 0.5, 2, 10} ms and peak pressure in {10, 50, 200} kPa; a harmonic sweep from 100 Hz to 20 kHz for the same configurations. Protected region: a stated backing medium (air and water) so the impedance ratio is varied.

## Solver and engine

- In-process time-domain solver: `pinneapple_simulation.numerical_solvers.fdm` and `elasticity3d_fdm` for the one- and two-dimensional propagation, with an absorbing boundary condition added; `beam_fe` and `plane_stress_fem` for the two-dimensional structural check and a periodic-cell eigenvalue path for the band structure.
- Higher-fidelity cross-check: `pinneapple_simulation.external_solvers.calculix` and `openradioss` for an explicit dynamics run on two configurations, which is the independent implementation.
- Band structure and transmission: `pinneapple_analysis.inspection.acoustic` for the 1-D wave conventions and the spectral post-processing of the transmitted signal.
- Layout optimisation: `pinneapple_design.topology` for the material distribution within the barrier at fixed mass, with the wave objective.
- Uncertainty: `pinneapple_analysis.uncertainty.margin.margin_study` over the material moduli, the layer thicknesses and the mounting stiffness.
- Recording: `pinneapple_lab.experiment`.

## What is missing (the work)

1. **A two- and three-dimensional transient wave solver with an absorbing boundary**: the in-process path for a heterogeneous elastic or acoustic medium, with a PML or a first-order absorbing condition, and with the transmitted-traction recording at the protected interface. `elasticity3d_fdm` handles the elastic field and not the transient wave with a heterogeneous modulus and an absorber.
2. **A band-structure calculator for a periodic unit cell**: `bloch_bands(cell, materials, k_path)` returning the dispersion relation, so the stop bands are computed and compared with the transmission minima. Without it the barrier's band is an observed outcome and not a predicted design quantity.
3. **An equal-mass barrier generator** producing each configuration family at a fixed areal mass, with the thickness derived and reported, so no comparison is at unequal mass.
4. **An impulse and peak-pressure metric layer** with the incident pulse deconvolved and reported, so the reduction is against the applied pulse and not a nominal figure.
5. **A mounting-imperfection model**: a stated joint stiffness or a bypass path around the barrier, included in at least one configuration, with its effect on the transmission reported. A real barrier's performance is often limited by how it is fixed, not by its interior.
6. **A linearity gate**: the peak pressure above which the linear model's assumption fails for the stated material, computed and reported, so no result is quoted outside the model's validity.

## Experiment protocol

1. Verify the time-domain solver against the analytic one-dimensional pulse in a homogeneous medium, and against the closed-form reflection and transmission coefficients at a single impedance interface, at three mesh refinements.
2. Verify the absorbing boundary by propagating a pulse to the boundary with nothing behind it and reporting the reflection coefficient of the boundary itself; this number bounds every transmission result.
3. Verify the band-structure calculator against the analytic dispersion for a periodic layered medium, which is closed form.
4. Equal-mass configuration set: 6 families by 2 backing media, with the mass constraint verified and reported per case.
5. Transient sweep: 4 pulse durations by 3 peak pressures by 12 base configurations = 144 runs, each recording transmitted impulse, peak pressure and energy.
6. Harmonic sweep: 12 base configurations over 100 Hz to 20 kHz at 200 points, giving the transmission spectrum and the stop bands.
7. Optimisation: the material layout optimised at fixed mass for the lowest transmitted impulse at one pulse duration, with 5 restarts.
8. Cross-check: two configurations through the CalculiX or OpenRadioss explicit path.
9. Uncertainty: material modulus, layer thickness and joint stiffness varied, 200 draws, reporting the distribution of the reduction.
10. Compute budget: minutes per two-dimensional run, of order 10^2 to 10^3 runs — order 10^2 CPU-hours.

## Baselines, references and verification

- The analytic homogeneous pulse, the closed-form interface coefficients and the analytic periodic-layer dispersion are the three references, each closed form and each checked before the campaign.
- The absorbing boundary's own reflection coefficient is a numerical-quality metric that bounds the achievable transmission result, and it is reported for every run set.
- The solid wall at equal mass is the primary baseline, and no barrier configuration is reported without it.
- No barrier at all is the second baseline: it defines the incident pulse's impulse and peak, which the reductions are expressed against.
- The CalculiX or OpenRadioss explicit run is the independent implementation check.
- Energy conservation: incident equals reflected plus transmitted plus absorbed, within numerical tolerance, at every run.

## Metrics

- Transmitted impulse `I_t`, N·s/m², and the reduction relative to the solid wall at equal mass, percent, which is the headline.
- Peak transmitted pressure, kPa, and its ratio to the incident peak.
- Transmission spectrum `T(f)`, dB, with the stop-band edges at a stated attenuation.
- Areal mass, kg/m², reported per configuration, and thickness, mm, derived from it.
- Reduction relative to no barrier, percent, reported as the second comparison and never as the first.
- Uncertainty spread on the reduction, as an interval from the Monte Carlo.
- Linear-model validity limit: the peak pressure at which the material's linear assumption fails, kPa.

## Deliverables

- The transient solver with the absorbing boundary, the band-structure calculator, the equal-mass generator and the metric layer as importable code with the verification tests.
- The transmission spectra and the band structures for all configurations, as data and figures, with the stop bands marked.
- The 144-run transient table with the mass and thickness per row, as CSV.
- The comparison figure: reduction against solid wall at equal mass, against pulse duration, for the six families, with the optimised case and the joint-imperfection case included.
- A wave-propagation animation for the best case and for the solid wall, side by side, from the video pipeline.
- A `pinneapple_lab` manifest per run, with the pulse parameters, material properties and joint stiffness.
- A report whose first paragraph gives the reduction at equal mass and the range over which it holds.

## Acceptance criteria

- The solver reproduces the analytic interface coefficients within 2 percent and the homogeneous pulse within 2 percent at the finest mesh, with monotone convergence.
- The absorbing boundary's reflection coefficient is below a stated threshold (1 percent is the target) for the run set; a higher value invalidates the transmission result and is reported as such.
- The band-structure calculator reproduces the analytic periodic-layer dispersion within 1 percent at the sampled wave vectors.
- Every configuration's areal mass is within 1 percent of the target, reported with its thickness.
- The energy partition closes within 1 percent for every reported run; a run that fails is excluded and listed.
- The reduction is reported against both baselines, with the equal-mass solid wall as the primary, and against the optimised configuration; a comparison against no barrier alone fails the issue.
- The stop-band edges from the spectrum match the predicted band edges from the band structure, and any mismatch is reported.
- No result is quoted above the peak pressure at which the linear assumption fails.

## Realizability and evidence class

`PLAUSIBILITY_CLASS`: `established` for wave propagation in a layered medium and for impedance-mismatch shielding, both standard acoustics; `extrapolated` for a barrier of the stated performance under a blast-like loading, because nonlinearity, spall and the mounting dominate in that regime and none are modelled; `hypothetical` for a barrier that attenuates over a wide band without a mass penalty, which the band-gap physics excludes. Axes this run fills: `mathematical_consistency` (closed-form coefficients, analytic dispersion, energy partition), `numerical_stability` (absorbing-boundary reflection, mesh convergence, cross-implementation), `physical_plausibility` (material moduli and densities, the linearity limit), `uncertainty_sensitivity` (modulus, thickness, joint stiffness). `experimental_validation` is not assessed: no barrier is tested. `technological_feasibility` is partly addressed by the mass and thickness budget.

To move up one class, in order: (1) a measured transmission spectrum for a fabricated barrier under a stated acoustic or shock loading, since the joints, the mounting and the material damping all move the answer and none are modelled; (2) a nonlinear material model and a failure criterion, since the near-field regime is where such barriers are claimed to work and where the linear model is invalid; (3) a demonstration at equal mass against a solid wall, which is the comparison that a laboratory test must make and that published claims often omit; (4) a multi-loading condition, since a barrier tuned for one pulse duration is a filter and not a shield. Item 1 is the decisive step and the equal-mass condition in item 3 is the one that decides whether the result stands.

## Risks and failure modes

1. **The absorbing boundary reflects and manufactures an attenuation.** Mitigation: its reflection coefficient is measured and reported, and the run set is invalid below a stated threshold; this is the first acceptance item for a reason.
2. **The comparison is at unequal mass.** Mitigation: the mass is enforced in the generator and verified per row, and thickness is derived from it.
3. **The result is quoted outside the linear regime.** Mitigation: the linearity limit is computed from the material and reported with the numbers.
4. **The mounting is omitted and the modelled performance is unattainable.** Mitigation: a joint-stiffness and bypass case is included, and its reduction in performance is reported.
5. **A single frequency or a single pulse duration is reported as the barrier's performance.** Mitigation: the spectrum and the four pulse durations are both required, and the band edges are an acceptance item.

## Out of scope

- Blast-structure interaction, near-field detonation and any explosive-specific scenario.
- Nonlinear material behaviour, compaction, fracture and spall.
- Design for a specific protection standard or certification.
- Barrier construction, mounting hardware and cost.
