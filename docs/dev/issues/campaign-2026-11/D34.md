## Summary

A three-dimensional environment is built so that its local geometry is ordinary everywhere and its global connectivity is not: walking in a straight direction returns the traveller to a place that is elsewhere, light follows the same identified boundaries, and gravity stays coherent with the floor. The experiment produces the environment, a loop-closure test that verifies the identification is consistent, and the list of points at which a viewer can notice the construction. Every path is computable; there is no step that is explained by it being a game.

## Question

For an environment built by identifying the faces of a polyhedral fundamental domain, does every closed walk return the traveller to the same state, and at which locations does the identification become visually detectable?

## Why run it

- Identified-boundary spaces are real mathematics with known examples — a flat three-torus, a twisted bundle — and building one that a person can walk through tests whether the transformation layer is correct, which is a different and stricter test than rendering a picture of it.
- The loop-closure test is the honest criterion: an environment that looks convincing but fails to close is a rendering trick, and the test tells the two apart.
- It shares the identification machinery with D16 and D38, so the marginal cost is the navigation layer and the audit, and the three issues form one coherent piece of work.

## Physical model

The space is a quotient of Euclidean three-space by a discrete group of isometries: a fundamental domain (a cube, a hexagonal prism, or a rectangular box with a twist identification) and a set of face pairings that generate the group. A point in the environment is an equivalence class `[x]`, and a walk is a path in the cover, projected down.

Three physical layers must agree for the construction to be coherent:

1. **Geometry.** Rigid-body motion with collisions against the domain faces, where a crossing maps the state through the pairing transformation rather than stopping at a wall. The transformation includes a rotation, so a walker that crosses a twisted face returns rotated, and the composition of two crossings must equal the group product.
2. **Light.** Straight-line propagation in the cover, projected to the domain: a ray that leaves a face re-enters at the paired face with the paired direction. This is what makes distant parts of the environment visible through the identification, and it is the mechanism by which the construction is visible at all.
3. **Gravity.** A stated constant field with a stated direction relative to the domain, so that floors stay floors and the walker's "down" is coherent across a crossing. A pairing that maps the gravity direction to something else is a design choice that must be stated, since it changes whether the space feels like a room or like a puzzle.

The loop-closure test is the core: for a closed walk in the cover, the accumulated transformation must be the identity in the group, and the numerical test is that the state after the walk equals the state before it to a stated tolerance. A walk whose accumulated transformation is a non-identity group element closes in the space but returns the walker transformed, and that is a correct outcome of a non-simply-connected space rather than an error — so the test must distinguish "consistent" from "identity", which is the subtlety.

Dimensionless groups and parameters: the ratio of the domain size to the walker size, the holonomy of the group (which determines the residual rotation on a loop), and the ratio of the corridor length to the identification scale. Validity: this is geometry and rigid-body kinematics. It says nothing about whether such a space could exist physically — it cannot, in three dimensions with Euclidean local geometry, except as a quotient that requires identification — and that distinction is the whole point of the exercise and must be stated.

## Geometry and inputs

Domains: a 40 m cube with opposite faces identified (a three-torus), the same with a 90 degree twist on one pairing (a non-orientable-like bundle), a hexagonal prism with a 60 degree rotation identification on the ends, and a rectangular box with 2:1 aspect and a half-turn identification. Content inside the domain: a corridor network, a set of pillars at known positions, and a marker object placed at a known point so that its multiple images serve as the loop-closure visual.

## Solver and engine

- Scene construction: `pinneapple_twin3d.scene` (`Scene`, `add_part`, `add_field`, `export`, `with_viewer=True`, and the `usd=True` path with `add_physics`/`set_gravity`), which supplies the geometry, the viewer and the glb export.
- Rendering: `pinneapple_blender.cycles` for the images and `pinneapple_blender.render.build_scene` for the headless path; the identification-aware ray tracing is new code that reuses the camera and scene conventions.
- Navigation and collision: a ray-cast-based walker in the experiment module, with the collision handled against the domain faces and the crossing handled by the pairing transformation.
- Trajectory and closure checks: the numerical integrators in `pinneapple_simulation.numerical_solvers` for the walker's continuous motion, and `pinneapple_analysis.verification` for the audit's own consistency.
- Recording: `pinneapple_lab.experiment`.

## What is missing (the work)

1. **A paired-boundary geometry layer**: `pinneapple_twin3d/topology/identified.py` holding the fundamental domain, the face pairings with their transformations, and `map_point(x, face)` and `map_direction(d, face)` returning the paired location and direction. Identified boundaries exist in the fluid solver work of D16 and not in the geometry or scene layer.
2. **A walker with crossing transforms**: `Walker` with position, orientation and velocity, collision against the faces and the crossing transformation applied on contact, including the composition of two crossings. This is the navigation test that nothing in the library performs.
3. **A loop-closure auditor**: `close_loop(walker, path)` returning the accumulated transformation, whether it is the identity, and the residual. The distinction between "returns transformed" and "fails to close" is the whole scientific content and must be an explicit return value, not an inference.
4. **An identification-aware ray tracer** for the visual: a ray that crosses a face continues in the paired direction, with a stated bounce budget, so the multiple images of the marker are geometrically correct rather than painted.
5. **A detectability audit**: the set of positions and orientations from which a viewer can see the same object twice, or see a corridor that should be occluded, computed by sampling viewpoints and reporting the fraction. This is the deliverable that says where the trick is visible.
6. **A consistency gate on the pairings**: the group generated by the pairings is checked for the relations that make it a proper quotient (the pairings compose to the identity where they must), before any content is placed.

## Experiment protocol

1. Verify the pairing layer on the three-torus: a straight walk that crosses each face once must return to its start position with the identity transformation, and the analytic check is that the accumulated translation equals the domain period.
2. Verify the twisted pairing: the accumulated transformation after a loop must equal the stated holonomy, which is closed form for a single twist; the test compares against that value.
3. Verify the ray tracer on the three-torus against the analytic image positions for the marker object: the image lattice is closed form, and the first four images must be at their predicted coordinates.
4. Walk campaign: 4 domains by 50 random closed walks each, reporting the accumulated transformation, the identity fraction, and the residual distribution.
5. Detectability audit: 4 domains by 500 sampled viewpoints, reporting the fraction from which an anomaly is visible and a map of the sensitive regions.
6. Content check: the corridor network and pillars placed in one domain, with a walkthrough rendered, to confirm that the content does not accidentally give away the identification.
7. Compute budget: the walk and audit are minutes; the renders are the cost — order 10^1 to 10^2 CPU-hours.

## Baselines, references and verification

- The three-torus aperiodicity and its image lattice are the analytic reference for both the walker and the ray tracer; a construction that does not reproduce them is wrong.
- The single-twist holonomy is the second analytic reference, and it is the one that distinguishes a correct twisted identification from an untwisted one.
- Composition consistency: crossing a face and crossing back must produce the identity transformation, and this is checked at every crossing, which is a cheap invariant that catches most pairing errors.
- A plain un-identified room is the baseline for the detectability audit: the anomaly fraction from it should be zero, and a nonzero value indicates a bug in the audit.
- The rendered image from a stated viewpoint is compared against an independently computed ray-traced image from the same camera, so the renderer is verified against the tracer.

## Metrics

- Closure identity fraction: the fraction of closed walks whose accumulated transformation is the identity, dimensionless.
- Residual closure error for the identity walks, in metres and radians, against the stated tolerance.
- Holonomy distribution for the twisted domains: the rotation angle and translation of the accumulated transformation, which should cluster at the group's elements.
- Anomaly-visible fraction: the fraction of sampled viewpoints from which the same object is visible twice or a corridor is visible through a wall, dimensionless.
- Sensitive-region area, m², as a map.
- Ray-budget exhaustion fraction for the rendered images, so the visual's numerical quality is auditable.

## Deliverables

- The paired-boundary layer, the walker, the loop-closure auditor and the identification-aware ray tracer as importable code with the analytic tests.
- The environment for each of the four domains, as scene files with the viewer, so a reader can walk it.
- The walk table: accumulated transformations, identity fraction and residuals, as CSV.
- The anomaly map per domain, as a figure, with the sensitive regions marked on the floor plan.
- Rendered stills and a walkthrough video from the campaign pipeline, with the anomaly locations annotated in a second version.
- A `pinneapple_lab` manifest per domain, with the pairing definition and the seeds.
- A report whose first paragraph states what the loop-closure test showed and where the construction is detectable.

## Acceptance criteria

- The three-torus straight walk returns to its start position within 1e-9 relative to the domain size, with the identity transformation.
- The twisted pairing's accumulated transformation matches the analytic holonomy within 1e-9 in rotation and 1e-9 relative in translation.
- Crossing a face and immediately crossing back produces the identity transformation at every sampled crossing, with no exception.
- The ray tracer reproduces the first four analytic image positions of the marker within one pixel at the stated camera resolution.
- The detectability audit returns zero anomalies for the plain un-identified room baseline; a nonzero result fails the issue.
- Every closed walk is classified as identity, non-identity group element, or failure to close, and the three counts sum to the walk total.
- The report states that the space cannot exist as a three-dimensional Euclidean object and is a quotient construction, in the same section as the environment description.

## Realizability and evidence class

`PLAUSIBILITY_CLASS`: `established` as a geometry and computer-graphics construction, since quotients of Euclidean space by isometry groups are standard mathematics and have been rendered before; `hypothetical` as a physical space, because a locally Euclidean three-dimensional space with nontrivial topology can be a quotient but cannot be embedded in three dimensions, and the identification is not a mechanism; `fictional` as an engineered structure. Axes this run fills: `mathematical_consistency` (analytic three-torus, analytic holonomy, composition identity, image lattice), `numerical_stability` (closure residuals, ray-budget adequacy), `physical_plausibility` in the narrow sense that gravity and light are treated consistently and their disagreement would be detectable. `experimental_validation` is not assessed. `technological_feasibility` is not assessed; the environment is a computational object.

There is no physical class to move up. The honest boundary is that the construction demonstrates the consistency of a quotient geometry and says nothing about how a physical identification could be realised, which in three dimensions it cannot be. The report must state that, and the value of the issue is the audit tooling plus the demonstration that a locally ordinary space can be globally strange without any inconsistent step.

## Risks and failure modes

1. **The rendering carries the argument and the consistency is not tested.** Mitigation: the loop-closure audit is a required deliverable with acceptance criteria, and the renders are secondary outputs.
2. **The walker's crossing transformation is applied as a translation only, and a twisted pairing silently becomes untwisted.** Mitigation: the single-twist holonomy is an analytic acceptance test and would fail; the composition invariant is checked at every crossing.
3. **The detectability audit is a bug and reports a clean result.** Mitigation: the plain-room baseline must return zero, and a domain with a deliberately unpaired face is included as a positive control that must return a high anomaly fraction.
4. **The viewer cannot represent the identification and the scene looks broken.** Mitigation: the exported scene carries the pairing data and the identification-aware viewer path is used, with a stated fallback that shows the fundamental domain only.
5. **The gravity direction is inconsistent across a crossing and the "coherent gravity" claim is false.** Mitigation: the gravity mapping is a stated design choice per pairing and the walker's down vector is checked after each crossing, with the result reported.

## Out of scope

- Hyperbolic and other non-Euclidean geometries; this is the Euclidean quotient case only.
- Multi-user networking, persistence and any game mechanics.
- Non-Euclidean physics beyond gravity direction and light propagation.
- Any claim about the physical realisation of an identified space, and any portal-transport device claim.
