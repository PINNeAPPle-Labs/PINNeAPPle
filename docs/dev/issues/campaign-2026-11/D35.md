## Summary

The radiation-pressure force available from a structured light field is computed for a range of structures and powers, and compared with the weight of any macroscopic object and with the forces that hold a small particle in a trap. The answer is a table in newtons per watt, with the object-size scaling attached, and the conclusion it supports is not a matter of degree: at any power a laboratory can deliver, the force on a macroscopic rigid structure is smaller than its weight by many orders of magnitude, and the regime where the force matters is trapped micro-particles. This is the short issue of the series and it is deliberately so.

## Question

At a stated input power, what force does a structured light field exert on an object of stated size, and at which object size does that force exceed the object's weight?

## Why run it

- It is the cleanest available example of a claim that is resolved by a single division: force over weight, and the ratio is so small that no refinement of the concept can rescue it.
- The number is worth having exactly: newtons per watt, with the scaling with object size, is the figure that a discussion about such a structure should start from, and it is small enough to be memorable.
- The trap case is genuinely established engineering, so the same calculation that refutes the large-scale claim also calibrates against measured trap stiffnesses, which is the verification the experiment needs.

## Physical model

Radiation pressure on a perfectly absorbing object at normal incidence is `F = P/c`; on a perfectly reflecting object it is `2P/c`. For a structured field, the additional possibility is a gradient force, but a gradient force requires a field gradient over the object's extent, and a gradient over a macroscopic object is bounded by the field's own wavelength-scale structure, so the gradient force scales with the object's volume while the field can only be shaped at its wavelength.

**Force per unit power.** For an object of projected area `A` intercepting a beam of power `P` spread over area `A_beam`, the incident intensity is `I = P/A_beam`, and the force is `F = (2 I A / c) * f`, where `f` depends on the reflectance and the geometry (equal to one for a perfect reflector at normal incidence). The maximum force per watt is therefore `2/c`, about 6.7 nN/W, and that is an upper bound for any arrangement that does not concentrate the field below the diffraction limit.

**The comparison.** A 1 kg object weighs about 9.8 N. At 6.7 nN/W, holding it requires of order 1.5 GW of intercepted power. A 1 g object requires about 1.5 MW. The ratio is the whole result, and it is independent of the structure: no arrangement of optical binding or of an optical lattice changes the momentum flux per watt, because the momentum flux is what the watt is.

**The trap case.** For a particle of radius `a`, the gradient force is `F ≈ (2 pi a^3 / c) ((m^2-1)/(m^2+2)) grad(I)` and the scattering force `F_sc = (128 pi^5 a^6 / (3 lambda^4 c)) ((m^2-1)/(m^2+2))^2 I`. The ratio of gradient to scattering scales as `lambda^4/a^3`, which is why trapping works below about 100 nm to a few micrometres and fails above it. For a 1 µm particle with a stated numerical aperture and 100 mW, the achievable trap stiffness is of order 10^-6 to 10^-5 N/m, and the measured values are in that range — that agreement is the verification.

Validity and omission: classical electrodynamics with the Maxwell stress tensor, in the Rayleigh regime for the small particle and geometric optics for the large object. The model omits the mechanical response of any real structure, the thermal load (which for the powers involved would destroy any material before the force mattered), and it says nothing about whether a light-formed structure could be self-supporting — the energy density required exceeds that of any material by orders of magnitude, and that is reported as a separate number rather than argued.

## Geometry and inputs

Structures: a collimated beam, a focused Gaussian beam at three numerical apertures, a standing-wave lattice, and an optical-binding configuration of two counter-propagating beams. Objects: a perfect reflector plate of 1 cm² and of 1 m²; a sphere of radius 0.1, 1, 10, 100 µm and 1 mm with a stated refractive index; and a stated 1 kg reference mass. Powers: 1 mW, 100 mW, 1 W, 100 W, 1 MW. One additional calculation: the energy density required for a light field to exert a pressure equal to the yield strength of a stated structural material, which is the number that decides whether a light structure could support a load at all.

## What is missing (the work)

1. **A radiation-force calculator**: `pinneapple_physics/radiation_force.py` with `force_per_power(reflectance, geometry)` returning the force per watt with its derivation stated, `gradient_force(a, n, wavelength, intensity_gradient)` and `scattering_force(...)`, plus a `force_to_weight_ratio(...)` helper. The trap machinery is shared with D32 and this module is the thin version of it.
2. **A Maxwell-stress cross-check** for the structured-field cases, computed by surface integration of the stress tensor over a closed surface around the object, so the closed-form forces are verified against the field solution where a field solution exists.
3. **The Mie crossover**: the Rayleigh and Mie paths computed on the same object over a size sweep, with the crossover size and the disagreement at the crossover reported.
4. **The self-support calculation**: the energy density required for a light field to develop a pressure equal to a stated material's yield strength, and the comparison with the energy density of the field at the damage threshold of any real mirror, which is the number that decides whether a light structure can be load-bearing.
5. **A scaling table generator** producing the newtons-per-watt and force-to-weight entries across the object and power ranges, with the validity regime of each entry.
6. **A verdict record** through `pp.futures`, so the `CONTRADICTED` outcome for a macroscopic rigid structure and the `established` outcome for trapping are recorded as assessments and not as prose.

## Experiment protocol

1. Verify `F = 2P/c` against a stated measured radiation-pressure result on a reflecting surface, and against the Maxwell-stress integration for a plane wave on a plate, which must agree to numerical tolerance.
2. Verify the gradient and scattering forces against the Mie solution in the size range where Mie applies, and against measured trap stiffnesses for the optical tweezers case, reporting the agreement.
3. Scaling sweep: 5 object sizes by 5 powers by 4 field structures = 100 rows, each with force, force per watt, weight, ratio and validity flag.
4. Crossover determination: the object size at which the gradient force stops exceeding the scattering force, for three wavelengths, computed by the Mie path.
5. Self-support calculation: the required energy density for a load-bearing field, for three materials, compared with the achievable energy density at the mirror damage threshold.
6. Record the two `pp.futures` verdicts with their axis scores.
7. Compute budget: closed forms are instant, the Mie and stress-tensor paths are seconds — order 10^0 CPU-hours.

## Baselines, references and verification

- `F = 2P/c` and the measured radiation-pressure results are the primary references; the Maxwell-stress integration is the independent route.
- The Mie solution is the reference above the Rayleigh limit, and the trap stiffness measurements are the reference for the small-object case.
- Weight is the comparison that ends the macroscopic claim, and it is a baseline with no adjustable parameter.
- The mirror damage threshold bounds the achievable intensity, and any row whose required intensity exceeds it is labelled as unattainable rather than reported as a design.

## Metrics

- Force `F`, N, and force per watt `F/P`, N/W, per row.
- Force-to-weight ratio, dimensionless, by construction the headline number.
- Trap stiffness `k`, N/m, for the small-object case, against the measured range.
- Gradient-to-scattering force ratio, dimensionless, and the crossover size.
- Required intensity and its comparison with the damage threshold, dimensionless.
- Self-support: required field energy density against the material's yield strength pressure, as a ratio.
- Verdict per claim: `CONTRADICTED`, `CONDITIONAL` or `established`, from `pp.futures`.

## Deliverables

- The radiation-force module and the scaling-table generator as importable code with the `2P/c` and Mie verification tests.
- The 100-row scaling table, as CSV and as a rendered table, with the validity flag and the required intensity per row.
- The force-to-weight figure, log scale, with the weight line and the trap regime marked.
- The crossover figure: gradient and scattering force against particle size for three wavelengths.
- The self-support table for three materials, with the ratio to the damage-limited intensity.
- The two recorded `pp.futures` verdicts with their per-axis scores.
- A report whose first paragraph gives newtons per watt and the power needed to hold one kilogram.

## Acceptance criteria

- The computed force on a perfect reflector matches `2P/c` within 0.1 percent and the Maxwell-stress integration within 1 percent.
- The gradient and scattering forces match the Mie solution within 5 percent in the Mie regime, and the crossover size is reported.
- The trap stiffness agrees with the measured literature range for the stated particle and power, or the discrepancy is reported and explained.
- Every table row states its validity regime and its required intensity; no row is reported whose required intensity exceeds the stated damage threshold without that label.
- The force-to-weight ratio is computed for the 1 kg reference and reported in the summary as a number with its power requirement.
- The self-support calculation is reported as a ratio and the report states plainly whether a light field could be load-bearing.
- The verdict for a macroscopic rigid structure is `CONTRADICTED` and for trapping is `established`, and both are recorded through `pp.futures` rather than asserted in prose.

## Realizability and evidence class

`PLAUSIBILITY_CLASS`: `established` for radiation pressure and for optical trapping, both measured and used in instruments; `contradicted` for a macroscopic rigid structure formed and held by light, which the force-to-weight ratio excludes by eight or more orders of magnitude at attainable powers; `fictional` for a load-bearing structure of light. Axes this run fills: `mathematical_consistency` (`2P/c`, Maxwell stress, Mie), `numerical_stability` (crossover determination, field-solution convergence), `physical_plausibility` (measured trap stiffnesses, damage thresholds), `uncertainty_sensitivity` (power and material variation). `experimental_validation` is not performed here, though the subject is experimentally established at small scale and the report should credit the measurements. `technological_feasibility` is assessed only in the negative direction, through the intensity requirement against the damage threshold.

To move a hypothetical variant up one class — an optical structure that is not held by radiation pressure but merely shaped by light, for instance a photo-polymerised scaffold — the requirements would be: (1) a distinction between a structure formed by light and a structure held by it, since the second is what fails; (2) a material with a damage threshold several orders above the best available mirror; (3) a mechanism other than momentum transfer for the load path, since momentum flux per watt is fixed by the watt; (4) a statement of what would be tested, which at present would be a measurement of an effect that the ratio already rules out. Item 3 is the only one that could change the verdict, and it is a different subject.

## Risks and failure modes

1. **The calculation is read as an engineering discouragement when it is a physical bound.** Mitigation: the report states which quantity is bounded (momentum flux per watt) and which is not (everything about using light to shape material).
2. **The trap case is conflated with the macroscopic case in the summary.** Mitigation: the two verdicts are recorded separately through `pp.futures` and both appear in the summary.
3. **The Mie crossover is asserted rather than computed.** Mitigation: the crossover is computed from the two paths on the same object and reported with the disagreement at the boundary.
4. **The self-support number is presented as a design constraint rather than an impossibility.** Mitigation: it is reported as a ratio with the damage-limited intensity, and the report states what the ratio means.
5. **A structure's mechanical strength is ignored and the force is compared with weight alone.** Mitigation: the report notes that weight is the most favourable comparison available, so the conclusion is stronger, not weaker, than the number suggests.

## Out of scope

- Any design of a light-formed structure or of its optical system.
- Thermal effects and material damage beyond the threshold comparison.
- Optical binding between many particles, and collective optical matter, except as a marginal note on the scaling.
- Any application in space, where weight is absent but inertia is not.
