## Summary

A device creates a flow that turns approaching dust particles or droplets away from a protected region, using moving air instead of a solid wall. The effectiveness is governed by the particle Stokes number, and that is the scientific content: a particle much smaller than the flow's turning scale follows the flow and is deflected, a particle much larger keeps its momentum and passes through the deflector, and the crossover is a number. The experiment produces the deflection efficiency as a function of Stokes number, particle size and wind speed, plus the particle size below which the device's own flow cannot help because the particles simply follow it around, and the power the device needs.

## Question

For a stated deflector geometry and jet momentum, what fraction of approaching particles is prevented from entering the protected region, as a function of particle Stokes number, and at which particle size does the deflector become ineffective for each of the two reasons — too little inertia to be separated, or too much?

## Why run it

- The Stokes number dependence is a genuine threshold phenomenon with a non-monotonic answer: efficiency first rises with particle size and then falls, and the peak is where the design should sit. That shape is the result.
- Air curtains and jet deflectors are used industrially and in dust-protection contexts, so a quantitative efficiency map is directly useful rather than speculative.
- It exercises a coupled flow-and-particle capability with a clean metric, which is the piece the library needs for deposition and aerosol problems generally.

## Physical model

The continuous phase is incompressible flow past a body, with a jet issuing from the device into an oncoming cross-flow:

    div u = 0,  du/dt + (u·grad)u = -grad p/rho + nu lap(u),  plus the jet inlet condition

The dispersed phase is Lagrangian, with each particle satisfying

    m_p dv/dt = 3 pi mu d_p (u - v) f_D  + m_p g  + F_Saffman + F_added

The Stokes number is `St = tau_p U / L`, with `tau_p = rho_p d_p^2 / (18 mu)` the particle relaxation time, `U` the approach speed and `L` the characteristic length of the flow's turning (the jet diameter or the deflector's width, and the choice must be stated because the answer depends on it).

Two limits are the whole physics. For `St << 1` the particle follows the streamlines, so the flow decides where it goes and the deflector works if the flow turns. For `St >> 1` the particle is ballistic and passes through the deflected flow; the flow cannot stop it, only slow it, and eventually only a solid barrier can. The crossover is at `St` of order one, and there the Saffman lift and the added mass, which are small elsewhere, matter.

Validity and omission: incompressible flow, one-way coupling (the particles do not modify the flow, which is reasonable at the loadings used and must be checked against the stated mass loading), no turbulence modelling beyond a stated approach, no particle-particle collisions, and no wall deposition model beyond a sticking probability. The model omits electrostatics, which for dust is often the dominant adhesion mechanism, and it omits the device's own jet becoming a dust source by entraining particles. Those omissions are stated because a real dust-protection claim would live or die on them.

## Geometry and inputs

Deflector geometries: a single slot jet at 30, 60 and 90 degrees to the cross-flow; a pair of opposed jets forming an air curtain; and a curved-surface Coanda deflector. Jet exit velocity from 5 to 40 m/s through a 20 mm slot; approach wind speed from 0.5 to 10 m/s. Particles: diameters from 1 to 500 µm, densities of 1000 and 2650 kg/m³ (a droplet and a sand grain), released upstream in a stated distribution across the protected aperture. Protected region: a 0.3 m by 0.3 m aperture 0.5 m downstream of the deflector.

## Solver and engine

- Continuous phase: `pinneapple_simulation.numerical_solvers.immersed_boundary_fdm` and `cylinder_wake` for the in-process path, `internal_flow`/`external_flow` for the inlet and the domain, and `external_solvers.openfoam` for the higher-fidelity case (a standard incompressible solver with a jet inlet).
- Dispersed phase: a Lagrangian tracker built on `pinneapple_simulation.particle_dynamics.particles`, shared with D31, with the drag correlation, the Saffman lift and the added mass.
- Droplet-specific: `pinneapple_simulation.numerical_solvers.sph` and `isph` for one configuration where the droplet breakup or the coalescence is wanted, explicitly as an excursion and not as the main path.

## What is missing (the work)

1. **A Lagrangian particle tracker with the drag, Saffman lift and added-mass terms**: `pinneapple_simulation/transport/lagrangian.py` (shared with D31), with `track(flow, particles, dt, seed)` and per-particle fate reporting — entered the protected region, deflected past it, or deposited. Neither the lift term nor the fate classification exists today.
2. **A deflection-efficiency metric**: `efficiency(fates)` returning the fraction prevented from entering the region, with the denominator defined as the particles that would have entered with no deflector at all, which requires the undeflected baseline run to be a required part of the metric.
3. **A Stokes-number calculation with the length scale stated as a design parameter**, since the efficiency curve depends on which `L` is used and a curve without its `L` is uninterpretable.
4. **A two-way coupling check**: the mass loading at which the one-way assumption breaks, computed from the particle volume fraction, and one two-way run to show the difference. The one-way assumption is the model's main approximation and it must be quantified.
5. **A jet-into-crossflow validation**: the flow solution against published jet-in-crossflow data (centreline decay, penetration depth), since the deflected jet's shape is what the particle deflection depends on and the library's existing cases are not jets.
6. **A sticking and deposition model** with a stated probability, so the deposited fraction is bounded rather than ignored, and the distinction between deflected and deposited is maintained.

## Experiment protocol

1. Verify the flow solver on the deflector-less case against published flow past a blunt body, and on the jet-in-crossflow against the centreline decay and penetration depth data, at three mesh refinements.
2. Verify the particle tracker against the analytic response of a particle to a step change in the flow velocity, which gives the relaxation time, and against a sedimentation case with a known terminal velocity.
3. Undeflected baseline: the particle flux through the protected aperture with no device, per size and wind speed, which is the denominator of the efficiency.
4. Deflection sweep: 5 geometries by 5 jet velocities by 5 wind speeds by 8 particle sizes = 1000 flow-particle runs, with 10^4 particles each and three seeds.
5. Efficiency curve: for each geometry and wind speed, the efficiency against Stokes number, with the peak located and reported.
6. Size limits: for each configuration, the particle size below which the efficiency falls because the particles follow the flow, and above which it falls because they do not, both reported as numbers.
7. Power: the device's air power from the jet velocity and the mass flow, against the deflected flux.
8. Two-way coupling: the mass loading at which the one-way assumption breaks, plus one two-way run at that loading.
9. Compute budget: minutes to hours per three-dimensional flow solve, of order 10^2 solves — order 10^2 to 10^3 CPU-hours, dominated by the flow.

## Baselines, references and verification

- The blunt-body and jet-in-crossflow data are the flow references, and the step-response and terminal-velocity cases are the tracker references.
- The undeflected flux through the aperture is the baseline the efficiency is expressed against, and it is a required run for every configuration, not a nominal number.
- A solid wall across the aperture is the physical alternative: the deflector's efficiency is compared with it, and its cost in obstruction is stated, since a wall is trivially efficient.
- The one-way to two-way comparison is an internal consistency check on the model's central approximation.
- Energy: the jet's kinetic power against the deflected particle flux gives a cost per unit of protected flux, which is the number that decides whether the device is worth building.

## Metrics

- Deflection efficiency `eta`, fraction of the undeflected flux prevented from entering the protected region, dimensionless, with a confidence interval.
- Particle Stokes number `St`, with its length scale stated, and its relation to the observed efficiency.
- Particle size limits `d_min` and `d_max`, µm, where the efficiency falls below a stated threshold from below and from above.
- Deposited fraction and pass-through fraction, reported separately from the deflected fraction.
- Jet air power, W, and the power per unit of deflected flux, W per (kg/m²/s).
- One-way versus two-way difference in `eta` at the stated loading.
- Flow-solution agreement with the jet-in-crossflow data, as a percentage on penetration depth.

## Deliverables

- The Lagrangian tracker, the efficiency metric and the Stokes-number parameterisation as importable, tested code.
- The efficiency curves over Stokes number for all configurations, as data and figures, with the peak and the two size limits marked.
- The 1000-run table with efficiencies, sizes, powers and seeds, as CSV.
- Flow-field and particle-trajectory renders for the best configuration, coloured by fate, from the visualisation pipeline.
- The comparison against a solid wall across the aperture, with its obstruction cost stated.
- A `pinneapple_lab` manifest per run, with the geometry, jet condition and seed.
- A report whose first paragraph gives the peak efficiency and the two particle-size limits.

## Acceptance criteria

- The flow solution matches the published jet-in-crossflow penetration depth within 10 percent and the centreline decay within 10 percent at the finest mesh.
- The tracker reproduces the analytic step response time constant and the terminal velocity within 2 percent.
- Every efficiency is computed against a run of the same configuration with no deflector, and the baseline flux is reported with it.
- The efficiency curve is reported as a function of Stokes number with the length scale stated, and the peak efficiency and both size limits are given as numbers.
- The deflected, deposited and passing fractions sum to one, within the particle count's statistical error.
- The one-way and two-way results are compared at the stated loading, and the difference is reported.
- The comparison against the solid wall is included, and the report does not claim efficiency without stating the wall's value.

## Realizability and evidence class

`PLAUSIBILITY_CLASS`: `established` for jet-in-crossflow aerodynamics and for particle tracking by Stokes number; `extrapolated` for a deflection efficiency in a real dusty environment, because the one-way coupling, the electrostatics and the open boundary of a real site are all absent; `hypothetical` for a device that holds a clear region against particles in a strong wind without a physical barrier, since at large Stokes number no flow can. Axes this run fills: `mathematical_consistency` (jet-in-crossflow data, tracker references, fraction closure), `numerical_stability` (mesh convergence, seed repeatability, particle count), `physical_plausibility` (the Stokes-number limits against known dust-size physics, the jet power against real air curtains), `uncertainty_sensitivity` (wind, jet velocity, particle density). `experimental_validation` is not assessed. `technological_feasibility` is partly addressed by the power requirement.

To move up one class, in order: (1) a wind-tunnel measurement of the efficiency in a particle-laden flow, since the one-way coupling and the boundary conditions of a real site are the largest model gaps; (2) electrostatics, because dust adhesion and agglomeration are dominated by charge and the model ignores it; (3) a two-way coupled model at realistic loadings, since a device used in a dust storm operates at a loading where the flow is modified by the particles; (4) a full-scale installation with an open boundary, which is the only test that establishes the site-level efficiency. Item 1 is the necessary next step.

## Risks and failure modes

1. **The Stokes-number length scale is chosen after seeing the result.** Mitigation: `L` is a stated design parameter fixed before the sweep, and the efficiency curve is reported with the `L` used.
2. **The efficiency is computed against a nominal flux rather than a computed undeflected baseline.** Mitigation: the undeflected run is required per configuration and the denominator is reported.
3. **The one-way coupling flatters the result at realistic loadings.** Mitigation: the break-down loading is computed and one two-way run is reported.
4. **The packet of particles is too small and the efficiency is noise at the tails.** Mitigation: 10^4 particles, three seeds, and confidence intervals on every efficiency.
5. **The device is compared against nothing.** Mitigation: the solid wall across the aperture is a required comparison, with its obstruction cost, so the efficiency is not reported in isolation.

## Out of scope

- Dust adhesion and removal from surfaces, and any cleaning claim.
- Electrostatics and charge effects on the particles.
- Droplet breakup, evaporation and phase change beyond the single excursion run.
- Device hardware, nozzle design, blower selection and noise.
