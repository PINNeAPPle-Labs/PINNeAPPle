## Summary

A navigable room whose local geometry is ordinary but whose global connectivity is not: the walls are identified in pairs, so walking straight returns the traveller somewhere else, and light follows the same identifications so that what is seen through a wall is a real distant part of the space. The experiment delivers the environment, an audit that checks the construction is consistent — path closure, energy, and the agreement of light and gravity — and the list of the specific points at which a viewer would notice that something is wrong.

## Question

Does a room built by identifying its walls satisfy the consistency audit at every reachable state, and at which of those states can a viewer detect the identification from the rendered image alone?

## Why run it

- It is the minimal demonstration of the point the whole identified-boundary family exists to make: a locally ordinary space can be globally strange, with every step computable and nothing explained by fiat. That is a stronger claim than a picture of an impossible shape.
- The audit is reusable: the closure, energy and light-gravity agreement checks apply to any identified environment, including the periodic duct and room cases in D16 and the quotient spaces in D34.
- The detectability list is the honest half of the result and is the part a reader can verify by walking the scene themselves.

## Physical model

A rectangular room with six walls, of which three pairs are identified: `x = 0` with `x = L_x`, `y = 0` with `y = L_y`, and a height pairing with a stated half-turn. A point in the room is a representative of an equivalence class, and every physical quantity must be single-valued on the class.

Three consistency requirements, each with its own audit.

1. **Path closure.** A closed walk in the cover returns to the same point with the accumulated transformation being a group element. The audit records the transformation and classifies it: identity, a non-identity element of the group (which is consistent and expected), or failure to close (which is an error). The distinction is the same one that matters in D34 and it is the first place a naive implementation goes wrong.
2. **Energy and momentum.** A moving object that crosses an identification must carry its momentum consistently: the velocity is mapped through the same transformation as the position. A walker that carries a ball, throws it across an identification and catches it on the return must find the same ball, with the same speed, and the audit checks the invariant.
3. **Light and gravity agreement.** A ray that leaves through an identified wall enters at the paired wall with the mapped direction; a dropped object falls along the mapped gravity direction. The audit checks that a vertical ray and a falling object agree about which way is down, because a construction in which they disagree produces a scene that looks right in a still image and is inconsistent in motion — that is exactly the kind of error the audit exists to catch.

Parameters: the room's aspect ratios, the pairing transformation for each wall, and the rotation applied on each pairing (identity, half-turn, or quarter-turn where the aspect ratios allow). The dimensionless content is the holonomy of the pairings: a closed loop's accumulated rotation, which determines whether a viewer who walks a loop returns facing a different direction.

Validity and omission: Euclidean local geometry with a quotient topology, rigid-body motion and geometric optics. It cannot represent a space whose local geometry is curved, and it says nothing about whether the identification could be physical — it cannot, in three dimensions — which is stated and not argued. The room is also empty of people, and the detectability question is about a camera viewpoint rather than a human observer, so no claim is made about what a person would or would not notice.

## Geometry and inputs

Rooms: a 10 m by 6 m by 3 m box with all three pairs identified; the same with the height pairing rotated; an L-shaped room whose two ends are identified, producing a loop of length `L`; and a room with one pair of walls identified and the others closed, which is the mild case. Contents: a corridor partition, four distinguishable marker objects in different colours, and a floor grid pattern, all chosen so that positional mistakes are visible. Viewpoints: a 2 m grid over the floor at eye height, plus 200 randomly sampled viewpoints with a stated seed.

## What is missing (the work)

1. **The room-scale pairing layer**, shared with D34: the fundamental domain, the per-wall transformation, and `map_point`, `map_direction` and `map_vector` (for velocity and acceleration under the identification). The D16 work builds the operator for a field solver; the geometry and velocity mapping for a walker is the new piece here.
2. **The path-closure audit**: `audit_closure(walker, path)` returning the accumulated transformation, its classification (identity, group element, failure), and the residual, with the classification as an explicit output value and not an inference from a numeric threshold.
3. **The energy and momentum audit**: a projectile thrown across an identification and caught on return, with the invariant checked — same speed, mapped direction, same landing point after the equivalent set of crossings.
4. **The light-gravity agreement audit**: the consistency check between the vertical direction inferred from a rendered image and the gravity direction used by the physics, reported as a disagreement in degrees, per viewpoint. This is the check that catches a scene that looks right and moves wrong.
5. **The detectability audit**: for each sampled viewpoint, whether a marker is visible more times than it exists, or a corridor is visible through an identified wall, or the vertical is inconsistent; reported as a per-viewpoint verdict and mapped.
6. **A second, independent image path**: a simple ray-traced image computed from the geometry and the pairing directly, compared with the rendered Cycles image, so a scene error cannot hide inside a renderer setting.

## Experiment protocol

1. Verify the pairing layer on the fully identified box: a straight walk along each axis must return to its start after crossing the room once, with the accumulated transformation equal to the stated pairing.
2. Verify the closure audit's classification on a deliberately mis-paired wall: the audit must report a failure to close, and a correctly paired wall on the same loop must report a group element. A test that cannot distinguish them fails the issue.
3. Verify the light path against an analytic image position: with a floor grid and a fully identified box, the markers' images form a lattice whose spacing is closed form, and the first four must match.
4. Audit campaign: 4 rooms by 200 closed walks each (random, seeded), plus 100 thrown-projectile cases per room, reporting closure classification and the momentum invariant.
5. Light-gravity agreement: 4 rooms by 200 sampled viewpoints, reporting the disagreement in degrees and the fraction exceeding a stated threshold.
6. Detectability: the same viewpoints, reporting the anomaly fraction and a map of the sensitive regions on the floor plan.
7. Renders: a walkthrough per room, plus the annotated version marking the anomaly locations; and the independent ray-traced comparison image for one viewpoint per room.
8. Compute budget: the audits are minutes; the renders dominate — order 10^1 to 10^2 CPU-hours.

## Baselines, references and verification

- The analytic image lattice of the fully identified box is the light-path reference, and the analytic accumulated transformation per pairing is the closure reference; both are closed form.
- The mis-paired-wall case is the negative control for the closure audit, and a plain room with no identifications is the negative control for the detectability audit, which must return zero anomalies.
- The independent ray-traced image is the reference for the rendered image, compared in image space through `error_map`.
- The momentum invariant is checked by construction: the projectile's speed and the mapped direction after the crossings, against the analytic expectation.
- `pinneapple_analysis.verification.convergence` is applied to the audit's own sampling, so the reported anomaly fraction has a stated resolution.

## Metrics

- Closure classification counts: identity, non-identity group element, failure, per room, summing to the walk total.
- Residual closure error, in metres and radians, for the identity and group-element walks.
- Momentum invariant error for the thrown projectiles: relative speed change and direction error in degrees.
- Light-gravity disagreement, degrees, per viewpoint, and the fraction exceeding the stated threshold.
- Anomaly fraction, the fraction of viewpoints from which the identification is visible, dimensionless, with a confidence interval.
- Image-space discrepancy between the rendered and independently ray-traced images, as a fraction of the dynamic range.

## Deliverables

- The pairing layer, the three audits and the independent ray tracer as importable code with the analytic tests and both negative controls.
- The four room environments as scene files with the viewer, so a reader can walk them.
- The audit tables per room, as CSV, with the closure classifications, momentum errors and light-gravity disagreements.
- The anomaly map per room on the floor plan, and the list of the specific viewpoints where a viewer notices the trick.
- A walkthrough video per room, plus the annotated version marking the detectable locations.
- A `pinneapple_lab` manifest per room, with the pairings and the seeds.
- A report whose first paragraph says whether the audit passed and where the construction is detectable.

## Acceptance criteria

- The pairing layer reproduces the analytic accumulated transformation for each pairing within 1e-9 in rotation and 1e-9 relative in translation.
- The closure audit classifies the mis-paired control as a failure to close and the correctly paired control as a group element; a test that cannot distinguish them fails the issue.
- The rendered image matches the independent ray-traced image within a stated image-space discrepancy, and the first four images of a marker match their analytic positions within one pixel.
- The momentum invariant holds within 1 percent in speed and 1 degree in direction for every reported projectile.
- The light-gravity disagreement is reported per viewpoint, and every viewpoint exceeding the threshold appears in the detectable list.
- The detectability audit returns zero anomalies for the plain control room; a nonzero result fails the issue.
- The report states that the room is a quotient construction that cannot exist as a three-dimensional object, in the same section as the environment description.

## Realizability and evidence class

`PLAUSIBILITY_CLASS`: `established` as a geometry, rendering and navigation construction, since quotient spaces are standard mathematics; `hypothetical` as a physical room, because the identification cannot be realised in three Euclidean dimensions; `fictional` as an engineered space. Axes this run fills: `mathematical_consistency` (analytic transformations, analytic image lattice, two negative controls), `numerical_stability` (audit sampling resolution, ray-budget adequacy, closure residuals), `physical_plausibility` in the narrow sense that light and gravity must agree, which is the consistency requirement the audit enforces. `experimental_validation` is not assessed. `technological_feasibility` is not assessed.

There is no physical class to move up. The value is the audit tooling and the demonstration that a locally ordinary space can be globally strange with no inconsistent step; the report must not imply that the construction could be built, and the detectability list is the honest measure of how far the illusion carries.

## Risks and failure modes

1. **The walker's crossing applies the position map but not the velocity map, and the momentum audit is vacuous.** Mitigation: the momentum invariant is a required metric with its own acceptance threshold, and the thrown-projectile case is designed to fail loudly if the velocity mapping is missing.
2. **The closure audit cannot distinguish a group element from a failure.** Mitigation: both negative controls are required, and the classification is an explicit returned value rather than a threshold on a residual.
3. **The rendered image and the independent tracer differ for an irrelevant renderer reason, and the check is dismissed.** Mitigation: the discrepancy is reported with its cause attributed, and the analytic image positions provide a second, renderer-independent check.
4. **The detectability audit is a bug and reports a clean result.** Mitigation: the plain control room must return zero and a deliberately unpaired-wall room must return a high fraction; both are in the protocol.
5. **Gravity direction is mapped inconsistently and the room "works" in stills but not in motion.** Mitigation: the light-gravity agreement audit is per viewpoint and is an acceptance item, and the projectile audit runs in motion.

## Out of scope

- Non-Euclidean local geometry, portals as a transport device, and any physics beyond rigid-body motion and geometric optics.
- Human perception of the construction, and any claim about what a person would or would not notice.
- Multi-user navigation, persistence and any interactive application beyond the bundled viewer.
- Any physical realisation or construction claim.
