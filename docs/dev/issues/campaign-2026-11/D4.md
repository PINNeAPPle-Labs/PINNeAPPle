## Summary

A deployable exoskeleton that folds out of a pack, locks its joints around a body model and routes actuator torque so that lifting a load produces acceptable joint moments. The experiment designs the frame and the torque distribution, checks the deployed structure under the lift load, and reports the margins. It makes no human-safety claim: the numbers are mechanical, and the issue says so in every deliverable.

## Question

For a stated lift task and a stated torque budget per actuator, what frame topology and torque distribution minimise the joint moments experienced by the wearer's body model, and what does the deployed structure weigh?

## Why run it

- Exoskeletons are a real product category where the design trade (assistance versus added mass versus joint loading) is well defined and simulable without a human subject.
- It exercises multibody kinematics, structural analysis and mass budgeting together — three capabilities that already exist — and produces a Pareto front rather than a single design.
- It is the honest form of "armour that appears": deployability is a kinematics problem with a stowed-volume constraint, not a materials miracle.

## Physical model

**Kinematics.** A humanoid body model with joint limits and segments of stated mass and inertia; the exoskeleton adds links and joints, some actuated, some passive, some lockable.

**Statics and dynamics.** Joint moments from a quasi-static lift plus an inertial term for a stated motion profile; the exoskeleton's actuators apply torques, and the residual moments at the body model's joints are what the design minimises.

**Structure.** The deployed frame carries the actuator reaction loads plus the payload share it takes; linear statics with a buckling check on slender members.

**Constraints.** Actuator torque and rate limits, joint travel, stowed volume, deployed interference with the body model, and a total mass limit.

**Deliberately out of scope:** the human's physiology, comfort, fatigue, soft tissue loading and safety certification. The body model is a rigid-body proxy and is labelled as such.

## Geometry and inputs

Body model segments and dimensions, a stowage volume, the lift task (payload, reach, speed profile), and the actuator set (torque, rate, mass). The exoskeleton topology is the design variable: which joints are actuated, where the links run, and how the frame folds.

## What is missing (the work)

1. **A deployable-mechanism model**: joints that can lock and unlock, with the stowed geometry and the deployment path checked for interference.
2. **A body-model interface**: a rigid humanoid proxy with joint limits and segment inertias, and a way to report residual joint moments per task.
3. **A torque-allocation optimiser**: how the available actuator torque is distributed across joints and phases of the lift to minimise residual moments.
4. **A mass-and-stowage budget**: total mass, stowed volume and actuator mass, so assistance cannot be bought with unlimited structure.
5. **A deployment-path check**: continuous collision-free motion from stowed to deployed around the body model.

## Experiment protocol

1. Validate the multibody model against a hand-computed two-link statics case, and the structural model against a cantilever closed form.
2. Baseline: the lift task with no exoskeleton; record the joint moments, which is what the design must reduce.
3. Optimise the topology and torque distribution for residual moments subject to the constraints; record the Pareto front of residual moments versus added mass.
4. Check the deployed structure under the worst load and the deployment path for interference.
5. Report the stowed volume and the mass breakdown.
6. Compute budget: seconds per multibody evaluation, minutes per structural check; the optimisation needs thousands of evaluations — order 10^1–10^2 CPU-hours.

## Baselines, references and verification

- No-exoskeleton lift as the reference for assistance.
- A hand-computed two-link statics case for the multibody model, and a cantilever closed form for the structure.
- Actuator torque and rate limits respected by every accepted design (checked, not assumed).
- Mass and stowage budget as hard constraints.

## Metrics

- Residual joint moments at the body model, N·m per joint, and their reduction relative to the no-exoskeleton lift.
- Added mass, kg, and stowed volume, litres.
- Structural margin of the deployed frame: utilisation ratio under the worst load, including buckling.
- Actuator utilisation: fraction of available torque used per joint per phase.
- Deployment path: minimum clearance to the body model, mm, and deployment time, s.

## Deliverables

- The deployable-mechanism model, the body-model interface and the torque-allocation optimiser.
- The Pareto front of residual moments versus added mass.
- The structural and buckling checks, the deployment path report and the stowage budget.
- A deployment and lift animation, from the campaign video pipeline, with residual moments plotted per phase.

## Acceptance criteria

- The multibody and structural models reproduce their hand-computed references within the stated tolerance.
- Every accepted design respects actuator torque and rate limits, the mass limit and the stowed volume.
- The deployment path is collision-free by an independent check, with the minimum clearance reported.
- The deployed frame passes the structural and buckling checks with the stated factor at the worst load.
- No deliverable claims human safety, comfort or physiological benefit; the body-model limitation is stated in the summary and in every figure caption.

## Realizability and evidence class

`PLAUSIBILITY_CLASS`: `established` for exoskeletons, `extrapolated` for a fast-deploying full-body frame. Axes: `mathematical_consistency` (statics and structure against closed forms), `numerical_stability` (multibody step and mesh convergence), `physical_plausibility` (actuator limits, mass budget, buckling), `uncertainty_sensitivity` (payload and speed profile variation). `experimental_validation` stays not assessed — and the issue is explicit that no human-subject claim can be made from this model; `technological_feasibility` is partly addressed by the actuator and mass budgets.

## Risks and failure modes

1. **The body model is treated as a person.** Mitigation: the rigid-proxy limitation is in the summary and in captions; no safety language anywhere.
2. **Assistance bought with mass**, producing a net loss. Mitigation: the Pareto front includes the added mass on the same axis as the benefit.
3. **Deployment path unverified**, giving a design that cannot be put on. Mitigation: an independent interference check as an acceptance criterion.
4. **Buckling ignored** on slender frame members. Mitigation: buckling is part of the structural check.
5. **A dramatic deployment render standing in for a mechanical result.** Mitigation: the residual-moment plot is the deliverable; the render carries the numbers.

## Out of scope

- Physiology, comfort, fatigue and human safety certification.
- Control law design for intent detection; only a prescribed torque profile is used.
- Battery and thermal design for the actuators.
