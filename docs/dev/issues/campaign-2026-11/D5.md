## Summary

One vehicle that flies, floats and dives by reconfiguring. The experiment does not try to show that such a vehicle is a good idea; it quantifies the conflict. Mass and buoyancy must close in three media, aerodynamic and hydrodynamic requirements pull the hull in opposite directions, and the transition states — crossing the surface — are where the assumptions of both models fail. The deliverable is the trade-off surface, including the region where no configuration satisfies all three missions.

## Question

For stated air, surface and submerged missions, what is the feasible region of a single reconfigurable vehicle, and which requirement is the binding constraint at each point of the trade-off surface?

## Why run it

- Triple-medium vehicles exist in research and are always compromised; a quantified Pareto surface is more useful than another concept drawing.
- It exercises the honest core of the campaign: the experiment's value is the conflict it exposes, not a hero render.
- The pieces exist — flight 6-DOF, hydrodynamic resistance models, buoyancy bookkeeping — so the work is integration plus a trade study, which is a realistic scope for one issue.

## Physical model

**Air.** Lift and drag from a panel or VLM model in cruise, and rotor thrust where the concept uses rotors; power from a stated propulsion efficiency; the 6-DOF model of `pinneapple_simulation.flight` supplies trim and stability.

**Surface and submerged.** Resistance from a stated model (ITTC-style friction plus form and wave resistance, or a published correlation) with a Froude-number-based check; buoyancy from displaced volume with a ballast variable; submerged added mass and the fact that control authority changes with the medium.

**Mass and volume budget.** Structural mass scaled from wetted area and internal volume, propulsion mass, energy storage from the mission energy requirements, ballast capacity. This budget is what makes the problem hard, and it must close for every candidate.

**Transitions.** A stated transition strategy (ballast blow, flying out of the water, or a planing phase), with the recognition that neither the aerodynamic nor the hydrodynamic model is valid at the interface — so the transition is scored by a conservative bound, not by a solved mixed-phase field.

**Deliberately out of scope:** multiphase CFD of the transition, structural design, and any weaponisation.

## Geometry and inputs

A parametric hull (length, beam, fineness, wing or rotor set, ballast volume) and three missions with stated ranges, speeds and payloads. The design variables are hull geometry, wing/rotor area, ballast capacity and the propulsion split.

## What is missing (the work)

1. **A hull resistance model**: friction, form and wave resistance with a stated validity range in Froude number, plus the submerged case with added mass.
2. **A closed mass/volume/buoyancy budget** as a single checked function, so an infeasible candidate is rejected before it is analysed further.
3. **A parametric reconfigurable hull** with the wing/rotor and ballast states as discrete configurations, and the aerodynamic coefficients regenerated per configuration.
4. **A transition bound**: a conservative estimate of the force and power needed to leave and enter the water, with the model's invalidity stated.
5. **The trade study driver**: the Pareto surface over mission weights, with the binding constraint recorded per point.

## Experiment protocol

1. Validate the aerodynamic model against a published case for a similar configuration, and the resistance model against a published hull form at two Froude numbers.
2. Establish three single-medium baselines: the best air vehicle, the best surface craft and the best submersible for the same payload — the references the omnivehicle must be compared against.
3. Run the budget filter over the design space and record the infeasible fraction and its binding constraint.
4. Optimise the omnivehicle for a weighted combination of the three missions; repeat for the mission weightings that trace the surface.
5. Report the transition bound per candidate and the configurations where it is prohibitive.
6. Compute budget: seconds per mission evaluation, minutes per aerodynamic configuration regeneration — order 10^1–10^2 CPU-hours.

## Baselines, references and verification

- The three single-medium optima: the omnivehicle's penalty is measured against them, not against nothing.
- Published aerodynamic and hydrodynamic references for the model validation.
- Budget closure as a hard check on every reported candidate.
- Transition bound as an inequality that every accepted design satisfies.

## Metrics

- Mission performance per medium (range, endurance, payload), against each single-medium baseline.
- Penalty ratio: omnivehicle performance divided by the best single-medium performance, per mission.
- Mass and buoyancy closure: residual, kg and litres.
- Structural utilisation of the wing/spar under the worst load.
- Transition cost: energy and power, and the number of candidates for which it is prohibitive.

## Deliverables

- The resistance model, the budget checker, the reconfigurable hull generator and the transition bound.
- The three single-medium baselines with their validation.
- The Pareto surface over mission weightings, with the binding constraint annotated per point.
- A render of one candidate in all three configurations, from the campaign video pipeline.

## Acceptance criteria

- Both models reproduce their published references within the stated tolerance.
- Every reported candidate closes its mass, volume and buoyancy budget within the stated tolerance.
- The omnivehicle's penalty against each single-medium baseline is reported as a number, for every point of the surface.
- The transition bound is satisfied by every accepted design, and the designs it rejects are counted.
- The report identifies the binding constraint at the extremes of the trade-off surface.

## Realizability and evidence class

`PLAUSIBILITY_CLASS`: `established` for each medium separately, `extrapolated` for one vehicle doing all three. Axes: `mathematical_consistency` (budget closure, model references), `numerical_stability` (coefficient regeneration convergence), `physical_plausibility` (published validation, Froude-number validity), `uncertainty_sensitivity` (mission weighting and payload variation). `experimental_validation` is not assessed; `technological_feasibility` is partly addressed by the budget, not by a hardware design.

## Risks and failure modes

1. **Models used outside their validity** — aerodynamic coefficients in ground effect or at the surface. Mitigation: stated validity ranges and refusal outside them.
2. **The budget is closed by wishful mass scaling.** Mitigation: mass model documented with its basis, and the margin reported.
3. **The trade study hides an infeasible design as "low performance".** Mitigation: the budget filter runs before analysis and its rejections are counted.
4. **A single weighted objective producing one number.** Mitigation: the Pareto surface with per-point binding constraints is the deliverable.
5. **The transition is assumed, not bounded.** Mitigation: an explicit conservative bound and the statement that the interface is not modelled.

## Out of scope

- Multiphase CFD of the water-entry and exit.
- Structural detail design, manufacturing and certification.
- Autonomy and control law design beyond trim and stability.
