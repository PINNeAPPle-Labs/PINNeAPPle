## Summary

A thin reflective membrane changes a heliocentric orbit using solar radiation pressure alone, and the experiment optimises the attitude history that achieves a stated transfer. The object is not the sail concept but the trajectory: given an areal density, a reflectance model and a start and end orbit, what change is available, how long it takes, and how much of the answer is lost to wrinkling and to the optical model. The result is set against a propulsive reference for the same transfer, so the reader sees the exchange rate between months of patience and kilograms of propellant.

## Question

For a sail of stated areal density and optical properties, what is the largest achievable change in orbital elements for a stated transfer time, and how much does that change move when the sail force model includes wrinkling and non-specular reflection rather than an ideal flat mirror?

## Why run it

- It is a real mission class with a real demonstration behind it, so the model has something to be checked against, and the honest number — the transfer takes months at sub-millimeter-per-second-squared acceleration — is itself the finding.
- The library already carries the two ingredients that are usually the expensive ones: a two-body-plus-J2 propagator and a closed-form rocket model for the propulsive reference. The work is the force model and the optimiser.
- The visual is unusually good for the cost: an attitude schedule over a year, plotted as the sail angle against true anomaly and the resulting spiral in the orbital-element plane, is a legible result with no rendering budget.

## Physical model

Radiation pressure on an element with normal `n` at heliocentric distance `r`:

    P(r) = S/r^2,  S = 1361 W/m^2,  p = P/c
    dF = -(2 p A / c) * [ rho_a cos^2(theta) + (2/3) rho_s cos(theta) - ... ] n  (idealised)

Written in the form actually used: the force on a flat, partially specular mirror splits into a normal component scaling with `cos^2(theta)` and a tangential component scaling with `cos(theta) sin(theta)`, with the split set by the absorptivity and the specular fraction. In the ideal fully reflective case the force is `2 P A cos^2(theta)/c n`, which normalised by the solar gravitational attraction gives a lightness number `beta` of order 0.1 for 5 g/m². The dimensionless groups that decide the regime are `beta` (the ratio of radiation to gravitational acceleration), the sail areal density `sigma` in g/m², and the ratio of the transfer time to the orbital period.

Two-body plus `J2` dynamics carries the sail; the sail force is added as a state-dependent acceleration. The validity limits: the flat-membrane optical model holds only while the sail is nearly flat, the force model assumes the sail subtends a small solid angle at the Sun so that `1/r^2` is adequate, and no thermal re-radiation asymmetry is included, which for a real membrane is a non-negligible torque term. The model cannot show billowing, creasing, or the electrostatic cling that dominates deployment; those are the effects it deliberately leaves out, and the wrinkling sensitivity study is a parameterisation of their consequence, not a simulation of them.

## Geometry and inputs

The membrane is a square of side `L` in {20, 40, 70} m, areal density in {3, 7, 15} g/m², with four boom-free corner-tensioned configurations. Wrinkling is represented by a scalar effective flatness factor and a tilt-angle bias, both swept, plus a coarse membrane stress solve for the static shape of one configuration. Start orbit: 1 AU circular, 5 deg inclination. Targets: raise aphelion by 0.1 AU, raise inclination by 5 deg, and lower perihelion by 0.15 AU. The control variable is the cone and clock angle history, discretized into segments of 10 deg of true anomaly.

## Solver and engine

- Propagation: `pinneapple_physics.astro.conjunction.propagate_two_body_j2` for the base dynamics; `pinneapple_simulation.numerical_solvers.nbody_rebound` for the multi-body consistency check.
- Trajectory optimisation: a direct method on the segment-wise attitude history, with `pinneapple_design.design_optimizer` for the global search and a hand-written single-shooting or multiple-shooting refinement.
- Propulsive reference: `pp.closed_form` for the analytic burn, cross-checked against an integrated finite-burn propagation.
- Structural check: `pinneapple_simulation.numerical_solvers.solid_fem` for the membrane static shape of one configuration.
- Recording: `pinneapple_lab.experiment`; visualisation through `pp.dashboard` and `pinneapple_twin3d.scene`.

## What is missing (the work)

1. **A sail force model** in `pinneapple_simulation/spacecraft/solar_sail.py`: `force(attitude, r, sigma, optical)` returning the acceleration vector for a flat membrane with absorptivity, specular fraction and a flatness degradation factor, plus the torque about the center of pressure. This module does not exist.
2. **An attitude-history parameterisation** with the cone and clock angles as the control, and the mapping from segment schedule to a continuous function used by the integrator.
3. **The trajectory optimiser**: a direct multiple-shooting wrapper that reuses the J2 propagator and the Jacobian from finite differences, with the objective being an orbital-element error and not a distance.
4. **A wrinkling-to-optics coupling**: a coarse membrane solve producing an effective flatness factor and a mean tilt, feeding item 1, so the sensitivity is a computed quantity and not a hand-set parameter.
5. **A propulsive comparison table** that puts the same transfer against chemical and electric propulsion at a stated specific impulse, using one code path.
6. **A mission-validity gate** that rejects schedules where the sail passes inside a stated thermal limit or where the local cone angle is outside the optical model's range.

## Experiment protocol

1. Verify the propagator: a pure two-body case against the analytic Kepler solution, and the `J2` secular rates against the standard first-order expressions, at four step sizes, reporting the convergence order.
2. Verify the closed-form propulsive reference against an integrated finite burn for three burn durations.
3. Baseline runs: a fixed Sun-facing attitude, a locally optimal cone-angle law, and the optimised schedule, for each of the three targets.
4. Attitude-history optimisation: 12 targets-times-configurations, each with 5 random restarts, 3 sail sizes and 3 areal densities — order 10^2 propagations per case, each a few seconds.
5. Wrinkling sensitivity: the flatness factor swept over 0.7 to 1.0 for the best schedule per target, reporting the change in the achieved element change.
6. Widened validity: the same schedules re-run with a specular fraction of 0.9, 0.8 and 0.6, so the reader sees how much of the result is the ideal-mirror assumption.
7. Compute budget: order 10^1 to 10^2 CPU-hours; the propagator is cheap and the cost is the optimiser.

## Baselines, references and verification

- The analytic Kepler solution and the standard `J2` secular drift rates as the propagator's reference.
- The impulsive and finite-burn rocket solution as the propulsion reference, from `pp.closed_form` and an independent integration.
- Energy and angular-momentum bookkeeping: for a purely gravitational plus radiation field, the specific orbital energy change per unit time equals the work done by the sail force, which is a checkable invariant on every step.
- The published IKAROS mission result as an order-of-magnitude reference for achievable `beta` and for the timescale, not as a numerical target.

## Metrics

- Achieved change per target: `Δa_p`, `Δi`, `Δq`, in AU and degrees, at the stated final time.
- Characteristic acceleration `a_c` in mm/s², computed at 1 AU from the force model.
- Lightness number `beta`, dimensionless, from `a_c` divided by the local solar gravity.
- Transfer duration to reach the target within a stated tolerance, days.
- Propellant-equivalent mass: the propellant a rocket would need for the same change, kg, from the closed-form solution.
- Sensitivity of `Δa_p` to the flatness factor, dimensionless, from the sweep in step 5.

## Deliverables

- The sail force module, the attitude parameterisation and the trajectory optimiser as importable code.
- A results table of achieved element change, duration and propellant-equivalent mass per target and configuration, as CSV.
- Figures: the attitude schedule, the orbital-element spiral, the sensitivity curve against flatness and specular fraction.
- A `pinneapple_lab` manifest per run, with the seed, the force-model parameters and the code revision, so any row of the table can be reproduced.
- A short report that states the acceleration class and the duration plainly in the first paragraph.

## Acceptance criteria

- The propagator reproduces the analytic two-body solution to better than 1e-6 relative over one period, and converges at the expected order as the step is halved.
- The propulsive reference from `pp.closed_form` agrees with the integrated finite burn within 2 percent for burns shorter than 5 percent of the period.
- The orbital energy change computed from the sail force equals the change in the integrated energy within 1 percent per case.
- The optimised schedule beats the fixed Sun-facing attitude on every target by a reported margin, and beats the locally optimal law on at least two of the three.
- The report gives the achieved change with the ideal-mirror force model and with the degraded model, and states the difference as a number.
- No schedule is reported outside the force model's validity range without that fact being flagged in the same table.

## Realizability and evidence class

`PLAUSIBILITY_CLASS`: `established` for radiation pressure and for the transfer dynamics; `extrapolated` for a specific mission's achieved performance at low areal density, where the wrinkles and the boom mass are the open engineering. Axes this run fills: `mathematical_consistency` (energy and angular-momentum bookkeeping, propagator convergence), `numerical_stability` (integrator order, optimiser restart spread), `physical_plausibility` (radiation-pressure magnitude against the measured Lebedev and Nichols-Hull results and against IKAROS), `uncertainty_sensitivity` (flatness, specular fraction, areal density). `experimental_validation` stays not assessed: no sail is tested here. `technological_feasibility` is partly addressed by the areal-density and boom-mass budget and not by a hardware design.

To move up one class, in order of how much it would buy: (1) a measured force model for a real membrane including the wrinkle-induced torque, which no flat-mirror parameterisation substitutes for; (2) areal densities near 3 g/m² with the boom and tensioning mass included, demonstrated on orbit rather than projected; (3) a thermal model showing the membrane survives the perihelion passage of the stated transfer; (4) attitude control authority at the torque levels the wrinkled membrane actually generates. Items 1 and 4 are the ones this experiment's result is most sensitive to.

## Risks and failure modes

1. **The ideal-mirror force model flatters the result.** Mitigation: the specular and flatness sweep is a mandatory part of the protocol, and every reported number carries the model it came from.
2. **The optimiser finds an attitude schedule that exploits a model artifact** — for example a singularity at exactly edge-on attitude. Mitigation: a validity gate on the cone angle and a re-check of the best schedule at finer time resolution.
3. **Wrinkling is a scalar fudge factor presented as physics.** Mitigation: the factor is derived from a coarse membrane solve for one configuration, and the report states plainly that it is a parameterisation.
4. **The comparison with propulsion is not like-for-like.** Mitigation: the propellant-equivalent mass is computed with the same transfer and the same final time, and the specific impulse is stated.
5. **A transfer time long enough that the orbit precesses under perturbations not in the model.** Mitigation: one run with the full `J2` plus a stated drag-free assumption, and the perturbation budget reported.

## Out of scope

- Deployment dynamics, boom design and packaging.
- Thermal-structural analysis of the membrane beyond the coarse static shape.
- Attitude control system design and sensor suite.
- Any interstellar or non-Keplerian application.
