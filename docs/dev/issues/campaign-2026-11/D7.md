## Summary

A magnetically levitated stage holds a payload at a controlled gap against a disturbance, and the experiment reports the three numbers that decide whether such a stage is usable: the levitation stiffness, the closed-loop disturbance-rejection bandwidth, and the load range over which both hold. The physics is magnetostatics plus eddy currents and a control loop that exists only because the static field configuration is unstable. The design variables are the magnet and coil layout and the controller gains, and the central result is where saturation, thermal limits and the gap nonlinearity cut the envelope.

## Question

For a stage of stated mass and a coil set of stated copper budget, what levitation stiffness and disturbance-rejection bandwidth are achievable over a stated gap range, and at which point does coil saturation or thermal limit bind before the closed-loop stability margin does?

## Why run it

- Magnetic levitation is established engineering, so the experiment is not a feasibility argument; it is a design-envelope computation with a control layer, which is exactly the kind of result an issue can deliver in one pass.
- The Earnshaw result makes the control loop mandatory rather than optional, so the experiment has an unarguable physical content: the open-loop system is unstable in at least one axis, and the loop's stability margin is a hard number.
- It reuses the magnetostatic and eddy-current parts of the library directly, and the missing piece — a force-current-gap characteristic calibrated against the field solution — is reusable by every other magnetic actuator issue in the series.

## Physical model

Magnetostatic field from the coil and magnet set, `∇ x H = J`, `∇·B = 0`, with a linear or saturating `B(H)` from the material model. Force on the moving element by the Maxwell stress or by the co-energy derivative:

    F = dW/dz,  W = (1/2) ∫ B·H dV  ->  F ≈ k I^2 / (g + g0)^2  for a simple actuator

The linearised stiffness at an operating point is `k_z = -∂F/∂z`, negative for a permanent-magnet pair, which is Earnshaw's theorem expressed for this geometry. Eddy currents in the conductive target produce a velocity-dependent force `F_eddy ≈ -c dz/dt` with a cutoff frequency set by the skin depth, which is the damping the loop can use and also a phase lag that limits bandwidth.

Dimensionless groups that set the regime: the ratio of gap to pole pitch (below about 0.2 the field is quasi-uniform and the simple force law holds; above about 0.5 fringing dominates), the magnetic Reynolds number of the moving element, and the ratio of the desired bandwidth to the eddy-current cutoff frequency. The model omits hysteresis in the core (available through `pinneapple_simulation.electromagnetics.hysteresis` but not used in the reduced model), thermal drift of the coil resistance beyond a stated linear coefficient, and any mechanical flexibility of the stage — it is a rigid-body model in one translation and one rotation axis.

## Geometry and inputs

Three actuator layouts: a single-axis opposed pair (the baseline), a four-coil array in a square arrangement, and a Halbach-style permanent-magnet array with a single control coil per axis. Pole pitch in {20, 40, 80} mm, gap in {0.5, 1, 2, 4} mm, payload mass in {0.5, 2, 10} kg. Coil budget expressed as copper mass and current density limit; a stated convective or radiation thermal path for the coil. Disturbances: a step in payload mass, a sinusoidal base excitation from 0.5 to 200 Hz, and a force impulse.

## What is missing (the work)

1. **A force-current-gap characteristic extractor** in `pinneapple_simulation/electromagnetics/actuator.py`: `force_characteristic(geometry, currents, gaps, material)` that drives the existing field solvers over a grid of operating points and returns `F(I, g)` with its derivatives, plus an error estimate per point. This is the piece every magnetic actuator issue needs and none has.
2. **A linearisation and state-space assembly step** that turns the tabulated characteristic, the eddy-current force and the payload mass into an `A, B, C, D` model per operating point, with the ill-conditioned cases flagged rather than silently fitted.
3. **A closed-loop design and margin analysis**: gain selection for a stated actuator and sensor set, with gain and phase margins, and a saturation-aware check so the reported bandwidth is one the actuator can actually deliver at the stated disturbance amplitude.
4. **A thermal budget** for the coil that converts a duty cycle and a current into a temperature rise, and refuses the operating point when the rise exceeds the stated limit.
5. **An envelope map**: the region of (load, gap, bandwidth) where all three constraints — stability margin, saturation, thermal — are satisfied simultaneously.

## Experiment protocol

1. Verify the magnetostatic path against a known-answer case: the analytic field of a coil pair or a published actuator geometry, with mesh refinement at three levels and a reported GCI.
2. Verify the eddy-current path against the analytic low-frequency limit for a conducting slab in a moving field, and against `eddy_current_fdm` on the same geometry.
3. Extract the characteristic `F(I, g)` on a 15 by 8 grid of current and gap for each of the three layouts; check the fitted exponents against the analytic `I^2/(g+g0)^2` law where it applies and report where it breaks.
4. Linearise at five operating points per layout and design the loop for three target bandwidths each; record margins and saturation margins.
5. Disturbance runs: step, sinusoidal sweep, impulse — 45 closed-loop time-domain runs, 60 s each at 10 kHz sampling in the reduced model.
6. Thermal sweep over duty cycle for the best design per layout.
7. Uncertainty: Monte Carlo on gap offset, coil resistance and payload mass, 200 draws per operating point, using `margin_study`.
8. Compute budget: minutes per field solve, thousands of solves — order 10^2 CPU-hours including the mesh-refinement study.

## Baselines, references and verification

- The analytic `I^2/(g+g0)^2` force law and the analytic eddy-current damping of a slab are the two closed-form references; the numerical path must reproduce them inside the geometry where they are valid.
- Mesh refinement with GCI at three levels on the baseline layout.
- The rigid-body open-loop model must show exactly one unstable mode for the opposed pair, matching Earnshaw; if it shows zero, the field solution or the force differentiation is wrong.
- Baselines the design must beat: a passive permanent-magnet bearing of the same mass (zero bandwidth above the mechanical resonance, but no power), and a mechanical stage with a stated stiffness and damping. The comparison is at equal payload and equal gap.

## Metrics

- Levitation stiffness `k_z`, N/µm, from the slope of `F(z)` at the operating point, open-loop and closed-loop effective.
- Negative stiffness magnitude and the loop gain required to invert it, dimensionless.
- Disturbance-rejection bandwidth `f_bw`, Hz, from the `-3 dB` point of the sensitivity function.
- Gain margin and phase margin, dB and degrees, from the linearised loop.
- Peak coil current and current density, A and A/mm², and the corresponding temperature rise, K.
- Maximum disturbance amplitude rejected at the stated bandwidth before saturation, N or µm.
- Load and gap range at which all constraints hold, as an area in the (m, g) plane.

## Deliverables

- The characteristic extractor and the state-space assembly as importable modules with tests against the two analytic limits.
- A table of stiffness, bandwidth and thermal margin per layout and operating point, as CSV.
- The envelope maps (load against gap, with the binding constraint labelled per region) as figures.
- The frequency responses with margins annotated; the time-domain disturbance traces at the edge of the envelope.
- A `pinneapple_lab` manifest per field solve, so a row of the table can be reproduced from its geometry and current.
- A render of the best layout with the field magnitude and the gap displacement shown together.

## Acceptance criteria

- The magnetostatic path reproduces the reference within the stated tolerance at the finest mesh, and the GCI on the levitation force is below 5 percent.
- The eddy-current path matches the analytic low-frequency damping within 10 percent over the frequency range where the analytic form is valid.
- The open-loop reduced model shows an unstable eigenvalue whose magnitude matches the negative closed-loop stiffness within 10 percent.
- Every reported bandwidth is accompanied by a saturation margin at the stated disturbance amplitude, and no bandwidth is reported for an operating point that saturates.
- The thermal budget rejects at least one operating point per layout, and the rejected point is listed with its temperature.
- The envelope map identifies the binding constraint in every region, and the boundary between regions is resolved to one grid cell.

## Realizability and evidence class

`PLAUSIBILITY_CLASS`: `established`. Magnetic levitation with closed-loop control is in service in transport, in turbomachinery and in precision stages; nothing in this experiment requires new physics. Axes this run fills: `mathematical_consistency` (characteristic consistency with the field solution and with the analytic force law), `numerical_stability` (mesh convergence, linearisation conditioning), `physical_plausibility` (Earnshaw's instability appears where it should; eddy damping matches the analytic limit), `uncertainty_sensitivity` (Monte Carlo on gap, resistance, mass), and `technological_feasibility` partly, through the thermal and copper budgets. `experimental_validation` stays not assessed: no hardware is built.

There is no class to move up. The honest limitation is not plausibility but scope: this is a model-level envelope, and the gap between it and a working stage is manufacturing tolerance, sensor noise, coil non-uniformity and thermal drift, none of which a simulation settles. The report must say that the numbers are an upper bound on what a real stage achieves, and must name the three effects most likely to close the gap.

## Risks and failure modes

1. **The force characteristic is fitted from too few field solves** and the linearisation carries a large hidden error. Mitigation: a grid-convergence study on the characteristic itself, with the fit residual reported per operating point.
2. **The bandwidth is computed from a model with no unmodeled dynamics.** Structural modes of the stage and the sensor are absent, so the reported bandwidth can exceed what a real loop tolerates. Mitigation: state the omitted dynamics explicitly, and report the bandwidth together with the first omitted mode's frequency as an assumption of the design.
3. **Saturation is checked at the nominal disturbance and not at the worst case in the sweep.** Mitigation: the envelope map is computed at the stated worst-case disturbance, and any point where the required current exceeds the limit is excluded rather than annotated.
4. **Thermal steady state is assumed while the duty cycle is intermittent.** Mitigation: the thermal budget is a time-averaged model with the averaging window stated, and the peak temperature is reported separately.
5. **The comparison with the passive bearing is at unequal gap.** Mitigation: the baseline is evaluated at the same gap and payload, and the difference in gap capability is reported as a separate column rather than folded into the comparison.

## Out of scope

- Rotordynamics and multi-axis levitation for a rotating machine.
- Superconducting bearings and their quench behaviour.
- Sensor and power-electronics design, including the switching ripple.
- Vibration isolation of the payload above the stage — that is the subject of D15.
