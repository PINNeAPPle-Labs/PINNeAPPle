## Summary

A legged platform some 50 m across and several hundred tonnes walks over unprepared ground while keeping its deck level. The ground decides whether it works: pad bearing pressure sets pad size, pad size sets leg mass, mass sets pressure again. The experiment couples a multibody gait to a Bekker–Wong foot–soil model across a terrain ladder and reports ground pressure, energy per metre, maximum slope, tipping margin and the terrain class where the design fails.

## Question

Is there a leg count, pad area and gait that crosses a defined terrain ladder below a stated ground pressure, and at which terrain class does it fail?

## Why run it

Walking draglines prove the principle at 10^3 tonnes; nothing public fixes where it stops, because the limit is soil bearing capacity, a site property. The model returns that boundary as a curve and the number a customer asks for first, ground pressure in kPa. It also settles one common error: more legs lower the load per leg, not necessarily the pressure under the pad.

## Physical model

Rigid multibody dynamics with unilateral contact, M(q)q̈ + h(q,q̇) = τ + Σ_f J_fᵀλ_f, λ_f ≥ 0 inside a cone |λ_t| ≤ μλ_n. The soil closes the system: pressure–sinkage p(z) = (k_c/b + k_φ)z^n (Bekker), shear–slip τ(j) = (c + p tan φ)(1 − exp(−j/K)) (Janosi–Hanamoto), and bearing q_ult = cN_c + qN_q + ½γBN_γ (Terzaghi) at the pad width, which bounds a single large pad. Stability is the support-polygon condition; tipping margin is the smallest normalized distance from the center-of-mass projection to the polygon edge.

Regime groups: sinkage z/b, cohesion number c/(γB), friction angle φ, Froude number Fr = v/√(gL), pad aspect ratio, and duty factor, which must exceed 0.5 for static stability. Validity: quasi-static to moderately dynamic walking, homogeneous soil per pad. Left out: at this scale soil fails on a slip surface far larger than the pad, so bearing capacity is extrapolated past the plate tests it derives from; layered ground and wind on the deck are not modeled.

## Geometry and inputs

Parametric machine — 6 or 8 legs, leg length, pad radius, deck size, joint and torque limits — from `geometry.gen.sdf_shapes` and `domains`. Terrain is procedural: heightfield with slope class, roughness spectrum and a soil class per region, hashed per case. Optimized: leg count, pad radius, leg length, gait phase. Sole external input is the soil parameter table, provenance `measured`.

## Solver and engine

`pinneapple_simulation.multibody` (`MultibodyTree`, `arm` helpers) for the tree, foot contact in a new soil-coupled stepper. `external_solvers.mujoco` and `.newton_physics` cross-check contact; if either disagrees on flat hardstand, the in-house stepper is the suspect. Leg members go to `solid_fem` or `beam_fe`.

## What already exists in PINNeAPPle (verified)

- `pinneapple_simulation.particle_dynamics.terramechanics`: `SoilParams` (Bekker–Wong set, Wong contact-angle coefficients) and `WheelParams` — dataclasses only, no pressure–sinkage evaluation, no solver.
- `pinneapple_simulation.multibody.tree` (`MultibodyTree`, `Body`, `RevoluteJoint`), `multibody.arm` (`fixed_base_forward`, `energy_balance`, `link_world_points`).
- `numerical_solvers.solid_fem`, `beam_fe`; `external_solvers.mujoco`, `.newton_physics`; `design.topology` (`Q4Grid`, `Compliance2D`, `MMA`); `uncertainty.margin.margin_study`, `pp.futures`, `pinneapple_lab`.

## What is missing (the work)

1. **A foot–soil model that evaluates rather than stores**: extend `terramechanics` with pressure from sinkage, shear from slip, a per-pad penetration solve, Terzaghi capacity as a hard limit.
2. **A statically stable gait planner at scale**: foot placement on a heightfield, support polygon, center-of-mass path with margins, swing trajectories within joint limits — proposed `pinneapple_simulation.legged_gait`.
3. **A terrain generator**: heightfield classes with slope, roughness and soil zones, seeded and hashed — proposed `geometry.gen.terrain`.
4. **The coupling loop**: soil impulses fed back into the multibody step with a time step stable for stiff soil.
5. **The measurement layer**: ground pressure, energy per metre from joint work, tipping margin, failure slope.

## Experiment protocol

Four soil classes, four slopes (0, 5, 10, 15 degrees), three roughness amplitudes, one payload; 24 designs (6 or 8 legs, three pad radii, two leg-length ratios) by Latin hypercube; 60 to 100 walks of 30 s at a 1 ms step. Convergence: halve the step and refine the heightfield once, accept when pressure and energy move less than 5 percent. Five seeds per case, reported as a distribution. Budget order 200 CPU-hours. Logged: state, per-foot sinkage, slip and pressure, joint work, margin.

## Baselines, references and verification

Closed-form first: Bekker sinkage under a circular plate, Janosi–Hanamoto shear in direct shear, Terzaghi capacity under one pad, each reproduced to 1 percent before any walk runs. Standing on hardstand, foot reactions must sum to the weight and the center of pressure must match the closed-form centroid. Baselines to beat: tracked vehicle on the same soil model, fixed platform, hand-designed tripod gait.

## Metrics

Ground pressure p_g, kPa, maximum foot reaction over contact area. Energy per metre e = ∫Σ|τq̇|dt over distance, kWh/m. Maximum slope β_max, degrees, completed with margin above 0.1. Tipping margin m, dimensionless. Failure class: first ladder cell where more than half the seeds fail.

## Deliverables

Soil and terrain inputs with provenance; manifests through `pinneapple_lab`; per-run time series in compressed columnar form, order 1 GB; contact-pressure figure and failure-boundary map; one rendered walk; a report naming the design that survives the ladder, or the class where none does.

## Acceptance criteria

- Soil model reproduces the three closed-form results to 1 percent.
- Standing on hardstand, total foot reaction equals weight to 0.1 percent.
- Pressure and energy change by less than 5 percent under refinement.
- Each design's failure class and margin at failure are stated.
- In-house contact and the `mujoco` cross-check agree to 5 percent.

## Realizability and evidence class

`PLAUSIBILITY_CLASS`: `extrapolated` for the walking platform, `hypothetical` for a factory that produces while walking. Legged heavy machines exist and static-stability gait planning is textbook; extrapolated are bearing capacity at pad diameters far beyond the plate tests and deck tolerance under leg impulses at this size. The run fills `mathematical_consistency` (closed-form soil checks), `numerical_stability` (step and grid convergence), `physical_plausibility` (pressures the soil can carry, positive margins) and part of `uncertainty_sensitivity` (soil parameters, terrain seeds). `experimental_validation` and `technological_feasibility` stay not assessed: no bench, no field record, no plate-load data here, no built machine. To reach `established` four measurements are required, in order of cost: bearing capacity validated at meter-scale pads on these soils; actuator torque and power density measured at scale, so mass is not assumed; a scaled structural test of deck tolerance under walking impulses; mobility data on broken, layered ground. None is a modeling improvement.

## Risks and failure modes

1. The bearing extrapolation decides the answer. Mitigation: report the boundary against the capacity factor.
2. Contact stiffness forces a tiny time step. Mitigation: implicit penalty contact, checked against the explicit result once.
3. The gait is scored on terrain it was tuned on. Mitigation: held-out seeds fixed before tuning.
4. Peak pressure from one foot at one instant reads as a design value. Mitigation: report peak and average.
5. A convincing render is read as evidence. Mitigation: the headline panel is the failure boundary.

## Out of scope

- Deck and production equipment design; the deck is a mass and a tolerance.
- Foundation improvement, mats or roads.
- Any claim about a built machine, its cost or construction time.
