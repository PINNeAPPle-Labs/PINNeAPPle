## Summary

A panel array on a body whose local reflectance and emission are set per panel is optimised so that the rendered appearance of the body matches its background from a defined set of viewpoints and wavelengths. The physics is radiometry, not texture animation: the result is an image difference, and its value depends entirely on the observer, the illumination and the band. The experiment's honest deliverable is the matching number inside the optimised viewpoints and, more importantly, the number outside them, because the second one is what says whether the technique is a concealment method or a viewpoint-specific illusion.

## Question

With a panel array of stated angular resolution and a stated illumination model, what is the minimum achievable image difference between a body and its background inside the optimised viewpoints, and how quickly does that difference degrade as the observer leaves them?

## Why run it

- It converts a fictional claim into a measurement with a defined observer, and the degradation curve outside the optimised set is a result that can be reported even when it is unflattering.
- The rendering stack is present and headless, so the expensive part of an appearance study — physically based rendering of a parametrized BRDF over a geometry — is a matter of assembling existing pieces.
- The optimisation is over a low-dimensional parameter field on a known geometry, which is a tractable problem, and it produces a demonstration figure that is legible without a long caption.

## Physical model

Rendering is the rendering equation over the visible hemisphere:

    L_o(x, w_o) = L_e(x, w_o) + ∫ f_r(x, w_i, w_o) L_i(x, w_i) (n·w_i) dw_i

with the panel BRDF `f_r` parameterised as a diffuse lobe plus a specular lobe (a microfacet distribution with a roughness and a specular reflectance), plus a directional emissivity for the thermal band if the study includes it. The design variables are the per-panel diffuse albedo (three channels), the specular reflectance and roughness, and optionally a per-panel emission temperature. The image metric is a perceptual difference between rendered radiance images of the body and of the background behind it.

Dimensionless groups and regime: the Fresnel scale, the roughness-to-wavelength ratio (the model is a geometric-optics microfacet model and is invalid where roughness is comparable to or smaller than the wavelength in the wave-optics sense), and the solid angle subtended by the observer's viewpoint set relative to the hemisphere. Validity limits: geometric optics only, no diffraction, no polarization, no subsurface scattering, and a diffuse illumination model that is a stated skylight plus a sun disk. The model cannot represent what a real adaptive surface does at the panel seams, and it deliberately omits any sensing and control loop — the panel state is assumed known and set, which is the most generous assumption available.

## Geometry and inputs

A body of 2 m by 1 m by 0.6 m with a 32 by 16 panel grid on each face, panel size 6 cm. Three backgrounds: a uniform matte green wall, a textured foliage-like procedural surface, and a sky-to-ground gradient under a sun at 45 degrees elevation. Viewpoint set: a symmetric ring of 8 directions at 5 m, plus the two extreme oblique directions. Illumination: one sun direction and one overcast sky, using the renderer's sky model. Spectral scope: three RGB channels in the visible band; one secondary run in a single mid-infrared band with emissive panels, to show that the visible-band result does not transfer.

## Solver and engine

- Rendering: `pinneapple_blender.cycles` (`CyclesScene`, `add_surface`, `add_plane`, `camera`, `render`, `render_frames`, `available()`), headless Blender through the subprocess path.
- Geometry and scene assembly: `pinneapple_twin3d.scene` for the body and the background planes, with the panel mesh generated parametrically.
- Optimisation: `pinneapple_design.design_optimizer` over the panel parameter field, with a coarse-to-fine schedule because each render is expensive; a surrogate over panel index is used inside the loop.
- Comparison metric: `pinneapple_analysis.validation.error_map.error_map` over the rendered images plus a stated perceptual difference.
- Recording: `pinneapple_lab.experiment`.

## What is missing (the work)

1. **A panel-array appearance model** in `pinneapple_design/appearance/panels.py`: `panel_material(index, params)` producing a renderer-ready material from the parameter vector, and the inverse mapping from a desired reflectance to a parameter vector — with the physical realizability bounds on the achievable reflectance stated as constraints rather than discovered by the optimiser.
2. **A viewpoint and illumination sampler** that generates the camera ring, the sun positions and the background radiance images that the body must match, with a fixed seed per configuration.
3. **An image metric with a stated perceptual form** — a spatial-frequency-aware difference, not a raw pixel mean — with the justification for the chosen form and its sensitivity to the renderer's sampling noise quantified.
4. **A degradation study driver**: rendering the optimised panel state from the full viewpoint hemisphere and reporting the metric as a function of angular distance from the optimised set. This is the experiment's main result and no part of it exists.
5. **A realizability check** on the optimised panel parameters: any panel whose required reflectance falls outside a stated physical range of real coatings is flagged, and the metric is recomputed with that panel clamped, so the reader sees the cost of physical panels.

## Experiment protocol

1. Verify the renderer: render a calibrated reference — a grey card and a known reflectance standard under a stated illumination — and check that the rendered radiance matches the analytic diffuse prediction within a stated tolerance. Check that render sampling noise falls below the metric's resolution as sample count rises.
2. Establish baselines: a uniformly grey body at the mean background colour, a body with the best single uniform reflectance per background, and the untouched body. These are the numbers the panel array must beat.
3. Optimise panel parameters for the 8-viewpoint set on each background: 3 backgrounds by 2 illuminations, 6 optimisations, each with a coarse random search over panel groups followed by local refinement.
4. Report the metric inside the optimised set and over the full hemisphere at 15 degree resolution — 3 backgrounds by about 400 viewpoints at reduced sample count.
5. Realizability pass: clamp out-of-range panels and recompute the inside-set metric and one degradation curve.
6. Thermal-band run: one configuration with emissive panels in the mid-infrared, reported separately and not compared numerically with the visible result.
7. Compute budget: minutes per render at full quality, of order 10^4 renders — order 10^2 CPU-hours, dominated by rendering.

## Baselines, references and verification

- The calibrated grey-card render is the renderer's reference; the diffuse analytic prediction under the stated illumination is the check.
- Render sampling convergence: the metric's value at increasing sample counts, so the reported difference is above the noise floor.
- The uniform-reflectance optimum is the physical baseline: a panel array that cannot beat a single well-chosen paint is not a result in favour of the panel array, and the report must say so.
- The degradation curve is checked for symmetry: the metric from a viewpoint mirrored about the illumination plane should match within the render noise, and a failure indicates a bug in the camera setup, not physics.

## Metrics

- Image difference `D_in`, dimensionless perceptual units, between the rendered body patch and the background patch at matched image coordinates, averaged over the optimised viewpoints.
- Image difference `D_out(alpha)`, the same quantity as a function of angular distance `alpha` in degrees from the nearest optimised viewpoint.
- Half-angle `alpha_50`, degrees, at which `D_out` reaches twice `D_in`.
- Reflectance range used, per channel, compared with the stated physical range of real coatings; the fraction of panels clamped.
- Illumination sensitivity: the change in `D_in` for a 10 degree change in sun azimuth.
- Render cost per viewpoint-second, so the degradation study's scope is auditable.

## Deliverables

- The panel material model, the viewpoint sampler, the metric and the degradation driver as importable modules.
- Rendered image pairs (body and background) for the optimised set and for three viewpoints on the degradation curve, per background.
- The degradation curves as data and as a figure, with `D_in` and `alpha_50` marked.
- The optimisation log per configuration, with parameters, seed and metric value, plus a `pinneapple_lab` manifest.
- A short animation from the campaign video pipeline, panning from an optimised viewpoint to one where matching fails, with the metric on screen — this is the deliverable that carries the point without a caption.
- A report that states in its first paragraph that the result is appearance matching for a defined observer set, not invisibility.

## Acceptance criteria

- The calibrated grey-card render matches the analytic diffuse prediction within 5 percent in each channel.
- The render sampling noise in the metric is below one tenth of the smallest reported difference.
- The optimised panel array beats the best uniform reflectance on every background by a reported margin, or the report states that it does not and stops the claim there.
- The degradation curve is reported to a half-angle `alpha_50` for every configuration, with the value as a number.
- The number of panels clamped by the realizability bound is reported, and the metric with clamping is given alongside the unconstrained one.
- The mirror-symmetry check passes within the noise floor, and any case where it does not is diagnosed rather than excluded.

## Realizability and evidence class

`PLAUSIBILITY_CLASS`: `established` as a rendering and radiometry exercise; `hypothetical` as a concealment technology for a moving body under changing illumination, because the panel state must be sensed and driven, and no active sensing or control is modelled here. Axes this run fills: `mathematical_consistency` (rendering equation and the calibrated reference), `numerical_stability` (sampling convergence, optimiser repeatability), `physical_plausibility` (reflectance bounds of real coatings, illumination sensitivity), `uncertainty_sensitivity` (sun azimuth, texture variation). `experimental_validation` is not assessed — nothing is measured on a surface — and `technological_feasibility` is only partly addressed by the panel-parameter realizability check.

To move up one class, in order: (1) a panel technology whose reflectance and emission can be commanded over a range that brackets the required values, with a switching time shorter than the observer's motion timescale; (2) a sensing and control loop that estimates the background radiance from the body's own vantage point in real time, which is the actual hard problem and is entirely absent here; (3) a demonstrated result across the full hemispherical viewpoint set and not only the optimised directions; (4) operation under illumination that changes during the observation, since the static optimization result says nothing about a moving sun. Item 2 is the one that decides whether the subject is engineering or fiction.

## Risks and failure modes

1. **The metric flatters the result.** A raw pixel difference on a textured background can be small while the object is obvious. Mitigation: report at least two metric forms and state which one is the acceptance metric and why; include one perceptual form.
2. **The optimiser exploits a rendering artifact** — for example matching the background's pixel noise rather than its mean radiance. Mitigation: backgrounds are supplied at high sample count and the metric is computed on denoised, deterministic radiance.
3. **The result is a viewpoint-specific illusion reported as concealment.** Mitigation: the degradation curve is a primary deliverable with acceptance criteria; a report that gives only `D_in` fails the issue.
4. **Reflectance parameters are physically impossible.** Mitigation: the realizability bound is imposed on the parameter box before optimisation, and any clamped panels are reported with their cost.
5. **The illumination model is too simple for the claim.** Mitigation: the illumination is stated, and the sensitivity to a 10 degree sun change is a metric, so the reader can see how the answer depends on it.

## Out of scope

- Sensing and control of the panel state; any real-time adaptation.
- Metasurface and plasmonic design, and any wave-optical resonant structure.
- Radar, thermal-contrast and multispectral concealment beyond the single mid-infrared demonstration run.
- Any claim about detectability by a real sensor suite or an adversarial observer model.
