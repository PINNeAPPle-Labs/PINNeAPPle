Physics plan

  • rigid-body dynamics of the car along the track (Newton's second law along the path)
  • unilateral contact: normal force N = m (v^2 kappa + g cos theta) >= 0
  • rolling resistance C_rr N and aerodynamic drag 0.5 rho C_d A v^2
  • clothoid (Euler spiral) entry and exit: curvature rising linearly to 1/R
  • track strip in bending under the peak contact load (simply supported between stand clamps)

Build instructions

  1. Measure the car's rolling resistance first: on a straight strip of the track material tilted by an angle a, find the smallest angle at which the car keeps rolling at constant speed; C_rr = tan(a). This design completes the loop for C_rr up to 0.0695.
  2. Print or cut the track strip (PLA (Ingeo 4043D), 1.5 mm thick) from track.stl / track.step: 134 cm of running surface, 40 mm wide, with 8 mm side rails.
  3. Shape the loop: top arc radius 8.4 cm, entry and exit clothoids 21.0 cm long; the exit is shifted 5 cm sideways so it clears the entry.
  4. Clamp the strip to a stand every 10 cm; the loop is 18.8 cm tall.
  5. Ballast the car to 31 g (+-5 %).
  6. Set the launcher to 2.47 m/s (+-5 %); check with a phone slow-motion video over the 30 cm run-in.
  7. First runs: start 10 % below the design speed and raise it until the car completes the loop; the operating window table gives the speed range that works.

Limits and assumptions

  • The car is a point on its centre-of-mass path (1-D model) or a sphere (Newton); wheel bounce, steering and track joints are not modelled.
  • Newton's check has no friction and no air, and its contact dissipates energy: it confirms the 3-D track lets the car go round with margin, not the 1-D model's speeds to a tolerance.
  • Rolling resistance and drag are envelopes, not measurements: the design covers them, a car outside them (bent axle, dirty wheels) may not complete the loop.
  • The track strength check is a beam under a point load between clamps; the clamps, the stand and fatigue of the strip are not checked.
  • Toy scale only: nothing here applies to rides that carry people.
  • the car is a point on its centre-of-mass path for the 1-D model; Newton models it as a rigid sphere of the wheel radius
  • rolling losses are lumped into one coefficient C_rr = force / normal force
  • wheels: four solid PLA discs, their rotation adds inertia (not weight)
  • air at 20 °C, 1 atm (density from the ideal gas law)
  • the track is rigid and does not move (clamped to its stands)
  • crr_max = 0.05 (assumed design envelope, not a measured value: the design must work for any value in the range; measure yours (build step 1))
  • cd_range = 1.2 (worst case of the bluff-body range; a cube in cross-flow is about 1.05 (Hoerner, Fluid-Dynamic Drag, 1965))
  • launch_scatter = 0.05 (assumed design envelope, not a measured value: the design must work for any value in the range; measure yours (build step 1))
  • track_material = pla_4043d (template default: printable on a desktop 3-D printer)
  • support_spacing = 0.1 m (template default (build step: clamp the strip every 10 cm))
  • safety_factor = 2 (template default (engineering convention for a known static load; state yours))
