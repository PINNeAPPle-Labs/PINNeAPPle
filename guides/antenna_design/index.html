    Antenna design: an end-fire Vivaldi with an external FEM solver¶

    This guide runs, end to end, a study of an antipodal Vivaldi antenna (AVA) for 2–4 GHz with a 10 dBi target, the kind of study usually done by hand in a FEM simulator: a few variants, a table of S11 and gains, a plot. Here the FEM solver is EMerge (open source, pip install emerge), the antenna is an open parametric model written for this library, and PINNeAPPle adds the physical limits, the checks, the surrogate errors measured honestly, the design loop and a trust card per prediction.

    Everything lives in pinneapple_simulation.electromagnetics.antenna; the example examples/use_cases/antennas/vivaldi_2to4ghz.py only calls it.

    The same audit written as a datasheet -- the external solver's table read back, checked against the end-fire bound, and rendered to PDF without a new solve -- is the pattern in Design reports.

    pip install emerge                                                         # GPL-2.0, called, never copied
python examples/use_cases/antennas/vivaldi_2to4ghz.py --quick              # 3 designs x 2 frequencies, ~3 min
python examples/use_cases/antennas/vivaldi_2to4ghz.py                      # 16 designs x 5 frequencies + fine runs
python examples/use_cases/antennas/vivaldi_2to4ghz.py --package <dir>      # an external study package

    1. How long must the antenna be? (bounds)¶

    A Vivaldi radiates end-fire, along the board. Its gain is bounded by its length L, not by its broadside area: an end-fire travelling-wave antenna is at best a Hansen–Woodyard line source, D ≈ 7.28 L/λ (ordinary end-fire: D ≈ 4 L/λ; C. A. Balanis, Antenna Theory, ch. 6). The aperture formula G = η 4πA/λ² applies to a broadside aperture; used on a 100 × 200 mm Vivaldi it gives an optimistic bound for the wrong direction.

    from pinneapple_simulation.electromagnetics.antenna import bounds

bounds.min_length(10.0, 2e9)               # 0.2059 m: 10 dBi at 2 GHz needs L >= 206 mm (HW limit)
bounds.min_length(10.0, 2e9, "ordinary")   # 0.3747 m
bounds.feasibility_frequency(10.0, 0.206)  # 2.00e9 Hz: below it no 206 mm design reaches 10 dBi
    f [GHz] L for 10 dBi, Hansen–Woodyard [mm] ordinary end-fire [mm]
    2.0 206 375
    2.5 165 300
    3.0 137 250
    3.5 118 214
    4.0 103 187

    The feasibility frontier (lowest frequency where the target fits a board of length L): 2.75 GHz for 150 mm, 2.00 GHz for 206 mm, 1.58 GHz for 260 mm. A 10 dBi requirement down to 2 GHz on a board shorter than 206 mm is infeasible whatever the flare, the slots or the director; no simulation is needed to know it.

    2. The open AVA model (ava)¶

    AVAParams + layout describe the antenna in millimetres; layout is pure NumPy and raises InvalidGeometry for designs that cannot be built (arms thinner than 0.5 mm, slots cutting through an arm, a director touching the flare), which the DOE skips. simulate builds it in EMerge:

    • substrate εr 3.55, tan δ 0.003, h 1.524 mm; PEC conductors of zero thickness;
    • top layer: 50 Ω microstrip (width from Pozar eq. 3.197: 3.41 mm) then an exponential flare towards +x; bottom layer: ground of width Wg and length Lo, a linear taper to the strip width (the transition / balun), then the mirrored flare; optional corrugation slots (nslot, Ls, ws, ps) on the outer edges and an elliptical director (dir_a, dir_b, dir_x);
    • air box with air · λ of margin and a second-order absorbing boundary, lumped 50 Ω port at the board edge;
    • mesh size res · λ at the simulated frequency (one mesh per frequency);
    • per frequency: complex S11, directivity, IEEE gain and realized gain at boresight (+y) and at the peak, radiation efficiency, the far field (θ, φ, E_θ, E_φ) in ff_<f>GHz.npz, and the radiated power two ways (integral of the pattern, Poynting flux through the box) for the power-closure check.

    3. Running a parametric CLI solver (runner)¶

    CLISimulator(argv, workdir) runs any script that takes --params JSON --freq GHz --out DIR (flags configurable) and writes result.json: one process per design and per frequency, a timeout, the exit status and the peak memory of each child (os.wait4). Lessons measured on this 4-core, 15 GB container and built in:

    • EMerge's PARDISO backend can die with SIGBUS without a usable MKL: the AVA CLI uses SuperLU (emerge._emerge.solver._PARDISO_AVAILABLE = False); fallback_args re-runs a sample once after a SIGBUS;
    • three frequencies in one process with SuperLU went past 15 GB and were SIGKILLed: one frequency per process (also gives each frequency its own λ-sized mesh);
    • grid.model_S fails with a single frequency: the CLI reads grid.S;
    • the first EMerge run compiles Numba kernels (about 4 min here, 375 s for the first 3 GHz solve); later runs load them from the cache.

    read_table / write_table handle the (design, frequency) table: tag, the parameters, f_GHz, S11_dB, D_bore_dBi, G_bore_dBi, Greal_bore_dBi, D_peak_dBi, Greal_peak_dBi, rad_eff (CSV, TSV or whitespace).

    4. Checks of every row (checks)¶

    antenna_checks(row) returns a TrustReport (supports / contradicts / not_run):

    check relation critical
    realized_gain G_realized = G + 10 log10(1-|S11|²) no
    efficiency_is_G_over_D e = G/D no
    efficiency_le_1 e <= 1 (+1 %) yes
    peak_ge_boresight peak >= boresight no
    directivity_le_hw D <= 7.28 L/λ + 0.5 dB no
    s11_resolved a vector-fitted (interpolated) S11 needs >= 5 solved frequencies no
    power_closure pattern power = Poynting flux through the box, within 10 % no

    Missing inputs make a check not_run, never a silent pass.

    5. Surrogates and honest errors (surrogates)¶

    Four models, all predicting a column from (design, frequency): the per-frequency mean (baseline), the HW limit plus one fitted offset, ridge regression on physical features (log L/λ, W/λ, Wa/λ, slots on/off, director on/off), and a Gaussian process on the same features with the HW limit as prior mean (its std is the stated uncertainty). lodo(table, target) leaves one design out at a time — all its frequencies together — because leaving one row out lets the model see the same design at the neighbouring frequencies. holdout uses a fixed test set. coverage95 says how often the truth fell inside ±1.96 std.

    6. Design loop (design, study)¶

    • doe(n): LHS or Sobol over AVA_RANGES (configurable), unbuildable designs skipped and counted;
    • optimize(models, freqs): NSGA-II (pinneapple_design.design_optimizer) maximising the minimum realized gain over the band and minimising Lb·W, with max S11 <= -10 dB over the band, inside the span of the trained designs;
    • active_learning(simulate, table, freqs): GP variance picks the next designs (pinneapple_data.active_learning.VarianceBasedAL), EMerge verifies them, and the error of the prediction made before the simulation is recorded;
    • multi_fidelity(coarse, fine, target): many coarse runs (res 0.25 λ), a few fine ones (0.2 λ); the coarse-to-fine bias per frequency, validated leaving one fine design out;
    • prediction_card(model, params, f, train): in the training domain (feature space, critical), buildable (critical), std below 1 dB, below the HW limit, LODO error known and below 1.5 dB.

    study.open_study(out) chains all of it; study.package_study(dir, out) reproduces an external package.

    7. Measured results (open model, this container)¶

    All numbers below were measured by vivaldi_2to4ghz.py (full mode) on a 4-core, 15 GB container, EMerge 2.8.9, SuperLU, files in examples/use_cases/antennas/results/ (summary.json, results_*.csv, figures). Coarse mesh res = 0.25 λ (3 160 to 9 721 tetrahedra), air margin 0.25 λ, fine mesh res = 0.2 λ.

    Cost. 125 EMerge processes (one frequency each), all ok: median 10.8 s, peak memory 2.85 GB per process, 1 595 s of solver time, 1 633 s for the whole study (DOE, fine runs, checks, LODO, NSGA-II, verification, active learning). --quick (12 processes): 184 s. The very first EMerge run on a machine adds about 4 min of Numba compilation. The first 3 GHz solve, repeated later through the runner, gave the same S11 to the last digit (−19.431 dB).

    DOE. 16 buildable designs out of 31 LHS samples (15 rejected by layout before any solve).

    Against the target. No simulated design (20 designs × 5 frequencies, plus the optimiser's pick) reaches 10 dBi realized gain anywhere in the band. Best realized boresight gain per frequency: 5.4 dBi at 2 GHz, 7.1 at 2.5, 7.9 at 3.0, 9.5 at 3.5, 9.1 dBi at 4 GHz. The peak directivity stays 1.6 dB (best case) and 4.3 dB (median) below the Hansen–Woodyard limit of each design's length, so directivity_le_hw never failed; at 2 GHz even the limit of the longest board (260 mm) is 11.0 dBi. Only 3 of the 20 designs keep S11 ≤ −10 dB over the whole band.

    Checks. 80 coarse rows: 525 checks support, 35 contradict, all of them power_closure (35 rows, REVIEW); realized_gain, e = G/D, e <= 1, peak ≥ boresight and the HW limit hold on every row. Fine rows (20): 12 power_closure failures. The pattern power and the Poynting flux differ by 7.2 % (median) and up to 34.6 %. On the default design at 3 GHz the gap does not shrink with the mesh (13.2 %, 14.0 %, 15.1 % for res 0.25, 0.2, 0.15 λ) but it moves with the air margin: at 0.5 λ the far field gives more power than the surface flux and the efficiency becomes 1.018, which efficiency_le_1 (critical) rejects. Gains and efficiencies of this model at these settings therefore carry an uncertainty of order 10 % (≈ 0.5 dB); directivity, computed from the pattern itself, does not depend on that normalisation.

    Surrogates, leave-one-design-out (16 designs, 80 rows), RMSE [dB]:

    target frequency mean HW + offset linear features GP
    realized gain, boresight 1.76 1.58 1.70 1.83
    peak directivity 1.43 1.14 1.08 1.17
    S11 4.22 4.66 4.07 4.28

    With 16 designs and 14 parameters the physical features only beat the per-frequency baseline by 0.2–0.35 dB on gain and directivity, and no model predicts S11 (4 dB error, the features do not see the flare rate or the transition that set the match). The GP's 95 % intervals contain 82–94 % of the held-out values.

    Multi-fidelity (4 designs at both meshes, 20 pairs). Coarse against fine: 0.62 dB RMSE on realized gain, 0.49 dB on peak directivity, 3.49 dB on S11. A per-frequency bias learnt on the other fine designs does not reduce it (0.62, 0.55, 3.61 dB): the coarse-to-fine difference is design-dependent, not a constant offset, so with these meshes more fine runs, not a bias correction, are needed.

    NSGA-II on the GP (800 evaluations, 1.3 s, 83 designs on the front). The pick with the highest predicted minimum gain (Lb 249 mm, W 130 mm, 2 slots, director) was predicted at 5.75 ± 0.90 dBi at 2 GHz and simulated at 5.37 dBi, with 7.81 ± 1.53 predicted against 8.69 dBi at 4 GHz; its band minimum (5.37 dBi) did not beat the best DOE design (5.39 dBi), and its S11 is −9.8 dB at 3 GHz where the surrogate predicted at most −10.9 dB over the band. Its trust card: in domain, buildable, below the limit, but REVIEW because the LODO error of the GP (1.83 dB) is above 1.5 dB.

    Active learning (2 rounds × 2 designs, verified in EMerge). Prospective RMSE of the GP on the new designs before simulating them: 2.01 dB (round 1, 16 training designs, 70 % inside the 95 % interval), 1.60 dB (round 2, 18 designs, 90 %).

    What this says about the 10 dBi goal. At 2 GHz the target needs L ≥ 206 mm in the HW limit and this AVA family reaches about 4 dB less than its limit, so 10 dBi at 2 GHz with an AVA of this size class is not supported by any simulated design; at 3.5–4 GHz the best designs come within 0.5–1 dB of it.

    8. Reproducing an external study package (--package)¶

    A package is a local folder with the (design, frequency) table and, optionally, the CLI script that produced it, plus an optional package.json (table, script, argv, flags). Nothing is copied into the repository. --package <dir> checks every row, draws the measured directivity against the end-fire limits, computes the LODO errors of the four surrogates, and re-runs one design (--rerun <tag>, --rerun-freqs n) through the runner to compare with the table. --interpolated tells the checks the package's S11 came from a vector fit (the s11_resolved check then needs at least 5 solved frequencies).

    Measured on a package made from the full run above (the coarse table and a copy of the AVA CLI in a folder outside the repository, package.json naming them): 80 rows, 16 designs, 5 frequencies, the same check counts and LODO errors as above, and design d05 re-run at 2 and 2.5 GHz (9.6 s and 10.4 s, 0.96 and 1.03 GB) reproduced every column of the table exactly (difference 0.0). Whole --package run: 59 s.

    9. The antenna in 3-D (scene3d)¶

    study.render_3d({"base": {}, "best": best_design}, "results/3d") (or vivaldi_2to4ghz.py --3d, also run at the end of the full mode) re-solves each design at the five frequencies with its near field (AVAParams.nf_step, mm, on two cut planes: the substrate mid-plane and the vertical plane through the end-fire axis, 15 mm beyond the board, inside the air box up to 4 GHz) and builds, on the generic studio scene (pinneapple_tools.visualization.studio):

    • geometry: the laminate (translucent) and the 35 µm copper of both faces (flares, slots, director, feed line) extruded from ava.layout, <name>_antenna.glb, and per solved frequency <name>_<f>GHz.glb / .usda with the balloon (fields gain_dBi, directivity_dBi) and the cuts (E_dB);
    • radiation balloon in the antenna's frame, centred on the board, radius 0.6 Lb (G - G_max + 30 dB) / 30 dB, coloured by the IEEE gain 4πU/P_acc in dBi on jet, one gain scale for all frequencies; the far field is the 37 × 73 (θ, φ) grid that ava already computes (ff_<f>GHz.npz, now with P_rad, P_acc, S11);
    • near field: |E| in dB below the plane's 99.5th percentile (the singular feed gap would otherwise set the whole scale; clipped at 0 dB, shown down to −40 dB), and the instantaneous Re(E_x e^{jωt}) over one period (ωt 0–345° in 24 steps), E_x being the polarisation of the aperture field;
    • viewers (one self-contained HTML each, three.js from the library, opens from disk): <name>_viewer.html (orbit / zoom, frequency slider stepping through the solved frequencies only, layers antenna / balloon / cuts, head-up D peak, G peak, realized gain at +y and S11) and <name>_phase_viewer.html (ωt slider, play);
    • Blender Cycles (pip install bpy): <name>_beauty.png (copper on the laminate), <name>_balloon.png (balloon at 3 GHz with the jet gain bar), <name>_sweep/<name>_sweep.gif / .mp4 (2 → 4 GHz, one frame per solved frequency) and <name>_phase/<name>_phase.gif / .mp4 (one period at 3 GHz).

    The balloon is checked against the table: its maximum directivity equals the row's D_peak_dBi (same far-field grid and the same P_rad); render_3d records the largest difference and the example turns it into a check (< 1e-6 dB). A short dipole's sin²θ pattern gives 1.76 dBi on the same grid (test).

    Measured (vivaldi_2to4ghz.py --3d, the base design AVAParams() and the best design of section 7, 4 cores, EMerge 2.8.9 + SuperLU, bpy 5.2.3, CPU Cycles):

    base best

    peak directivity of the balloon, 2 → 4 GHz 5.12, 6.92, 7.61, 8.13, 8.72 dBi 7.09, 8.43, 9.69, 10.64, 11.27 dBi
    balloon peak − table D_peak_dBi (5 frequencies) 0.0 dB 0.0 dB
    S11, 2 → 4 GHz −7.0, −16.7, −19.4, −22.3, −25.5 dB −16.9, −24.0, −9.8, −12.4, −12.2 dB
    power_closure failures (section 7) 3 of 5 5 of 5
    Blender: beauty + balloon + 5-frame sweep + 24-frame phase, 960 × 540 169 s 166 s

    The whole --3d run took 460 s. Re-solving the best design for the 3-D views reproduced the rows of the study table to the last digit (the near-field sampling does not change the solve), and the base design at 3 GHz gave the same S11 (−19.43 dB) as its first run. Each viewer loads and draws in about 10 s in headless Chromium with software WebGL (screenshots below); a GPU browser is faster.

    10. What PINNeAPPle adds to a manual study of variants¶

    • The right physical limit. The end-fire bound 7.28 L/λ (and 4 L/λ), not the broadside aperture formula: it says before any simulation that 10 dBi at 2 GHz needs 206 mm, and from which frequency a given board can reach it.
    • Automatic checks of every number. Realized gain against S11, e = G/D, e <= 1, peak against boresight, directivity against the limit, power closure, interpolated S11 with too few points. In this study they flagged what a table of variants hides (section 7).
    • Honest surrogate error. Leave-one-design-out and held-out errors for four models, the baseline included, and the calibration of the stated uncertainty; a surrogate that does not beat the per-frequency mean is reported as such.
    • DOE, optimisation and active learning. Space-filling designs with geometric validity, NSGA-II on gain, match and area, and active learning whose prospective error is measured against the solver.
    • Multi-fidelity. Coarse meshes for many designs, fine ones for a few, the bias between them measured.
    • A trust card per prediction. Domain, buildability, uncertainty, physical limit, known error: a prediction outside them says so.
    • Seeing it in 3-D, from the solved fields. The copper and the laminate, the gain balloon at every solved frequency in the antenna's frame, the near field on two cuts, in a viewer that is one HTML file and in Cycles renders, with the balloon tied to the table it came from.
    • Reproducibility on another machine. One command, an open model, the solver called with recorded settings, per-process time and memory, tables and figures written next to the script, and the same flow as a Flow Studio node (lib.antenna.limits, lib.antenna.package, lib.antenna.open_study, lib.antenna.render_3d; flow antenna_vivaldi_limits).
