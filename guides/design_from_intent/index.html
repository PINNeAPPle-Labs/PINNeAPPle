    Design from intent¶

    pinneapple_design.intent turns a goal written in plain language into a design you can build: geometry, materials and operating conditions, with the margin to every failure mode, a trust report and the limits of what was checked. It never fills a gap with an invented number: a value it needs and cannot read becomes a question, a value it defaults says where the default comes from, and a request no template covers is refused with what would be needed.

    import pinneapple as pp

pp.design("a heat sink for a 40 W chip in still air")
# NeedsInput: What is the highest allowed temperature of the chip's case (or source)? [°C]

pkg = pp.design("a heat sink for a 40 W chip in still air", answers={"t_limit": (85, "°C")}, out_dir="hs")
print(pkg.summary())        # design, verdict with probability, trust decision, model calls
pkg.files["html"]           # hs/report.html; also report.pdf, design.json, geometry/*.step|stl|glb, check/

pp.design("an electric motor for my drone")
# OutOfScope (needs): needs an electromagnetics design template: the interior-PM machine FEA and drive ...

    The same call from a shell and from an agent:

    pinneapple design "a bracket that holds 2 kN" --answer arm_length=100mm --out bracket
pinneapple design --templates

    and the MCP tool design (pinneapple-mcp), which answers questions and refusals at once and runs the design as a job (poll job_status).

    The pipeline¶

    Each stage is a typed object you can inspect, test and replace.

    Stage Object What it does
    intent -> requirements formulate(text) -> RequirementSpec picks a template by keywords, reads every number unit pair, checks the unit's dimension and the template's range with pinneapple_data.physical_units, fills the template's slots; each value is stated (with the quote), answered, defaulted (with its source) or missing; missing critical values become Questions
    requirements -> physics plan(spec, template) -> PhysicsPlan governing physics, assumptions, design variables, the models at each fidelity with their availability on this machine, failure modes
    multi-fidelity optimisation optimise(...) -> OptimisationResult Latin-hypercube screening on the closed form, then the design-study core (pinneapple_design.design_optimizer: NSGA-II on the reduced model, or the GP surrogate-assisted loop when the reduced model costs seconds), distinct candidates (the knee of the Pareto front for several objectives)
    robustness robustness(...) -> Robustness Monte-Carlo propagation (pinneapple_analysis.uncertainty.tolerance.propagate) of the uncertain inputs to the probability that every margin stays >= 0, with a Wilson interval, the 5th percentile of each margin and the rank correlation of each input with the smallest margin
    verification verify(...) -> Verification the full solver on the chosen design, its own checks, and the comparison with the reduced and closed-form models output by output
    assessment assess(...) -> Assessment the trust report (pinneapple_analysis.trust.TrustReport) and the verdict: will work, likely to work (not verified by the full solver), marginal, will not work, unverified
    package DesignPackage geometry (STEP, STL, glTF, renders), bill of materials with cited properties, operating window, build instructions, every number with the model it comes from, model calls per fidelity, limits; report.html / report.pdf, design.json, check/ for pinneapple check

    Every template designs at the worst corner of its own uncertainty (the optimum is robust, not on the nominal edge), then the Monte Carlo measures the probability of success around it. When the full solver measures something the cheap models assumed (the bracket's stress-concentration factor), the design is re-checked with the measured value and re-optimised if it no longer meets its margins.

    An LLM is optional. Anything with extract(text, schema) -> dict (for example CallLLM("anthropic"), which wraps pinneapple_llm.call_llm) can propose the template and values; a value is used only if its quote is in the request and contains the number, and its unit has the slot's dimension. The deterministic parser always runs and wins on conflicts, so the pipeline works offline.

    The closed-form call counts include the Monte-Carlo samples when a template runs its robustness study on the calibrated closed form (the bracket). Each report also gives an estimate, not a measurement, of the time the full solver would have needed for every evaluation: the measured seconds of its one full call times the number of evaluations (about 650 h for the loop and 130 h for the bracket on the machine of this run; the measured call time varies between runs).

    (a) Loop-the-loop car¶

    Nothing in the request is a number, so every value is a default with its source: the rolling-resistance coefficient and the launcher scatter are design envelopes, not measurements (the design must work for any rolling resistance up to 0.05 and a launcher 5 % slow), the drag coefficient's worst case is the bluff-body end of the range (a cube is about 1.05, Hoerner), the strip material is the printable PLA with its datasheet yield strength.

    • Closed form: v_top^2 = v0^2 - 2 g z_top - losses with the losses at the mean speed, the top condition v_top^2 >= g (R - h), the peak load at the end of the entry clothoid.
    • Reduced: the 1-D along-track ODE (d(v^2)/ds with N = m (v^2 kappa + g cos theta), rolling resistance on N, drag, the wheels' inertia), RK4, vectorised: 6600 designs in 6.6 s. The car is a point on its centre-of-mass path, the track offset inwards by the centre-of-mass height h (12.5 mm for the car, 6 mm for Newton's sphere), so kappa = 1 / (R - h) on the top arc and N / (m g) = v_top^2 / (g (R - h)) - 1 at the top: 0.49 at the worst case (1.023 m/s, R - h = 7.14 cm), not the 0.27 a radius of R would give. Without losses it conserves energy to 0.3 % and reproduces the v0^2 = 5 g R threshold of a circular loop (tests).
    • Full: NVIDIA Newton (XPBD) on the 3-D track mesh with its side rails and the helical exit, at two time steps. Newton's contact dissipates energy on the curved mesh (5.7 % of the launch energy by the top here, with no friction at all, and it does not go away with a smaller step or a finer mesh), and its Coulomb friction, correct on its flat analytic case (stopping distance 0.5087 vs 0.5097 m), takes most of the energy of a rolling sphere on the curved mesh. So the Newton run is frictionless and judged against the worst-case envelope: the car must stay on the track (largest gap to the surface 0.1 mm) and keep at least the envelope's speed at the top (1.507 >= 1.023 m/s).

    The optimiser minimised the launch speed and the peak load on the car together; the knee of the front uses the longest clothoid allowed (2.5 R), which cuts the peak load to 5.0 g at the worst case, with a loop 8.4 cm in radius. Operating window (1-D model): launch 2.24 to 7.02 m/s (the upper end is the strip's yield / 2), any car from 10 g up, rolling resistance up to 0.0695 at the design speed minus the scatter.

    (b) Bracket, verified with CalculiX¶

    pp.design("a bracket that holds 2 kN") asks how far from the mounting face the load acts: the bending moment depends on it and there is no safe default. With 100 mm:

    • Closed form: cantilever beam theory (M c / I, P L^3 / 3 E I).
    • Reduced: an in-house hexahedral FE model of the L (C3D8I, the fillet as a staircase), 0.53 s per design, so the optimiser fits a GP surrogate of it and checks 19 designs with the FE model.
    • Full: CalculiX on a gmsh C3D10 mesh of the exact part (filleted corner, two M6 clearance holes, ISO 273), two mesh sizes. Checks: support reaction = load (1e-7), the arm's bending stress against M c / I at mid-arm (0.05 %), mesh convergence of the peak (1.2 %). The measured stress-concentration factor of the R = t fillet is 0.97 of the nominal stress (the peak is not at the fillet for this proportion), fed back into the reduced model.

    The bracket is sized at the worst corner of its stated uncertainty (load +10 %, plate -0.2 mm, yield 90 % of the datasheet value; all three labelled as assumptions in the report). The two M6 8.8 bolts (proof load from ISO 898-1) set the mounting-leg length: they are the limiting failure mode (5th-percentile margin 0.017). The STEP file is the OpenCASCADE B-rep from the gmsh script, with the exact holes and fillet.

    (c) Passive heat sink¶

    The request gives the power and "still air" (read as 0 m/s, quoted in the report); the allowed temperature has no safe default, so it is asked for. With 85 °C the template reuses the heat-sink sizer physics: the closed form is the hand calculation (Elenbaas / Bar-Cohen coefficient evaluated once with the fin root at the limit), the reduced model the converged resistance network, the full model the 3-D finite-volume solve of the base with its energy balance, spreading cross-check and grid convergence. The design is sized with the convection coefficient 15 % low and the interface resistance 50 % high (the engine's stated band and the paste's spread). In still air within the default 100 x 100 x 50 mm envelope only copper meets the limit at that corner: 1.23 kg, source (case) temperature 77.7 °C nominal, 83.9 °C with the coefficient 15 % low (the base underside peaks at 77.2 °C under the source; the paste's 10 K·mm²/W over 30 x 30 mm adds 0.44 K at 40 W). Operating window: up to 47.2 W at 25 °C, or 31.3 °C ambient at 40 W.

    (d) Glider wing¶

    The lift comes from the vortex-lattice code of pinneapple_design.aero (closed form: elliptic lifting line; reduced: a 3 x 10 lattice per half span; full: 8 x 32, with the lattice convergence of the span efficiency, Munk's e <= 1 and the lift slope below Helmbold's estimate as checks), the profile drag from turbulent flat-plate friction and Hoerner's thickness form factor. The wing is chosen for the lowest sink rate with the design speed taken at the heavy (+5 %), thin-air (-6 % density) and draggy (+25 % profile drag) corner, so the lift coefficient stays below the 0.8 design cap in every Monte-Carlo sample: span 2.0 m (the stated limit), aspect ratio 7.7, taper 0.33, NACA 2412, sink 0.44 m/s at 7.2 m/s (wing alone). No viscous solver ran: the report says so, and that a RANS check is the next level.

    (e) Out of scope¶

    pp.design("an electric motor for my drone") returns OutOfScope(kind="needs"): needs an electromagnetics design template: the interior-PM machine FEA and drive models exist (pinneapple_simulation.electromagnetics.machines), but no template sizes a machine from a request yet, and it would also need winding current-density, thermal and demagnetisation limits, which those models do not check. A motor template is the natural next one to write with the guide below. Weapons are refused outright (kind="refused"); a request no template matches lists the installed templates; a request a template matches but cannot honestly design (a loop for a roller coaster that carries people, a bracket under fatigue loading, a liquid-cooled heat sink) is refused by that template's check_scope with what it would need.

    Writing a template¶

    A new domain plugs in with one class and one register call. The worked example is pinneapple_design/intent/templates/example_spring.py, a helical compression spring (Shigley, ch. 10):

    import pinneapple_design.intent as I
from pinneapple_design.intent.templates.example_spring import CoilSpringTemplate

I.register(CoilSpringTemplate())
pkg = I.design("a steel compression spring that takes 50 N with 10 mm travel", verify=False)
print(pkg.summary())

    What a template declares, in the order the pipeline uses it:

    1. Selection and reading. keywords (word -> weight; the best total selects the template) and slots: each Slot has an SI unit (its dimension is what the parser matches), the words that point to it in a sentence, its valid range, and either critical=True (asked for when missing) or a default with a default_source. Phrases can set values through cues (("still air", 0.0)). check_scope(spec) refuses requests the physics does not cover; extra_questions(spec) asks for "one of these two" values.
    2. Physics. physics and assumptions (shown in the plan and the report), objectives (ObjectiveChoice with the words that select each one), failure_modes (each names an output that is a margin, >= 0 when the mode does not occur, typically capacity / demand - 1).
    3. Variables. variables(spec) -> [DesignVariable]; integers and choices (materials) are integer variables, and a value the user stated is fixed rather than optimised.
    4. Models. fidelities (closed form, reduced, full; each with the modules or programs it needs) and evaluate(level, X, spec, **options), which returns every margin and objective for a batch of designs. The full solver runs through verify(design, spec) -> FullResult with its own checks (equilibrium, energy balance, mesh convergence, a closed-form reference) and the outputs to compare with the reduced model (compare; compare_kind "at_least" / "at_most" for a solver known to be biased one way). reduced_is_expensive = True makes the optimiser fit a GP surrogate of the reduced model.
    5. Robustness. uncertainties(spec, design) -> [Uncertain] (nominal, spread, distribution and why this range), design_options(spec) (the worst corner the optimiser designs at), robust_level (a closed form calibrated to the best model that ran on the design, when the reduced model is too slow for thousands of samples).
    6. Package. geometry (closed Body meshes in metres), bill_of_materials (with the cited properties from pinneapple_design.intent.materials), operating_window, build_instructions, limits, figures.

    Rules that keep templates honest: every material property comes from materials.MATERIALS (each value carries its source) or from the user; every default has a default_source; an envelope that is assumed rather than measured says so in its source and in limits; a model that has no stall, fatigue, buckling or radiation says so in limits.

    Tests¶

    tests/test_design_intent.py: parsing offline, units and ranges, template selection, refusal, the LLM quote check, cited materials, each demo's models against closed forms (energy conservation and v_top^2 = g R for the loop, beam theory for the bracket, the finite-volume energy balance for the heat sink, Munk's e <= 1 for the wing), the CalculiX bracket when ccx and gmsh are installed, determinism with a fixed seed, the package files passing pinneapple check, the CLI, the MCP tool and a new template plugged in. The Newton loop check takes minutes and runs with PINNEAPPLE_SLOW=1.
