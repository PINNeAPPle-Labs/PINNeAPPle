    Fisher information and experimental design¶

    pinneapple_analysis.dynamics.fisher answers four questions about a model and a set of measurements:

    • how much the measurements say about the parameters;
    • what the best achievable precision is, on a parameter or on any quantity computed from the parameters;
    • which parameters cannot be told apart;
    • where and when to measure next.

    It is built on the models of pinneapple_analysis.inverse_problems: ForwardModel, CallableModel, ExpressionModel, and the SBML-based ODEForwardModel. It uses their derivatives through inverse_problems.forward.jacobian, in this order of preference:

    1. the model's own sensitivities;
    2. PyTorch autograd through predict_torch;
    3. central differences.

    For the full identifiability analysis (profile likelihood, posterior) it delegates to the existing analyze_identifiability. Results follow the toolkit's shared contract.

    Three quantities that are not the same¶

    Quantity Where What it is
    Theoretical bound CramerRaoBound, FIM.crlb() Cov(θ̂) ⪰ I(θ)⁻¹ for unbiased estimators, under the regularity conditions below. It depends on the model and the design, not on any estimator.
    Empirical performance estimator_performance(...) The spread of least-squares (Gaussian maximum-likelihood) fits of simulated data sets. It can exceed the bound (non-linearity, few data) or fall below it (bias, active parameter bounds).
    Bayesian uncertainty FIM.with_prior(P).crlb() The Laplace posterior covariance (I + P)⁻¹. A prior makes the estimator biased, so this is not a Cramér-Rao bound. The result says so in kind.

    Regularity conditions. Each result lists them as unchecked assumptions:

    • the likelihood is differentiable in θ;
    • its support does not depend on θ;
    • the estimator is unbiased;
    • the model is correct.

    What it does¶

    Task Call
    Information, Gaussian noise FisherInformation.gaussian(model, obs, parameters): JᵀΣ⁻¹J. With sigma_model= (noise that depends on θ, e.g. a relative error) it adds 2 ∂σ ∂σᵀ / σ².
    Information, counts FisherInformation.poisson(model, obs): Jᵀ diag(1/λ) J
    Information, any likelihood FisherInformation.from_loglik(loglik, theta, simulate, backend="fd" \| "torch" \| "jax"): E[s sᵀ] by Monte Carlo, with the standard error of each entry in mc_se
    Information, linear regression FisherInformation.linear_gaussian(X, sigma), exact
    Bounds fim.crlb(target): per-parameter std and correlations, plus target_std for g(θ) from ∇gᵀ I⁻¹ ∇g
    Identifiability ParameterIdentifiability(model, obs).local() (rank, null directions, status per parameter) and .full() (delegates to analyze_identifiability)
    Design ExperimentalDesign(model, candidates, base=..., prior_precision=...).select(n, "D" \| "A" \| "E" \| "c" \| "bayes-D", target=...)
    When to measure ObservationScheduler(model, (t0, t1), sigma=...).schedule(n, criterion) and .next_best(base)
    Value of one more measurement InformationGain(fim, model).of(new_obs, target): Δ log det, the expected information gain in nats, and the variance reduction of a target
    Comparing schedules SamplingStrategy.compare(model, {"uniform": ..., "log": ..., ...}), with the builders uniform, logspaced, random and endpoints
    Local sensitivities SensitivityAnalyzer(model).local(obs) (raw, relative and noise-scaled) and .target(g). Global Sobol and Morris analysis is in pinneapple_analysis.sensitivity.

    Targets¶

    A target can be any function of the parameters, for example a prediction at a given time, or a first-passage time computed by FirstPassageAnalyzer on the model's own predicted trajectory. It is differentiated by central differences in the physical parameters.

    Backends¶

    PyTorch and JAX are optional. They are imported only when backend="torch" or "jax" is requested. If the requested one is missing, BackendUnavailable gives the install command.

    Implementation notes¶

    Physical and scaled parameters. jacobian differentiates with respect to the scaled parameters z of ParameterSet: ln θ for log parameters, θ/|θ₀| for linear ones. The module converts to physical parameters, so every bound is in the parameter's own unit.

    Design. The design is a greedy selection from the candidates, followed by Fedorov exchange passes, with replicates allowed. The criteria are:

    • D: maximise log det;
    • A: minimise tr I⁻¹;
    • E: maximise λ_min;
    • c: minimise gᵀ I⁺ g;
    • Bayesian D: D with the prior precision.

    The c criterion accepts a singular information matrix when g lies in its range. This is the usual case: the best design for one target often cannot identify every parameter. For example, to estimate the time a decay reaches a level, measure near that time.

    The result is a local optimum of a combinatorial problem, not a certified global one. It is also locally optimal, that is, optimal at the nominal parameters.

    Identifiability. - A zero eigenvalue of I (relative threshold rtol) is a structurally non-identifiable direction at that point. The direction is reported as a parameter combination; for y = a b t it is d ln a = −d ln b. - A finite but large bound (above practical_threshold times the value) is a practical non-identifiability. - The verdict is numerical and local, not a symbolic proof.

    Examples¶

    When it works. Take the decay y = A e^{−kt} with σ = 0.05. The D-optimal two-point design is known to be {0, 1/k} (Box & Lucas 1959), and the scheduler finds it:

    import math
import numpy as np
from pinneapple_analysis.inverse_problems.forward import CallableModel, Parameter
from pinneapple_analysis.dynamics.fisher import ObservationScheduler

m = CallableModel(lambda th, inp: th["A"] * np.exp(-th["k"] * inp["t"]), inputs=["t"],
                  parameters=[Parameter("A", 2.0, 0.1, 10), Parameter("k", 0.5, 0.01, 5)])
sch = ObservationScheduler(m, (0, 10), n_grid=401, sigma=0.05)
print(sch.schedule(2, "D").estimate["times"])                # [0.0, 2.0]

tau = lambda th: math.log(th["A"] / 0.5) / th["k"]           # time at which y reaches 0.5
r = sch.schedule(3, "c", target=tau)
print(r.estimate["times"], r.uncertainty["target_std"])      # three points near tau = 2.77; std 0.1161

    The delta-method value for three measurements exactly at τ is σ/(k c √3) = 0.1155. The D-optimal design gives the same target a bound of 0.189.

    When it tells you the data cannot answer. With y = A e^{−kt} + B measured at a single time, the rank is 1, so two parameter combinations are invisible. Measured at two times, the rank is 2. Only a spread of times identifies all three parameters. ParameterIdentifiability(...).local() reports each case. With y = a b t no schedule helps: the null direction a/b is structural.

    When the bound is optimistic. Take four noisy points (σ = 0.3) of the decay. The least-squares estimate of k has efficiency 0.82 (95 % interval 0.75–0.90), and its bias is 0.023 ± 0.005. That is the empirical estimator falling short of the theoretical bound, and the benchmark row reports it.

    Cost¶

    Operation Cost
    Gaussian or Poisson information one Jacobian, so p extra model evaluations by finite differences (2p for central differences), or one sensitivity solve, plus O(n p²)
    Score Monte Carlo n_mc simulated data sets, each with 2p log-likelihood evaluations (or one autodiff pass)
    Design of n points from m candidates O(n m p³) greedy, plus O(passes · n m p³) exchange. The 401-candidate, 2-parameter designs above take under 0.11 s.
