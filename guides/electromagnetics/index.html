    Electromagnetics and electrodynamics¶

    pinneapple_simulation.electromagnetics (pp.em) solves electromagnetic problems in SI units and checks the results against independent references; pinneapple_physics.maxwell_checks (pp.maxwell) checks any electromagnetic field, from a solver or a neural network, against Maxwell's equations without a reference solution. This page gives the theory the code implements, what is verified and to which order, and the limits.

    Conventions¶

    • SI units throughout. Constants are CODATA 2022 (pp.em.constants): mu0 = 1.25663706127e-6 N/A² (measured since the 2019 SI redefinition), eps0 = 1 / (mu0 c²), eta0 = mu0 c.
    • Phasors are Re[X exp(+j omega t)] (engineering convention, as in Taflove & Hagness, Pozar, scikit-rf). A lossy medium has Im(eps_r) < 0; a wave travelling to +z is exp(-j k z). Physics texts (Jackson, Bohren & Huffman) use exp(-i omega t); their eps is the complex conjugate of the one here. pp.em.mie takes the index in this package's convention (m = n - j k, the same as miepython).
    • pp.em.units.check_equations() verifies that every term of every law below has the same SI dimension (pinneapple_data.physical_units, now with T, Wb, coulomb, farad, henry and siemens), and check_units({"B": "mT", ...}) flags fields declared in the wrong unit or scale before residuals are computed.

    Maxwell's equations¶

    Differential form (Jackson, Classical Electrodynamics, 3rd ed., sec. 6.1; Griffiths, Introduction to Electrodynamics, 4th ed., ch. 7):

    Law Differential Integral
    Gauss div D = rho oint_S D . dA = Q_enc
    Gauss (magnetic) div B = 0 oint_S B . dA = 0
    Faraday curl E = -dB/dt oint_C E . dl = -d/dt int_S B . dA
    Ampere-Maxwell curl H = J + dD/dt oint_C H . dl = I_enc + d/dt int_S D . dA

    with D = eps E, B = mu H, J = sigma E + J_s. Charge continuity d rho/dt + div J = 0 follows from the divergence of Ampere-Maxwell plus Gauss; Poynting's theorem du/dt + div S + J . E = 0, S = E x H, u = (E . D + B . H) / 2, from the curl equations (Jackson sec. 6.7).

    Interface conditions (Jackson sec. I.5): n x (E1 - E2) = 0, n x (H1 - H2) = K, n . (D1 - D2) = sigma_s, n . (B1 - B2) = 0. A perfect electric conductor (PEC) has n x E = 0.

    Formulations¶

    Regime Unknown Equation Where
    Electrostatics phi, E = -grad phi -div(eps grad phi) = rho solve_electrostatic (P1 FEM), kinds electrostatics, electrostatics_integral
    Magnetostatics (2-D) A_z, B = curl(A_z e_z) -div(nu grad A) = J + curl(nu Br) solve_magnetostatic (P1 FEM, Newton for nonlinear B-H), kind magnetostatics_az
    Magneto-quasistatics phasor A_z -div(nu grad A) + j omega sigma A = J_s + sigma u_c solve_time_harmonic (solid conductors with imposed currents), eddy_current_fdm (axisymmetric), kind eddy_current_az
    Full wave, time domain E, H curl equations kinds maxwell_tm, maxwell_te, maxwell_3d, maxwell_tm_integral
    Full wave, frequency domain phasor E_z lap E + k0^2 eps_r E = f kind maxwell_tm_frequency

    The A-formulation in a solid conductor carries the voltage per metre u_c as an extra unknown fixed by its net current (Jin, The Finite Element Method in Electromagnetics, 3rd ed., ch. 4 and 8), which is the 2-D A-V formulation.

    Integral form as a PINN loss¶

    The compiler kinds electrostatics_integral and maxwell_tm_integral impose the integral laws on a square control volume around each collocation point (Gauss-Legendre quadrature on its edges). They need only first derivatives and they see material interfaces: with eps jumping from 1 to 4 across a capacitor, phi = y satisfies eps lap(phi) = 0 on both sides, so the differential residual is exactly zero for this wrong field, while the flux of D through the squares that straddle the interface is not (tests/test_maxwell_checks.py::test_integral_gauss_sees_the_dielectric_interface_the_differential_form_misses).

    Materials¶

    • Medium(eps_inf, mu_r, sigma, poles=...) with Drude(omega_p, gamma), Lorentz(delta_eps, omega0, delta) and Debye(delta_eps, tau) poles (Taflove & Hagness, Computational Electrodynamics, 3rd ed., ch. 9). Each pole carries the coefficients of its auxiliary differential equation for time-domain solvers; the trapezoidal recursions reproduce chi(omega) at second order in dt (table below). is_passive checks Im(eps_r) <= 0.
    • Anisotropic(eps_r, mu_r, sigma) tensors, uniaxial(n_o, n_e, axis), rotations, and the two refractive indices of a plane wave along any direction from Fresnel's equation of wave normals. is_reciprocal is the symmetry of the tensors (a gyrotropic medium is not reciprocal).
    • Magnetic: linear LinearMaterial, Magnet (B = mu0 mu_r H + Br), measured BHCurve (monotone PCHIP), BrauerCurve, and FrohlichKennelly (B = mu0 H + H / (alpha + beta |H|), every quantity of the Newton solver in closed form). JilesAtherton(Ms, a, k, c, alpha) integrates the hysteresis model of Jiles & Atherton (J. Magn. Magn. Mater. 61, 1986) along an applied-field path; its dM/dH is derived from the model's own definitions (M = Mrev + Mirr), and c = 1 reproduces the anhysteretic curve.

    References in closed form¶

    pp.em.statics: capacitors (parallel plate, coax, spheres), point and line charges, infinite wire, exact Biot-Savart of straight segments (biot_savart, with the cancellation-free form beside long segments), circles, helices, the off-axis loop field in elliptic integrals, finite solenoid, Helmholtz pair, Maxwell's mutual inductance of coaxial loops, loop self-inductance. pp.em.waves: Fresnel coefficients, Brewster and critical angles, planar multilayers by characteristic matrices (Born & Wolf), skin depth and surface impedance, rectangular and circular waveguide cut-offs and modes, rectangular and cylindrical cavity resonances and the TE101 conductor Q (Pozar), Hertzian dipole fields and power, thin-dipole patterns, half-wave dipole resistance and directivity (Balanis), TMz scattering by a cylinder, surface-plasmon and plasma dispersion. pp.em.mie: Mie series for dielectric and perfectly conducting spheres (Bohren & Huffman; Wiscombe's series length), efficiencies, asymmetry parameter, amplitude functions.

    Physics checks of any field¶

    pp.maxwell.maxwell_checks(grid, fields, eps=..., mu=..., sigma=..., omega=None) judges a field without a reference, like the Darcy checks: Gauss's laws, Faraday, Ampere-Maxwell and continuity as relative residuals (||sum of terms|| / sum of ||term||, second-order differences), Poynting's energy balance over the grid box, PEC faces, and optionally a reference. Every residual is also computed on the grid with every other point: the ratio r(2h) / r(h) is about 4 when the residual is discretisation error (the field obeys the law) and about 1 when the field violates it, and the message says which. A law whose terms all vanish for the given components is reported as not informative instead of passed. tangential_continuity, pec_boundary, reciprocity and passivity cover interfaces and S-parameters. maxwell_trust_report gives the deploy decision.

    In CI:

    [[result]]
path = "fields.npz"           # x, y, (z), (t) axes; Ex..Hz / Bx.., Dx.., Jx.., rho; phasors as Ez_re / Ez_im
physics = "maxwell"
parameters = { eps_r = 1.0, mu_r = 1.0, sigma = 0.0 }   # omega = <rad/s> for phasors

    pinneapple check reports Gauss's laws under PC102, Faraday / Ampere-Maxwell / continuity under PC301 and Poynting's theorem under PC103 (tolerances maxwell-gauss, maxwell-faraday, maxwell-ampere, maxwell-continuity, maxwell-energy). save_maxwell_npz writes the file with SI units and check_config_entry the configuration block. It runs without torch.

    Time domain: FDTD on the Yee grid¶

    pp.em.fdtd solves the curl equations by the leapfrog scheme of Yee (1966): E at integer, H at half time steps, components staggered by half a cell (Taflove & Hagness, Computational Electrodynamics, 3rd ed., ch. 3). One code path covers 1-D, 2-D and 3-D: an axis with one node is a direction of invariance.

    import numpy as np
from pinneapple_simulation.electromagnetics import fdtd, media

grid = fdtd.YeeGrid((200, 200, 1), (1.5e-2, 1.5e-2, 1.5e-2))     # dt = 0.99 x the Courant limit
sim = fdtd.FDTD(grid, {"x": "cpml", "y": "cpml"}, npml=10)
sim.set_material(fdtd.fill_fraction(grid, lambda x, y, z: np.hypot(x - 1.5, y - 1.5) < 0.3), media.Medium(3.0))
sim.add_plane_wave(fdtd.PlaneWaveTFSF("x", "Ez", fdtd.modulated_gaussian(1e9, 0.6e9), ((30, 170), (30, 170), (0, 1))))
dft = sim.add_dft(["Ez", "Hx", "Hy"], [1e9])
sim.run_until(40e-9)
    • Stability. dt <= 1 / (c sqrt(sum 1/dx_i^2)) (TH eq. 4.65). YeeGrid refuses a larger step; the test shows grid-scale noise growing by 1e6 in 400 steps at 1.02 times the limit and staying bounded at 0.999.
    • Numerical dispersion. The scheme's waves obey (sin(omega dt/2) / (c dt))^2 = sum_i (sin(k_i dx_i/2) / dx_i)^2 (TH eq. 4.12, numerical_dispersion). The FDTD resonances match this relation to 1e-7 and the continuum at second order.
    • Energy. The scheme conserves (1/2) sum eps E^{n-1} . E^n + (1/2) sum mu |H^{n-1/2}|^2 exactly in a lossless cavity (energy(); constant to 1e-12 in the test).
    • Boundaries. pec, pmc, periodic, bloch (Floquet phase exp(-j k_b L), complex fields) and cpml, the convolutional PML of Roden & Gedney (2000) with polynomial sigma, kappa and linear alpha grading (TH sec. 7.9). Measured reflection 5 cells from the PML: -51 dB (5 cells), -87 dB (10), -106 dB (20).
    • Dispersive media by auxiliary differential equations solved with the E update: Drude (polarisation current, trapezoidal), Debye (polarisation, trapezoidal), Lorentz (polarisation, central), TH sec. 9.4-9.6.
    • Sources and plane waves. Soft and hard E sources, impressed currents (a Hertzian dipole is one cell of J). PlaneWaveTFSF is a total-field / scattered-field box driven by an auxiliary 1-D line with the same dx and dt. The masked-curl form works for any box in 1-D, 2-D and 3-D, and the leakage into the scattered-field region is 1e-16.
    • Sub-cell averaging. fill_fraction gives the fraction of each cell inside a shape, and set_material mixes eps arithmetically with it at the nodes. Each E component then takes the harmonic mean of its two nodes (the series rule for E normal to an interface; harmonic_eps=False switches it off). For E tangential to the surface this is the exact first-order correction (Farjadpour et al., Opt. Lett. 31, 2006):
    • the TMz cylinder converges at order 2, where the staircased one jumped between 1.2 % and 3.4 %;
    • on a sphere (E normal to part of the surface) harmonic averaging cuts the Q_sca error from 9.7 % to 2 %, but the error then plateaus near 2 % instead of converging. Full anisotropic (Kottke) smoothing is not implemented.
    • Monitors and output.
    • Probes and running DFTs (phasors in this package's exp(+j omega t) convention, E and H stamped at their own times).
    • poynting_flux_dft gives the power through a plane.
    • NearToFarField gives far-field patterns and radiated power from equivalent currents on a closed box (Balanis, Advanced Engineering Electromagnetics, eq. 6-122).
    • snapshot() returns node- and time-centred fields in the layout of maxwell_checks, so an FDTD run can be checked like any other field.

    Figures and animations: pinneapple_tools.visualization.electromagnetics (field_movie writes GIF with Pillow or MP4 with ffmpeg, field_map adds a material outline and Poynting arrows, radiation_pattern draws polar patterns). examples/electrodynamics/08_fdtd_gallery.py writes results/fdtd_cylinder_scattering.gif / .mp4, results/fdtd_cylinder_field_map.png and results/fdtd_dipole_pattern.png. Each figure comes with its check: the cylinder's field against the series of cylinder functions (1.7 % at 30 cells per wavelength), the snapshots through maxwell_trust_report (APPROVED), and the dipole pattern and power against the closed forms.

    Electric machines¶

    The A_z solver includes a polar ring mesher with a moving band, magnets, coils, (anti-)periodic constraints, Maxwell-stress (Arkkio) and co-energy torque, flux linkage, back-EMF, cogging, Steinmetz/Bertotti iron loss, resistance-limited magnet eddy loss, and an SPMMachine model with adjoint torque sensitivities used by the rotor topology optimisation (pinneapple_design.topology, guide). tests/test_motor_magnetics.py asserts: slotless SPM torque within 1e-3 of the exact subdomain solution by both Maxwell stress and co-energy (360 angular steps, rotor off the grid); Maxwell-stress and co-energy torque of the slotted 24-slot machine within 0.3 %; skin-effect impedance of a round wire within 0.3 % of the Bessel solution (the table below measures 8.55e-4); adjoint rotor-torque sensitivities within 1e-5 of finite differences, for linear and nonlinear iron. examples/topology_optimization/03_reluctance_rotor.py (run 2026-10-17) optimises a reluctance rotor to a mean torque of 38.10 N m with linear iron; re-evaluated with the nonlinear B-H curve the same rotor gives 13.44 N m, so saturation must be in the optimisation loop for thin-featured designs.

    Interior-PM traction motor: the 2004 Toyota Prius¶

    pinneapple_simulation.electromagnetics.machines models interior-magnet machines on conforming meshes:

    • geometry: parametric V-magnet rotors (VRotor: V opening and depth, magnet width and thickness, bridges, ribs, centre post, magnet segments) and round-bottom slots (RoundBottomSlots), the parametrisations of pyleecan's HoleM50 and SlotW11; prius_2004() holds the dimensions of pyleecan's Toyota_Prius.json (48 slots, 8 poles, 0.75 mm gap, 83.82 mm stack, stacking factor 0.95).
    • mesher: every polygon edge is a mesh edge (conforming Delaunay with Gabriel-edge recovery, concentric-shell splitting at small angles). Half a pole and half a slot pitch are meshed, mirrored and copied around, so all poles and slots have identical, symmetric elements: the no-load torque of the aligned rotor is 6e-13 N m and the cogging curve is antisymmetric with zero mean. The air gap holds three ring layers; only the middle one (moving band) is re-triangulated as the rotor turns.
    • steel: M400-50A (EN 10106) B-H curve and specific-loss table (50 Hz to 2.5 kHz), the data of pyleecan's material library (Apache-2.0), and the Prius magnet grade (Br 1.24 T at 20 degC, -0.1 %/K); stacked applies the stacking factor; TableIronLoss interpolates the catalogue table; fit_iron_loss fits k_h f^alpha B^beta + k_c (f B)^2 + k_e (f B)^1.5 with the classical term from resistivity and thickness.
    • pmsm.IPMMachine: Newton solution with the nonlinear B-H curve, Arkkio and co-energy torque, flux linkage, back-EMF, the dq frame (d-axis on the magnet flux), torque ripple, frozen-permeability flux split and L_d / L_q, MTPA by golden-section search on the FEA torque, iron loss per element in its own frame (table or fit) and resistance-limited magnet eddy loss.

    The FEA is checked against a published benchmark here and against a second, independent code (gmsh + GetDP) in the next section. FEMM (Windows-only) and pyleecan (its wheel needs a git-only dependency) do not run in this environment. The published reference is the torque against current angle of the Prius motor at 250 A peak computed by Yang, Krishnamurthy and Brown (IEEE IEMDC 2013), as tabulated in pyleecan's test suite. Their model and pyleecan's geometry are not the same file (centre post, magnet temperature, steel data), so agreement within a few per cent is the expectation, not a mesh error.

    Drive model: MTPA, flux weakening, efficiency map, WLTC energy¶

    pinneapple_simulation.electromagnetics.machines.drive turns a machine into a drive under inverter limits:

    • FluxMap.from_fea(machine, I_max): mean torque and dq flux linkages of the FEA on an (i_d, i_q) grid (saturation and cross-saturation included, odd/even symmetry in i_q), and per grid point the speed-free iron-loss sums and magnet-loss coefficient (IPMMachine.loss_components), so the core loss at any speed is k_h f^alpha S_h + k_c f^2 S_c + k_e f^1.5 S_e + c_m f^2 without new field solutions.     Step skew, tolerances and a 3-D view¶

    pinneapple_simulation.electromagnetics.machines.robustness:

    • Step skew by multi-slice 2-D FEA. A rotor of K axial segments, with segment k turned by delta_k = s (k - (K-1)/2). Each segment is a 2-D slice with the same stator and phase currents. The torque and flux linkage are the means over the slices. skewed_torque gives load torque and ripple, skewed_no_load gives cogging and flux linkage. Inter-segment axial flux and end effects are neglected, as in every multi-slice model. For a harmonic of order h per period lam, the slice mean multiplies the straight rotor's harmonic by the step-skew factor |sin(K h pi s / lam) / (K sin(h pi s / lam))| (step_skew_factor).
    • tolerance_model(geometry): the FEA mean torque and ripple as a function of remanence scale, magnet thickness, magnet width and air gap, for pinneapple_analysis.uncertainty.tolerance.propagate (JCGM 101 Monte Carlo).
    • skewed_field_3d extrudes each segment's 2-D solution into prisms turned by its skew angle. export.write_vtu and export.solution_to_vtu write VTK unstructured grids for ParaView.

    Magnet temperature, thermal network and demagnetisation¶

    pinneapple_simulation.electromagnetics.machines.thermal:

    • ThermalNetwork: isothermal nodes with heat capacities, conductances between nodes and to fixed-temperature boundaries, time-dependent sources; steady state and transients (backward Euler or trapezoidal rule). machine_network(geometry) builds the seven-node model of a water-jacketed machine (stator yoke, teeth, winding in the slots, end windings, rotor, magnets, shaft path) from the cross-section: cylindrical-shell conduction 2 pi k L / ln(r2/r1), slot insulation, axial conduction to the end windings, convection across the gap and at the end windings. The material, contact and heat-transfer data are the stated defaults of MachineThermalData (coolant 65 degC). They are inputs, not correlations fitted here, and illustrative: none is measured on a Prius.
    • IPMMachine(..., magnet_temperature=T): remanence Br(T) = Br20 (1 + alpha_Br (T - 20)), with alpha_Br = -0.1 %/K (the pyleecan Prius magnet). magnet_temperature_sweep evaluates MTPA torque, back-EMF and L_d, L_q with the FEA at each temperature.
    • demagnetisation_margin(machine, solution, Hcj): in every magnet element, the field opposing the magnetisation H_rev = -(B . m - Br) / (mu0 mu_r) against the intrinsic coercivity (the knee of a square-loop sintered NdFeB magnet). It returns the per-element ratio H_rev / Hcj and the demagnetised area fraction (ratio > 1). intrinsic_coercivity(Hcj20, T, beta) = Hcj20 (1 + beta (T - 20)). NDFEB_HCJ_MIN_KAM holds the minimum Hcj at 20 degC of each grade letter (N 12, M 14, H 17, SH 20, UH 25, EH 30, AH 35 kOe), the manufacturers' grade convention. beta = -0.55 %/K is a typical datasheet value for sintered NdFeB and an assumption here: use the grade's datasheet.

    Rotor design: FEA + Gaussian-process surrogate + NSGA-II¶

    pinneapple_simulation.electromagnetics.machines.design puts the FEA behind the design-study core of pinneapple_design.design_optimizer:

    • rotor_evaluator maps five rotor variables (magnet width W4 and thickness H3, V depth H0 and opening W0, bridge H1) to the FEA mean torque and ripple at a fixed current, plus the exact magnet mass. Invalid geometries are rejected without a solve.

    • NSGA-II that calls the FEA for every individual.

Mechanical stress, demagnetisation and the torque-speed envelope are not constraints of this study, so it does not claim a better rotor. It shows the method and its FEA-call saving.

    End effects: end windings and magnet end leakage¶

    Two effects outside the 2-D cross-section:

    • End windings. machines.endwinding models each coil end as a thin conductor on the cylinder at the mean slot radius: axially out of the stack by the overhang, a half circle over the coil span, then back.
    • The same path gives the end-turn length of the phase resistance (prius_phase_resistance, 0.16 m per coil end), so resistance and inductance share one geometry.
    • end_winding_inductance integrates the phase inductances with Neumann's double integral, using the thin-wire kernel 1 / sqrt(r^2 + g^2) with the wire's geometric mean distance g = a e^-1/4 (statics.filament_inductance). The coil bundle is a round conductor of the slot's copper area. Both ends are included, in air and with the stator end face as an infinitely permeable mirror (image currents); the two cases bound the laminated face.
    • The dq leakage L_end = L_aa - M_ab adds to both axes in the voltage equations (Drive(..., L_end=...)). It cancels in the torque 1.5 p (psi_d i_q - psi_q i_d).
    • Magnet end leakage. getdp.end_leakage_3d builds a 3-D magnetostatic model of half the stack, from the mid-plane to the end face, with an air region beyond the face: the cross-section extruded into prisms. The solver is GetDP, using a magnetic scalar potential and Picard iteration on the secant permeability. The model is compared with the same mesh without the end air, which is the extruded 2-D field. The ratio of the no-load air-gap flux under a pole corrects the 2-D magnet flux.

    The filament inductance is verified against closed forms (tests/test_emachine_ends.py):

    • a circular loop against Rosa's mu0 R (ln(8R/a) - 7/4), within 2e-3;

    pinneapple_simulation.electromagnetics.fem3d solves 3-D magnetostatics and time-harmonic eddy currents on first-order Nedelec (Whitney edge) elements on tetrahedra:

    3-D skew: cogging in 3-D against multi-slice 2-D¶

    pinneapple_simulation.electromagnetics.machines3d builds the surface-PM machine of SPMMachine in 3-D on the edge elements above.

    • SPM3D(machine, n_theta, stack_layers, end_length, end_layers) extrudes the 2-D polar mesh along the stack (extrude), with air beyond each end.
    • Every node ring carries n_theta equally spaced nodes, so rotor position and magnet skew are relabellings of the rotor elements of each layer, on one mesh for every angle and skew. Cogging therefore carries no remeshing noise.
    • The rotor core starts at r_inner, a flux-tangential boundary standing in for the shaft.
    • Each angle is a preconditioned CG solve: the tree-gauged matrix is factorised once (PARDISO when pypardiso is installed, SuperLU otherwise) and used as the preconditioner, so a solve takes 5 iterations.
    • The torque is Arkkio's Maxwell stress over the two gap annuli, as in the 2-D model.
    • skew.skew_study(model, totals, kinds) computes, for step skew (2 or 3 segments) and continuous skew (one offset per stack layer), the cogging over one period in 3-D, and in multi-slice 2-D: the mean of straight one-layer models at the same offsets, on the same mesh. The two differ only by the axial flux between segments and the end leakage. Rows are checkpointed (checkpoint=), so an interrupted study resumes.

    Two discretisation effects had to be removed before the numbers meant anything (both tested in tests/test_machines3d.py):

    • Mesh handedness. Cutting every prism into tetrahedra by increasing node number leans all cuts the same way round the axis: a helical bias that gave 0.1 N m of torque on the aligned rotor, where the 2-D model gives exactly 0. Ordering the cuts by alternating ring position removes it. The one-layer 3-D model then matches the 2-D code within 0.01 N m and is exactly odd about the aligned position.
    • Period of the mesh. The polar mesher's triangulation repeats every two angular steps. With 360 steps a 24-slot pitch is 15 steps, an odd number, so the discrete machine repeats only every 30 degrees. The 2-D code shows the same thing: T(16 deg) = 0.127 against T(1 deg) = 0.068 N m (the audit's "mesh-pattern dependence of a few % when the slot pitch has an odd number of steps"). The study uses 720 steps (30 per slot pitch) and whole periods, and skew_study refuses an odd number of steps per period.

    Two changes to solve_eddy made the fine meshes possible:

    • A null mode in the gauge. The tree gauge spans the air from two groups of roots, the outer surface and the plate (the potential A* must stay free in the conductor). A forest grown from both leaves one more mode: the gradient of a potential that is 1 on the plate and 0 outside, zero on every gauged edge. The tree-gauged system was singular, and both SuperLU and PARDISO stopped at a relative residual of 1e-4. tree_gauge(..., link_roots=True) joins the root groups with one more edge each; the residual is now 2e-14 and the loss did not change (5.19718 W against 5.19712 W on the coarse mesh, so the mode carried no field).
    • A symmetric factorisation. solve_eddy(..., solver="pardiso") (the default when pypardiso is installed) factorises the real symmetric indefinite form [[R, -I], [-I, -R]] of the complex symmetric matrix by LDL^T, then refines to rtol. On 197 000 unknowns this needs 7.7 GB against 13.6 GB for an LU of the nonsymmetric real form, and half the time. SuperLU on the complex matrix (solver="splu") gives the same answer to 1e-12 on the conducting slab.

    3-D end effects: magnet flux against L/D¶

    machines3d.endeffects.end_effect_study(machine, ratios) measures how much of the 2-D magnet flux a coil links in 3-D, for stacks of different length. A full-pitch search coil sits at mid-slot radius, centred on the aligned magnet. Its flux linkage is the line integral of A around it (search_coil_linkage). The arcs close it either in the stack end faces or 10 mm beyond them, in the air where real end turns run. The 2-D reference is the same layered stack with no end air: its end planes are flux-parallel, so the field does not vary along the axis, and the ratio k = lambda_3D / lambda_2D holds only the end effect, none of the in-plane discretisation.

    The line integral is computed exactly: the path is cut where it crosses element faces and each piece is integrated by the midpoint rule, which is exact for the linear A of a Whitney element. A plain midpoint rule over 400 points per side gave 0.5 % of error, as large as the effect: the tree gauge puts large gradients in A in the air, and a closed loop cancels them only if every piece is exact. With the exact integral the difference between the two coils equals the flux of B through the 10 mm band between them (3.1999e-6 against 3.2001e-6 Wb on a test mesh).

    3-D axial-flux machine: quasi-3D against full 3-D¶

    machines3d.afpm.AFPM3D(AFPMMachine(...)) meshes an axial-flux PM machine on a structured grid in (r, theta, z): radial and axial node lines per region, theta uniform over the full circle, each cell cut into six tetrahedra, the grid closed at theta = 2 pi by merging the seam nodes. Turning the rotor by a whole number of steps maps the grid onto itself, so rotor angle is a relabelling of the magnet cells and cogging carries no remeshing noise. Two topologies: "single" (one rotor, a slotted stator with a yoke) and "double_rotor" (a yokeless slotted stator between two rotors magnetised the same way, so the flux crosses the stator axially).

    • No helical bias. A plain six-tetrahedron split leans every cell the same way round the axis, which put 0.9 N m of torque on an aligned rotor on a coarse grid. Every other theta cell is now cut as the mirror image of its neighbour; the grid is then symmetric about every even theta node plane, the aligned rotor carries no torque (1e-11 N m) and the cogging is exactly odd. Rotor angles move by two steps at a time.
    • Torque is the Maxwell stress r B_theta B_z / mu0 averaged over each gap layer. Its sign is checked against the co-energy (T = dW'/d theta).
    • Quasi-3D on the same grid. quasi3d_models(n) cuts the active annulus into n radial slices on the same radial nodes, each solved alone with flux-parallel cylindrical faces: the flux cannot cross radius r_k, the assumption of the usual quasi-3D model (a 2-D machine per radius, curvature kept). One slice leaves out the fringing at the inner and outer radius; five slices also leave out the radial flux inside the annulus.
    • Flux linkage of a coil around a stator tooth at mid tooth height (exact line integral of A), with a magnet centred on the tooth.

    3-D axial-flux machine: surrogates with calibrated uncertainty and physics checks¶

    machines3d.afpm_surrogate makes the Axial-Flux Motor Designer's check in 3-D a simulator over four variables of the default design: magnet arc (0.6 to 0.9 of the pole pitch), slot opening (0.3 to 0.7 of the slot pitch), gap (0.7 to 1.5 mm) and magnet thickness (3 to 6 mm). Each design runs the quasi-3D estimate and the 3-D check on the designer's grid (192 steps round the axis, 8 x 4 mm, edges="fraction", 16 rotor angles per period) and returns the cogging peak-to-peak and the tooth flux linkage of both, and their ratios. physics_checks are what a surrogate must respect to be eligible, on a Sobol sample of the region: positive cogging and linkage; linkage rising with the magnet thickness and falling with the gap on at least 90 % of the sample; predicted ratios inside the measured band widened by 50 %. certify turns a surrogate's checks into a trust decision (pinneapple_analysis.trust.TrustReport) and records every check in a VeriPhysics evidence ledger with the SHA-256 of the dataset. Critical: the physics checks and the test R^2 >= 0.9. Not critical: the coverage of the conformal 90 % interval (at least two binomial standard deviations below 90 %) and the same two on the designs held out for extrapolation.

    python examples/electrodynamics/25_afpm_surrogates.py (run on 2026-10-31, 48 designs in 29 min, 54 s each; the campaign resumes): a maximin design (discrepancy 0.0051 against 0.020 for a random one of the same size); the 8 designs with magnets thicker than 5.5 mm held out for extrapolation, the rest split 28 / 6 / 6 into train, validation and test; Gaussian process (Matern 5/2), MLP ensemble and quadratic response surface compared by 5-fold cross-validation with cross-conformal 90 % intervals (pinneapple_autolab.study.benchmark).     Fixed while building this¶

    • eddy_current_fdm.annular_current_source normalised the coil current density by the nominal band area, so the injected current depended on how the band fell on the grid (0.75 N I in one case); it now injects exactly N I. axial_flux_density returned half the field on the axis (A / r with A = 0 there instead of its limit dA/dr), and eddy_current_density returned +j omega sigma A, the eddy current with the wrong sign for the solver's exp(+j omega t) convention. Together these explain the coil field "about 0.20 times" the loop formula recorded in the validation gallery.
    • The Newton solver of solve_magnetostatic could stall at a residual of 1e-7 because its Armijo test compared energy differences below round-off; near convergence it now takes the full Newton step (6 iterations to 3e-13 on the saturated iron ring).

    Limits¶

    • The FEM solvers are 2-D (planar or axisymmetric for eddy_current_fdm), first order (P1), and resolve geometry on the mesh (polar grids are staircased in angle). The 3-D edge-element solver (fem3d) is first order; its eddy-current solve is a sparse LU, practical up to some 1e4 unknowns, and it is not yet compared with a TEAM benchmark.
    • FDTD: uniform grids only. The sub-cell averaging is not the full anisotropic smoothing: curved surfaces with E normal to them keep an error of a few percent that does not converge (2 % on the Mie sphere below). The CPML is tuned for vacuum; inside dispersive or negative-permittivity media its reflection is not measured. Surface plasmon polaritons are verified only in closed form: an FDTD SPP run on a lossless Drude interface did not converge cleanly at affordable resolutions (4.9 % at 80 cells per wavelength), so no FDTD SPP number is claimed. NumPy, single thread: a 60^3 grid takes 19 ms per step with PEC walls and 38 ms with CPML on all sides (measured in the development container).
    • The differential compiler kinds do not differentiate material maps: jumps of eps, mu or nu are only seen by the integral kinds.
    • maxwell_checks uses central differences on tensor grids: fields with discontinuities (material interfaces, PEC edges) leave residuals there that are not errors of the field; crop them or check each region separately.
    • Machines: 2-D FEA; end effects as corrections (end-winding inductance from thin filaments, magnet end leakage as one 3-D no-load ratio applied through the remanence, not a coupled 3-D solution at load); skew by multi-slice, without inter-segment axial flux; magnets at a uniform temperature, iron loss from B waveforms with the elliptic-to-alternating split (no minor loops, no rotational-loss data), magnet eddy loss resistance-limited (valid while the skin depth exceeds the magnet size). The Bertotti fit to the M400-50A table has measured errors (see the notes above); the table model is exact at the catalogue points only.
    • Thermal: a lumped network whose material, contact and heat-transfer data are illustrative defaults, not measured or fitted to a Prius; one temperature per node (no hot spot inside the slot, magnets uniform). Hcj(T) is the grade minimum with an assumed -0.55 %/K coefficient. The demagnetisation check is static, a square-loop knee test on the 2-D field (no recoil-line modelling, no partial demagnetisation of later operation). The worst-element ratio does not converge under mesh refinement (field singular at the sharp magnet corners), so only area fractions are reported as results. The short-circuit peak 2 psi_pm / L_d is an estimate, not a transient simulation.
    • Drive: steady state only (no current-controller dynamics, no PWM ripple or harmonics losses), no inverter, mechanical or battery losses; the voltage limit is that of linear space-vector modulation.
    • JilesAtherton is the scalar model; its parameters must be fitted to measured loops of the material. illustrative_steel is a formula, not a measured grade.
