    3-D multiphysics optimisation of an electric motor on a pretrained surrogate¶

    A traction motor is the textbook multiphysics problem: the electromagnetic torque heats the windings and the magnets, the magnet temperature changes the remanence and the demagnetisation margin, the coolant flow sets the temperature, the skew sets the ripple, and the stack length sets all of them at once. Optimising it with the FEA in the loop costs one nonlinear field solve per candidate, which is why motor design still takes weeks.

    This page covers the module that closes that loop on a learned model of the whole chain: pinneapple_simulation.electromagnetics.machines.motor3d. The reference model is the physics; the surrogate is trained on it once; the search then runs thousands of designs per second with no FEA and no CFD per candidate, and the front it produces is re-checked against the physics.

    What exists in the library, and what this adds¶

    Piece Where Role here
    2-D nonlinear magnetostatic FEA of the cross-section, Prius 2004 geometry machines.IPMMachine, machines.prius_2004 the field solve of the reference model
    Air-gap mesher with a moving band, one sector copied around the circle machines.mesher the mesh
    Iron loss from the flux density over an electrical period, magnet eddy loss machines.IPMMachine.losses the loss channels
    End-winding resistance and leakage, outside the 2-D cross-section machines.endwinding, machines.drive the axial part of the winding
    Lumped thermal network, one node per part machines.thermal.machine_network the radial thermal model this module extends along z
    Demagnetisation margin against Hcj(T) machines.thermal.demagnetisation_margin the magnet check
    Axial-resolved thermal network motor3d.thermal3d hot spot place, not only level
    The coupled multiphysics chain as one call motor3d.model.Motor3DModel one design in, every KPI out
    Design of experiments + stored dataset motor3d.dataset the training set with its provenance
    Ensemble surrogate with a model card motor3d.surrogate fast, and honest about its error
    Search that runs on the surrogate only motor3d.optimize zero FEA per candidate
    The whole study as one command motor3d.workflow data, train, optimise, verify, report

    The reference model¶

    Motor3DModel.solve(design) runs the chain in one call. Each step is a piece of the library, and the coupling between them is what makes the problem multiphysics:

    1. electromagnetic -- IPMMachine.operating_point over a set of rotor angles at the design's current and current angle, with the magnet remanence at a first-guess magnet temperature;
    2. skew -- the stack is cut into n_segments axial slices twisted by skew_deg; each slice contributes the torque of the cross-section at its own electrical angle, so slot harmonics cancel as they do in a skewed machine, and the fundamental is reduced by the skew factor sin(x)/x;
    3. losses -- iron loss (stator and rotor) from the flux density over an electrical period, resistance-limited magnet eddy loss, and copper loss split between the slot and the end windings, with a winding resistance that depends on the stack length;
    4. cooling and temperature -- the axial network of thermal3d, whose jacket conductance follows the coolant flow as flow^0.8;
    5. back to the electromagnetics -- Br(T) of the grade corrects torque, iron loss (through B^2) and the demagnetisation margin, the copper loss is re-evaluated at the winding temperature and the network is solved again until the temperatures stop moving.
    from pinneapple_simulation.electromagnetics.machines.motor3d import Motor3DModel, Motor3DDesign, PRIUS

model = Motor3DModel()                       # 192-node gap mesh, 12 rotor angles, 4 axial segments
r = model.solve(PRIUS)                       # the 2004 Prius design at 250 A, 50 deg, 1500 rpm, 6 L/min
print(r["torque_Nm"], r["T_winding_C"], r["demag_ratio"])
print(r["T_winding_profile_C"])              # the winding along the stack, end to end

    Designs whose cross-section the mesher refuses come back valid=False with the reason, without a solve.

    The axial thermal network¶

    machine_network gives one temperature per part: enough for a level, not for a place. AxialThermalModel keeps the same radial conductances and gives every part n_segments nodes along the stack, linked by k A / dz, with the end windings as one node outside it and the jacket spread over the outer surface of every yoke segment.

    The check that it is the same physics: with n_segments=1 it reproduces machine_network to round-off (tests/test_motor3d.py::test_axial_network_with_one_segment_is_the_library_seven_node_model), and with more segments the winding becomes hottest in the middle of the stack instead of at a single averaged node.

    from pinneapple_simulation.electromagnetics.machines.motor3d import axial_network

net = axial_network(PRIUS.geometry(), n_segments=6)
P = net.powers(200.0, 400.0, 8000.0, 3000.0, 20.0, 40.0)   # yoke, teeth, slot Cu, end-winding Cu, rotor, magnets
T = net.network.steady(P)
print([round(T[net.index("winding", k)], 1) for k in range(6)])

    The dataset¶

    from pinneapple_simulation.electromagnetics.machines.motor3d import build_dataset

ds = build_dataset(1200, seed=20260101, workers=8, extra=[PRIUS])
ds.save("motor3d_data")
print(ds.meta["rejection_rate"], ds.meta["seconds_per_design"])

    The eleven design variables (motor3d.design.VARIABLES) cover the rotor cross-section (W4, H3, H0, W0, H1), the axial direction (L, skew_deg), the operating point (I_peak, gamma_deg, rpm) and the cooling (flow_L_min). They are declared once, with units and bounds, and the design space, the columns of the dataset and the inputs of the surrogate are all built from that declaration.

    The DOE is the library's (pinneapple_data.doe.design) with the geometry test as a feasibility filter, so no case is spent on a cross-section the mesher would reject -- and the rejection rate of the box is itself a result: at the corners of the space a third of the designs cannot be built.

    The surrogate¶

    MotorSurrogate3D is an ensemble of fully connected networks over the eleven inputs and 26 outputs: the fourteen scalars of OUTPUTS and the three axial profiles of four segments each. Strictly positive outputs are trained in log space, so a 300 W loss and a 30 N m torque train together.

    from pinneapple_simulation.electromagnetics.machines.motor3d import MotorSurrogate3D, SurrogateConfig

model = MotorSurrogate3D(SurrogateConfig(ensemble=5)).fit(ds)
print(model.card()["heldout"]["torque_Nm"])   # mae, r2, mean_rel, p95_rel, on designs never trained on
model.save("motor3d_model", version="v1")

    Three properties matter more than the size of the network:

    • the ensemble spread is the model's own uncertainty -- predict returns a mean and a spread per output;
    • validity: out_of_box reports variables outside the training box, extrapolation the distance to the data in units of its own spread;
    • the card: every saved model carries its held-out errors, its training-set description and the SHA-256 of its weights, and load refuses weights that no longer match the card.

    The model shipped with the library is load_pretrained().

    The optimisation, without FEA¶

    from pinneapple_simulation.electromagnetics.machines.motor3d import design_problem, optimize, verify_front

out = optimize(design_problem(surrogate=model), budget=4000, seed=0)
print(out["designs_per_second"], out["n_reference_solves"], len(out["front"]))    # ... 0 ...

ver = verify_front(out, n=8)          # the only place an FEA call is spent
print(ver["still_feasible"], ver["rank_agreement"], ver["errors"]["torque_Nm"])

    The problem is the one the application asks: maximise torque density and efficiency, minimise magnet mass, subject to the winding temperature, the magnet temperature, the demagnetisation ratio and the ripple. The search is NSGA-II from pinneapple_design.design_optimizer, and every candidate it evaluates is a network forward pass.

    verify_front is the honesty step: designs spread across the front are re-evaluated by the reference model, and the result reports the per-output error, whether the designs are still feasible under the same limits, and the Spearman agreement between the surrogate's and the reference's ranking of the front.

    The whole study¶

    python -m pinneapple_simulation.electromagnetics.machines.motor3d.workflow all --out motor3d_run --n 1200 --workers 8

    writes dataset/, model/ (weights and card), optimization.json, verification.json, benchmark.json, figures/ and report.md, and every step has its own subcommand so the expensive one can be re-run alone.

    Limits¶

    • the field is 2-D per axial segment: axial flux paths enter through the end-winding resistance and the thermal network, not through a 3-D field solve;
    • the coolant is a boundary conductance that follows the flow as flow^0.8, not a conjugate heat transfer solution;
    • the structural side (bridge stress at overspeed) is not evaluated;
    • the surrogate is valid inside the box of its card, and its spread is a proxy for uncertainty, not a calibrated interval.

    The alternative for the same goal is machines.design.hybrid_vs_fea_only, which keeps the FEA in the loop and uses a Gaussian process to decide which designs deserve a solve. That is the right tool when every design must be FEA; the surrogate here is the right tool when the chain is expensive enough to be worth learning once and the front is worth verifying at the end.
