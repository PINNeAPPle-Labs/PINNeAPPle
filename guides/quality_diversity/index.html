    Quality-diversity: a map of designs, and the map of where a model fails¶

    An optimiser answers one question: which design is best. A Pareto front answers a second one: which designs are not worse than another in everything. Neither says what kinds of design exist, and neither covers the space on purpose -- the search walks towards the optimum and abandons the rest.

    pinneapple_design.design_optimizer.quality_diversity keeps the loop and changes the bookkeeping. It maintains a grid over a behaviour space -- how the design behaves, not what its parameters are -- with one cell per behaviour and the best design found for that behaviour in each cell (MAP-Elites, Mouret & Clune 2015). The result is a map an engineer reads at a glance: the cheapest heat sink at each fin density, the airfoil with the least drag at each lift.

    The same archive, pointed at a model instead of at a design, is a failure map. Set the fitness to the model's own error and every cell holds the worst error the search could find for that behaviour, together with the design that produced it. The cells above a tolerance are the region where the model is wrong, handed back with the number, the design and the condition -- the out-of-domain region of the trust layer, found by search instead of declared by hand.

    The behaviour space¶

    A behaviour axis is what the design does. The ranges are part of the definition: a descriptor outside them cannot be placed and is counted as unplaceable, rather than clipped into an edge cell that would then lie about its behaviour.

    from pinneapple_design.design_optimizer.quality_diversity import (BehaviourAxis, BehaviourSpace, EliteArchive,
                                                                  Evaluation, QDConfig, QualityDiversitySearch)

behaviour = BehaviourSpace(
    axes=(BehaviourAxis("Rth", 0.4, 2.4, unit="K/W", why="the cooling the customer buys; the top is the junction limit"),
          BehaviourAxis("mass", 0.04, 0.17, unit="kg", why="the aluminium is the other half of the cost")),
    bins=(10, 10), labels="plate-fin heat sink, 50 x 50 mm base, 25 W")
    • why= is printed by the map and the report. An axis without a physical reason is a design parameter in disguise.
    • scale="log" for quantities that span decades (thermal resistances, pressure drops, pumping powers): a linear grid there spends most of its cells on values nobody builds.
    • space.condition(cell) is the behaviour interval of each axis, which is what makes a failing cell a condition and not a point.

    The axes come from the reference physics, or from the qualitative descriptors of the geometry -- pinneapple_design.qualitative.describe returns volume, wetted area, slenderness, sphericity, the flat areas and the sharp-edge length, all of which say what a body is to the flow without being the parameters that produced it. descriptor_vector reads them, and descriptor_axes builds a grid whose ranges are what the design box actually reaches:

    from pinneapple_design.design_optimizer.quality_diversity import descriptor_axes, descriptor_vector
from pinneapple_design.qualitative import describe

meshes = [describe(mesh) for mesh in campaign_meshes]          # any closed, outward-oriented body
space = descriptor_axes(meshes, ("slenderness", "sphericity"), bins=(8, 6),
                        why={"slenderness": "how much body the flow has to go around"})
descriptors = descriptor_vector(describe(new_mesh), ("slenderness", "sphericity"))

    Never from the model under test: if the axis were the surrogate's own output, a cell could not say what the part is.

    The archive¶

    EliteArchive(space, sense="min"|"max") holds the elites and counts what it refused. sense="min" ranks a cost to minimise (a design map); sense="max" ranks an error or a residual to maximise (a failure map).

    One evaluation is one Evaluation:

    Field Meaning
    value the number the archive ranks by inside a cell
    descriptors one value per behaviour axis; None or non-finite means "could not be placed"
    error optional per-channel breakdown ({"rth": 0.31, "mass": 0.02}), carried into the report
    valid False for a case that is not physically legitimate -- it is never archived
    note free text kept with the elite (the reference value, a solver warning)

    valid=False is the field that keeps a failure map honest: a "failure" found where the physics does not apply is not a failure of the model. In the heat-sink study below, 161 of 600 cases are refused because the part would not survive them at all.

    An archive can be full of cells and still be a point, so the module measures it:

    report = archive.diversity()        # cells, fill, design spread in the normalised box, largest cluster
archive.assert_diverse()            # raises CollapsedArchiveError instead of reporting a map that is a point
print(archive.summary(), report.text())

    The search¶

    result = QualityDiversitySearch(archive, bounds, evaluate, QDConfig(budget=600, seed=0)).run()

    The loop is the canonical one: evaluate a Latin-hypercube first batch, then repeatedly pick a parent from a uniformly chosen occupied cell, mutate it, evaluate the child, and let it replace the elite of its own cell when it beats it. Selection happens inside the cell, so the optimum of one behaviour never starves another -- which is exactly why the ask/tell GA of EvolutionaryDesignOptimizer is not reused here: selecting by objective value over one population is the pressure that empties the archive back into a single corner. What is shared with the GA is the parameter space (ParamSpace, its bounds and clipping) and the mutation conventions of DesignOptimizerConfig, so a QD run and a GA run are configured with the same knobs and sampled in the same box.

    QDConfig Default What it costs you
    budget 600 evaluations the run may spend, initial batch included -- the number the comparison quotes
    population 50 candidates per generation, and the parallel unit
    mutation_std 0.08 Gaussian step, in normalised design units, so one number means the same in every parameter
    line_share 0.35 share of children that move along a line, half of them towards another elite (a variation along the map, the ISO-line idea of CMA-ME)
    immigrant_share 0.1 share drawn uniformly in the box, so an unlucky archive can still be escaped
    init_fraction 0.5 share of the budget spent on the space-filling batch: a map has to exist before it can be improved
    workers 1 threads for a batch; only helps when evaluate releases the GIL, and the batch is collected in order, so the result does not depend on it

    uniform_search(bounds, evaluate, behaviour, budget=..., kind="random"|"latin") is the honest opponent: the same box, the same evaluator, the same number of evaluations, and the samples placed in the same grid, so the coverage and the failure set come out comparable.

    The failure map¶

    region, result = find_failure_region(search_box, evaluate_model_error, behaviour,
                                     budget=600, seed=0, tolerance=0.10, name="surrogate Rth error")
print(region.text(8))

    evaluate returns the error as Evaluation.value (larger is worse), the reference behaviour as descriptors, and valid=False where the case is not physically legitimate -- the case set is decided by the physics, never by the model under test, or the search would only find its own refusals.

    region.cells() gives the failing cells worst first, each with the error, the per-channel breakdown, the design and the behaviour interval; region.map_max is the worst error on the map, region.failing_fraction() how much of the mapped behaviour the model gets wrong, and region.to_json() the whole thing for a report.

    Measured: where the heat-sink surrogate fails¶

    examples/quality_diversity is the demo, and the numbers below are what it produced (seed, budget and tolerance are in each figure and in examples/quality_diversity/heat_sink_qd.json).

    A plate-fin heat sink is described by three design numbers -- fin count, fin height and approach air speed -- and the house closed-form model (pinneapple_physics.closed_form.plate_fin_heatsink) says what each one does. An MLP surrogate is trained on a campaign: ducted forced convection, 4-20 fins, 8-50 mm fins, 0.8-3.0 m/s, which is what a fan-cooled extrusion project would sample. The search is then pointed at a wider envelope that goes down to 0 m/s -- a fan failure, or the half of the catalogue that is passive -- and asked, at 600 evaluations:

    Search Worst verified error Failing cells (> 10 %) Cells filled Median cell error
    quality-diversity 66.4 % (62.4-72.3 %) 57.8 70.2 / 100 21.6 %
    random 57.7 % (54.0-62.0 %) 43.0 57.8 / 100 14.2 %
    Latin hypercube 62.6 % (55.7-71.4 %) 40.8 55.8 / 100 14.0 %

    Mean over five seeds, every method at the same 600 evaluations and over the same grid. Read it honestly: the failure search beats random sampling on all five seeds (+4.3 to +13.7 points), and beats Latin hypercube on the mean (+3.8 points) but only on three of five seeds (-8.0, +11.0, +16.5, -0.4, +0.1). After 600 evaluations the archive's advantage is in the shape of what it finds -- twice the cells above tolerance, a third more of the map covered, and a median cell error half again as large -- not in a per-seed guarantee over a space-filling design. examples/quality_diversity/experiment.json records the Latin-hypercube check as failed, because on a per-seed basis it is.

    The gradient is the story a single number cannot tell: the error is small where the surrogate was trained (low Rth, low mass) and grows towards the thick, high-resistance parts, reaching 63 % at Rth in [2.2, 2.4] K/W and mass in [0.118, 0.131] kg. That is the region a trust report should carry.

    Two cases check the map against what is already known, without telling the archive about them:

    • near pass (14 fins, 30 mm, 0.6 m/s): not a strange operating point -- it is below the campaign's 0.8 m/s floor, so the surrogate has never seen it. The archive reports 10.5 % and puts it in the failing set.
    • fan off (12 fins, 25 mm, 0 m/s): the physics can answer it (natural convection), but the junction lands above its limit, so the case is not buildable. The archive refuses it instead of reporting a large error that would mean nothing.

    The design map¶

    The same machinery with sense="min" and fitness = the fan power the design costs answers the other question: the least fan power that achieves each (Rth, mass) cell. 66 of 100 cells are filled, and the cheapest cell found is 0.12 mW at Rth in [2.0, 2.2] K/W, mass in [0.092, 0.105] kg (11 fins, 50 mm, 0.19 m/s).

    Blank cells say what was not explored and are left blank on purpose: a map that fills them with a colour would be claiming a design nobody found.

    What it does not do¶

    • Grid archives only. The issue asked for a grid or a CVT archive; the grid is what exists. A CVT archive (Vassiliades et al. 2018) would place the centroids where the data are instead of on a uniform lattice, which matters when the behaviour space is high-dimensional -- on a 10 x 10 map of a thermal plane it does not.
    • No gradient-aware emitter. CMA-ME's improvement emitter needs a differentiable archive; this is the black-box version, and line_share with the towards-another-elite direction is the part of it that survives without gradients.
    • Threads, not processes. workers helps only when the evaluator releases the GIL (numpy, torch).
    • A deterministic evaluator. The archive compares values across generations; a noisy evaluator makes those comparisons meaningless. Average the noise inside evaluate if you have to.
    • Not a validation. Regressing where a model is wrong says nothing about whether it is right where it was fitted; use pinneapple_analysis.trust and pinneapple_check for that.

    Where it plugs in¶

    The failure map is the input the rest of the stack asks for: the trust benchmark and the out-of-domain region of a trust report, active learning (pinneapple_data.active_learning) which wants the cases the model is worst at, adaptive fidelity, and examples/use_cases/physics_data_factory, which wants a design map instead of a random campaign.

    Running the demo¶

    python examples/quality_diversity/heat_sink_failure_map.py     # ~27 s; writes the figures, a small elites CSV and the numbers

    It writes heat_sink_elites.csv (one row per elite of both maps: cell, condition, value, design), heat_sink_qd.json (the comparison, the known-failure check, and the budget, seed and tolerance behind every number here) and the two figures above.
