    Validation gallery: one worked example per model family and per physics¶

    examples/validation/ shows how to validate your kind of model on your kind of physics. Every example has the same structure:

    1. Train or load the model, with data from the library's reference solvers or the zoo.
    2. Run pp.validate.
    3. Read the trust card. validation.gallery.conclude writes report.json, trust_card.json, report.sarif and failure.json to examples/_out/validation/<example>/.
    4. Run pinneapple check in CI.
    5. See one real failure the checks catch, on a broken variant of the same model.

    Everything runs on a CPU. CI runs the whole gallery at smoke size (.github/workflows/validation-gallery.yml; under 3 minutes on a 4-core machine) and fails if an example stops catching its failure. Run one example with python examples/validation/<file>.py [--full].

    # Model family Physics Validated with Failure caught
    01 PINN heat conduction (manufactured) {"pinn": poisson_2d()} boundary loss weight 0: PDE satisfied, walls not (pinn.boundary)
    02 FNO, as an ONNX file porous flow (Darcy) "darcy_2d" on fno.onnx permeability contrast 100 / 1 outside the training envelope (applicability.envelope)
    03 FNO and DeepONet steppers reaction–diffusion (Allen–Cahn) {"rollout": ...} DeepONet: spurious high-wavenumber energy before the pointwise error grows (rollout.spectrum)
    04 MeshGraphNet linear elasticity (3-D FEM cantilever) tabular spec on the tip deflection 30 kN query, 3× the largest training load: 4.2 mm predicted, 5.6 mm by FEM (applicability.range)
    05 POD + DMD incompressible flow (cylinder wake, LBM) {"rollout": ...} ROM fitted on the growing transient (|λ| = 1.008) keeps amplifying the wake (rollout.invariants)
    06 POD + Operator Inference weather-like advection {"rollout": ..., "conserved": ["mass"]} basis cut at 95 % of the energy: mass exact, small scales smeared (rollout.spectrum)
    07 MLP (zoo) forced convection (heat sink) zoo card 0.2 m/s and 600 W, outside the card's range (applicability.range)
    08 Gaussian process convection-cooled plate tabular spec with relations raw-feature GP: hotter with a better conductor, power linearity broken by 36 % (metamorphic)
    09 Neural ODE vs Hamiltonian NN molecular dynamics (Lennard-Jones, NVE) {"rollout": ..., "invariants": {"energy": E}} plain neural ODE: energy drifts 19 % although its training loss is lower (rollout.invariant.energy)
    10 Diffusion model porous flow, a sample per draw {"generative": {"physics": "darcy"}} joint (a, u) generator: pressures that do not solve the PDE for their permeability (data.physics)
    11 PhysicsNeMo FNO (external) 2-D magnetostatics, -div(ν grad A) = J {"pde": "darcy", "f": J} iron with μr = 1000, outside the training envelope (applicability.envelope)
    12 Least squares vs MLP structural dynamics (cantilever modes) tabular spec with scaling laws MLP breaks f ∝ √(E/ρ) by 6 % (metamorphic); the power law recovers the exponents exactly

    examples/validation/pinneapple-check.toml runs two of the models (12 and the zoo model of 07) through pinneapple check with the same relations and range checks. The other examples write report.sarif, which the GitHub Action uploads to code scanning.

    Library additions made for the gallery¶

    • pp.validate(sampler, {"generative": {...}}). A generative model is a sampler n -> {column: array}. Its samples are audited like a dataset: every sample against the PDE, diversity (mode collapse) and memorisation (generated samples that copy training samples).
    • Rollouts of state vectors (ndim = 0, e.g. particle systems or ROM coefficients) and user invariants ("invariants": {name: f(traj, ndim)}) in pp.validate rollout specs.
    • numerical_solvers.reaction_diffusion.ReactionDiffusion1D: Allen–Cahn and Fisher–KPP with ETDRK4. It is checked against the stationary tanh interface and the Bramson-corrected KPP front speed.
    • pinneapple_neural.utils.fit_supervised: a minimal Adam loop on (x, y) tensors.

    Honest results¶

    • The FNO of example 02 is rejected at smoke size. Its net boundary flux misses the source by 29 %, it has too much high-wavenumber energy and it breaks the reflection symmetry by 10 %. The gallery reports that rather than tuning it away; the envelope failure is caught regardless.
    • Spectrum floor. On Allen–Cahn, every network "failed" rollout.spectrum until the band was restricted to shells with energy above 1e-6 of the peak (amplitudes above 0.1 %). Below that the reference holds only round-off. This is now the default of spectral_drift.
    • Data loss and physics disagree. In example 09 the neural ODE fits the training data better than the Hamiltonian network (loss 0.011 vs 0.048) and still drifts in energy. In example 12 the MLP passes a loose error check on some test sets while breaking the scaling laws.
    • Two solvers were not used, because they failed a sanity check:
    • generate_magnetic_flux_leakage_synthetic: the peak leakage signal is not monotonic in defect depth and changes with the grid (36 × 14, 72 × 28, 144 × 56).
    • eddy_current_fdm at ω = 0: the on-axis field of a coil is about 0.20× the closed-form current-loop field, the same factor for two radii and four axial distances. This looks like a normalisation issue in the solver or its source helper. Examples 11 and 08 use the Darcy operator and the heat plate instead.
    • Smoke sizes are small. The FNO and DeepONet numbers (horizons, spectra) change with training length; the tests assert that each failure is caught and that the good model does not fail the same check, not the exact values.
