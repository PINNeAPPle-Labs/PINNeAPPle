A traction-motor model is only useful if you know where it is right and where it is not. We took the 2004 Toyota Prius interior-PM motor (48 slots, 8 poles, V magnets) and checked each layer against something independent.

What is in PINNeAPPle now (pinneapple_simulation.electromagnetics.machines, examples/electrodynamics/10 to 18):
- nonlinear 2-D FEA with torque, cogging, back-EMF, Ld/Lq and MTPA;
- a drive model built from the FEA flux map: efficiency map and WLTC energy;
- a thermal network plus a per-element demagnetisation check;
- multi-slice step skew and Monte Carlo tolerances;
- rotor design with a GP surrogate and NSGA-II;
- a cross-check with gmsh + GetDP, and slides generated from the results.

Measured, from the committed results:
- Torque vs current angle at 250 A: peak 345.6 N m against 353 N m published (Yang et al., IEMDC 2013). The rms deviation is 13.3 N m, the largest +25.3 N m at 90 deg; the causes are listed, not tuned away.
- Second code (gmsh + GetDP): torque within 1.09 N m (0.19 % at the peak), back-EMF fundamental +0.04 %. Cogging differs by 25 % and both codes converge to their own value; we report it as unresolved.
- GP + NSGA-II reaches the same hypervolume with 51 to 53 FEA solutions instead of 601 to 711, a 91 to 93 % saving over 3 seeds.

Limits are stated in the guide: the vehicle and thermal data are illustrative, the magnet grades are candidates (no public source gives the real one), and the 96.2 % peak efficiency excludes inverter loss. Verification tables are regenerated by the scripts themselves.

Guide and code: https://github.com/PINNeAPPle-Labs/PINNeAPPle/blob/main/docs/guides/electromagnetics.md
