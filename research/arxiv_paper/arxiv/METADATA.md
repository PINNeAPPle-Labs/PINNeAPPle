## Abstract (plain text, for the arXiv field)

```
Libraries for physics-informed learning are routinely compared by their published accuracy, on different problems, grids and budgets, so the comparison does not isolate the library. We report a preregistered evaluation whose protocol was committed before any result was produced, and whose every quantitative statement is regenerated by a script from an immutable run record. Three neural-network libraries for partial differential equations (PINNeAPPle, DeepXDE and PhysicsNeMo-Sym), one operator-learning baseline (neuraloperator) and the operator and inverse solvers of PhysicsNeMo are exercised on four forward benchmarks (Burgers, Poisson, Helmholtz, Allen-Cahn), one operator benchmark (FNO on Darcy 64x64) and one inverse problem (identifying the viscosity of Burgers), on one CPU, one thread per run.

Three results stand out. (i) Under an identical configuration the three PINN libraries are numerically the same: they start from the same loss to six significant digits, agree to four significant digits after 200 Adam iterations, and their paired per-seed error ratio after 2000 iterations is 1.00 [0.96,1.00] on Poisson and 1.00 [1.00,1.00] on Helmholtz; the cost per iteration is within +5% and -10% of DeepXDE's and 1.4-1.9x lower than PhysicsNeMo-Sym's. (ii) Under each library's own recipe and an equal 240 s CPU budget, the defaults dominate the outcome: relative L2 errors span a factor of 10 on Poisson and 2.4 on Helmholtz, and no recipe reaches a useful accuracy on Allen-Cahn. (iii) A protocol is not bookkeeping: applying it to the inverse problem exposed an adaptive loss-weighting rule in PINNeAPPle that inflated the identified viscosity by 12.5x; the fixed default reduces the error to 3.4x, still behind DeepXDE's 2.2x.

We then ask whether a prediction's physics can be checked without a reference solution. On a controlled corpus of 72 predictions over six problems, checks that need no reference (boundary and initial data, declared bounds, non-finiteness, PDE residual) flag 28 of 36 single-defect predictions, while the residual check is scale-invariant for a homogeneous equation and is limited by the finite-difference floor of the grid: 0.92 of the term scale for Burgers at the benchmark's own test grid, where it discriminates nothing. The 8 defects no reference-free check caught are exactly the wrong coefficient and the over-smoothed field of the three equations the checker cannot express. A verification layer inherits the expressiveness of its equation set.

We make no claim of statistical significance: with 3 to 5 seeds the exact sign test cannot reach p<0.05, so differences are reported with their spread. Everything here runs on a CPU; no GPU path was exercised, and the cost figures are single-threaded CPU measurements.
```

Note the substitutions made above for plain text: `64x64` for `64\times64`, `L2` for `L^2`,
`1.4-1.9x` for `1.4--1.9\times`, `Allen-Cahn` for `Allen--Cahn`, `p<0.05` written out. Keep them.
