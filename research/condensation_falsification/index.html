    Falsification campaign for physics condensation — pre-registration¶

    Pre-registered on 2026-10-25, before any of the runs below. This file states each hypothesis, the test, the metric and the kill criterion: the measured result that refutes the hypothesis. It is committed before the measurements. The results section is appended later; nothing above it changes after the runs, except obvious typos, which are listed in the change log at the end.

    The method under test is the one in physics_condensation.md: exact static condensation of repeated blocks, reuse of identical blocks, reduced (POD) interfaces with an error estimate, memoised nested dissection, learned block operators, and prior plus exception patches. The campaign tries to break it. A refutation is reported as prominently as a confirmation.

    Scale and references (declared up front)¶

    • Thermal: the 3-D multilayer package of the report, up to 128 x 128 blocks (2.4e7 fine cells). That is the memory limit of this container (4 cores, 15 GB).
    • The fine finite-volume solve (AMG-CG, pinneapple_condense.fv) is the reference wherever it fits, up to 64 x 64 blocks.
    • Beyond that, the reference is the exact condensation: the CG correction on the unreduced interfaces, run to 1e-10. Condensation is exact algebra, so this equals the fine FV solution. It is verified against the fine FV solve where both fit.
    • CFD: 2-D, steady, laminar, incompressible, on a staggered (MAC) finite-volume grid assembled as one sparse system.
    • OpenFOAM is not installed in this container, so the reference is the library FV solve of the same discretisation (direct or AMG).
    • That solver is validated first against:
      • plane Poiseuille flow (analytic);
      • the lid-driven cavity at Re = 100, centreline velocities of Ghia, Ghia and Shin, J. Comput. Phys. 48 (1982) 387;
      • the fully developed Nusselt number for parallel plates with one wall at uniform flux and the other adiabatic, Nu_Dh = 5.385 (Shah and London, 1978, as used by pinneapple_simulation.numerical_solvers.thermal_channel_2d).
    • Its mesh convergence is reported.
    • Meshless: RBF-FD (PHS r^3 + polynomials) and GFDM stencils of pinneapple_simulation.meshless.stencils, on point clouds generated from the implicit geometry (SDF).
    • Not measured:
    • Every number in the results section comes from a run in this container.
    • A hypothesis that could not be tested is reported as not tested, with the reason.

    Error tolerances are absolute temperatures (K) for the thermal package, as in the report. For the CFD cases they are relative, against the reference of the same discretisation: - flow rate or pressure drop; - the relative L2 norm of the field; - fin-base / wall temperature rise.

    B. CFD with a mesh (2-D, steady)¶

    A new generic layer, algebraic condensation, condenses any assembled sparse operator over a partition of its unknowns into blocks: - the separator consists of the cells on block boundaries; - for saddle-point systems, the separator also holds one pressure unknown per block; - block interiors with identical matrices are factorised once.

    This is what applies the method to non-symmetric and saddle-point operators.

    Verdicts¶

    Each hypothesis gets one verdict, based only on its kill criterion and the measured numbers: - validated: the kill criterion is not met; - refuted: the kill criterion is met; - partially validated: it holds at some sizes or parameters and is refuted at others; the boundary is stated; - not tested: the reason is given.

    What holds¶

    • Exactness everywhere.
    • Exact condensation, block-wise or algebraic, reproduces the global solve to round-off on every operator tried:
      • symmetric conduction with conductivity contrasts up to 1e5, anisotropy 100 and 0.01, 1-µm layers, alternating pitch: 1e-9 to 6e-8 K;
      • non-symmetric advection–diffusion up to Pe_b = 1000: 1e-13;
      • Stokes saddle points with one pressure unknown per block: 1e-12;
      • Picard-linearised Navier–Stokes and conjugate heat transfer up to Re 100: 1e-14;
      • meshless matrices: 1e-9.
    • So the algebra is never the problem.
    • Reduced interfaces degrade gracefully with physics. Contrast, anisotropy and thin layers change the m = 12 error by at most 2.2×.
    • For advection–diffusion the modes needed for 1 % grow only from 2 (Pe_b ≤ 10) to 3 (Pe_b 100–1000).
    • For Stokes 2 modes suffice, and for the Re 100 cold plate 3.
    • Recirculation crossing block faces is real but small at these Reynolds numbers: 4.5 % of the face flux at Re 100.
    • Far field (T1).
    • At fixed m the error grows about 3× for every 4× more blocks: m = 12 gives 1.4 mK at 256 blocks, 16 mK at 1,024, 52 mK at 4,096 and 0.21 K at 16,384.
    • The m needed for 0.1 K went 6 → 12 → 12 → 24. That is exactly the pre-registered limit of 2×, on a coarse m grid.
    • The exact correction fixes it at moderate cost:
      • PCG iterations to 1 mK are 2, 8, 8 and 32;
      • time to 1 mK is 5 s against 481 s for one fine AMG solve at 4,096 blocks.

    What breaks, and why¶

    1. Cost whenever blocks stop repeating (T4, T5, T6, T7, F1, F3).
    2. The method's advantage is reuse. With random variants the memoised hierarchy is slower than the flat condensation from V = 4 on (20 s against 4.9 s; 61 s against 6.6 s at V = 256), because almost no subtree repeats.
    3. With every block distinct (T4), the exact path costs more than the fine solve. At 32 × 32 it did not fit in memory: one dense block model per distinct block.
    4. Non-linear material laws make every block distinct at every Picard iteration: 4.4× (T6) and 4.5× (T7) slower than the fine non-linear solve, at the same accuracy.
    5. A numerically computed velocity field differs bit by bit between "periodic" blocks. Content-identical reuse then finds nothing (80 of 80, 44 of 44 blocks distinct), and condensed Picard is about 2× slower than the full one (F3).
    6. Cost against a sparse direct solver in 2-D (F2, M4).
    7. Even with 9 distinct of 288 blocks, condensing a 2-D Stokes system was 4.6× slower than one sparse direct solve.
    8. The factorisation of the separator dominates (its dense blocks fill in).
    9. In 2-D, nested dissection is already near-optimal, so there is little left to win for a single right-hand side.
    10. Sub-pitch features the modes cannot represent (T8).
    11. A single-cell power spike gives 1.66 K with 12 modes. The other adversarial maps (checkerboards, noise, corners) stay below 10 mK.
    12. The estimator is not fooled: its effectivity stays in 1.4–1.8. It flags the problem, and refinement or the exact correction has to act on it.
    13. Heterogeneous blocks with base-stack modes (T4).
    14. On a package where every block differs (voids, delamination ×30), the m-mode interfaces are off by 0.46 K at 16 × 16 blocks (0.68 K at 8 × 8). On uniform packages they are off by 2–16 mK.
    15. The learned-operator study of the track 1 report compared the learned path with all-exact blocks on the same m-mode interfaces, not with the fine solution. Its 4 mK agreement did not include this 0.68 K.
    16. The generic algebraic layer in 3-D (T6, T7, T10 at 16 × 16).
    17. The separator factorisation ran out of memory.
    18. The port-based block condensation of pinneapple_condense.substructure does not have this problem: it keeps faces separate.
    19. The generic layer is a correctness tool here, not a fast one.
    20. Meshless (M1–M4).
    21. RBF-FD converges for advection–diffusion but is 28–32× less accurate than FV at equal unknowns at low Pe. It is only competitive at Pe_b = 100.
    22. GFDM is unstable (errors up to 1e9).
    23. Collocated meshless Stokes does not converge reliably: RBF-FD goes 46 %, 50 %, 18 %; GFDM decreases but stagnates at 35 %.
    24. The FV-at-interfaces hybrid removes the interface failure of the report (its error is now spread over the band and the points alike), but stays 6–8× worse than FV everywhere. Its largest errors sit at the meshless points near the domain boundary and the band edge.
    25. Meshless blocks condense exactly and, with deterministic neighbour selection, are found identical. Condensation is still slower than one direct solve.
    26. Prior + exception patches on transport (E1).
    27. On advection-dominated fields the residual of a coarse prior is no better than random at choosing where to solve, and its selected blocks overlap the true top-error blocks by at most 0.69.
    28. The error of a coarse prior is carried downstream, away from where the residual is large.
    29. In the thermal case of track 1c (diffusive, local defects) the same method worked.

    Where the idea stands¶

    Condensation is exact and robust as algebra. Its accuracy-per-mode survives contrast, anisotropy, thin layers, moderate Péclet and Reynolds numbers. Its growth with package size is real but controllable (m doubling per 16× blocks, or the exact correction).

    Its speed comes only from repetition, and the campaign shows how fragile that is: - random variants; - non-linear material laws; - computed advection fields; - 2-D problems where sparse direct solvers are already efficient.

    Each of these removes the gain, sometimes entirely. A practical method needs: - identical-up-to-tolerance block matching, not bit-identical (computed velocities, temperature-dependent k); - parametric block operators (the learned path, with interface modes trained for heterogeneity); - H-matrix arithmetic for the separators.

    None of these is implemented here.

    Deviations from the pre-registration¶

    Listed here instead of changing the hypotheses above.

    1. F0, Poiseuille. The pre-registered criterion expected an exact parabola (1e-6). The ghost-cell wall treatment is O(h²) for curved profiles; the observed order is 2. That sub-criterion is met (refuted) as written.
    2. F2 / F3 metric. The velocity inlet fixes the flow rate, so the pressure drop at that flow rate (the equivalent permeability measure) replaces the flow-rate error.
    3. T7 law. "β such that R_c doubles over the range" is implemented as a linear rise from R_0 at the coolest to 2 R_0 at the hottest interface temperature of the linear solution, saturating at 2 R_0.
    4. An unsaturated law ran away: R up → T up → R up.
    5. A first run took the range from the die-top cells instead of the interface the law is applied to. That made the law inert, and it was corrected and re-run.
    6. Reduced scales (memory).
    7. T4 at 16 × 16 instead of 32 × 32 (killed at 13 GB).
    8. T6, T7 and T10 at 8 × 8 instead of 16 × 16: the separator factorisation of the generic layer ran out of memory. Those failures are kept as results.
    9. T1 has no m = 48 point at 16,384 blocks (beyond 15 GB).
    10. T4 "exact path" is the condensation of every block followed by the PCG correction to the fine solution. The all-exact-blocks solve of the learned study still uses m-mode interfaces (item 4 of "What breaks").
    11. Implementation defects found and fixed during the runs; affected studies re-run.
    12. The block count of the algebraic partition counted unknowns on the far boundary as a new block (F1–F3 re-run; verdicts unchanged).
    13. The meshless k-nearest-neighbour search broke ties differently in different blocks. M4 first measured 16 of 16 blocks distinct and a speedup of 0.26; after the fix, 9 of 16 distinct and 0.16.
    14. E1 budgets select a fraction of blocks; with one ring of halo the solved fraction is larger. It is reported as such.
